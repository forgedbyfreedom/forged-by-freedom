# The Next Wave: Bimagrumab, Myostatin Blockers, and the Experimental Compounds Rewriting Body Recomposition

Every few years a compound comes along that makes you stop and recalibrate what you thought was physiologically possible. Bimagrumab is one of those compounds.

Not because it's a better version of something we already have. Not because it shaves a few extra pounds off a GLP-1 curve. Because it does something that, until recently, clinical science had not demonstrated at scale in human beings: it simultaneously strips fat and builds lean mass. At the same time. In the same subjects. Without a structured exercise protocol. Without caloric restriction.

That sentence needs to sit for a moment, because it violates the central trade-off that has governed body recomposition thinking for decades. You can lose fat or you can gain muscle, and doing both at once requires either elite genetics, pharmaceutical assistance, or a very specific novice training window. Bimagrumab doesn't care about any of those constraints. It operates at a deeper level — the signaling architecture that governs whether your body builds tissue or breaks it down — and when you understand the mechanism, the results stop looking like an anomaly and start looking inevitable.

This article is a full briefing on bimagrumab, the ActRII pathway it targets, and the broader second-wave pipeline of metabolic compounds being developed alongside it. If you're already running GLP-1 protocols, you need to understand where this space is heading.

## Why Bimagrumab Is Different From Everything That Came Before

Most anabolic compounds work downstream. Testosterone, selective androgen receptor modulators, IGF-1 analogs — they all deliver a signal that says "build." They operate at the receptor level, amplifying an anabolic output.

Bimagrumab works upstream. It's a dual monoclonal antibody targeting both ActRIIA and ActRIIB — the two receptor isoforms that form the central signaling hub for the body's anabolic brake system. What does that brake system include? Myostatin. GDF11. Activin A. Activin B. All four converge on the same ActRII receptors. Bimagrumab blocks them simultaneously.

This is not the same as a myostatin inhibitor. Myostatin blockers (we'll cover trevogrumab and apitegromab shortly) are selective — they hit one ligand. Bimagrumab removes the entire brake assembly. The physiological consequences are meaningfully different.

Here's the piece most people miss: these same ActRII receptors are expressed in adipose tissue, not just muscle. In fat tissue, activin signaling actively suppresses two critical lipases — hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) and adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL) — the enzymes responsible for liberating stored triglycerides from adipocytes. Activin signaling also suppresses beta-3 adrenergic receptor expression in fat, which means it blunts the thermogenic and lipolytic response to catecholamines. Block the ActRII receptors and you remove all of that suppression simultaneously. You liberate lipolysis in adipose tissue at the same time you remove the anabolic brake in muscle. There's also evidence of brown adipose tissue thermogenesis activation through this pathway.

This is why bimagrumab produces a result no other compound class has matched at clinical scale: simultaneous anabolic and lipolytic activity through a single mechanism.

## The Number You Need to Know: -20.5% Fat, +3.6% Lean

In 2021, a Phase 2 randomized controlled trial published in JAMA Network Open enrolled obese and type 2 diabetic patients and treated them with bimagrumab via IV infusion every four weeks for 48 weeks. No caloric restriction protocol. No structured exercise intervention.

Fat mass decreased by 20.5%. Lean mass increased by 3.6%.

Simultaneously. In the same subjects. In less than a year.

That is the benchmark. And it was achieved in a population that was metabolically compromised — obese, diabetic — which makes it, if anything, an underestimate of what the mechanism can do in a healthier, more metabolically responsive subject.

Novartis originally developed bimagrumab for inclusion body myositis (IBM), an autoimmune muscle-wasting disease. The 2016 RESILIENT trial failed — not because the mechanism didn't work, but because IBM destroys muscle through T-cell mediated autoimmune attack. Building muscle faster doesn't matter if the immune system is continuing to destroy it. The compound worked. The indication was wrong. That distinction matters enormously for how you interpret the IBM failure.

Versanis Bio licensed bimagrumab from Novartis specifically targeting obesity, having correctly identified that the compound's dual mechanism was exactly what the obesity pharmacology space was missing. In July 2023, Eli Lilly acquired Versanis for $1.925 billion. The stated rationale was explicit: combine bimagrumab with tirzepatide to address the lean mass loss problem that plagues all GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonists.

## The BELIEVE Trial and What the 2026 Nature Medicine Data Shows

The BELIEVE Phase 2 trial ran 72 weeks and combined bimagrumab with semaglutide. The results, published in Nature Medicine in 2026, are the most compelling body recomposition data in the clinical literature.

Combined bimagrumab plus semaglutide produced:
- -22.1% total body weight
- -45.7% fat mass
- -58.2% visceral fat
- Lean mass loss of only -2.9%, compared to -7.4% with semaglutide alone

92.2% of the combination's weight loss came from fat. In semaglutide monotherapy, roughly 25-40% of total weight loss comes from lean tissue. The combination essentially eliminated lean mass loss as a meaningful side effect.

That visceral fat number deserves special attention. Visceral fat is not merely aesthetic — it's the metabolically active, inflammatory fat depot that drives cardiovascular risk, insulin resistance, and systemic inflammation. A 58.2% reduction in visceral fat at 72 weeks is a cardiovascular risk intervention in addition to a body composition intervention.

## Why Lilly Cancelled the T2D Trial — And Why That's Not a Failure Signal

In September 2025, Lilly cancelled the bimagrumab plus tirzepatide Phase 2b trial in type 2 diabetic patients. The stated reason was "strategic business reasons." The internet interpreted this as a failure. That interpretation is wrong.

The Phase 1 completed without safety issues. The compound wasn't cancelled. The T2D indication was deprioritized.

To understand why, you have to think about Lilly's business position. Tirzepatide's dominance in the type 2 diabetes market is already established. The regulatory path for adding bimagrumab to that indication requires demonstrating a meaningful benefit over existing standard of care in a population with complex comorbidities, multiple competing medications, and outcomes that are harder to characterize cleanly. The commercial upside of being best-in-class in an already-dominated indication is limited.

The obesity-without-T2D population is a different story entirely. That population has fewer confounders, a cleaner regulatory path, and a benefit narrative — preserving lean mass during GLP-1-driven weight loss — that is a genuine unmet need and a legitimate differentiator in a crowded GLP-1 market. The BELIEVE data makes this case compellingly.

A separate bimagrumab trial in obesity without type 2 diabetes is still running. Results are expected 2026-2027.

Lilly paid $1.925 billion for this asset. They didn't walk away from it. They pivoted to the indication where it will do the most commercial and clinical work.

## The Rest of the Pipeline: Selective Myostatin Blockers and Glucagon Co-Agonists

Two parallel approaches are solving the GLP-1 muscle loss problem from different directions.

**ActRII selective blockers**

Trevogrumab (Regeneron) targets myostatin selectively rather than the full ActRII ligand set. In Phase 2 COURAGE trials combined with semaglutide, trevogrumab preserved 50.8% of lean mass that would otherwise have been lost. Adding an activin A blocker to the triple combination pushed that to 80.9%, though with safety signals that complicate development. Selective is less powerful than dual blockade — consistent with what the mechanism predicts.

Apitegromab (Scholar Rock) takes a different approach: it targets latent pro-myostatin, the inactive precursor form, rather than the active peptide. In Phase 2 EMBRAZE combined with tirzepatide, apitegromab preserved 54.9% more lean mass versus tirzepatide alone. The next-generation compound SRK-439 shows an additional property of potential significance — it appears to attenuate fat regain after GLP-1 discontinuation. Given the well-documented rebound weight gain when GLP-1 therapy is stopped, a compound that blunts that regain has a substantial addressable market.

**Glucagon co-agonism**

Retatrutide is a triple agonist — GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptor — that achieved 28.7% weight loss at 68 weeks in Phase 3. The glucagon arm drives additional fat oxidation that single and dual incretins miss; glucagon directly stimulates hepatic fat metabolism and thermogenesis in ways that GLP-1 alone doesn't capture. The discontinuation rate at the 12mg dose was 18.2%, primarily driven by skin dysesthesia, which is a tolerability issue that needs resolution before this compound reaches its commercial potential.

Pemvidutide is a GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist that has posted the most compelling lean mass preservation data in its class: only 21.9% of weight loss came from lean mass, versus approximately 40% for semaglutide. The glucagon component appears to drive preferential fat oxidation and spare protein, which is exactly what the mechanism predicts and what the GLP-1 monotherapy literature consistently fails to deliver.

## The Synthesis: What This Pipeline Means

The pattern across all of these compounds points to a single conclusion: GLP-1 receptor agonists are extraordinarily powerful fat loss tools, but their Achilles' heel — 25-40% of weight loss coming from lean tissue — is a solvable problem, and the second wave of metabolic drug development is solving it.

Two mechanistic routes are converging on the same goal. ActRII blockade removes the anabolic brake, making muscle-building and fat-liberation both easier at the same time. Glucagon co-agonism drives preferential fat oxidation through hepatic and thermogenic pathways, reducing the catabolic tax on lean tissue. Bimagrumab represents the most validated and most powerful clinical demonstration of the ActRII approach. Pemvidutide and retatrutide represent the glucagon approach.

The Lilly acquisition was not a hedge. It was a $1.9 billion statement about where pharmaceutical body recomposition is heading: combinations that produce extraordinary fat loss while preserving or building lean mass, using mechanisms that operate at a level of physiological depth that no current commercially available compound reaches.

## Why GH Peptides Instead of Synthetic HGH?

Exogenous recombinant HGH (rHGH, also called somatropin) is effective. It's also expensive, requires a prescription in the US, and when dosed supraphysiologically, it tends to produce a sustained, non-pulsatile elevation in GH that blunts your pituitary's own output over time through negative feedback. Long-term users frequently experience desensitization and find it difficult to maintain normal endogenous GH secretion off-cycle.

GH peptides work differently. Rather than replacing your GH, they stimulate your pituitary to release it in bursts that closely mirror the natural pulsatile pattern your body uses — primarily the large pulse that occurs during deep slow-wave sleep. This means:

- **Cost:** A 3-month supply of CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin from a vetted research peptide supplier runs approximately $60–$120, compared to $1,200–$2,400 for an equivalent HGH protocol.
- **Legality:** In the US, research peptides occupy a legal gray area distinct from scheduled pharmaceuticals. They are not DEA-controlled substances. That said, they are not FDA-approved for human use, which is why they are sold for research purposes.
- **Physiological pulse preservation:** Because peptides work upstream — stimulating rather than replacing — your axis remains functional. You're amplifying what your body already does, not shutting it down.

The tradeoff is ceiling effect. GH peptides cannot match the supraphysiological GH levels achievable with 4–6 IU/day of rHGH. For competitive bodybuilders chasing extreme GH-driven effects, peptides are supplementary. For everyone else, they deliver meaningful results with a far cleaner risk-to-benefit profile.

## The Two Types: GHRH Analogues vs. GHRPs

To understand why specific peptides are stacked together, you need to understand the two receptor systems involved.

**GHRH Analogues** (Growth Hormone Releasing Hormone analogues) act on the GHRH receptor in the pituitary. They prime the pituitary and tell it to prepare a GH pulse. By themselves, they produce a modest, blunted release — functional, but submaximal.

**GHRPs** (Growth Hormone Releasing Peptides) act on the ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a). They trigger the actual release of GH. By themselves, they produce a stronger acute pulse but without the priming effect of GHRH stimulation.

Stack them together — a GHRH analogue with a GHRP — and the response is synergistic, not merely additive. The combined pulse can be 3–10x greater than either compound alone. This is the foundational logic behind every serious GH peptide protocol.

## CJC-1295: With DAC vs. Without DAC

CJC-1295 is a GHRH analogue. It comes in two versions, and the distinction matters significantly for how you run it.

**CJC-1295 without DAC (also called MOD GRF 1-29)** has a half-life of approximately 30 minutes. It produces a sharp, discrete GH pulse when injected and clears quickly. This is the version you want for pulsatile protocols — you inject it alongside a GHRP, you get a defined pulse, and the system resets. Most protocols call for 100 mcg per injection.

**CJC-1295 with DAC** (Drug Affinity Complex) extends the half-life to 6–8 days by binding the peptide to albumin in circulation. A single injection sustains elevated GHRH receptor stimulation for nearly a week. The appeal is convenience — one or two injections per week. The problem is that sustained GHRH receptor activation blunts the pulsatile nature of GH release and can lead to receptor desensitization with prolonged use. Some users also report water retention and mild insulin resistance similar to what is seen with continuous HGH exposure.

For most users running a performance or recovery protocol: **use CJC-1295 without DAC.** Keep the pulses sharp. Reserve the DAC version for situations where injection frequency is a hard constraint, and cycle it no longer than 8–10 weeks at a time.

## Ipamorelin: The Cleanest GHRP

Ipamorelin is a selective GHRP and the most popular peptide in this class for good reason. It binds the ghrelin receptor and triggers a strong GH pulse with a highly favorable side effect profile:

- **No significant cortisol elevation** (unlike GHRP-2 and Hexarelin)
- **No significant prolactin elevation**
- **Minimal hunger stimulation** (a major quality-of-life advantage)
- **No effect on ACTH**

The standard dose is 100 mcg per injection. At this dose, it produces a clean GH pulse that synergizes well with CJC-1295 without DAC. It is selective enough that you are not getting meaningful off-target hormonal noise — what you're getting is GH, and that's it.

For users who want the GH secretagogue effect without disrupting cortisol dynamics or dealing with hunger spikes, Ipamorelin is the default choice.

## GHRP-6 vs. GHRP-2: A Brief Note

These older GHRPs are effective and less expensive than Ipamorelin, but both come with trade-offs.

**GHRP-6** produces a strong GH pulse but significantly elevates ghrelin — the "hunger hormone" — in ways that Ipamorelin does not. Doses above 100 mcg reliably cause intense hunger within 20–30 minutes of injection. For users in a caloric deficit or who are insulin-sensitive, this is a real issue. It also produces modest cortisol and prolactin elevations.

**GHRP-2** is more potent per microgram than GHRP-6, produces less hunger stimulation, but elevates cortisol and prolactin more significantly. It is not ideal for prolonged protocols.

Both remain useful in specific contexts — GHRP-6's hunger effect can actually be an asset for hard gainers struggling to hit caloric targets. But for the majority of recomposition and recovery clients, Ipamorelin is the cleaner, more practical tool.

## The Gold Standard Stack: CJC-1295 No DAC + Ipamorelin

The combination most frequently used by FBF coaches with clients is:

- **CJC-1295 without DAC:** 100 mcg per injection
- **Ipamorelin:** 100 mcg per injection
- **Injected together** subcutaneously, in the same syringe (they are compatible)

**Timing matters.** GH is naturally released in pulses, with the largest pulse occurring 60–90 minutes after sleep onset during slow-wave sleep. To maximize benefit:

- **Minimum:** One injection per day, immediately before bed, on an empty stomach (fast 2–3 hours prior if possible — insulin blunts GH release)
- **Intermediate:** Two injections per day — pre-bed plus one in the morning fasted
- **Advanced:** Three injections per day — morning fasted, post-workout (at least 1 hour post-meal), and pre-bed

For most clients, two injections per day — particularly the pre-bed injection — produces meaningful, measurable results.

**Insulin management is real.** Food, especially carbohydrates, raises insulin, and elevated insulin directly suppresses GH release. If you inject with food in your system, you are blunting your own pulse. A 2–3 hour fast before injection is the minimum standard for effective dosing.

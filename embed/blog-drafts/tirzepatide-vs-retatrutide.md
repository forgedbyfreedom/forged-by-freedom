# Tirzepatide vs Retatrutide: Which GLP-1 Peptide is Right for Your Goals?

If you're an enhanced athlete or serious recomposition client, you've almost certainly heard about GLP-1 agonists by now. The conversation has moved way past Ozempic memes and celebrity weight loss. In 2025–2026, tirzepatide and retatrutide are the two compounds that serious coaches and clients are actually running — and the differences between them matter a lot depending on where you are and what you're trying to accomplish.

This article breaks down both compounds with real numbers, real tradeoffs, and zero hand-holding. We coach clients on both at FBF. Here's how we think about which one belongs in your stack.

## Tirzepatide: The Proven Workhorse

Tirzepatide (brand name Mounjaro / Zepbound) is a **dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist**. That dual mechanism is the whole story. GLP-1 agonists slow gastric emptying, reduce appetite, and improve insulin sensitivity. Tirzepatide stacks GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) receptor activity on top of that, which amplifies insulin secretion, improves lipid metabolism, and — critically for body composition clients — appears to do a better job preserving lean mass during aggressive fat loss compared to GLP-1-only compounds like semaglutide.

**Clinical numbers:** The SURMOUNT-1 trial put tirzepatide's average weight loss at **20.9% of body weight** at the 15 mg dose over 72 weeks. That is not a rounding error. For a 250 lb person, that's 52 lbs. Peak responders pushed past 25%.

**Dosing protocol:** Tirzepatide is dosed subcutaneously once weekly. Standard titration starts at 2.5 mg/week and steps up every 4 weeks — 2.5 → 5 → 7.5 → 10 → 12.5 → 15 mg. Most FBF clients hit their sweet spot between 5–10 mg/week. The titration isn't optional — it's how you stay ahead of the GI side effects.

**Availability:** Compounded tirzepatide from licensed pharmacies is widely available in the US right now and prices have come down significantly — expect $150–$300/month at therapeutic doses from a legitimate compounding pharmacy or telemedicine provider, depending on dose.

## Retatrutide: The Triple Threat

Retatrutide is where things get interesting. It's a **triple agonist: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptor** — all three hit simultaneously. That glucagon component is the differentiator. Glucagon is catabolic by nature (it drives hepatic glucose output), but at the doses dialed into retatrutide's pharmacology, glucagon receptor activation appears to drive significant increases in energy expenditure and thermogenesis rather than meaningful muscle catabolism — especially when protein intake and resistance training are maintained.

**Clinical numbers:** The Phase 2 TRIUMPH trial data published in 2023 is striking. At the 12 mg dose over 48 weeks, retatrutide produced average weight loss of **24.2% of body weight** — with some participants exceeding 30%. That outpaces tirzepatide in a shorter timeframe. The glucagon axis appears to drive the additional fat oxidation beyond what dual agonism alone achieves.

**Dosing protocol:** Retatrutide is also once-weekly subcutaneous. Titration typically starts at 2 mg/week, stepping up through 4 mg → 8 mg → 12 mg over 12–20 weeks depending on tolerance. Rushing titration is a fast track to nausea, vomiting, and losing compliance. Most of the FBF clients who have run it find their working dose between 6–10 mg/week. The 12 mg ceiling dose is aggressive — not everyone needs to go there.

**Availability:** Retatrutide is still in Phase 3 trials as of early 2026. It is not FDA-approved. That means sourcing is through research compound vendors and select compounding channels. Quality and purity vary. FBF vets suppliers rigorously and only works with clients using verified, tested product. This isn't a compound to be cavalier about sourcing.

## Head-to-Head: What Actually Matters

### Fat Loss Rate

Retatrutide wins on raw numbers. The triple-agonist mechanism — particularly that glucagon-driven thermogenic upregulation — drives faster and deeper fat loss than tirzepatide in direct timeline comparisons. If pure fat loss velocity is the goal and you're on a deadline (contest prep transition, aggressive recomp window), retatrutide has the edge.

That said, tirzepatide at 15 mg is not leaving anything on the table. For most clients who aren't in a rush, the difference is meaningful but not night-and-day once you account for individual response variance.

### Muscle Preservation

Both compounds preserve lean mass significantly better than caloric restriction alone. The GIP receptor component in both compounds appears to be the primary driver here — GIP has anabolic and lean-tissue-sparing properties that pure GLP-1 agonism lacks.

The glucagon component in retatrutide raised early concerns about muscle catabolism. Current clinical data does not support that concern at therapeutic doses in the context of adequate protein intake (1+ g/lb of goal bodyweight) and resistance training. FBF clients on retatrutide who are training and hitting protein targets are holding muscle just fine. Clients who aren't training and aren't hitting protein targets lose muscle on any aggressive deficit — the peptide doesn't change that equation.

### Side Effect Profile

Both share the same class of GI side effects: nausea, reduced appetite (which is the point), occasional vomiting, constipation or loose stools during titration, and potential gallstone risk with rapid fat loss.

Retatrutide's side effect burden is modestly higher, particularly during titration. The glucagon receptor activity adds some cardiovascular considerations — heart rate increases have been observed in trials, typically 5–10 bpm at higher doses. This is worth monitoring in clients with pre-existing cardiovascular concerns.

Tirzepatide is better characterized at this point, with a larger clinical dataset and more real-world user experience behind it. If you're conservative by nature or new to GLP-1 agonists, tirzepatide is the cleaner starting point.

### Cost and Availability

Tirzepatide is the clear winner here. FDA approval means compounded access at scale, established supply chains, and prices that have dropped dramatically. Retatrutide requires more diligence to source correctly and typically costs more. For clients outside the US, sourcing dynamics differ — but the same principle applies: the more established compound is easier to obtain reliably.

## Who Should Run Which

**Run tirzepatide if:**
- You're new to GLP-1 agonists and want established safety data behind you
- You want legal, compounded access through a telemedicine provider
- Your fat loss goal is substantial but not extreme, or your timeline is 6+ months
- You have any cardiovascular history that makes the glucagon receptor component a concern
- You're earlier in your recomp journey and building the lifestyle infrastructure alongside the pharmacological assist

**Run retatrutide if:**
- You've already run tirzepatide or semaglutide and want to push further
- You have a meaningful amount of fat to lose and want to compress your timeline
- You're in a well-supervised protocol with training and protein dialed in
- You understand the sourcing landscape and are working with a coach who vets supply
- You're comfortable with the fact that you're running a pre-approval compound and have appropriate oversight

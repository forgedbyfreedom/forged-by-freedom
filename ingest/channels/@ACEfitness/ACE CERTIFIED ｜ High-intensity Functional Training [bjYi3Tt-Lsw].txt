High-intensity functional training or HIFT involves a combination of cardiorespiratory and muscular training exercises performed at high intensity and HIFT can be implemented several different ways as an accessory work for strength athletes, as cross training for endurance athletes, or also a standalone exercise training. There's really three key takeaway messages from this month's article. First is HIFT is effective. We already know from research that high-intensity interval training is as good as or superior to continuous moderate intensity training, but HIFT tends to be more time efficient. So if we combine HIFT with muscular training, we get augmented benefits and we get benefits in a number of different important fitness parameters including cardiorespiratory fitness, muscular strength, as well as anaerobic power. The second key takeaway is that HIFT is safe. Injury incident rates of HIFT are comparable, actually in most instances, lower than other forms of training such as weight training, power lifting, and running. And then the third key takeaway is that research has demonstrated that HIFT is enjoyable.

This is a chinup exercise. The benefit of doing a chinup position is that it keeps the forearm parallel to one another which reduces stress at the elbow. It's important to step up to the bar on an appropriately sized box or bench, we want to take a palm up grip with the wrist about shoulder width apart so that the wrist, the elbow and the shoulder are on one straight line. Keep the chest lifted, the spine straight and the legs squeezed together and think about pulling your chest up right between your hands and slowly bringing your body back down under control. The one key tip for the chinup is maintain a tight grip throughout the movement. You want to think about squeezing the bar between your hands like you're squeezing water out of a sponge. Perform only as many chinups as you can under control with good form. When teaching the chinup to your client, encourage your clients not to use momentum. You want to teach your client how to be able to pull their body up just using their upper body strength of their arms and upper back. Your client may only be able to get one or two repetitions at a time which is fine. Start with one or two repetitions, rest and continue doing them. One recommendation is to do a couple repetitions, move on to another exercise and then return back to the chinup after your client has rested a little bit.

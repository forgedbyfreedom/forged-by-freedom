Developing an elite mindset. Teaching athletes to choose their perspective. You see, an elite mindset doesn't mean arrogance or perfection. It means resilience, adaptability, and decisions. Because decisions determine your future, but your decisions are largely gonna be impacted by your elite mindset. You see, there's a great parable about two stone cutters, and when asked what they were doing, one says, I'm cutting stone, can't you see? The other says? I'm building a cathedral can't you see? Same task, different mindset. One sees labor, the other sees purpose. That's an elite mindset. Seeing meaning in the mundane purpose in the process. When you teach your athletes as a coach to choose their perspective. Everything changes. You empower them by educating them on mindset. A miss shop becomes feedback. A benching becomes motivation. Adversity becomes their advantage. Opportunity is around you everywhere. I remember working with the college softball team. They adapted a acronym called W.I.N. as their mantra. It stood for What's Important Now? They stopped dwelling on bad calls errors the past, and they started focusing on the next pitch. And when they did that, they started winning the next pitch. And that simple shift took them from the middle of the pack to be Southeastern Conference Champions. So were you as a coach helping your athletes to see cathedrals? Or just cutting stones?

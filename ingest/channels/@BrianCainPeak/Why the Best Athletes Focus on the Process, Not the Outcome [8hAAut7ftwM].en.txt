Process over outcome at the foundation of that approach is controlling the things that you can control. Three controllables every match, energy, effort, and execution. It reminds me of a great parable of the two archers. The first archer focuses entirely on hitting the bullseye. The second focuses on his breath stance, pulling the string in the release. Well guess who hits the mark more often? The Archer focused the process. You see, teaching athletes to focus on what they can control gives them power. It frees them from outcome and anxiety and anchors them in the present. So as a coach, are your athletes defined by the results? Or they see success as the execution of their process?

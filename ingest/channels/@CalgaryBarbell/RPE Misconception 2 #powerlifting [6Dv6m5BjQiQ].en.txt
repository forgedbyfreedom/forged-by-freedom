RP is just there to let coaches be lazy. Write a number in like a man. This one couldn't be further from the truth. I'd argue that putting a percentage that your lifter has to hit no matter what into a spreadsheet is far more lazy. Using RP well as a coach means that you're taking the time to educate and contextualize the use of RP with your athlete. You're teaching them what an RP8 means to you, what you expect to see when an RP8 is written, and then they're having to do the work on their end to put the right weight on the bar and execute to a standard. All of this information is contained within me saying RP8 to an athlete, but all the subtext and the context and the nuance and circumstance of that RP8 are all things that need to be built up over the course of developing a relationship with the athlete. So RP is really a a linguistic tool. It's a way of quantifying and communicating more effectively with your athlete while teaching them that they have to be more independent and they have to do a better job in order to meet your expectations.

Let's start off with one of my favorite movements that highlights something that lifters mess up all of the time. The long pause bench press. If you want to build a big bench, you've got to be strong and skilled while the bar is on your chest. Many raw lifters think that their lockout is their weakness when in actuality what happens is they lose position either while touching their chest or while pressing off of the chest. Long pause bench is a classic and it's in damn near every program that I write. Get better at pausing, get better at benching. If we can be stronger in that bottom position and in breaking inertia, we're going to put kilos on the bench. Period. You never heard period? No. No. I'll usually program these for no more than about four reps and no less than about a seven RP because we want some intensity and we want some specificity on this movement. I'll also differentiate between a twocount pause bench and a 3ount pause bench. Often swapping between those in programming. So the key to long pause bench is to make sure that the bar is motionless. No bounce, no momentum during the pause. And be careful at how quickly you count. Every single week, I remind lifters that their long pauses need to be longer.

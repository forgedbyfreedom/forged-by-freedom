The fifth and final accessory movement that I want to include here is the leg press. Most gyms have one. While it might not be the most specific, and you might know a number of people who can leg press a lot and can't squat for it can be a really great tool if the bottleneck in your powerlifting training is getting more work on your legs. Because this doesn't load the back, you can get away with hammering volume on this thing. And I also just have a personal bias towards it. When I was rehabbing my knee and coming back from 2 years of my knee feeling like absolute garbage, this baby made it feel better every time. Things to note about the leg press are number one, get a very full range of motion. Doing quarter reps on the leg press is akin to curling in the squat rack, and you don't want to be that person. When you're using the leg press, I would try to do something similar to your squat stance on here if you can on the plate. And also the back angle on most leg presses is adjustable. So adjust it up or down so you can get a more comfortable experience. A lot of the times when I hop onto a leg press and somebody else has had it set, feels terrible. You adjust that back angle correctly, you're smooth sailing. There are great many other non-specific leg building exercises that you could include on this list. hack squats and belt squats and other kind of contraptions, pendulum squats and things like that. I'm sure those are all great. Go ahead and try those out if you want. But I like the leg press.

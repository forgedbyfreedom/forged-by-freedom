Mistake number four, poor elbow positioning. I think a lot of lifters in the squat will try to use their elbows and either pushing their elbows forwards or backwards to try to make up for a lack of tension in the upper back. When lifters drive their elbows forwards out of the bottom of their squat, they're trying to make up for the fact that they haven't created a stable and tight upper back position to start with. And by wedging the shoulder joint underneath it as they come up, in some cases, you can have what approximates to a band-aid solution in keeping the upper back tight. However, in the long run and with maximal or super maximal weights in the case of a powerlifting meet, this will break down. The other issue with elbow positioning is a lot of more novice lifters who maybe aren't good at creating a stable shelf to unrackck and walk out with will push their elbows up behind them to to flare their shoulder blades off of their rib cage and create a shelf for the bar. This is because they're afraid to be in any kind of hinge when picking and unrackcking. My advice when it comes to elbow position, and this will kind of logically follow if we get the shoulder blades and upper back packed the way we need to, is that with your elbows, if you're going to think about queuing them, think about queuing them in towards your sides or in towards the center of your low back. Imagine there's a bullseye in the middle of your low back. You're trying to pull your elbows towards it. This should help you put your shoulder blades in a position where they're going to be tight and stable, and you're not going to need to be jerking your elbows forwards or backwards to make up for that lack of tightness.

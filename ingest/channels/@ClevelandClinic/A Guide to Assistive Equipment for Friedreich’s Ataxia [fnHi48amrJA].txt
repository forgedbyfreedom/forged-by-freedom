 if you or a loved one has Friedrich's Ataxia, you know it can make everyday activities challenging. And because F-A is a progressive disease, your needs may change over time. But the right assistive equipment can help you maintain mobility. They can also help you keep up your energy, prevent injuries, and make your day-to-day a little easier. Let's explore some of the most commonly used solutions for people with F-A. Number one, canes. Canes help with balance and stability. They can be especially useful in the early stages of F-A. But getting the right kind for your needs is important. A healthcare provider can help you choose the right size, material, and design. Number two, walkers. Walkers can provide additional support while walking. Standard walkers have rubber-tipped feet for stability. And wheeled walkers and rollators offer easier maneuverability. Which is best for you depends on your level of ability and the specific use. Walkers can provide more stability if you have trouble with balance. Rollators may be a better choice for getting around outside. Number three, wheelchairs. As walking becomes more difficult, a wheelchair can provide reliable mobility. They can be self-propelled or pushed by someone else. Power wheelchairs offer even greater independence and joystick controls. Number four, scooters. Mobility scooters are powered by batteries and have handlebars for steering, making them useful both indoors and out. And many of them can be broken down to transport easily. Scooters can be easier to maneuver than wheelchairs. But a wheelchair may be more comfortable for longer periods of time. Check with your insurance to see if you qualify for a wheelchair or scooter. Number five, home modifications. Inside your home, installing handrails can provide you with support and peace of mind. They can be especially helpful to help you navigate hallways, bathrooms, and around staircases. And stair lifts can help you navigate multiple floors without the risk of falling. Six, adaptive clothing. If your fine motor skills have been affected by FA, dressing yourself can become difficult. The right clothing can make getting dressed easier and more comfortable. Look for shirts with magnetic closures instead of buttons, pants with elastic waistbands instead of zippers, and shoes with fabric fasteners instead of laces. As FA progresses, your needs may change. A healthcare professional, like a physician or physical therapist, can help you find the assistive devices and mobility aids that will work best for you.

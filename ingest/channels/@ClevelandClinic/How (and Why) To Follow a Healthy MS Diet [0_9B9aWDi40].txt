Some foods are known to calm inflammation, while others fire it up. The more you can swap out inflammatory foods, the more room you make for inflammation fighters. Another benefit, eating well can help lower your risk for other chronic conditions that make managing MS even harder. That includes things like diabetes, heart disease, and obesity. So where do you start? Foods that can help include fruits and vegetables, whole grains like oats, quinoa, and brown rice, lean proteins like fish, poultry, beans, and nuts, extra virgin olive oil, and low-fat or fat-free dairy. And the foods you want to scale back on? They include red meat, full-fat dairy, fried or processed foods, added sugars, refined carbs like white bread and pasta, and butter, mayo, and creamy salad dressings. Some people find that avoiding or limiting gluten or dairy products helps too, but that's not the case for everyone. If you are looking for a pre-planned diet that can help with MS, check out the Mediterranean diet. It's packed with heart-healthy, anti-inflammatory foods, and is easy to follow. Or talk to a healthcare provider to personalize your plan. There's no perfect MS diet, but there is a perfect strategy. Choose whole, nourishing foods most of the time. Cut back on processed, sugary, and fatty foods. And pay attention to how your body responds.

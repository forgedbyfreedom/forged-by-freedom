 Your heart's aortic valve plays a vital role in how your body works. As blood circulates through your heart, it makes its way to your aortic valve, which allows blood to flow out to the rest of your body. But when you have aortic stenosis, this valve can become narrowed or blocked, typically by a calcium buildup. In some cases, people are born with abnormalities of their valve that may progress stenosis over time. This makes it more difficult for blood to move through. So how quickly does aortic stenosis progress? It can vary from person to person. Your doctor will use tests like a cardiac CT scan or an echocardiogram to determine the stage of your aortic stenosis. Based on your test results, your aortic stenosis may be classified as mild. Your valve opening is more than 1.5 square centimeters. Moderate. Your valve opening is between 1.1 to 1.5 square centimeters. Severe. Your valve opening is less than 1.0 square centimeters. Certain risk factors can contribute to how fast it develops, like being older than 65, having high blood pressure, having high cholesterol, having coronary artery disease, CID, having kidney disease, using tobacco products. Studies show that on average, aortic stenosis gets narrower between 0.1 square centimeters to 0.3 square centimeters every year. There's a chance you could have aortic stenosis and not even know it. You may not have any symptoms until your aortic stenosis progresses to the moderate or severe degree. That's why it's important to talk to your healthcare provider about symptoms like chest pain, fainting, and shortness of breath. You can also be proactive about your heart health to limit how quickly your aortic stenosis advances. Your doctor may recommend following a heart-healthy diet, taking medications or even surgery or non-surgical options like TAVR, Trans-Catheter Aortic Valve Replacement.

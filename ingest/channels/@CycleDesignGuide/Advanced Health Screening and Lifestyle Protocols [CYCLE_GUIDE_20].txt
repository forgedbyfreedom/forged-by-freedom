FORGED BY FREEDOM CYCLE DESIGN GUIDE
GUIDE 20: ADVANCED HEALTH SCREENING AND LIFESTYLE PROTOCOLS
Compiled by the Forged by Freedom coaching team — Wendy and Bryan Antonelli

This guide covers three critical health areas frequently overlooked by enhanced athletes:
sleep apnea screening, mental health and neurochemistry monitoring, and diuretic safety
for peak week and contest prep. These topics directly impact bloodwork interpretation,
cycle safety, and long-term health outcomes.

================================================================================
SECTION 1: SLEEP APNEA SCREENING FOR ENHANCED ATHLETES
================================================================================

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is one of the most underdiagnosed conditions in the
enhanced bodybuilding population. The combination of increased muscle mass, fluid
retention from anabolic compounds, and neck hypertrophy creates a perfect storm for
airway obstruction during sleep.

SUBSECTION: WHY ENHANCED ATHLETES ARE AT ELEVATED RISK

Anabolic-androgenic steroids contribute to OSA through multiple mechanisms:

1. Neck circumference growth — Testosterone and other androgens cause hypertrophy of
   neck musculature (sternocleidomastoid, scalenes, trapezius). A neck circumference
   above 17 inches in males or 16 inches in females is a major risk factor.

2. Fluid retention — Testosterone, nandrolone, and especially dianabol and anadrol
   cause significant water retention. This fluid accumulates in upper airway tissues,
   narrowing the pharyngeal space during sleep.

3. Soft tissue thickening — Androgens cause thickening of pharyngeal soft tissues
   including the tongue, soft palate, and lateral pharyngeal walls. Growth hormone
   compounds this effect through IGF-1-mediated tissue growth.

4. Body mass — Enhanced athletes carrying 220+ pounds regardless of body fat percentage
   are at elevated risk simply due to mass distribution and thoracic pressure when supine.

5. GH-induced tissue growth — Growth hormone at doses above 2-3 IU per day accelerates
   soft tissue growth in the airway. The combination of GH plus testosterone is
   particularly problematic for OSA development.

Scott McNally on Blood Sweat and Gear has discussed how many competitive bodybuilders
develop sleep-disordered breathing during off-season phases when body weight is highest,
and how this directly impairs recovery and growth.

SUBSECTION: SCREENING TOOLS AND ASSESSMENT

STOP-BANG Questionnaire — Score 1 point for each:
- Snoring: Do you snore loudly (louder than talking or heard through closed doors)?
- Tired: Do you often feel tired, fatigued, or sleepy during daytime?
- Observed: Has anyone observed you stop breathing during sleep?
- Pressure: Do you have or are you being treated for high blood pressure?
- BMI: BMI greater than 35 (or simply being a 230+ pound muscular male)
- Age: Age over 50
- Neck: Neck circumference greater than 17 inches (male) or 16 inches (female)
- Gender: Male sex

Scoring interpretation:
- 0-2: Low risk for OSA
- 3-4: Intermediate risk — consider home sleep test
- 5-8: High risk — strongly recommend polysomnography (in-lab sleep study)

For enhanced athletes, a score of 3 or higher should prompt evaluation. Most enhanced
males over 220 pounds will score at least 3-4 based on neck size, gender, and body
mass alone.

Epworth Sleepiness Scale — Rate likelihood of dozing (0-3) in 8 situations:
- Score above 10: Excessive daytime sleepiness, warrants evaluation
- Score above 15: Severe sleepiness, urgent evaluation needed

SUBSECTION: WHEN TO GET A SLEEP STUDY

Indications for sleep study in enhanced athletes:
- Neck circumference above 17 inches with any snoring
- Partner reports witnessed apneas (breathing stops during sleep)
- Unrefreshing sleep despite 7-8 hours in bed
- Morning headaches (from overnight CO2 retention)
- Nocturia (waking 2+ times to urinate — related to ANP release from apnea)
- Hematocrit persistently elevated above 52% despite hydration and phlebotomy
- Blood pressure resistant to medication (refractory hypertension)
- Unexplained fatigue despite adequate sleep duration

Types of sleep studies:
- Polysomnography (PSG): In-lab, gold standard. Measures EEG, EMG, ECG, SpO2,
  airflow, respiratory effort, body position. Cost: $500-3000 with insurance.
- Home Sleep Test (HST): Portable device measuring airflow, SpO2, respiratory effort.
  Cost: $150-500. Adequate for moderate-to-severe OSA but may miss mild cases.

AHI (Apnea-Hypopnea Index) interpretation:
- Less than 5: Normal
- 5-14: Mild OSA
- 15-29: Moderate OSA
- 30+: Severe OSA

SUBSECTION: IMPACT ON BLOODWORK AND PED SIDE EFFECTS

Sleep apnea directly amplifies multiple PED side effects:

Hematocrit elevation — OSA causes intermittent hypoxia during sleep, which stimulates
erythropoietin (EPO) production by the kidneys. This compounds the polycythemia already
caused by testosterone. An athlete on 500mg testosterone per week with untreated OSA may
see hematocrit climb to 54-56%, whereas the same dose without OSA might only reach 50-52%.

Blood pressure — OSA causes sympathetic nervous system activation during apneic episodes.
This raises baseline blood pressure and makes hypertension harder to control with standard
interventions like telmisartan or lisinopril. Many cases of "treatment-resistant
hypertension" in bodybuilders are actually undiagnosed OSA.

insulin sensitivity — OSA impairs glucose metabolism through cortisol elevation and
sympathetic activation. Athletes on GH who develop elevated fasting glucose may find that
treating OSA improves their glucose numbers more than adding metformin.

Cardiovascular risk — The combination of OSA plus elevated hematocrit plus high blood
pressure plus left ventricular hypertrophy (from both AAS and OSA) creates compounding
cardiac risk. This is why cardiac screening is especially important for heavy athletes.

Recovery and growth — OSA fragments sleep architecture, reducing time in deep sleep
(stages 3-4) where GH secretion peaks. Athletes spending money on exogenous GH while
having untreated OSA are undermining their own recovery.

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle has noted that many competitive bodybuilders in the off-season
develop sleep apnea that resolves or improves significantly during contest prep as body
weight drops and fluid retention decreases.

SUBSECTION: MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES

CPAP therapy — Continuous positive airway pressure is the gold standard treatment.
Modern auto-CPAP machines adjust pressure automatically. Compliance is key — must be
used every night for full benefit. Many athletes resist CPAP but the improvement in
sleep quality, blood pressure, and hematocrit is dramatic.

Weight management — Not always applicable for bodybuilders who are intentionally heavy,
but during cruise or TRT phases, allowing body weight to settle to a more manageable
level can significantly improve AHI scores.

Positional therapy — Some athletes only have apnea when sleeping on their back (supine).
A positional therapy device or simply training to sleep on your side can reduce AHI by
50% or more in positional OSA.

Mandibular advancement devices — Custom-fitted oral appliances that advance the lower
jaw forward, opening the airway. Effective for mild-to-moderate OSA. Cost: $500-2000
for custom devices from a dentist.

Compound considerations:
- During heavy bulking cycles (especially with GH + test + wet compounds), monitor for
  OSA development even if no prior history
- Consider reducing or eliminating GH if OSA is severe and unresponsive to CPAP
- Nandrolone and dianabol cause more fluid retention and may worsen OSA more than drier
  compounds like primobolan or masteron
- Contest prep typically improves OSA as body weight, fluid retention, and neck
  circumference all decrease

SECTION 2: MENTAL HEALTH AND NEUROCHEMISTRY MARKERS
================================================================================

The impact of performance-enhancing drugs on mental health is one of the least discussed
but most significant aspects of enhanced bodybuilding. Every major compound class affects
neurotransmitter systems, and understanding these effects is critical for both safety
and quality of life.

SUBSECTION: HOW PEDS AFFECT NEUROTRANSMITTER SYSTEMS

Testosterone and dopamine — Testosterone directly modulates dopaminergic activity in
the mesolimbic reward pathway. Supraphysiological testosterone doses (500mg+ per week)
significantly elevate dopamine signaling, which explains the confidence, motivation, and
drive that athletes report. However, this also means that returning to baseline or
sub-baseline testosterone (during PCT or after discontinuation) can cause a relative
dopamine deficit, contributing to depression and anhedonia.

Testosterone and serotonin — Testosterone has complex interactions with serotonergic
systems. Moderate doses tend to stabilize mood, while very high doses can increase
irritability and reduce impulse control. The relationship is dose-dependent and
individual — some athletes tolerate 750mg per week with stable mood while others
become irritable at 300mg.

DHT and neurosteroids — Dihydrotestosterone and its metabolites (including
3-alpha-androstanediol) are potent positive modulators of GABA-A receptors, producing
anxiolytic and mood-stabilizing effects. This is why DHT-derivative compounds like
masteron and proviron are often reported to improve mood and confidence. Conversely,
5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride) can cause depression and
anxiety by blocking this pathway.

SUBSECTION: 19-nor COMPOUNDS AND MENTAL HEALTH

The 19-nor family (nandrolone, trenbolone, MENT/trestolone) has the most significant
impact on mental health of any compound class:

Nandrolone (Deca/NPP) and depression — Nandrolone is metabolized to dihydronandrolone
(DHN) which competes with DHT at neurosteroid receptor sites but has much weaker
activity. The result is a functional DHT deficiency in the brain, which can cause:
- Low mood, flat affect, emotional blunting
- Reduced libido despite adequate testosterone levels
- Anhedonia (inability to feel pleasure)
- Social withdrawal
- These effects can persist for weeks to months after discontinuation due to nandrolone's
  long metabolite clearance (18+ months detectable)

This is why experienced coaches recommend keeping testosterone higher than nandrolone in
any stack — the DHT from testosterone helps counterbalance the neurosteroid-depleting
effects of nandrolone. Scott McNally and Dave Crosland on ThinkBig recommend at least a
1.25:1 ratio of test to nandrolone, with some athletes needing 2:1 or higher.

Trenbolone and neurotoxicity — Trenbolone is the most neurologically active AAS:
- Strongly binds progesterone receptors in the brain, affecting GABA systems
- Causes significant sleep disruption (insomnia, night sweats, vivid dreams/nightmares)
- Increases aggression through effects on the amygdala and prefrontal cortex
- Can cause anxiety, paranoia, and irritability — commonly called "tren sides"
- Some users report dissociative symptoms or emotional dysregulation
- Effects are dose-dependent: 200mg per week is dramatically different from 500mg per week
- Night sweats and insomnia compound mental health effects through sleep deprivation

MENT (Trestolone) — Generally better tolerated mentally than nandrolone or trenbolone:
- Does not convert to DHN (unlike nandrolone), so less neurosteroid depletion
- Moderate progestogenic activity — less than trenbolone
- Some users report improved mood and wellbeing compared to testosterone alone
- However, high doses (25mg+ per day) can cause mood instability in susceptible individuals

SUBSECTION: MONITORING MARKERS FOR MENTAL HEALTH

PHQ-9 Depression Screening — A 9-question validated screening tool:
- Score 0-4: Minimal depression
- Score 5-9: Mild depression — monitor, consider lifestyle interventions
- Score 10-14: Moderate depression — strongly consider dose reduction of offending compounds
- Score 15-19: Moderately severe — professional evaluation recommended
- Score 20-27: Severe depression — professional intervention required, consider
  discontinuing all non-TRT compounds

GAD-7 Anxiety Screening — A 7-question validated screening tool:
- Score 0-4: Minimal anxiety
- Score 5-9: Mild anxiety — monitor
- Score 10-14: Moderate anxiety — evaluate compound contributions
- Score 15-21: Severe anxiety — professional evaluation, dose modification

Recommended screening frequency:
- Baseline before starting any cycle
- Every 4 weeks during cycle
- Weekly during PCT (highest risk period for depression)
- Monthly for 3 months after PCT completion

SUBSECTION: crashed estrogen AND MENTAL HEALTH

One of the most common causes of acute mental health crisis in enhanced athletes is
estrogen (E2) crashing from aromatase inhibitor (AI) overcorrection:

Symptoms of crashed E2:
- Severe anxiety and panic attacks
- Irritability and emotional volatility
- Joint pain and stiffness (synovial fluid depends on E2)
- Insomnia despite fatigue
- Depersonalization and brain fog
- Suicidal ideation in severe cases
- Loss of libido and erectile dysfunction

The threshold for problems is typically below 15-20 pg/mL for most men. Some athletes
feel terrible below 25 pg/mL.

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle has discussed how new users who get bloodwork showing E2 at
45-50 pg/mL panic and crash their estrogen with too much anastrozole or aromasin, not
realizing that 40-60 pg/mL is often perfectly fine for an enhanced athlete on 500mg
testosterone — the standard lab range of 10-40 does not apply.

Recovery from crashed E2:
- Discontinue all AI immediately
- Wait — estrogen will recover as aromatase enzyme is regenerated (takes 3-7 days
  for anastrozole discontinuation, longer for aromasin since it is a suicidal inhibitor
  and requires new enzyme synthesis over 7-14 days)
- Small dose of dianabol (10-20mg for 3-5 days) can accelerate E2 recovery due to high
  aromatization rate — this is a well-known trick discussed by multiple coaches
- If on anastrozole, switching to a lower dose or less frequent dosing is safer than
  switching to aromasin mid-cycle

SUBSECTION: PROLACTIN ELEVATION AND MOOD

Elevated prolactin (from 19-nor compounds, certain medications, or pituitary issues)
causes significant mood effects:
- Apathy and lack of motivation
- Anhedonia (inability to feel pleasure)
- Sexual dysfunction (erectile dysfunction, delayed orgasm, reduced libido)
- Emotional blunting — feeling "flat" or disconnected
- Can mimic symptoms of clinical depression

Management (see CYCLE_GUIDE_17 intervention ladders for full protocol):
- P5P (pyridoxal-5-phosphate) 100-200mg daily as first-line
- Cabergoline 0.25-0.5mg twice weekly if P5P insufficient
- Pramipexole as alternative to cabergoline (less common in bodybuilding)
- Address root cause — reduce or eliminate 19-nor compounds if prolactin persistently
  elevated above 25 ng/mL despite treatment

SUBSECTION: PCT AND MENTAL HEALTH

The post-cycle period is the highest risk time for mental health issues:

Why PCT is mentally difficult:
- Testosterone drops from supraphysiological to subnormal levels
- Estrogen fluctuates unpredictably as HPTA recovers
- Dopamine signaling decreases from loss of exogenous testosterone stimulation
- Cortisol rises relative to the now-low testosterone (catabolic shift)
- Strength and size losses are psychologically distressing
- Sleep quality often deteriorates
- Libido crashes, which affects relationships and self-esteem

Timeline for hormonal and mood recovery during PCT:
- Weeks 1-2: Worst period. Testosterone at nadir, SERM beginning to stimulate LH/FSH.
  Mood is lowest, fatigue highest, libido nonexistent.
- Weeks 3-4: LH and FSH begin to rise. Slight mood improvement. Still significantly
  below baseline.
- Weeks 5-8: Testosterone gradually climbing. Mood stabilizing. Energy improving but
  not back to normal.
- Weeks 8-12: Most athletes feel "functional" again. Full hormonal recovery may take
  3-6 months depending on cycle length and compounds used.
- Note: 19-nor compounds can suppress HPTA for 12-18+ months. PCT after nandrolone or
  trenbolone cycles takes significantly longer than testosterone-only cycles.

Strategies for maintaining mental health during PCT:
- Maintain exercise routine even when motivation is low — exercise is a potent
  antidepressant through BDNF and endorphin release
- Prioritize sleep: melatonin 0.5-1mg, magnesium glycinate 400mg, ashwagandha 600mg
  before bed
- Social support — isolation worsens PCT depression significantly
- L-tyrosine 500-1000mg in the morning for dopamine precursor support
- 5-HTP 100-200mg in the evening for serotonin support (do not combine with SSRIs)
- Vitamin D3 5000 IU daily — low vitamin D worsens depression and is common in athletes
  who cut calories or avoid sun
- Omega-3 fatty acids 2-4g daily — anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective
- Set realistic expectations: you WILL lose some size and strength. This is normal.
  The goal is to keep as much as possible while recovering health.

SUBSECTION: RED FLAGS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE PROFESSIONAL HELP

These symptoms require immediate professional evaluation regardless of cycle status:
- Suicidal ideation or self-harm thoughts
- Severe aggression that threatens relationships or safety
- Psychotic symptoms (hallucinations, delusions, severe paranoia)
- Dissociative episodes (feeling detached from reality, depersonalization)
- Severe insomnia (less than 3 hours per night for 3+ consecutive nights)
- Panic attacks occurring multiple times per week
- Inability to perform daily functions (work, self-care, relationships)

If these symptoms occur, the priority is safety, not the cycle. Discontinue offending
compounds, seek professional help, and inform the treating provider about all substances
being used. Many mental health professionals are more understanding about PED use than
athletes expect, and withholding this information prevents proper treatment.

IFBB Pro Paul Barnett on Anabolic Bodybuilding has spoken candidly about mental health
challenges during and after heavy cycles, emphasizing that mental health is as important
as physical health markers in determining whether a cycle is sustainable.

SECTION 3: DIURETIC SAFETY PROTOCOLS FOR PEAK WEEK AND CONTEST PREP
================================================================================

Diuretics are the single most dangerous category of drugs used in competitive
bodybuilding. More acute medical emergencies and deaths in bodybuilding are attributable
to diuretic misuse than to any other drug class, including insulin. Understanding proper
protocols, monitoring, and emergency response is critical.

SUBSECTION: CLASSES OF DIURETICS USED IN BODYBUILDING

Loop Diuretics — Furosemide (Lasix), Bumetanide (Bumex)
- Mechanism: Block sodium-potassium-chloride cotransporter (NKCC2) in the thick
  ascending limb of the Loop of Henle
- Effect: Most powerful diuretic class. Can cause rapid, profound water loss.
- Potassium loss: SEVERE — obligatory potassium wasting
- Onset: Oral 30-60 minutes, IV 5 minutes
- Duration: 4-6 hours (furosemide), 4-6 hours (bumetanide)
- Risk level: HIGH — electrolyte depletion can be rapid and life-threatening
- Typical contest dose: Furosemide 20-40mg, taken 2-4 hours before stage
- Maximum safe dose for a single contest use: 80mg (higher doses dramatically
  increase cardiac risk)

Thiazide Diuretics — Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ)
- Mechanism: Block sodium-chloride cotransporter in distal convoluted tubule
- Effect: Moderate diuresis, slower onset than loop diuretics
- Potassium loss: Moderate
- Used sometimes in combination with loop diuretics (synergistic but very dangerous)
- Less common in modern bodybuilding due to availability of furosemide

Potassium-Sparing Diuretics — Spironolactone, Triamterene, Amiloride
- Mechanism: Spironolactone blocks aldosterone receptors; triamterene and amiloride
  block epithelial sodium channels (ENaC) in collecting duct
- Effect: Mild diuresis but preserves potassium
- Spironolactone has anti-androgenic effects (can cause gynecomastia) — generally
  avoided by male bodybuilders for this reason
- Triamterene sometimes used in combination with furosemide to offset potassium loss
- Amiloride similarly used as potassium-sparing complement

Osmotic Agents — Glycerol
- Not a true diuretic but used for water manipulation
- Mechanism: Draws water into the intravascular space, then kidneys excrete the excess
- Safer alternative to pharmaceutical diuretics
- Glycerol loading: 1-1.5g per kg body weight with 25-30ml water per gram of glycerol,
  taken 60-90 minutes before stage
- No direct electrolyte depletion risk (unlike pharmaceutical diuretics)
- Can cause headache, nausea, and GI discomfort at higher doses

SUBSECTION: WHY DIURETICS ARE THE NUMBER ONE CAUSE OF ACUTE EMERGENCIES

The combination of diuretics with the pre-contest state creates a perfect storm:

1. Athletes are already dehydrated from water restriction protocols
2. Glycogen depletion from carb depletion phases further reduces intracellular water
3. Extended contest prep has already reduced electrolyte reserves through chronic
   caloric restriction and excessive water intake (dilutional hyponatremia risk)
4. Multiple PEDs affecting kidney function and fluid balance simultaneously
5. Stress hormones (cortisol, catecholamines) are elevated from extreme dieting
6. Athletes are making dosing decisions while cognitively impaired from dehydration,
   sleep deprivation, and extreme caloric restriction

The margin of error is extremely small. The difference between "dry and full" and
"cardiac emergency" can be as little as 20mg of furosemide or a few hundred milligrams
of potassium.

Scott McNally and Dave Crosland on Blood Sweat and Gear have repeatedly emphasized
that diuretics should be an absolute last resort, used only by experienced competitors
with coach supervision, and that many athletes would look better on stage without them
because over-diuresis causes the flat, depleted look that judges penalize.

SUBSECTION: ELECTROLYTE MONITORING DURING DIURETIC USE

Critical electrolytes to monitor:

Potassium (K+):
- Normal range: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L
- Danger zone: Below 3.0 mEq/L (risk of cardiac arrhythmia)
- Critical: Below 2.5 mEq/L (immediate medical emergency — can cause cardiac arrest)
- Loop diuretics can drop potassium 1.0-1.5 mEq/L within hours
- Supplementation: Potassium chloride (KCl) 20-40 mEq with each dose of furosemide
- Food sources: Bananas (420mg per banana), potatoes, avocados, coconut water
- WARNING: Potassium supplementation must be careful — hyperkalemia (above 5.5) is
  also life-threatening. Do not blindly megadose potassium.

Sodium (Na+):
- Normal range: 136-145 mEq/L
- Danger zone: Below 130 mEq/L (hyponatremia — confusion, seizures, coma)
- Critical: Below 125 mEq/L (severe hyponatremia — medical emergency)
- Paradoxically, athletes who water-load and then cut water can develop dilutional
  hyponatremia BEFORE diuretics are even introduced
- Sodium loading followed by sodium cutting is a common peak week strategy but must
  be carefully managed

Magnesium (Mg2+):
- Normal range: 1.7-2.2 mg/dL
- Danger zone: Below 1.5 mg/dL
- Critical: Below 1.0 mg/dL (can potentiate cardiac arrhythmia)
- Often depleted in athletes from chronic dieting, sweating, and diuretic use
- Supplementation: Magnesium glycinate 400-800mg daily during peak week
- Magnesium depletion makes potassium depletion harder to correct — always replace
  magnesium before or alongside potassium

Calcium (Ca2+):
- Normal range: 8.5-10.5 mg/dL (total) or 4.5-5.5 mg/dL (ionized)
- Danger zone: Below 8.0 mg/dL total or 4.0 mg/dL ionized
- Symptoms of low calcium: muscle cramps, numbness, tingling, Chvostek sign
- Less commonly affected by diuretics alone but can be depleted in severely restricted
  athletes

SUBSECTION: CARDIAC MONITORING CONSIDERATIONS

When to consider ECG/EKG monitoring during diuretic use:
- Any athlete over 35 using pharmaceutical diuretics for the first time
- Any athlete with a history of cardiac arrhythmia or palpitations
- Any athlete using diuretics in combination with stimulants (clenbuterol, caffeine,
  ephedrine) — this combination dramatically increases arrhythmia risk
- Any athlete who has been on a prolonged severe deficit (16+ weeks of contest prep)

QT prolongation risk — Hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia both prolong the QT interval,
increasing risk of torsades de pointes (a potentially fatal cardiac arrhythmia). The
QT interval should be below 470ms for males and 480ms for females. Prolongation above
500ms is a medical emergency.

SUBSECTION: FUROSEMIDE (LASIX) PROTOCOL

Conservative approach for experienced competitors ONLY:

Pre-stage protocol:
- Day before show: No diuretics. Final water cut to 500ml or less after 6 PM.
- Morning of show (4-6 hours before prejudging):
  - Start with 20mg furosemide orally
  - Supplement with 20 mEq potassium chloride
  - Monitor urination — expect significant output within 60 minutes
  - If additional drying needed 2-3 hours before stage, consider ONE additional 20mg dose
  - Maximum total dose: 40-60mg for the entire show day
  - NEVER exceed 80mg total in 24 hours

Critical timing notes:
- Furosemide peaks at 1-2 hours and lasts 4-6 hours
- If you look flat on stage, the diuretic dose was too high — there is no fix at this point
  except rehydration and carbs backstage between prejudging and finals
- Dehydration from diuretics cannot be reversed quickly. Overdiuresis means a flat,
  stringy look that is WORSE than holding a little water

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle has detailed his approach to peak week extensively, generally
recommending that athletes achieve 90% of their conditioning through diet and water
manipulation, reserving diuretics only as a final touch — if needed at all.

SUBSECTION: SAFER ALTERNATIVES TO PHARMACEUTICAL DIURETICS

Natural diuretic approaches (in order of recommendation):

1. Water manipulation protocol (no drugs required):
   - 7 days out: 2 gallons per day
   - 5 days out: 2.5 gallons per day
   - 4 days out: 2 gallons per day
   - 3 days out: 1.5 gallons per day
   - 2 days out: 1 gallon per day
   - 1 day out: 500ml sipped throughout day, stop by 6 PM
   - Show day: Sips only as needed
   The body continues excreting water at the high rate even as intake drops, creating
   a natural diuretic effect without electrolyte depletion.

2. Sodium manipulation:
   - 7-5 days out: Sodium loading (5000-7000mg daily) — increases aldosterone suppression
   - 3-2 days out: Moderate sodium (2000-3000mg daily)
   - 1 day out: Low sodium (1000mg or less)
   - Show day: Small amounts of sodium with carb meals to drive carbs into muscle
   Note: Extreme sodium cutting (near zero) often backfires because aldosterone rebounds
   and the body holds water more aggressively.

3. Glycerol loading (as described in osmotic agents section above):
   - Safer than pharmaceutical diuretics
   - Creates intravascular volume expansion followed by renal excretion
   - No direct electrolyte depletion
   - Can be combined with water manipulation safely

4. Natural diuretic supplements:
   - Dandelion root extract 500-1500mg — mild diuretic, preserves potassium
   - Caffeine 200-400mg — mild diuretic effect, also enhances vascularity
   - Vitamin C 1000-2000mg — mild diuretic, also supports cortisol management
   - These are significantly weaker than pharmaceutical diuretics but also significantly
     safer

SUBSECTION: CARB LOADING PROTOCOLS DURING PEAK WEEK

Carb loading works synergistically with water manipulation:

Depletion phase (5-3 days out):
- Reduce carbs to 50-100g per day from fibrous vegetables only
- Continue high protein (1g per pound body weight)
- Moderate fat (0.3-0.4g per pound)
- High-volume depletion workouts (high rep, full body, glycogen-depleting)
- This phase creates the "sponge" for subsequent carb loading

Loading phase (2-1 days out):
- Begin carb loading with easily digestible sources: white rice, rice cakes, cream of
  rice, sweet potatoes, white potatoes
- Day 2 out: 2-3g carbs per pound body weight
- Day 1 out: 2-3g carbs per pound body weight (adjust based on fullness assessment)
- Keep fat very low (under 30g) to minimize water retention with carbs
- Keep fiber low to prevent bloating and GI distress
- Protein moderate (0.8g per pound)

Show day:
- Small carb meals every 2-3 hours leading up to stage: rice cakes with honey, candy,
  peanut butter
- Assess fullness between meals — if getting too full or spilling over (water under
  skin returning), reduce carb intake
- A small amount of sodium with carb meals helps drive glucose and water into muscle cells
  (sodium-glucose cotransporter, SGLT1)

SUBSECTION: RED FLAG SYMPTOMS DURING PEAK WEEK

Stop diuretic use and seek medical attention if any of these occur:

- Severe muscle cramps (especially in calves, quads, or intercostal muscles) — indicates
  electrolyte depletion, likely potassium and/or magnesium
- Heart palpitations, irregular heartbeat, or chest pain — possible cardiac arrhythmia
  from electrolyte imbalance. This is a MEDICAL EMERGENCY.
- Confusion, disorientation, or difficulty speaking — possible severe hyponatremia or
  hypoglycemia. MEDICAL EMERGENCY.
- Syncope (fainting) or near-syncope — dehydration, orthostatic hypotension, or
  cardiac arrhythmia
- Severe headache with visual changes — possible hypertensive crisis or severe dehydration
- Numbness or tingling in extremities — electrolyte imbalance, particularly calcium
  or magnesium
- Inability to urinate despite fluid intake — possible acute kidney injury from
  severe dehydration
- Seizures — severe hyponatremia or other electrolyte emergency. Call 911 immediately.

SUBSECTION: EMERGENCY PROTOCOL

If an athlete collapses or shows signs of severe dehydration or electrolyte crisis:

1. Call 911 immediately — do not wait to see if they recover
2. If conscious: Begin oral rehydration with an electrolyte solution (Pedialyte, coconut
   water, or water with 1/4 tsp salt and 1/4 tsp potassium chloride per 16 oz)
3. If unconscious: Place in recovery position, ensure airway is clear, begin CPR if no
   pulse detected
4. Provide paramedics with: list of all drugs used (including PEDs and diuretics), timing
   of last dose, fluid intake over past 24-48 hours, any known medical conditions
5. Do NOT administer additional diuretics, stimulants, or insulin
6. Monitor temperature — severe dehydration can cause hyperthermia

At the hospital: IV normal saline is the standard treatment. Potassium replacement via
IV if labs confirm hypokalemia. Continuous cardiac monitoring until electrolytes normalize.

SUBSECTION: CASE STUDY — PEAK WEEK GONE WRONG

Anonymized case from competitive bodybuilding:

Athlete profile: Male, 28, 225 lbs contest weight, running test prop 150mg EOD,
masteron prop 100mg EOD, winstrol 50mg daily, clenbuterol 120mcg daily

Peak week protocol:
- Water loading 2.5 gallons days 7-5, tapering to 1 gallon day 3, 500ml day 2
- Carb depletion days 5-3, loading days 2-1
- Sodium loading days 7-4, cut to near zero days 3-1
- Furosemide 40mg morning of show

What went wrong:
- Athlete took an additional 40mg furosemide 2 hours after first dose because he did
  not think he looked dry enough (total 80mg in 3 hours)
- Combined with 120mcg clenbuterol (beta-2 agonist — increases heart rate and potassium
  shifting into cells)
- Had not supplemented potassium with either furosemide dose
- Backstage before prejudging, experienced severe calf cramps, followed by heart
  palpitations and dizziness
- Collapsed backstage, was found with irregular pulse
- Emergency medical team administered IV saline and potassium
- Labs showed: K+ 2.8 mEq/L, Na+ 128 mEq/L, Mg2+ 1.2 mg/dL
- ECG showed prolonged QT interval and premature ventricular contractions (PVCs)
- Hospitalized for 48 hours for electrolyte correction and cardiac monitoring

Lessons:
- Never double-dose diuretics based on visual assessment alone — you cannot see
  subcutaneous water clearly when dehydrated
- Clenbuterol plus furosemide is an especially dangerous combination due to combined
  effects on potassium
- Always supplement potassium with loop diuretics
- Have an emergency plan and medical contact at every show
- The athlete looked WORSE from over-diuresis — flat and stringy instead of full and dry

SUBSECTION: EXPERT PERSPECTIVES ON PEAK WEEK SAFETY

Scott McNally on Blood Sweat and Gear regularly advises that the best peak weeks are
boring peak weeks — meaning minimal last-minute changes, no panic decisions, and
trusting the 16-20 week prep process to deliver conditioning without dangerous
last-minute interventions.

Dave Crosland on Drugs N Stuff has discussed how many coaches over-complicate peak week
with elaborate water and sodium manipulation protocols when a simple and consistent
approach of moderate water reduction and strategic carb loading produces better results
with far less risk.

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle has shared his experience that competitors who come in their
best conditioning typically used the simplest peak week protocols, and that complex
diuretic and water manipulation schemes are more likely to cause problems than to
improve the physique on stage.

IFBB Pro Paul Barnett on Anabolic Bodybuilding has emphasized that peak week is not
the time to make up for shortcomings in the prep. If you are not lean enough 2 weeks
out, no amount of water and sodium manipulation will fix that — you simply are not
ready for that show.

PRE-PCT:
- Full bloodwork panel
- PHQ-9 and GAD-7 baseline before PCT begins (will use for comparison during PCT)
- Evaluate sleep quality

DURING PCT (weekly self-assessment):
- PHQ-9 weekly for first 4 weeks (highest risk period)
- Sleep quality log
- Social connection check — are you isolating?
- Physical activity log — are you maintaining exercise?

PEAK WEEK (if competing):
- Baseline electrolytes 7 days out (especially if using pharmaceutical diuretics)
- Blood pressure twice daily during water manipulation
- ECG if using diuretics for first time or if over age 35
- Emergency contact and plan established before show day
- Coach or trusted person present backstage with rehydration supplies

CRUISE / TRT LONG-TERM:
- Standard quarterly bloodwork
- Annual sleep study if neck above 17 inches or STOP-BANG score 3+
- Annual PHQ-9 and GAD-7
- Annual echocardiogram if 5+ years of enhanced use
- CIMT every 2-3 years if cholesterol chronically elevated

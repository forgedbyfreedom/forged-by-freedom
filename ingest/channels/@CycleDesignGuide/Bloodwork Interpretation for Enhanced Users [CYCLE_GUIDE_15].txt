=== WHY BLOODWORK IS NON-NEGOTIABLE ===

Every enhanced user MUST get bloodwork. You cannot manage what you cannot measure. Anabolic steroids affect liver enzymes, kidney function, lipid profiles, hematocrit, hormone levels, and a dozen other health markers simultaneously. Running gear without bloodwork is flying blind — you have no idea what is happening inside your body until something goes seriously wrong.

Dave Crosland on Drugs N Stuff: "If you're not getting bloodwork, you have no business using gear. Period. You're guessing, and guessing with your health is stupid."

Scott McNally on Blood Sweat and Gear: "I have seen guys who looked perfectly fine on the outside but their bloodwork was a disaster. Liver values through the roof, hematocrit in the danger zone, lipids completely wrecked. You would never know without the labs. The blood tells the truth."

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle: "Bodybuilders used to just go by feel. That era is over. We have the tools now to monitor everything. There is zero excuse not to get labs done regularly. It costs less than a month of gear."

Paul Barnett on Anabolic Bodybuilding: "I get bloodwork every single time I make a change to my protocol. New compound? Bloodwork. New dose? Bloodwork. Something feels off? Bloodwork. It is the only way to make intelligent decisions about your health."

Skipp Hill on It's Just Bodybuilding: "The guys who last in this sport are the ones who treat their health like a priority. Bloodwork is the foundation of that. If you cannot afford bloodwork, you cannot afford to use gear."

=== WHEN TO GET BLOODWORK ===

Timing your bloodwork correctly is just as important as getting it done. Drawing labs at the wrong time gives you misleading data.

PRE-CYCLE BASELINE (2-4 weeks before starting):
- This establishes YOUR personal normal values
- Everyone's baseline is different — what is normal for you may be abnormal for someone else
- You need this reference point to compare against mid-cycle and post-cycle labs
- If your baseline is already abnormal, you need to address that BEFORE starting a cycle
- Get this done when you are completely off everything, including SERMs and AI

MID-CYCLE CHECK (Week 6-8):
- Assess how your body is responding to the compounds
- Dial in aromatase inhibitor dosing based on estradiol levels
- Check hematocrit — this is when it starts climbing
- Evaluate liver stress if running orals
- Adjust cabergoline dosing if on 19-nors based on prolactin levels
- This is your opportunity to make corrections before the cycle ends

END OF CYCLE / PRE-PCT (Last week of cycle):
- See where your body stands before entering recovery phase
- Evaluate total damage to lipids, liver, kidneys
- Check hematocrit before PCT (may need to donate blood)
- Provides data for planning your PCT protocol

POST-PCT (4-6 weeks after PCT ends):
- The most important panel — verifies whether your HPTA has recovered
- Compare LH, FSH, total Testosterone, and free Testosterone to your pre-cycle baseline
- If hormones are still suppressed, you may need extended PCT or medical intervention
- Lipids and liver values should be trending back toward baseline

CRUISING / TRT (Every 3-4 months):
- Even on a "safe" TRT dose, you must monitor health markers
- Hematocrit creeps up over time on testosterone
- Lipids can still be affected at TRT doses
- Kidney and liver function should remain stable — if not, investigate

IF YOU FEEL "OFF" — GET BLOODWORK IMMEDIATELY:
- Do not wait for your scheduled panel
- Symptoms like extreme fatigue, mood crashes, sexual dysfunction, chest pain, or visible swelling warrant immediate labs
- Your body is telling you something — listen to it and verify with data

Dave Crosland on Drugs N Stuff: "The mid-cycle check is where you catch problems early. I cannot tell you how many guys I have talked to who felt fine at week 6 but their hematocrit was 56%. That is stroke territory. They had no idea until they got the labs."

=== WHAT TO ORDER — THE COMPLETE PANEL ===

This is the comprehensive panel every enhanced user should understand. Each marker tells you something specific about what the gear is doing to your body.

--- HORMONES ---

total TESTOSTERONE:
- Standard natural range: 300-1000 ng/dL
- On cycle: Will be supraphysiological — expect 1500-5000+ ng/dL depending on dose and compounds
- 500mg/week testosterone typically produces levels of 2000-3500 ng/dL (individual response varies significantly)
- Off cycle / post-PCT target: 400-800 ng/dL
- Below 300 ng/dL post-PCT = you have NOT recovered — extended PCT or medical intervention needed
- Draw at TROUGH (morning, before next injection) for accurate reading

free TESTOSTERONE:
- More important than total Testosterone — this is the bioavailable fraction actually doing work
- On cycle: Very high, often off the standard lab scale
- Off cycle target: 15-25 pg/mL
- Low free Testosterone despite normal total = elevated SHBG binding up your test
- Orals lower SHBG which increases free Testosterone relative to total

ESTRADIOL (E2) — SENSITIVE ASSAY (LC/MS):
- This is the single most important marker to manage on cycle
- Standard natural range: 20-40 pg/mL
- On cycle with AI: Aim for 20-50 pg/mL
- Over 60 pg/mL = Gynecomastia risk increases significantly. Water retention, mood swings, elevated blood pressure
- Under 15 pg/mL = crashed estrogen. Joint pain, cracking joints, depression, dead libido, insomnia, dry skin, brain fog. crashed E2 feels worse than high E2 for most men
- The SENSITIVE assay (LC/MS method) is MANDATORY for men. The standard immunoassay (ECLIA) is wildly inaccurate in males and can be off by 30-50%. Always request the sensitive/LC-MS assay specifically

Scott McNally on Blood Sweat and Gear: "Estrogen management is the difference between a good cycle and a miserable one. But you cannot manage it by feel alone. Some guys aromatize like crazy, some barely at all. The only way to know YOUR conversion rate is bloodwork."

LH (LUTEINIZING HORMONE):
- Natural range: 1.5-9.3 mIU/mL
- On cycle: Will be near zero — your pituitary stops producing LH when exogenous testosterone is present
- Post-PCT recovery target: 3-10 mIU/mL
- If LH is still 0 or near 0 after completing PCT, your HPTA recovery has failed — you may need HCG restart protocol or medical evaluation
- LH is the primary signal that tells your testes to produce testosterone

FSH (FOLLICLE STIMULATING HORMONE):
- Natural range: 1.5-12.4 mIU/mL
- On cycle: Suppressed to near zero, same as LH
- Post-PCT recovery target: 2-8 mIU/mL
- FSH is critical for spermatogenesis — if fertility is a concern, monitor this closely
- Recovery of FSH often lags behind LH recovery

PROLACTIN:
- Normal range: 2-18 ng/mL
- Elevated by 19-nor compounds (Nandrolone/Deca, NPP, Trenbolone)
- 18-20 ng/mL = Monitor closely, may need intervention
- Over 20 ng/mL = Sexual dysfunction risk increases (erectile issues, anorgasmia, low libido). Start or increase cabergoline
- Over 30 ng/mL = Aggressive intervention needed — cabergoline 0.5mg 2x/week, potentially add P-5-P B6
- High prolactin with normal estrogen and testosterone = the prolactin itself is causing sexual dysfunction

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle: "Every single guy I have seen with sexual dysfunction on Deca or Tren, the first thing I check is prolactin. Nine times out of ten that is the culprit. Get it under control with cabergoline and the problems resolve."

SHBG (SEX HORMONE BINDING GLOBULIN):
- Normal range: 20-50 nmol/L
- Oral steroids crash SHBG significantly (Winstrol, Anavar, Dianabol, Proviron)
- Below 10 nmol/L = Very low. While this means more free Testosterone, it also indicates significant liver stress from orals
- High SHBG = Less bioavailable testosterone. Proviron 25-50mg/day can lower SHBG
- SHBG is produced by the liver — crashed SHBG is a surrogate marker for liver impact

DHEA-S:
- General adrenal health marker
- Normal male range: 100-500 mcg/dL (age-dependent, declines with age)
- More relevant for females and older males (40+)
- Extremely low values may indicate adrenal fatigue
- Not dramatically affected by most AAS but good to have on baseline

PROGESTERONE:
- Normal male range: <0.5 ng/mL
- Can rise significantly on 19-nor compounds (Deca, Tren)
- Not always included in standard panels but useful if sexual dysfunction persists despite normal prolactin and estradiol levels
- Elevated progesterone can contribute to water retention, mood changes, and erectile dysfunction independent of E2/prolactin

--- LIVER ---

AST (SGOT):
- Normal range: <40 U/L
- Elevated by oral steroids AND heavy resistance training (AST is present in muscle tissue)
- Mildly elevated 40-80 U/L on orals is common and expected
- 80-100 U/L = Concerning. Evaluate whether you should continue orals
- Over 100 U/L = Drop oral dose or discontinue. Increase TUDCA to 1000mg/day
- Over 200 U/L = Stop all oral steroids IMMEDIATELY. This is serious liver stress
- Important context: A heavy leg day can elevate AST to 80-100+ purely from muscle damage. This is why you avoid training 24-48 hours before bloodwork

ALT (SGPT):
- Normal range: <40 U/L
- MORE liver-specific than AST — this is your primary liver damage indicator
- ALT elevated WITH normal AST = Almost certainly liver-related, not from training
- AST elevated with normal ALT = More likely from muscle damage (training)
- Both elevated = Liver stress, especially if on orals
- Same concerning thresholds as AST: over 100 = worry, over 200 = stop orals

Skipp Hill on It's Just Bodybuilding: "People look at AST and freak out. You have to look at ALT alongside it. If your AST is 90 but your ALT is 30, you probably just trained hard before your blood draw. If both are 90, now you have a problem."

GGT (GAMMA-GLUTAMYL TRANSFERASE):
- Normal range: <60 U/L
- More specific marker of liver damage than AST or ALT
- NOT elevated by exercise — so if GGT is high, it is genuinely liver-related
- Elevated GGT alongside elevated AST/ALT confirms hepatic stress
- Also elevated by alcohol consumption — be honest about intake

BILIRUBIN:
- Normal range: 0.1-1.2 mg/dL
- Bilirubin is a waste product the liver must process and excrete
- Elevated bilirubin = Liver struggling to clear waste
- Mildly elevated (1.2-2.0): May be Gilbert's syndrome (benign genetic condition) — check baseline
- Over 2.0 mg/dL with other liver markers elevated = Significant hepatic dysfunction
- Very high bilirubin causes jaundice (yellowing of eyes and skin) — this is a medical emergency

ALBUMIN:
- Normal range: 3.5-5.0 g/dL
- Albumin is a protein produced by the liver
- Low albumin = Liver is not producing protein properly. This is a very concerning sign of advanced liver damage
- Below 3.5 g/dL alongside elevated liver enzymes = Seek medical evaluation
- Also drops with severe kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome)

--- KIDNEYS ---

BUN (BLOOD UREA NITROGEN):
- Normal range: 7-20 mg/dL
- Can be elevated on high-protein diets — up to 25 mg/dL is generally acceptable for bodybuilders eating 200-300g+ protein daily
- Over 30 mg/dL = Kidney stress. Investigate further
- Dehydration also elevates BUN — ensure you are well-hydrated before blood draw
- Consistently elevated BUN alongside elevated creatinine = Kidney function declining

CREATININE:
- Normal range: 0.7-1.3 mg/dL
- Naturally higher in muscular individuals due to greater muscle mass and creatine turnover
- Up to 1.5 mg/dL may be YOUR normal if you carry significant muscle mass
- Over 1.8 mg/dL = Investigate kidney function regardless of muscle mass
- Trenbolone is particularly harsh on kidneys — monitor creatinine closely on Tren
- Creatine supplementation can also mildly elevate creatinine

eGFR (ESTIMATED GLOMERULAR FILTRATION RATE):
- Normal: >60 mL/min/1.73m2
- 60-90 = Mildly reduced (but may be a calculation artifact in muscular individuals)
- Below 60 = Kidney impairment. Needs medical evaluation
- Below 30 = Serious kidney disease
- CRITICAL NOTE: Standard eGFR formulas (CKD-EPI, MDRD) use creatinine and significantly UNDERESTIMATE kidney function in very muscular people. A 250lb lean bodybuilder may show eGFR of 55 with perfectly healthy kidneys. Cystatin C-based eGFR is MORE ACCURATE for bodybuilders — request this specifically if your standard eGFR is borderline

Paul Barnett on Anabolic Bodybuilding: "I had a doctor tell me my kidneys were failing based on my eGFR. I weighed 280 pounds at the time. Got the cystatin C test done and my kidneys were perfectly fine. The standard formula just does not work for people our size. Every bodybuilder needs to know this."

BUN/CREATININE RATIO:
- Normal: 10:1 to 20:1
- High ratio (>20:1) can indicate dehydration, GI bleeding, or high protein intake
- Low ratio (<10:1) can indicate liver disease or low protein intake
- Use in conjunction with individual BUN and creatinine values for complete picture

--- LIPIDS ---

TOTAL CHOLESTEROL:
- Normal: <200 mg/dL
- On cycle: Will likely be elevated. AAS dysregulate cholesterol metabolism
- 200-240 = Borderline. Manageable with supplements and cardio
- Over 240 = High. Consider lipid-support supplements aggressively

LDL (LOW-DENSITY LIPOPROTEIN — "BAD" CHOLESTEROL):
- Normal: <100 mg/dL
- Optimal: <70 mg/dL
- On cycle: Expect 100-160+ mg/dL. Orals are significantly worse than injectables for LDL elevation
- Trenbolone is the worst offender for lipid destruction among all AAS
- Testosterone alone at moderate doses may only mildly elevate LDL
- Over 160 mg/dL = Cardiovascular risk is significantly elevated. Aggressive intervention needed

HDL (HIGH-DENSITY LIPOPROTEIN — "GOOD" CHOLESTEROL):
- Normal: >40 mg/dL (>60 is ideal)
- AAS CRUSH HDL — this is one of the most consistent and concerning side effects
- On cycle: HDL may drop to 15-30 mg/dL
- Below 20 mg/dL = Very concerning. Your cardiovascular protection is severely compromised
- Management: Citrus bergamot 1000mg/day, 30-45 minutes of cardio 4-5x/week, fish oil 3-4g/day
- HDL recovers relatively quickly off cycle (4-8 weeks) but remains suppressed while on

Dave Crosland on Drugs N Stuff: "HDL is the one that worries me the most with long-term users. You can have a guy on TRT for years and his HDL just sits at 25. That is decades of reduced cardiovascular protection. You need to be doing cardio, taking your citrus bergamot, and watching this number."

TRIGLYCERIDES:
- Normal: <150 mg/dL
- Elevated by poor diet, AAS use, growth hormone, and especially insulin use
- Over 200 mg/dL = Needs dietary and supplemental intervention
- Over 500 mg/dL = Pancreatitis risk. Medical intervention needed
- Fish oil 3-4g/day is the most effective supplement for lowering triglycerides

Lp(a) (LIPOPROTEIN A):
- Genetic marker — largely determined by your DNA
- Not significantly affected by AAS but important to know your baseline
- Elevated Lp(a) = Increased cardiovascular risk that supplements and lifestyle cannot easily fix
- If elevated, you should be extra cautious with compounds that worsen lipids

ApoB (APOLIPOPROTEIN B):
- Better predictor of cardiovascular risk than LDL alone
- Each atherogenic particle (LDL, VLDL, IDL) carries one ApoB molecule
- Normal: <90 mg/dL. Optimal: <60 mg/dL
- Should be tested at baseline and monitored on cycle
- Provides a more complete picture of cardiovascular risk than standard lipid panel alone

--- BLOOD CELLS (CBC) ---

HEMATOCRIT (HCT):
- Normal range: 38-50%
- AAS increase erythropoiesis (red blood cell production), especially Equipoise (Boldenone), high-dose Testosterone, and Anadrol
- Over 50% = Getting elevated. Monitor closely, increase hydration
- Over 52% = Blood is becoming dangerously thick. Clot risk is increasing. Plan to donate blood or get therapeutic phlebotomy
- Over 54% = DONATE BLOOD OR GET THERAPEUTIC PHLEBOTOMY IMMEDIATELY. This is the #1 acute danger marker on cycle. Risk of stroke, pulmonary embolism, and heart attack rises dramatically
- This is not theoretical — elevated hematocrit kills people

Scott McNally on Blood Sweat and Gear: "Hematocrit is the one that can actually kill you acutely. Your cholesterol being bad for 12 weeks is not going to give you a heart attack tomorrow. But a hematocrit of 56% can give you a stroke tonight. Check it, manage it, do not ignore it."

HEMOGLOBIN:
- Normal range: 13.5-17.5 g/dL
- Correlates directly with hematocrit — elevated together = polycythemia
- Over 18 g/dL = Same concern as elevated hematocrit. Blood is too thick
- Hemoglobin carries oxygen — more is not better when it makes your blood viscous

RBC COUNT (RED BLOOD CELL COUNT):
- Normal range: 4.5-5.5 million/mcL
- Elevated by AAS, especially Equipoise and high-dose testosterone
- Follows the same pattern as hematocrit and hemoglobin
- All three elevated together confirms AAS-induced polycythemia

WBC COUNT (WHITE BLOOD CELL COUNT):
- Normal range: 4,000-11,000/mcL
- Elevated = Possible infection, inflammation, or immune response
- Low = Immune suppression. Can occur post-cycle when the body is stressed
- AAS do not typically cause dramatic WBC changes, but monitor as part of overall health
- Very high WBC (>15,000) with no obvious infection warrants medical investigation

PLATELETS:
- Normal range: 150,000-400,000/mcL
- Low platelets (thrombocytopenia) = Increased bleeding risk
- Very high platelets (thrombocytosis) = Increased clotting risk, especially combined with elevated hematocrit
- AAS do not commonly affect platelets directly but monitor alongside hematocrit for complete clotting risk assessment

--- CARDIAC ---

CRP (C-REACTIVE PROTEIN):
- Normal: <1.0 mg/L
- General inflammation marker — elevated by AAS use, heavy training, illness, or injury
- 1.0-3.0 mg/L = Moderate inflammation
- Over 3.0 mg/L = Significant systemic inflammation. If persistent, this increases cardiovascular risk
- High-sensitivity CRP (hs-CRP) is the preferred test
- AAS are pro-inflammatory — expect some elevation on cycle

HOMOCYSTEINE:
- Normal: <10 umol/L
- Cardiovascular risk marker — elevated homocysteine damages blood vessel walls
- AAS can elevate homocysteine
- Over 15 umol/L = Increased cardiovascular and clotting risk
- Easy to manage: B6 (50-100mg/day), B12 (1000mcg/day), and Folate (800mcg/day) effectively lower homocysteine

BNP or NT-proBNP:
- Cardiac stress and heart failure marker
- Detects early heart enlargement and ventricular strain
- Should be baseline tested before first cycle and monitored annually for long-term users
- Elevated levels indicate the heart is under strain — this is the marker that catches cardiac remodeling early
- If persistently elevated, get an echocardiogram

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle: "BNP is the one most guys do not know about. If you have been using gear for years, especially with high-dose testosterone and growth hormone, you need to know your BNP. It tells you if your heart is being stressed before you get symptoms."

BLOOD PRESSURE (not bloodwork but CRITICAL):
- Target: <140/90 mmHg (ideally <130/80)
- On cycle: Check DAILY with a home monitor. No exceptions
- If consistently >140/90: Lifestyle interventions (sodium reduction, cardio, potassium intake)
- If consistently >150/90: Medicate. Telmisartan 40-80mg daily (preferred — also has cardioprotective and anti-inflammatory properties) or Lisinopril 10-20mg daily
- Over 180/110 = Hypertensive crisis. Seek immediate medical attention
- AAS, especially Dianabol, Anadrol, and high-dose Testosterone, significantly elevate blood pressure

--- OTHER IMPORTANT MARKERS ---

FASTING GLUCOSE:
- Normal: 70-100 mg/dL
- HGH and MK-677 can significantly raise fasting glucose through insulin resistance
- 100-125 mg/dL = Pre-diabetic territory. Monitor closely, consider reducing GH dose or adding metformin
- Over 126 mg/dL = Diabetic range. Medical intervention needed
- insulin use demands extremely careful glucose monitoring — hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia are both dangerous
- Must be truly fasted (10-12 hours, water only) for accurate reading

HbA1c (GLYCATED HEMOGLOBIN):
- 3-month average of blood sugar control
- Normal: <5.7%
- 5.7-6.4% = Pre-diabetic. If you are using GH or insulin, this needs immediate attention
- Over 6.5% = Diabetic. Discontinue or reduce GH, add metformin, consult physician
- Essential test for anyone using growth hormone, MK-677, or insulin

Skipp Hill on It's Just Bodybuilding: "The guys running high-dose growth hormone for months on end without checking their blood sugar are playing with fire. I have seen guys become legitimately diabetic from GH abuse. Get your HbA1c checked."

PSA (PROSTATE SPECIFIC ANTIGEN):
- Normal: <4.0 ng/mL
- AAS can elevate PSA — testosterone is converted to DHT which stimulates prostate growth
- Get a BASELINE before your very first cycle
- Rising PSA trend over time (even if still under 4.0) = Investigate prostate health
- Over 4.0 ng/mL = Prostate evaluation needed (ultrasound, possible biopsy referral)
- DHT-derived compounds (Masteron, Proviron, Winstrol) may have more prostate impact
- Men over 35 should include PSA in every panel

THYROID (TSH, free T3, free T4):
- TSH normal: 0.5-4.0 mIU/L (optimal 1.0-2.5)
- free T3 normal: 2.3-4.2 pg/mL
- free T4 normal: 0.8-1.8 ng/dL
- AAS can affect thyroid function
- growth hormone can lower T3 — at higher GH doses (4+ IU/day), T3 supplementation (25-50mcg/day) may be needed to prevent hypothyroid symptoms
- Symptoms of low T3: fatigue, cold intolerance, sluggish metabolism, constipation, brain fog
- Trenbolone is known to affect thyroid markers in some users

FERRITIN:
- Normal range: 30-300 ng/mL
- Iron storage marker
- Can get dangerously low from frequent blood donation (done to manage elevated hematocrit)
- Below 30 ng/mL = Iron depleted. Supplement with iron (ferrous bisglycinate 25-50mg/day with vitamin C for absorption)
- Below 15 ng/mL = Severely depleted. You may develop anemia even with elevated hematocrit
- If you are donating blood every 8 weeks to manage HCT, monitor ferritin at every blood draw

VITAMIN D (25-HYDROXYVITAMIN D):
- Optimal: 40-60 ng/mL
- Below 30 ng/mL = Insufficient. Impacts testosterone production, immune function, bone health, and mood
- Supplement: 5000 IU/day for most people. Retest in 8 weeks and adjust
- Crucial for overall hormonal health — vitamin D functions as a hormone itself

IGF-1:
- Reflects growth hormone output
- Age-dependent normal ranges
- Useful for monitoring GH dosing efficacy
- Also indicates liver health (IGF-1 is produced by the liver in response to GH)
- Baseline measurement before starting GH allows you to see the dose-response

=== ENHANCED vs STANDARD RANGES ===

Standard laboratory reference ranges are calibrated for natural, sedentary adults. They are NOT appropriate for interpreting bloodwork in enhanced athletes. You must understand the context of each marker.

MARKERS WHERE "OUT OF RANGE" IS EXPECTED ON CYCLE:
- total Testosterone: Will be far above range (1500-5000+ ng/dL). This is the point of using exogenous testosterone. Not a cause for concern in itself
- free Testosterone: Similarly supraphysiological. Expected
- LH and FSH: Will be suppressed to near zero. This is NORMAL on cycle — exogenous testosterone shuts down the HPTA. Only a concern if they remain zero POST-PCT
- AST: May be 40-80 purely from intense training. Context matters — look at ALT alongside it
- Creatinine: Muscular individuals routinely show 1.2-1.5 mg/dL with perfectly healthy kidneys
- eGFR: Standard formulas UNDERESTIMATE kidney function in muscular people. Cystatin C-based eGFR is the accurate test for bodybuilders
- BUN: High-protein diets (200-300g+/day) naturally elevate BUN to 20-28 mg/dL

MARKERS WHERE "IN RANGE" STILL NEEDS ATTENTION:
- Estradiol at 55 pg/mL: Technically near upper normal range, but for an enhanced male this is getting high. Gyno risk is increasing
- HDL at 35 mg/dL: Technically "low normal" on the lab sheet, but trending down rapidly on cycle means it will keep dropping
- Hematocrit at 50%: Within "normal" range but for someone on EQ and 500mg test, this will climb to 53-55% by cycle end without intervention

MARKERS WHERE THE STANDARD RANGE IS ACCURATE REGARDLESS:
- Liver enzymes (ALT, GGT, bilirubin, albumin): Liver damage is liver damage. These ranges apply to everyone
- Lipids (LDL, HDL, triglycerides): Cardiovascular risk does not care how muscular you are
- Hematocrit: Blood viscosity physics apply equally to everyone. Over 52% is dangerous for anyone
- Fasting glucose and HbA1c: Diabetes does not discriminate based on fitness level
- Blood pressure: Hypertension is hypertension
- CRP, homocysteine, BNP: Inflammatory and cardiac markers apply universally

Scott McNally on Blood Sweat and Gear: "You have to be your own advocate with doctors. Most doctors see your testosterone at 3000 and panic. They see your creatinine at 1.4 and say your kidneys are failing. You need to educate yourself so you can have an informed conversation and know which numbers actually matter."

=== HOW TO ORDER BLOODWORK ===

THROUGH YOUR DOCTOR:
- Tell them you want to monitor your health markers
- You do not have to disclose AAS use, but being honest with a supportive doctor gives you the best care
- Some doctors will order comprehensive panels through insurance

DIRECT-TO-CONSUMER LAB SERVICES (No doctor needed):
- DiscountedLabs.com: Excellent bodybuilder-specific panels at competitive prices
- Marek Health: Founded with the enhanced community in mind. Comprehensive panels with telehealth follow-up
- PrivateMDLabs: Wide selection of individual tests and panels
- LabsMD: Another solid option for self-ordered labs
- These services provide a lab requisition — you take it to Quest Diagnostics or LabCorp for the actual blood draw

COST:
- Basic hormone panel: $50-100
- Comprehensive panel (hormones + liver + kidneys + lipids + CBC): $150-250
- Full enhanced athlete panel (everything above + cardiac markers + thyroid + metabolic): $200-350
- This is less than the cost of one vial of testosterone. Budget for it

PREPARATION FOR ACCURATE RESULTS:
- Fast 10-12 hours before the draw (water only). This is essential for accurate lipid and glucose readings
- Draw at TROUGH levels: Morning, before your next injection. This gives you the lowest point in your dosing cycle and the most useful hormone data
- Avoid heavy training 24-48 hours before the draw. Intense exercise falsely elevates AST, ALT, CK, and can affect other markers
- Avoid alcohol for 48-72 hours before the draw
- Stay well hydrated the day before (dehydration elevates hematocrit, BUN, and other markers artificially)
- Take your regular supplements and medications as normal (unless your doctor says otherwise)
- Note the time of your last injection on your lab order — this helps interpret hormone levels accurately

Dave Palumbo on RXMuscle: "Guys always ask me where to get bloodwork. It has never been easier. You can order everything online, walk into a LabCorp, and have results in 48 hours. The cost is nothing compared to what you spend on gear and food. Make it a non-negotiable part of your budget."

=== RED FLAGS — WHEN TO STOP YOUR CYCLE ===

These values represent immediate danger. If you see ANY of the following, take action NOW — not next week, not after you finish the vial.

STOP ALL ORAL STEROIDS IMMEDIATELY IF:
- ALT over 200 U/L
- AST over 200 U/L with ALT also significantly elevated
- GGT over 200 U/L
- Bilirubin over 2.5 mg/dL with elevated liver enzymes
- Albumin below 3.0 g/dL
- Jaundice (yellow eyes or skin) — this is a medical emergency

DONATE BLOOD / THERAPEUTIC PHLEBOTOMY IMMEDIATELY IF:
- Hematocrit over 54%
- Hemoglobin over 18.5 g/dL
- If you cannot donate (deferred or ineligible), seek therapeutic phlebotomy through a doctor

REDUCE DOSE OR DISCONTINUE CYCLE IF:
- eGFR below 45 (using cystatin C method for accuracy)
- Creatinine over 2.0 mg/dL
- Blood pressure consistently over 160/100 despite medication
- LDL over 200 mg/dL with HDL below 20 mg/dL (catastrophic lipid profile)
- Fasting glucose over 140 mg/dL
- HbA1c over 6.5%
- BNP or NT-proBNP significantly elevated above your baseline
- Resting heart rate consistently over 100 bpm
- PSA doubling or rising above 4.0 ng/mL

SEEK IMMEDIATE MEDICAL ATTENTION IF:
- Chest pain, pressure, or tightness
- Sudden severe headache (could be stroke from elevated HCT)
- Sudden vision changes
- Shortness of breath at rest
- Swelling in one leg (possible DVT — deep vein thrombosis)
- Coughing blood
- Jaundice
- Dark brown/cola-colored urine (rhabdomyolysis or severe liver dysfunction)
- Extreme abdominal pain with nausea (possible liver/kidney crisis)

Dave Crosland on Drugs N Stuff: "I do not care what cycle you are on or what your goals are. If your bloodwork shows red flags, you stop. Full stop. No show, no competition, no amount of gains is worth dying over. I have buried friends in this sport. Do not be another statistic."

Paul Barnett on Anabolic Bodybuilding: "Ego kills in this game. I have seen guys get bloodwork back showing their liver is in crisis and they say they will just add more TUDCA and keep going. That is insanity. Your body is telling you to stop. Listen to it."

=== BLOODWORK FOR WOMEN ===

Female enhanced athletes require different panels with different reference ranges. Virilization markers must be monitored closely.

ESSENTIAL FEMALE PANEL:

total TESTOSTERONE:
- Normal female range: 15-70 ng/dL
- On low-dose Anavar (5-10mg/day): May rise to 80-150 ng/dL
- Over 200 ng/dL = Significant virilization risk. Reduce dose or discontinue
- Women are far more sensitive to androgens — small changes in testosterone produce large effects

free TESTOSTERONE:
- Normal female range: 0.1-6.4 pg/mL
- Even mildly elevated free Testosterone causes androgenic effects in women
- Monitor closely — this is more predictive of virilization than total Testosterone

ESTRADIOL:
- Normal female range: Varies dramatically with menstrual cycle phase
- Follicular phase: 30-120 pg/mL
- Ovulatory peak: 130-370 pg/mL
- Luteal phase: 70-250 pg/mL
- AAS can suppress estradiol in women — low estrogen causes bone density loss, vaginal dryness, mood disruption
- Women should NOT use aromatase inhibitors unless specifically indicated — crashing female estrogen is extremely harmful

SHBG:
- Normal female range: 18-144 nmol/L
- Often significantly elevated in women on oral birth control (can reach 200+ nmol/L)
- High SHBG binds up free Testosterone — may reduce both desired and undesired androgenic effects
- AAS (especially orals) lower SHBG — stopping birth control while on Anavar creates a double drop that can spike free androgens

DHEA-S:
- Normal female range: 35-430 mcg/dL (age-dependent)
- Adrenal androgen marker — elevated levels may indicate adrenal overproduction
- Useful for assessing overall androgenic load in women
- Some women supplement DHEA — this will elevate the reading

PROLACTIN:
- Normal female range: 2-29 ng/mL (higher than males)
- Elevated by 19-nors, stress, and some medications
- Very high prolactin can disrupt menstrual cycle and cause galactorrhea (breast milk production)
- Monitor if using any 19-nor compounds (which women generally should NOT use)

THYROID (TSH, free T3, free T4):
- Same ranges as men apply
- Women are more susceptible to thyroid disruption
- AAS and cutting protocols can suppress T3
- Hypothyroid symptoms in women on gear: extreme fatigue, inability to lose fat despite severe deficit, hair thinning, cold extremities

LH AND FSH:
- Vary with menstrual cycle phase
- AAS will suppress both, potentially causing amenorrhea (loss of menstrual cycle)
- Cycle cessation is common and expected on AAS — but prolonged amenorrhea post-cycle warrants medical evaluation
- Recovery of menstrual cycle post-AAS can take 2-6+ months

LIVER, KIDNEY, LIPID, AND CBC PANELS:
- Same markers and ranges as men
- Women tend to have lower baseline hematocrit (36-44%) — AAS-induced elevation is still a concern
- Lipid disruption from AAS affects women equally

VIRILIZATION MONITORING:
- Bloodwork alone does not catch virilization — you must monitor physical symptoms:
- Voice deepening (often irreversible)
- Clitoral enlargement (often irreversible)
- Facial hair growth
- Acne on jaw, chest, and back
- Male-pattern hair thinning
- At FIRST sign of virilization: STOP the compound immediately. Some effects are permanent

Skipp Hill on It's Just Bodybuilding: "Women need to be especially careful with bloodwork because the margin of error is much smaller. A male can be at double his natural testosterone and be fine. A female at double her natural testosterone is already virilizing. The dose-response curve is completely different."

=== INTERPRETING YOUR RESULTS — PRACTICAL APPROACH ===

Step 1: Compare everything to YOUR baseline. Population averages matter less than your personal trend.

Step 2: Look at markers in context, not isolation. One elevated value may mean nothing. Multiple related markers trending in the same direction tells a story.

Step 3: Trend over time is more important than any single reading. One high AST reading could be from a hard workout. Three consecutive high readings with GGT also elevated = genuine liver problem.

Step 4: Address the worst markers first. If your hematocrit is 55% and your LDL is 140, donate blood today and worry about lipids tomorrow.

Step 5: Adjust your protocol based on data. This is the entire point. If your estradiol is 70, increase your AI. If your prolactin is 25, increase cabergoline. If your liver is struggling, drop the orals. Let the numbers guide your decisions.

Scott McNally on Blood Sweat and Gear: "Bloodwork is not just a checkbox. It is a tool. Every set of labs should inform a decision. Either everything is fine and you continue as planned, or something needs adjusting. Either way, you are making an informed choice instead of a blind one."

SEXUAL PERFORMANCE AND INTIMACY — COMPLETE REFERENCE GUIDE

================================================================
SECTION 1: OVERVIEW — WHY SEXUAL HEALTH IS PART OF PERFORMANCE
================================================================

Sexual performance is not separate from athletic and physical optimization — it is a direct downstream output of hormonal health, cardiovascular function, neurological balance, and body composition. Testosterone, estrogen, nitric oxide, dopamine, oxytocin, and cortisol all intersect in sexual function. A man or woman who has optimized their physique but has poor libido, erectile dysfunction, or anorgasmia has an incomplete health picture.

For enhanced athletes and TRT/HRT users, sexual function is one of the most sensitive indicators of hormonal balance. Estrogen that is too low or too high tanks libido and erection quality. Prolactin elevation from compounds like Deca or Tren kills libido and can cause erectile dysfunction (deca dick). Testosterone drives desire in both sexes. HGH and peptides improve vascular function and nerve sensitivity. This guide covers compounds, techniques, and biological mechanisms for both males and females.

SECTION 2: MALE SEXUAL PERFORMANCE
================================================================

--- 2A. PHYSIOLOGY OF ERECTION ---

Erection is a neurovascular event. Sexual arousal triggers release of nitric oxide (NO) from endothelial cells and nerve endings in penile tissue. NO activates guanylate cyclase → cGMP → smooth muscle relaxation → arterial dilation → blood flows into corpora cavernosa → erection.

The enzyme PDE5 (phosphodiesterase type 5) degrades cGMP. PDE5 inhibitors block this degradation, sustaining erection.

Key drivers of erectile quality:
- Testosterone levels (total and free)
- Nitric oxide bioavailability (endothelial health, arginine/citrulline status)
- Cardiovascular health (blood pressure, blood flow)
- Dopamine signaling (libido and arousal)
- Prolactin (must be low — elevated prolactin from 19-nor compounds is a primary cause of ED in enhanced athletes)
- Estradiol (must be in range — both too high and too low impairs erection)
- DHT (contributes to erection quality and sensitivity)

--- 2B. ERECTILE DYSFUNCTION (ED) IN ENHANCED ATHLETES — CAUSES ---

1. deca dick — Nandrolone (Deca Durabolin, NPP) suppresses endogenous testosterone and DHT while providing a progestin-dominant anabolic environment. Low DHT + elevated prolactin = libido crash + poor erection quality. Fix: run testosterone at least 2:1 ratio over nandrolone, keep prolactin in check with cabergoline or pramipexole.

2. PROLACTIN ELEVATION — Caused by nandrolone, trenbolone, and other 19-nor compounds. Elevated prolactin directly suppresses LH, testosterone production, and dopamine signaling. Target prolactin <15 ng/mL. Treat with: Cabergoline 0.25mg 2x/week, or Pramipexole 0.25-0.5mg/night.

3. ESTROGEN IMBALANCE — Both high estrogen (from aromatization) and crashed estrogen (from over-use of AIs) impair erection. crashed E2 is more common in enhanced athletes who over-AI. Target E2 20-30 pg/mL for most men on TRT/cycle. Do not over-suppress.

4. TESTOSTERONE TOO LOW — Below 400 ng/dL total T, libido and erection quality decline for most men. On TRT or cycle this shouldn't be an issue, but during PCT or post-cycle when testosterone is suppressed, ED is expected.

5. CARDIOVASCULAR COMPROMISE — Anabolic steroids, especially orals, increase LDL, decrease HDL, and can cause endothelial dysfunction. Poor vascular health = poor nitric oxide signaling = poor erections. Long-term management: omega-3s, CoQ10, TMAO monitoring, blood pressure control.

--- 2C. PDE5 INHIBITORS (ERECTION COMPOUNDS) ---

PDE5 inhibitors are the gold standard for erectile function. All work by blocking PDE5 to sustain cGMP signaling and prolong smooth muscle relaxation in penile vasculature.

SILDENAFIL (Viagra)
- Dose: 25-100mg PRN (as needed), taken 30-60 min before activity
- Duration of action: 4-6 hours
- Half-life: ~4 hours
- Food interaction: high-fat meals delay absorption
- Best for: on-demand use, rapid onset
- Side effects: headache, flushing, visual disturbance (blue tinge — PDE6 cross-reactivity), nasal congestion
- Bodybuilding note: some athletes use low-dose sildenafil (25-50mg) for muscle pumps — it causes systemic vasodilation and increases nutrient delivery during training

TADALAFIL (Cialis)
- Dose: 5mg daily (daily dosing) OR 10-20mg PRN
- Duration of action: 24-36 hours — the "weekend pill"
- Half-life: ~17.5 hours
- No food interaction
- Best for: daily use, spontaneity, also used for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)
- Side effects: back pain/myalgia (unique to tadalafil), flushing, headache
- Bodybuilding note: daily 5mg tadalafil is widely used by enhanced athletes for cardiovascular benefits, improved blood flow, and baseline erectile support while on cycle

VARDENAFIL (Levitra)
- Dose: 5-20mg PRN
- Duration: 4-5 hours
- Half-life: ~4-5 hours
- More potent per mg than sildenafil
- Better option for men who don't respond well to sildenafil
- Side effects: similar to sildenafil

AVANAFIL (Stendra)
- Dose: 50-200mg PRN
- Duration: 6 hours
- Fastest onset of all PDE5 inhibitors — works in 15 minutes
- Fewest side effects, most selective for PDE5
- Most expensive, less widely available

CONTRAINDICATIONS FOR ALL PDE5 INHIBITORS:
- Do NOT combine with nitrates (nitroglycerin, amyl nitrite/poppers) — severe hypotension, potentially fatal
- Use caution with alpha-blockers
- Use caution in severe cardiovascular disease
- Blood pressure should be monitored — all PDE5 inhibitors cause modest BP reduction

--- 2D. PT-141 (BREMELANOTIDE) — MELANOCORTIN-BASED LIBIDO/ERECTION COMPOUND ---

PT-141 is a synthetic analog of alpha-MSH (alpha-melanocyte stimulating hormone) that acts on melanocortin receptors (MC3R, MC4R) in the central nervous system to drive sexual arousal. Unlike PDE5 inhibitors which work peripherally (blood vessels), PT-141 works centrally — it activates desire and arousal at the brain level.

FDA-approved as Vyleesi for female hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD). Used off-label for both males and females for libido enhancement.

Mechanism: MC4R activation in hypothalamus → increased dopaminergic tone → heightened sexual arousal, desire, and spontaneous erection in men.

Dosing (men):
- 0.5-2mg subcutaneous injection 1-2 hours before sexual activity
- Start at 0.5mg to assess tolerance
- Effect onset: 45-90 minutes
- Duration: 6-12 hours

Side effects:
- Nausea (most common, dose-dependent, often resolves with lower dose)
- Flushing, hyperpigmentation with repeated use
- Transient blood pressure elevation
- Spontaneous erections (which is the point)

Stack notes: PT-141 + tadalafil is a common stack — PT-141 handles central arousal/desire, tadalafil handles the vascular component. This combination is highly effective for psychogenic ED or performance anxiety situations.

--- 2E. NITRIC OXIDE PRECURSORS AND SUPPLEMENTS ---

L-ARGININE / L-CITRULLINE
- Arginine is direct NO precursor; citrulline is more bioavailable and converts to arginine in kidneys
- L-Citrulline: 3-6g pre-training or pre-sex
- Citrulline malate: 6-8g for combined ergogenic and vascular effects
- Synergistic with PDE5 inhibitors

PYCNOGENOL (Pine Bark Extract)
- Works synergistically with L-arginine to enhance NO production
- Clinical dose: 80-120mg/day
- Studies show significant improvement in erectile function when combined with arginine

ZINC
- Required cofactor for testosterone synthesis and NO signaling
- Deficiency is associated with reduced testosterone and libido
- Dose: 15-30mg elemental zinc daily with food

MACA ROOT
- Adaptogenic; improves libido without directly altering hormones
- Dose: 1.5-3g/day
- Has evidence for both male and female libido enhancement

SECTION 3: FEMALE SEXUAL PERFORMANCE
================================================================

--- 3A. PHYSIOLOGY OF FEMALE SEXUAL RESPONSE ---

The female sexual response parallels the male in many neurological and vascular ways. Arousal triggers increased blood flow to the clitoris and vaginal walls via nitric oxide signaling — the same mechanism as penile erection. The clitoris is a large erectile structure (only the glans is visible; the internal structure extends internally). Lubrication is a vascular transudation — fluid from vaginal blood vessels passing through the vaginal walls — driven by arousal.

Female sexual response components:
1. DESIRE/LIBIDO — Driven by testosterone (yes — testosterone is critical for female sexual desire), dopamine, and estrogen
2. AROUSAL — Blood flow to clitoris and vaginal walls; lubrication
3. ORGASM — Involuntary muscle contractions, dopamine/oxytocin surge
4. SATISFACTION/REFRACTORY — Oxytocin and prolactin release post-orgasm; women generally have shorter or no refractory period

Key hormones for female sexual function:
- TESTOSTERONE — Primary driver of female libido. Low testosterone (common in post-menopausal women, women on oral contraceptives which raise SHBG, women post-oophorectomy) = low desire, anorgasmia, reduced sensitivity
- ESTRADIOL — Maintains vaginal tissue health, lubrication, and clitoral sensitivity. Low estradiol (perimenopause, post-menopause) causes vaginal atrophy (GSM), painful intercourse (dyspareunia), reduced sensitivity
- PROGESTERONE — Generally libido-neutral to slightly inhibiting; balances estrogen
- DHEA (Dehydroepiandrosterone) — Converts to both testosterone and estrogen locally in vaginal tissue; key for GSM treatment
- OXYTOCIN — Released during orgasm and bonding; promotes pair bonding and increases likelihood of orgasm

--- 3B. FEMALE SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION — TYPES AND CAUSES ---

HYPOACTIVE SEXUAL DESIRE DISORDER (HSDD)
- Most common female sexual dysfunction
- Persistent low or absent desire causing distress
- Primary causes: low testosterone, hormonal contraceptives (raise SHBG → lower free testosterone), stress, relationship issues, depression, low estrogen
- FDA-approved treatments: Addyi (flibanserin — daily pill, acts on serotonin/dopamine), Vyleesi (PT-141 — injectable, PRN)
- Most effective clinical treatment: testosterone therapy (off-label but well-supported by evidence)

GENITOURINARY SYNDROME OF MENOPAUSE (GSM) / VAGINAL ATROPHY
- Low estrogen causes thinning of vaginal walls, reduced lubrication, dyspareunia, increased UTI risk
- Treatment: local estrogen (vaginal cream, ring, tablet) is first-line; systemic HRT; DHEA suppositories (Intrarosa — FDA approved)
- Ospemifene (Osphena) — oral SERM that acts like estrogen in vaginal tissue without uterine stimulation

ORGASMIC DISORDER
- Difficulty achieving orgasm or markedly diminished intensity
- Causes: low testosterone, insufficient stimulation, SSRIs (major cause — SSRIs blunt orgasm in both sexes), anxiety, relationship factors
- Treatment: address testosterone levels, switch or reduce SSRI if contributing, sensate focus techniques, vibrator use, clitoral stimulation education

DYSPAREUNIA (PAINFUL INTERCOURSE)
- Most common cause in pre-menopausal women: vulvodynia, vaginismus, endometriosis
- Most common cause post-menopause: GSM/vaginal atrophy
- Treatment: lubricants, vaginal estrogen, pelvic floor physical therapy for vaginismus

--- 3C. TESTOSTERONE THERAPY FOR WOMEN — THE MOST UNDER-PRESCRIBED TREATMENT ---

Testosterone is the most important hormone for female sexual desire and it is chronically under-prescribed. Evidence-based clinical data strongly supports testosterone therapy for women with HSDD.

Research findings:
- Women with low testosterone report significantly lower sexual desire, activity, and satisfaction
- Testosterone therapy in women consistently improves desire, arousal, lubrication, orgasm frequency, and sexual satisfaction
- Post-menopausal women show greatest response
- The International Society for the Study of Women's Sexual Health (ISSWSH) and International Menopause Society (IMS) both recommend testosterone for post-menopausal HSDD

Female testosterone dosing:
- Target: physiological range 15-70 ng/dL (NOT the male range)
- Typical protocol: testosterone cream/gel 0.5-2mg/day applied to inner thigh, labia, or forearm
- Some practitioners use injectable testosterone at very low doses (2-5mg/week)
- Enhancement: female athletes and bodybuilders typically use 5-20mg/week testosterone (with monitoring for virilization — deepening voice, clitoral enlargement, hair growth)

PT-141 FOR WOMEN:
- FDA-approved (Vyleesi) at 1.75mg subcutaneous injection PRN up to 1 hour before activity
- Acts on MC4R centrally — drives desire and arousal regardless of hormonal status
- Particularly effective for HSDD when psychological/neurological factors are primary
- Side effects: nausea (30% of women — take with anti-nausea medication if needed), flushing
- Onset: 45 minutes, duration 24 hours

DHEA (PRASTERONE) FOR VAGINAL HEALTH:
- FDA-approved as Intrarosa vaginal insert 6.5mg/day for dyspareunia due to GSM
- Converts locally to estrogen and testosterone in vaginal tissue
- Improves lubrication, tissue health, sensitivity, and reduces pain
- Systemic DHEA (25-50mg oral) also used for libido enhancement in peri/post-menopausal women

OSPEMIFENE (OSPHENA):
- Oral SERM — acts as estrogen in vaginal tissue, neutral/slight antagonist in breast and uterus
- 60mg daily for dyspareunia in post-menopausal women
- Does NOT require vaginal application

--- 3D. HRT AND SEXUAL FUNCTION IN WOMEN — THE EVIDENCE ---

The evidence for HRT improving sexual function in peri- and post-menopausal women is overwhelming and the medical establishment has been too slow to act on it post-WHI study (whose methodology was fundamentally flawed).

Benefits of systemic HRT for sexual function:
- Estradiol restores vaginal lubrication and tissue health
- Testosterone component (when included) restores desire and orgasm intensity
- Progesterone (micronized) is mood-neutral and protects uterus
- Overall quality of life, mood, energy, cognitive function all improve — indirect benefits to sexual function

Types of HRT for optimal sexual outcomes:
- ESTRADIOL patch or gel (not oral conjugated equine estrogens — bioidentical E2 is preferred)
- PROGESTERONE micronized (Prometrium) — not synthetic progestins which blunt libido
- TESTOSTERONE cream/gel added when libido remains low despite adequate E2/P4

ORAL CONTRACEPTIVES AND SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION:
- Combined oral contraceptives (COC) elevate SHBG → bind free testosterone → lower bioavailable testosterone → reduced libido, lubrication, and orgasm intensity in many women
- This is a severely under-discussed side effect
- Non-hormonal contraception or progestin-only methods (no SHBG elevation) may be better options for women experiencing sexual side effects

SECTION 4: TECHNIQUES AND EDUCATION
================================================================

--- 4A. ANATOMY BASICS FOR OPTIMIZING PLEASURE ---

MALE:
- Frenulum — highly sensitive area on underside of glans where foreskin attaches; most sensitive point on the penis for most men
- Corpus cavernosa — paired erectile chambers filling with blood on arousal
- Prostate — accessible via perineum or rectally; stimulation can intensify orgasm (prostate/P-spot orgasm)
- Perineum — area between scrotum and anus; rich in nerve endings, responds to pressure

FEMALE:
- Clitoris — the primary organ of female sexual pleasure. The visible glans is ~1cm but the total structure is 9-11cm internally, wrapping around the vaginal canal. 80%+ of women require direct clitoral stimulation to orgasm
- G-spot (Gräfenberg spot) — located 2-3 inches inside the anterior (front) wall of the vagina; stimulation produces different orgasm quality and in some women triggers ejaculation (squirting — ejaculatory fluid from Skene's glands, not urine)
- Cervix — some women experience intense pleasure from deep cervical stimulation; others find it painful — requires communication
- Vaginal walls — contain mechanoreceptors but fewer nerve endings than clitoris; penetration alone is insufficient for most women to orgasm

--- 4B. ORGASM — PHYSIOLOGY AND OPTIMIZATION ---

MALE ORGASM:
- Two phases: emission (seminal fluid gathering) and expulsion (ejaculation)
- Driven by sympathetic nervous system activation
- Average duration: 3-10 seconds of rhythmic contractions
- Refractory period: increases with age (minutes at 18, can be hours at 50+)
- Testosterone and dopamine are primary drivers of orgasm intensity
- Serotonin antagonizes dopamine post-orgasm → refractory period (why SSRIs delay/prevent orgasm)
- Non-ejaculatory orgasms: through edging and prostate stimulation, males can experience multiple orgasms without ejaculation

FEMALE ORGASM:
- Clitoral orgasm: most common, requires direct clitoral stimulation
- Vaginal/G-spot orgasm: internal stimulation via G-spot; distinct quality, often described as deeper
- Blended orgasm: simultaneous clitoral + internal stimulation; typically most intense
- Cervical orgasm: deep penetration stimulating cervix; not all women experience this positively
- Average duration: 8-35 seconds — significantly longer than male orgasm
- Multiple orgasms: women generally lack a refractory period; multiple orgasms are achievable with continued stimulation
- Key variables: sufficient arousal time, clitoral stimulation, hormonal status (testosterone, estrogen), psychological safety

ORGASM-ENHANCING COMPOUNDS:
- TESTOSTERONE: Increases intensity and frequency of orgasm in both sexes. Most under-prescribed treatment for female anorgasmia.
- PT-141: Drives arousal centrally — orgasm is more likely when arousal is heightened
- OXYTOCIN: Nasal spray oxytocin (intranasal) used by some practitioners to enhance bonding and orgasm intensity; also reduces anxiety
- SEROTONIN MANAGEMENT: SSRIs significantly blunt orgasm. If on an SSRI and experiencing orgasm difficulties, speak with prescriber about switching to bupropion (Wellbutrin — actually enhances sexual function), mirtazapine, or adding buspirone
- DHEA: Improves sensitivity and tissue health in women; associated with improved orgasm in post-menopausal women

--- 4C. PRACTICAL PERFORMANCE TECHNIQUES ---

FOR MEN:
- EDGING (orgasm control): Bringing arousal to near-orgasm repeatedly before ejaculating. Builds orgasm intensity, prolongs intercourse, and with practice allows non-ejaculatory orgasms. Requires awareness of the "point of no return."
- KEGEL EXERCISES: Strengthening pubococcygeus (PC) muscle through repeated contraction/release (50-100 reps/day). Improves ejaculatory control, erection quality, and orgasm intensity. Directly measurable in cycle-trained athletes.
- BREATHING: Slow, controlled breathing during high arousal delays ejaculation and keeps performance anxiety in check.
- WARM-UP TIME: Allow 30-45 minutes of progressive arousal before penetration — allows full engorgement and maximizes sensitivity.
- COMPOUND TIMING: Tadalafil taken nightly or sildenafil 45-60 min prior. PT-141 1-2 hours prior if using.

FOR WOMEN:
- CLITORAL FOCUS: 80%+ of women require direct clitoral stimulation to orgasm. Penetration alone is insufficient for most. Positions that allow clitoral stimulation during penetration (CAT position, woman-on-top leaning forward) significantly improve orgasm rates.
- AROUSAL TIME: Female arousal is slower than male in most cases. Full physical and psychological arousal — sufficient lubrication, clitoral engorgement, vaginal ballooning — typically takes 15-20+ minutes. Rushing this is the primary cause of unsatisfying sex for women.
- KEGEL EXERCISES: PC muscle strength improves orgasm intensity and control. Also treats urinary incontinence. 100-150 reps/day.
- LUBRICATION: Always use lubricant. Even pre-menopausal women benefit. Water-based for most situations; silicone-based for longer duration. Avoid glycerin-containing lubes (yeast infection risk). Avoid petroleum products with condoms.
- VIBRATION: Vibrators applied to the clitoris are the single most effective tool for women who struggle to orgasm. There is no medical or physiological downside to vibrator use.

SECTION 5: PHARMACOLOGICAL STACK PROTOCOLS
================================================================

MALE ED + LIBIDO PROTOCOL (enhanced athlete ON CYCLE):
- Tadalafil 5mg daily (baseline vascular support)
- Cabergoline 0.25mg 2x/week (prolactin control if on 19-nors)
- Anastrozole or aromasin titrated to keep E2 20-30 pg/mL
- L-Citrulline 3g pre-activity
- Zinc 15-30mg/day
- PT-141 0.5-1mg PRN if central desire is low

FEMALE LIBIDO + ORGASM PROTOCOL (PERI/POST-MENOPAUSAL):
- Estradiol patch or gel (standard HRT dose — coordinate with prescriber)
- Progesterone 100-200mg micronized (Prometrium) nightly
- Testosterone cream 0.5-1mg/day applied to inner thigh
- DHEA vaginal insert if dyspareunia present (Intrarosa)
- PT-141 1.75mg PRN (Vyleesi) for acute desire enhancement
- Maca 2g/day for general libido support

FEMALE LIBIDO PROTOCOL (PRE-MENOPAUSAL, ON OCP):
- Consider switching from combined OCP to non-hormonal or progestin-only method to restore free testosterone
- Testosterone cream low-dose if libido remains low after OCP change
- Maca 2-3g/day
- DHEA 25mg daily if appropriate age/hormonal context
- Address any SSRI contribution to anorgasmia with prescriber

SECTION 6: BLOODWORK MARKERS FOR SEXUAL HEALTH
================================================================

MALES — TARGET RANGES FOR OPTIMAL SEXUAL FUNCTION:
- Total Testosterone: 700-1000 ng/dL (TRT target) or higher on cycle
- Free Testosterone: >150 pg/mL
- Estradiol (E2): 20-30 pg/mL (sensitive assay)
- Prolactin: <15 ng/mL (>20 = investigate, >30 = treat aggressively)
- SHBG: 20-50 nmol/L
- DHT: 30-85 ng/dL
- LH/FSH: suppressed on cycle (expected), recovering post-cycle
- Blood Pressure: <130/80 for optimal vascular/erectile function

FEMALES — TARGET RANGES FOR OPTIMAL SEXUAL FUNCTION:
- Total Testosterone: 40-80 ng/dL (physiological range; some women benefit up to 100+)
- Free Testosterone: >5 pg/mL
- Estradiol: follicular phase 30-100 pg/mL; HRT target 60-150 pg/mL
- Progesterone: luteal phase or HRT 5-25 ng/mL
- DHEA-S: 150-300 ug/dL
- SHBG: 30-90 nmol/L (low SHBG = more free testosterone)
- Prolactin: <25 ng/mL

SECTION 7: PSYCHOLOGICAL AND RELATIONSHIP FACTORS
================================================================

Performance anxiety is the most common cause of situational ED in younger men. The brain is the primary sexual organ. Anxiety activates the sympathetic nervous system → vasoconstriction → impaired erection. Physical performance capacity does not protect against psychological ED.

Management:
- Acknowledge it is normal and extremely common
- Remove pressure — focus on mutual pleasure rather than performance metrics
- Sensate focus exercises (non-goal-oriented touch that removes performance pressure)
- Breathing and mindfulness during sexual activity
- PDE5 inhibitors used temporarily while addressing psychological component
- Therapy if chronic — sex therapy is highly effective and evidence-based

For women: Safety, trust, and psychological arousal must precede physical arousal. The autonomic nervous system must shift to parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) state for arousal to occur — fight-or-flight and sexual arousal are incompatible. Women who feel unsafe, anxious, or distracted cannot fully arouse or orgasm regardless of physical stimulation.

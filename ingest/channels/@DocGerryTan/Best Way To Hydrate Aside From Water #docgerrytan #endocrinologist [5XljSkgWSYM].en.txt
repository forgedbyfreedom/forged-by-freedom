Post exercise hydration is fundamental to replace fluid loss from sweat. Now, this study evaluated rehydration and gastrointestinal symptoms for each of the three beverages. First water, second sports drink, and third skim lactosefree milk. Now the study clearly showed that skimmed milk showed higher fluid retention versus water. Skimp milk was more effective than water in post exercise hydration, showing greater fluid retention for the 3-hour followup and presenting with low gastrointestinal symptoms, just similar to those with water and energy drinks. Apparently, the slow release effect is because milk contains protein and fat and electrolytes like sodium and potassium. Thereby, it stays in the stomach longer and is absorbed more slowly into the bloodstream. Furthermore, participants who drunk milk typically produce less urine in the hours following exercise compared to those drinking water.

Evidence from epidemological studies suggest that vitamin D deficiency is associated with reduced insulin release and development of type 2 diabetes. In this study led by WCen, they analyzed 39 randomized control trials involving 3,000 participants to see if vitamin D actually helps manage blood sugar. They found that vitamin D group compared to place group significantly reduced A1c by.3% fasting blood glucose reduced by 49 mill moles per liter with improvement in insulin resistance. Furthermore, the meta analysis revealed that vitamin D is most effective as a complimentary treatment when the following conditions are met. First, those started with low vitamin D levels saw the biggest improvement. Those highdose short-term supplementation was more effective than low doses over a very long period of time. and the effects were more prominent in patients who were overweight, including those starting with an A1c of 8% or higher, showed more significant progress. Study also found that a dose as high as 2,000 international units per day provided a mark improvement in glycemic control and fasting blood sugar as long as the duration of supplementation was over 12 weeks.

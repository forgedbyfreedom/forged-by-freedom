The study published in the Journal of Clinical Biochemistry investigated whether the order in which food is eaten can affect blood sugar levels in patients with type 2 diabetes. It explored how changing the order of food intake, specifically eating vegetables first before carbohydrates, can affect blood sugar levels based on glucose excursions via CGMS or A1c levels after 2.5 years. In the study, the quantity was such that one meal had 500 g of vegetables with meals containing around 21 g of dietary fiber. The study showed that the group where they eat vegetables first before carbohydrates had significant improvement in A1c with A1c dropping from 8.3 to 7.1%. Now, the vegetables first meals were fairly high in fiber, which likely plays a major role. The type of vegetables, how they are prepared, the total meal composition like protein, fat, and starch can also influence the effect. Remember, adherence over long term is key.

Early dinner or more than 12-hour fasting between dinner and breakfast has many advantages such as weight loss, lower risk of diabetes, including cancer and death. Whereas late dinner is not a healthy habit. In fact, in a recent randomized crossover trial, they found that eating dinner at 6 versus 900 p.m. of same calorie meal led to lower mean 24-hour blood glucose levels, and improved substrate oxidation, fat versus carbohydrate burning the next morning. The study showed that late dinner eaters had peaked blood glucose levels almost 20% higher, and fat burning reduced by 10% compared to early eaters. Most likely because with early dinners, there tends to be less late night snacking, possibly fewer total calories consumed, better appetite regulation, and possibly increase fat oxidation. Likewise, early dinner can be part of your protocol of intermittent fasting with your first meal around noon. Tips to adopt an early dinner habit is trying to finish your main evening meal 2 to three hours or more before bedtime so digestion is underway before you lie down.

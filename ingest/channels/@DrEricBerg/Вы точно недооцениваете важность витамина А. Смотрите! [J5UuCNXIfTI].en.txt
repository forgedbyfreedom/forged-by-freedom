Vitamin A helps differentiate certain cells, meaning that with a vitamin A deficiency, cells that are not related to the eyes can form in the eyes. The same thing happens with the skin, both outside and inside the body. If you have rough, dry skin, this may be caused by a vitamin A deficiency because the cells don't form the cells that should be there; they form a rougher version of these cells. Vitamin A is very, very important for the immune system. It's especially important for fighting viruses. Fertility depends on vitamin A. We are often told about its toxicity. But what about vitamin A deficiency? And how can this affect a growing child? As we remember, fat-soluble vitamins penetrate directly into DNA and affect genes and chromosomes. This especially affects the growth and development of a child.

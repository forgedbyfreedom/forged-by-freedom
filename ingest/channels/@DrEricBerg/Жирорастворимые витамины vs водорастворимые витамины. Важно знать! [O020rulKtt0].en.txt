What is the difference between fat-soluble vitamins and water-soluble vitamins? There is a huge difference at the cellular level. Fat-soluble vitamins penetrate the cell membrane and enter directly into the nucleus and bind to receptors that can activate certain genes, while water-soluble vitamins are more like coenzymes; they act as auxiliary particles that do not penetrate the nucleus itself. So, fat- soluble vitamins are very, very important: vitamins A, D, E, K1, K2. There are other fat-soluble substances in vegetables, and so on. These are phytonutrients, especially carotenoids such as lutein and zeaxanthin. They are essential for the eyes and have an anti-cancer effect. Chlorophyll from some plants is also useful.

Recently we asked an interesting question about why I become angry and irritable during fasting. This is a relevant question because many have experienced this for themselves. I will tell you why this happens. There are three main types of fuel that our body uses: sugar, ketones, and fatty acids. Therefore, when we reduce carbohydrates and sugar and begin to produce ketones as the main fuel, we use only 40 percent of the ketones produced, the remaining 60 are fatty acids. The switch responsible for converting sugar to ketones does not always work well. If you have a history of insulin resistance, the body's adaptation to changes will take some time. During fasting, you may experience irritation, become angry, be grumpy, and unfriendly. Stress resistance may also decrease. And all this happens for only two reasons. The first years of insulin resistance greatly impede the access of glucose to cells, because insulin is needed to absorb glucose. If you have insulin resistance, the cells do not receive glucose, and now you are on a low-carb diet, you will lack glucose even more, because now you  You depend on ketones and fatty acids. The problem is that the brain is the only organ that can't use fatty acids. It only has access to ketones and glucose. You haven't yet fully transitioned to using ketones, so your brain begins to suffer, and along with it, your mood. You have two options: you can simply wait until your body fully adapts, your metabolism will become more efficient and flexible, and your brain will switch to ketones. Or, during the transition period, add additional ketones to your diet. You can use M-City oil, either in powder form or something like Diton, but they're not cheap. The main thing is to remember that this is only a temporary transition. Don't give up on this. You need to go through it, reduce carbohydrates, and you'll feel great.

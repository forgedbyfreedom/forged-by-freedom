The pancreas regulates blood glucose levels by secreting insulin. This hormone is produced in areas of the gland known as the islets of Langerhans, which are filled primarily with beta cells. Coming from the beta cells, insulin helps glucose enter various cells, such as skeletal muscle cells. Their walls contain insulin receptors that help cells absorb glucose for energy. Increasing carbohydrate consumption per unit of time leads to excess insulin in the blood. This condition is called insulin dipper. To protect against excess insulin, cells do not allow their receptors to bind to insulin. This blocks the entry of glucose into cells. This condition is called insulin resistance. To cope with high glucose levels, insulin converts glucose into fat, which leads to obesity. This further increases insulin levels. Over time, beta cells become depleted and reduce insulin production. As a result, high blood glucose levels occur. insulin resistance and low insulin production together lead to a variety of health problems. Over time, the number and volume of islets decreases, insulin becomes smaller, and blood sugar rises too high. This is diabetes.

How different are salmon and cod? Which of these two fish is healthier? First of all, I want to point out that when choosing salmon or cod, make sure that they are commercially caught and farmed. There is a common misconception that cod is preferable because eating fatty fish is thought to clog arteries. This is a complete lie.  Let's make a comparison.  Take 85 g of salmon and cod.  Salmon will have 25.4g of protein, while cod will have 22.8g. Salmon will have 1.2g of saturated fat and much less, only trace amounts of saturated fat in cod. Let's go over vitamin B3.  We have 85g of each fish.  Salmon contains 45% of the daily value of B3, while three-barreled salmon contains only 10%. B12 45% vs 15%. B6 45% vs 10%. B2 25 versus only 4%. B1 - 15% versus 6%.  As we can see, salmon contains much more B vitamins. Fats 6% versus 2%. Both types of fish contain some mercury, but there's no need to worry about it because they also contain quite a bit of selenium, in about equal amounts.  Selenium is used in the body by some enzymes to combat the toxic effects of mercury.  But look at the huge difference here.  Salmon contains 2.208 mg of omega-3 fatty acids, while cod contains only 171 mg.  As you can see, salmon is definitely the winner. Many people eat cod in dishes such as fish and chips, where it is deep fried.  Typically this fish is farm raised and is not nearly as healthy.  I also advise that when you eat fish, eat it with the skin on.  Both skin and subcutaneous fat contain a lot of useful things. So, here are my conclusions about the comparison of salmon and cod.

And if you're not yet an Olympian, or you're an athlete who's pro-bound, or a leader, or anyone who wants to make the most of pressure, you can use the same pieces of advice. And here they are. One, embrace the competitive spirit. Competition does not have to mean the threat of comparison. This environment is charged for a reason. When prepared, committed people come together with a shared purpose. Energy rises. But that energy is not a threat. It's fuel. Embracing the competitive spirit means allowing the environment to elevate you rather than intimidate you. This is not only about beating others. It's about letting the environment draw out your best. Two, you belong. You were chosen. There are many ways to be chosen. Selection processes, they do differ. But the outcome is the same. You are here. You belong. Imposter syndrome has no place this week. Not because doubt never shows up, but because doubt is not evidence. Preparation is not luck. You don't need to justify your presence. Three, trust the strengths that got you here. This is not the week to reinvent. Now, you may notice others doing things differently, different routines, different approaches. That does not mean you should change. This is not the time to experiment. Your strengths got you here. And remember, familiarity stabilizes the nervous system. Consistency protects confidence. Stay true to your plan. Four, reframe social comparison as coopetition. If comparison shows up, or I should say when it does, reframe it. This is cooperative competition. Think this. Thank you for showing up today, opponents. Thank you for pushing the standard. I'm going to use your presence to bring out my best. Sometimes you will learn from others. Other times they will learn from you. That exchange is sports personship. Competition can be sharpened this way. Five, have fun and take it all in. You are allowed to enjoy this experience. Watch other sports. Support other athletes. Laugh. Play. Immerse yourself in the experience. The Olympics are not only about the moment you compete. They are about the entire event. So ask yourself, how do I want to remember the Olympics? Not just the performance, the whole thing. And this applies far beyond the Olympics. You can use this approach in anything that you do, in any competition or performance moment. A championship, a presentation, a leadership decision, a difficult conversation, a life transition. The principles are all the same. Belonging. Trust. Preparation. Presence. Purpose. High pressure environments don't require more effort. They require better organization of attention. Let's bring it all together. The Olympics can be about trust. Trust your preparation.

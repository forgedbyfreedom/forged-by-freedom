 Most people have already given up on their New Year's resolutions by now, but you don't have to. I'm Dr. John Mitchell, and here are six tips to keep going. Think big, but start small. It's okay to think big about starting new habits, but start off with small steps. Want to run a 5K? Start with a short distance, then build up over time. Don't wait for motivation. Action creates momentum. One thing you can do is touch the task. If your goal is to read a long book, touch the task by opening it to page one and reading the first paragraph. Plan for setbacks. You may not make it to the gym today, but that doesn't mean your workout plan is doomed. Remind yourself that progress is full of ups and downs, and start planning for tomorrow. Connect your behavior to your values. A resolution isn't just changing the behavior. It's important to you because it touches on a value, like living a healthy life, having better relationships, or growing as a person. Make it enjoyable. Sometimes we neglect this when setting resolutions. Celebrate small wins. Paraport the friend to encourage each other. There are a lot of ways to find joy in the journey. Finally, keep resetting your intentions. All the advice in the world won't get you to establish a new behavioral routine. Don't quit pivot. Every new day is a chance to reset your intention to a resolution. Start again and keep moving forward. Here's to a happy, healthy new year with resolutions that stick.

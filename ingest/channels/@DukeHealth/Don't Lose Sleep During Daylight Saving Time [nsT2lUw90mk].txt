 An hour of sleep is a big deal. My name is Andrew Spector and I'm one of the sleep medicine physicians here at Duke. Sleep is very important to cognitive health. The increased sleepiness that you experience from a shorter night of sleep just means you're not functioning at your peak. Even just one hour can trigger an increased risk of heart attacks. Car accidents are also more common. One of the most important things to do before you go into the time change or really any anticipated loss of sleep would be to start banking sleep a couple nights in advance. You'll just be a little bit more resilient to that loss of an hour. Set your clocks forward that hour before you've gone to bed because you've already started the process of adapting at that point. The earlier you go to bed on the new time change, you'll wake up and feel better the next morning. When you do wake up the next morning, get outside, get that bright sunshine. That helps reset your circadian rhythm to the new time quicker. It's not unusual for people to struggle with the one hour time change for a day or two, but your body will relatively quickly adapt.

Kids may start with a low-grade fever, then develop mouth ulcers and red bumps or blisters on hands, feet, and sometimes the trunk or buttocks. There's no cure for hand, foot, and mouth disease, but you can manage symptoms like pain and fever with acetaminophen or ibuprofen. Cold drinks or popsicles help soothe mouth pain and keep kids hydrated. Yes, adults can get hand, foot, and mouth disease, but it's usually milder. Parents can catch it from their kids, especially if they're sharing food or drinks. There's no vaccine to protect us from hand, foot, and mouth disease. Good hygiene is the key. Wash your hands well, avoid sharing drinks, and keep sick kids home until their symptoms have improved and they've been fever-free for 24 hours. Hand, foot, and mouth disease can be tough on little ones, but with good care and hygiene, most kids recover quickly. For more information, visit dukehealth.org.

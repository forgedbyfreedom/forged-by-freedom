 Have you ever considered using saffron for depression? Here are some reasons why you may or may not want to. Saffron is a spice derived from the saffron-crogus flower that may have a variety of biological effects. In clinical trials, people with mild to moderate depression receiving saffron have pretty consistently reported reductions in symptoms of depression as well as increased rates of remission compared to placebo. A handful of studies have also compared saffron to commonly used antidepressants like SSRIs and have generally reported similar levels of effectiveness. Still, there are some limitations that should be considered. The studies on saffron have mostly been small and short in duration, usually including under 100 people for 12 weeks or less. Compared to antidepressants like SSRIs, this represents a much weaker evidence base in terms of both safety and effectiveness. Most trials have also been performed in Iran, so we don't have a lot of data confirming that these benefits are generalizable to other populations.

Having said this, if we evaluated other\h strength and hypertrophy research papers,\h\h it becomes evident that regardless of\h whether we are talking about performing\h\h 5 or 10 sets of the German Volume\h method, both are likely suboptimal. Remember, with German volume training, you\h perform sets of 10 repetitions with a 60%\h\h one-rep max load, while using\h 60-90 seconds of rest between sets. The first issue here relates to how far\h from failure you would be on your sets. With a 60% one-rep max load, you can generally\h perform approximately 20 repetitions. As a result, your 1st set\h with German volume training\h\h is roughly 10 repetitions away from failure. For optimizing muscle hypertrophy,\h\h you'd likely want to be 5 or fewer\h repetitions away from failure each set. Now, your successive sets will get closer and\h closer to failure, and your later sets will\h\h definitely be well within 5 or fewer repetitions\h from failure, thanks to cumulative fatigue. In fact, there is data demonstrating\h some individuals cannot fully complete\h\h 10 repetitions on the final few\h sets of German volume training. However, this still does not resolve the fact\h the first few sets are probably suboptimal for\h\h building muscle. Therefore, it would likely\h be more efficient and productive to set up\h\h your training in a way where your working sets\h are at or within 5 repetitions from failure. The second problem is that you only\h rest 60-90 seconds between sets. Compound exercises are typically used with\h German volume training, and as thoroughly\h\h examined in our rest interval video, the\h research suggests with compound exercises,\h\h 2.5 or minutes of rest between sets is optimal\h for building muscle, as well as likely strength.

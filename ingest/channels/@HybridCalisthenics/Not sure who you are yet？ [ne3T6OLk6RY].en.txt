My friend, if you're not sure who you are yet, it doesn't necessarily mean you're lost. You could just be trying new things. So, in the earlier part of my college days, I studied in California, and a lot of things were still new to me then. People would say, "Hey, come to this party." I'd be like, "Uh, sure, I guess." So, a lot of us go through a season of saying yes to things just to see [music] what sticks. But a few years later, I studied in Germany, and there I actually talked with people less, had a better time, and got along with people more. Now, I wasn't antisocial, but by that point, I knew that I like to build things, work out, and study calisthenics, which is what I was doing at the time. Other people seem to pick up on that. We all had a good time. Trying new things and realizing it's not for you is still part of learning and growing. Doesn't mean you're failing at life. You're just crossing things off the list. And over time, I think this is how you become more comfortable with yourself. As you start to learn more about what you do want, it gets easier to say no to things you don't want. So, if you're in that middle part, you are not alone. It can be part of the learning process and you can get more comfortable as you go.

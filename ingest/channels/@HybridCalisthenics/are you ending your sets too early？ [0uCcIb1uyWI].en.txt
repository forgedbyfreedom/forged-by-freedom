I used to aim for perfect form in my pull-ups and that would actually slow down my progress. And that's because if you go through a full range of motion, the muscles that are emphasized up here are quite different than the ones that are emphasized down here. The ones up here are smaller and may tire out first, but because I wanted to be strict with my form, I might end a set where I could no longer get up there even if I had plenty in the tank for down here. So, some of my larger, stronger back muscles were not pushed as much as they could be. And the first possible solution is you could just do a dedicated set just to the upper part of the range. But some people might say, "Wow, you're ego lifting." And to that you could say, "Well, ego backwards is just OG E." Which doesn't mean anything, but neither does what they said. So, alternatively, you could just finish out your set even after you can't get quite up here. You still do a partial range of motion, which really won't be that many more reps because these are still getting worked. And then you can call it done. Have a beautiful day.

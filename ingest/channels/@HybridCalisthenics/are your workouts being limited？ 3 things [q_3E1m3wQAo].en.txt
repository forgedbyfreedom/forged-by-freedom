Here are three limiters that often stop beginners from training the muscles they want to train and three helpers to help you get strong now while training your weak spots. When I ask people to do as many push-ups as they safely can. Sometimes what stops them is not their chest or the shoulders but actually their wrist. And if that's you then you can try doing push-ups on your fist with a cushion or you can use dumbbells so your wrist can stay more neutral during a push-up so you can work your chest and your arms more. Then you can do some wrist mobility work afterwards. And second, if your heels come up during your squats then don't worry, you're not alone. It happens to a lot of people. And if you build up properly, it's actually okay to squat this way. Some people do it on purpose, but it is a little bit harder on the knees and it can be a bit harder to balance. So, it may help you to have a squat wedge or something under your heels. And this can help you feel a bit more stable during your squats. Also, as a temporary workaround, slightly wider stance and maybe feet slightly out help some people get into position and then they can work some ankle mobility afterwards. And third, sometimes grip strength stop some people from doing their pull-ups. You can use some lifting straps to help you hold on. And then you can do some assisted hangs or easier variations to help you build grip strength afterwards. I don't think it's cheating to use the tools that you have available to you, especially if it helps you get stronger faster.

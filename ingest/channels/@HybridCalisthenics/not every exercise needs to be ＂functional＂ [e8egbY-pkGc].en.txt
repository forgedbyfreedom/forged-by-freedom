Not every exercise needs to be functional. And here's what I mean. You may have heard that training should imitate life. And I mostly agree with this. The exercises we do should help us improve at the things we want to do or want to do better. But then you also have exercises like the front raise or the bicep curl where some people will say, "Well, that's just not functional because I'll never do that out in nature." And the retort to that is usually, "Uh-huh. It's very functional if you want to build big biceps and shoulders." And the retort to that is like, "Well, I want I don't want to build big biceps and shoulders." And like, "Well, then you don't have to do them." It's like, "Well, then I won't do them then." And they're like, "Fine, fine. Never talk to me or my shoulder again." And while those can be good just for the sake of building muscle, we're still using those same muscles when we do exercises that we perceive as more functional. I've used this comparison a lot, but you can train your software by doing whatever exercises you like that look like the things you want to get better at, but that software is still firing on the same hardware components. And if you make those parts bigger and or stronger, even with movements that don't look very functional, then the movements you like may get stronger and better as well. Have a beautiful day.

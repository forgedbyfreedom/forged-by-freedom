I've done calisthenics for a few years, and this is one of the most useful things I've learned. And it's something that's arguably most important for beginners and people way more advanced than me for slightly different reasons. As you train, your muscles will develop and get stronger faster than your joints. And it's important for beginners because our bodies have the potential to be very strong, maybe even stronger than we realize. And if we haven't worked out before, we can make a lot of progress quite quickly. And because this progress can be very motivating, we might sometimes ignore some nagging joint issues because we think it's normal when it's not. So, it might be worth it to spend some time on some easier exercises, even if you're strong enough to move on, so you can build up some reps and learn the difference between effort and pain. And it's a bit ironic because some people say beginners aren't strong enough to hurt themselves. But you work out for a few weeks and congratulations, you're strong enough to hurt yourself now. And for some more advanced people who are way stronger than I am, a lot of them are trying to push the limits of what they can do and really on the edge of that 1 second hold, 2C hold, one rep. But remember, pushing the limit of your strength is also pushing the limit of your ability to protect yourself. So, if you're feeling a bit iffy, maybe spend some time on a slightly easier variation with higher reps.

Self-assisted exercises are great for helping some people make progress, but a lot of people leave out a key detail. See, the difference between assisting a little bit and assisting a lot doesn't seem like that much, but it can be like 10 or 20 lbs, which is a lot for a single arm exercise. And the reason that's important is you might be getting stronger even if you're doing the same amount of reps. When I first did these, I would leave bruises on my own wrist. And when the bruises stopped and I was doing the same amount of reps, I figured I was assisting less, so I got stronger. But thankfully, there is a better way than that. You can make it more measurable if you use a type of assistance where you aren't able to assist as much even if you're trying as hard as you can. For example, gripping higher can be easier because you can get a better grip. Gripping lower is harder. Similarly, gripping all the way around easier, using flat palm harder. But it's also important to make your assistance specific to the thing you're training because you can use things like fewer fingers and that will feel harder and it will feel tempting to make it more measurable. But in my opinion, this doesn't transfer quite as well to something like the one- arm pull-up. But if it works well for you, that's great. Have a beautiful day.

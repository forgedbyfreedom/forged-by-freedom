People sometimes argue high reps versus low reps and they both have benefits. But one extra benefit of higher reps is it allows you to practice the movement even if you're already strong enough for heavier weight. See, at one point you were probably a baby. Maybe you're still a baby and you were like, "Ah, too weak to lift head." And people were like, "Bro, what are you doing? Can't you even walk? Walking is so easy, dude. Look at me." And then you may have lifted your head and then you may have started walking. Might have taken some practice first. But we probably didn't master all movement then. And we continue learning movements this way through practice pretty much our entire lives. Even ones that look relatively simple, like just having straight arm strength. There's small little parts that we practice and measure through each repetition as we master it, like strengthening the arms a bit and what it might feel like to rely on your shoulder. You can combine this with gradually adding weight and using lower reps because there's some skill involved in that as well. We can push ourselves while maybe having some patience with ourselves.

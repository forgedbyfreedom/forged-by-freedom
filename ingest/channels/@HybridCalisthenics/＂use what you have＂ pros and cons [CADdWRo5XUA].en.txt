One of my favorite things about home workouts is also one of my least favorite things and that is the idea of using what you have. I love the idea that most of us can get fit and strong with just our body and gravity. And I still believe that. But when it comes to something like a pull-up bar, I know that not everyone has access to one, but after like 10 years of trying to get beginners to work with things like tables or elbow presses, I would now start with 10 sets of trying to get a good pull-up bar. You can buy, build, or rent. Now, if what you're doing is working or really all you have, then by all means, please keep doing it. But I have noticed that some beginners may struggle with angles and technique where they're essentially limited by weaker muscles rather than stronger ones. Or they might be frustrated by a lack of progress, but the thing they're trying to use is hard to grip and holding them back. So tree branches and stuff can be great. I have used those, too. But once you're pretty sure that you want to be serious about fitness, then getting some good workout equipment that you can afford might be a worthwhile upgrade. Not financial advice.

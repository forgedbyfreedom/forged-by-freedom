Are antiviral medications beneficial for patients with mild to moderate influenza? A recent study published in JAMAMA internal medicine suggests that antivirals make little difference in the outpatient setting, although blox may be superior to standard care in some patients. This systematic review and meta analysis compared influenza antiviral drugs to placebo, standard care, or another antiviral in individuals with non severe influenza. Non- severe influenza was defined as an illness that did not require hospitalization. In total, the authors identified 73 randomized clinical trials involving more than 34,000 participants. Three main findings are clear to me. One, compared with standard care or placebo, none of the antiviral drugs studied had any effect on mortality. Two, for low-risk outpatients, none of the antiviral drugs reduce the risk of hospital admission. Meanwhile, for high-risisk outpatients such as those over the age of 65, people with chronic lung, heart, or kidney disease, and immunosuppressed individuals, only bloxave may prevent hospitalization. Compared with standard care placebo, you would need to treat 63 high-risk individuals with Blox to prevent one hospitalization. Three, antivirals reduced average symptom duration by about a half day to one day. Voxavir did not increase rates of adverse events. However, Ulsa Tamavir probably slightly increased the rates of adverse events. How will this study change your practice heading into the winter respiratory virus season?

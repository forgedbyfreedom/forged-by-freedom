Imagine signing up for treatment that has no proven benefit at a facility that has no regulations to vouch for its safety. That's what seems to be happening at IV hydration spas across the US. I'm Kate Schwitzer with Jamma Medical News and I reported on this fast growing industry which promises a full menu of alluring wellness treatments from immunity boosters to hangover cures. But do they work and are they safe? Let's take a look. In a new study published in JAMAMA Internal Medicine, researchers found that not a single US state had enacted any laws regarding IV hydration spas. Although many states did have policies addressing oversight of certain aspects of these spas, as one expert told me, they've been able to slip between the cracks of state and federal regulation. As it is, the FDA already has limited authority over the medical spa industry. That's because the facilities are considered independent compoundingarmacies. They can offer versions of FDA approved products like IV fluids that are often combined with other components such as vitamins, minerals, and electrolytes, which aren't under FDA purview. To better understand practices at these spas, the researchers made secret shopper phone calls asking about their services, and they reviewed their websites. All sites included claims about the benefits of IV treatments, but only two of them actually cited a source. There's limited clinical data supporting IV therapy in this setting. A medical toxicologist noted that a vitamin deficiency, for instance, does not mean you need it in access. Experts hope that this research will lead to more regulation of medical spas, but they recognize the challenge facing clinicians. As one study author told me, his goal is for these findings to open the door for more productive conversations between clinicians and their patients about the reasons they're pursuing care in these non-regulated settings.

to post-operative cardiovascular complications a study in the current issue of JAMA investigates the association between unrecognized OSA and post-operative cardiovascular events in patients undergoing major non cardiac surgery the researchers studied more than 1300 patients scheduled for major non cardiac surgery at eight hospitals in five countries patients were eligible for the study if they were 45 years or older had one or more risk factors for post-operative cardiovascular events and had no prior sleep apnea diagnosis participants underwent an overnight sleep study before their surgery either at home during the preceding month or in the surgical ward the night before surgery patients were classified as having mild moderate or severe OSA the primary outcome was a composite of myocardial injury cardiac death heart failure thromboembolism atrial fibrillation and stroke within 30 days of surgery let's move on to the results the authors found a high prevalence of OSA among 12 hundred and 18 patients who underwent a sleep study and surgery within 30 days after surgery 30% of pain patients with severe OSA 22% with moderate OSA and 19% with mild OSA experienced at least one of the events in the composite primary outcome among the patients without OSA 14% most of the events were myocardial injury defined as an increase in troponin t levels with or without ischemic symptoms or ECG changes in multi variable analyses controlling for perioperative factors known to adversely affect outcomes severe OSA remains statistically significantly associated with a higher rate of post-operative cardiovascular events hazard estimates for adverse cardiovascular outcomes also were higher among patients with mild and moderate OSA compared to those without but the difference was not statistically significant the authors conclude that unrecognized severe OSA is associated with an increased risk of 30-day post-operative cardiovascular complications the study provides observational evidence that OSA is a risk factor for perioperative complications it also hints that preoperative testing for OSA in patients at higher cv risk might identify those whose risk for complications might be reduced but it leaves open the question of how best to intervene in these patients and which interventions if any might best prevent myocardial injury and other adverse events

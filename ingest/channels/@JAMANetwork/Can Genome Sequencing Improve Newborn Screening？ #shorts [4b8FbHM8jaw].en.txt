Can genome sequencing improve newborn screening? This study that was just published in JAMAMA has some [music] answers. This is the Guardian study. It aims to enroll 100,000 newborns to see if genome sequencing can help detect more medical conditions early on. This paper reports preliminary results from the first 4,000 participants. Newborn screening in New York State, where this study took place, routinely screens for 57 different conditions at birth. The Guardian study screened for 156 different conditions. Families were also able to opt in to screen for an additional 99 conditions associated with neurodedevelopmental delay or [music] seizures. This study aims to enroll a diverse population based on parent reported race and ethnicity. Of the families asked to join the study, 72% ended up enrolling. After data validation, there were 120 newborns who screened positive for at least one of the conditions on this panel. 110 newborns tested positive for conditions that were not identified by traditional newborn screening. This included things like severe combined immuno deficiency, long QT syndrome, and Wilson [music] disease. For children with these conditions, early detection and intervention could help improve outcomes. The Guardian study is still ongoing, and there hasn't yet been enough time to see if these positive screening results actually translate into diagnosis. As experts will point out, your DNA is not your diagnosis. That [music] said, this is an important and large-scale study that demonstrates the feasibility and efficacy of using genome sequencing to improve newborn screening.

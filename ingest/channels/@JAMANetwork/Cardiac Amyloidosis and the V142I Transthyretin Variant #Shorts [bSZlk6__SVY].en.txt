Some data have shown that self-identified black individuals who have cardiac amaloid who may or may not have the B142i variant have more severe disease than white individuals and this is after adjusting for a lot of potentially confounding conditions at the time of initial care. Now this has raised the question of whether there are biological underpinnings for this observation. For example, whether there are differences in social determinance of health that might relate. And so our analysis supports the importance of the B142i variant in itself leading to more advanced markers of the disease. But this really doesn't discount the importance of social determinance of health or coorbidities in thinking about an individual person's trajectory over time. And so extending these concepts just a little bit further in our study for example we identified several coorbidities that portended similar relative risk to having the variance such as hypertension. Now the variant is much less common in black individuals than hypertension is and therefore the population attributable fraction for hypertension is much greater than the variant. Similarly, the social determinance of health likely have a much greater impact at the population level of risk than the variant. This does not minimize the importance of the variant, but highlights that cardiovascular outcomes really have much more complex underpinnings. And so, our team is really interested in trying to understand why do some carriers progress toward disease while others appear to have a benign trajectory despite both being carriers. And we hope that future work can help us understand and disentangle these clinical, social, and genetic determinants of disease progression.

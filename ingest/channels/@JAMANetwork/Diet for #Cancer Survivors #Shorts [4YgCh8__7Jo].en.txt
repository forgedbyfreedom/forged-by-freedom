Changes in diet can also support survivorship. Recommendations include an increase in fruits and vegetables and a decrease in red meat and alcohol use. Researchers evaluated over 900 patients with stage 3 colon cancer enrolled on CALGB8983, a randomized algivvent chemotherapy trial. Investigators assigned each patient a score based on body mass index, physical activity, and intake of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and red processed meats. Those that followed physical activity and nutritional guidelines had a longer survival than those with lower scores. These types of data suggest adherence to guidelines leads to improved outcomes. But we need to do better in encouraging our patients to exercise and maintain a healthy diet after a cancer diagnosis. Bowman and colleagues performed a cross-sectional study using survey data from the behavioral risk factor surveillance system. The investigators evaluated adherence to ACS nutrition and physical activity guidelines among over 9,000 cancer survivors. While 72% met criteria for physical activity and 68% were not obese, only 12% of respondents ate adequate fruits and vegetables.

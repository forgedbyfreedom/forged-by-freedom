The study surveyed almost 7,000 healthcare workers in the US. Direct care and support workers reported the highest rates of hardship. This category of workers includes occupations like home health aids, pharmacy aids, phabotamist, and dental assistance. Among this group of workers, almost 10% experience poverty. Almost 14% experienced housing insecurity, which means not being able to pay rent, mortgage, or utility bills. And about a quarter reported food insecurity, which is when the food you buy doesn't last, you're not able to afford balanced meals, or you need to skip meals because you don't have enough money. Physicians and surgeons reported the lowest rates of financial hardship. The researchers also surveyed nurses, other diagnosing or treating practitioners, and health technologists and technicians.

GLP-1s and beriatric surgery. Let's talk about it. A recent study published in JAMAMA surgery followed more than 30,000 patients with class 2 or class 3 obesity who were treated either with beriatric surgery or GLP1s. The main outcomes of their study were total weight loss and cost of treatment over 2 years. Patients who were treated solely with GLP-1s had higher costs than patients treated with beriatric surgery, a higher number of emergency department visits, and less weight loss. Among patients in whom weight loss data were recorded, those who underwent beriatric surgery lost a mean of 28% of their starting body weight, whereas patients treat the GLP-1s lost an average of 10%. Although this study looked at each treatment independently, another recent publication in JAMAMA surgery looked at the realworld use of GLP1s after beriatric surgery between 2015 and 2023. It revealed 1 in 10 patients who had undergone beriatric surgery initiated a GLP-1 medication within the first 5 years after their operation. The findings of these two studies combined show us that although beriatric surgery remains the most effective clinical treatment of obesity, this landscape is continuing to evolve and these two treatments are increasingly being used in combination before and after surgery.

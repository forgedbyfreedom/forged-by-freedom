Can hearing loss increase your dementia risk? A major study says it might, and there's something you can do about it. A study in JAMA Otolaryngology examined more than 573,000 adults age 50 and over in Denmark with follow-up through nearly a decade. The researchers reported that people with hearing loss had a higher risk of developing dementia. And the more severe the hearing loss, the greater the risk. Here's the really important part. People who did not use hearing aids for hearing loss had a 20% higher risk of dementia compared to those with normal hearing. Those who did use hearing aids, their risk was only 6% higher. That's a big difference. So, why does this matter? To some extent, hearing loss is a modifiable risk factor. The thinking is that when you can't hear well, your brain has to work harder just to process sound, leaving fewer resources for memory and cognition. People with hearing loss also tend to withdraw socially, and that isolation might accelerate decline. The bottom line, this research suggests that lowering dementia risk is an additional reason why people with hearing loss might want to consider using hearing aids. All the details are in this JAMA Otolaryngology article.

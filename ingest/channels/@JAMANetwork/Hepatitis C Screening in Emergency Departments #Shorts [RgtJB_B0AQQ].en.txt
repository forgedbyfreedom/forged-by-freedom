This is hepatitis C virus or heepsi. Hepsi infections have increased during the past decade driven largely by the opioid epidemic. Around 4 million people in the US are infected with hepatitis C and about 20% remain undiagnosed. But a new trial published in JAMAMA may have a method for identifying more cases at early stages, which is crucial because heepsi is responsible for about 15,000 deaths each year in the US despite the availability of treatment which can cure most patients. Emergency departments present an opportunity to increase HEPS screening since they provide medical care to lots of patients who might not otherwise access health care. The detect hepsi trial tested two different ways to screen adult patients for heepsi in three large urban emergency departments. Patients were randomly assigned to either non-targeted screening where testing was offered regardless of risk or targeted screening in which testing was offered based on risk assessment using criteria like age or history of drug use. Non-targeted screening identified about a third more patients with heepsi than targeted screening. Although this approach required testing more than twice the number of people. Most importantly, among patients that were diagnosed with heepsi, only small proportions from both screening groups received follow-up care and ultimately attained biologic cure.

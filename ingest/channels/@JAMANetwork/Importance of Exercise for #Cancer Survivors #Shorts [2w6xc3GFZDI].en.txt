A sedentary lifestyle should be avoided by cancer survivors. In a prospective cohort study in about 1500 cancer survivors followed for a median of 4.5 years, investigators found that those who were sedentary, especially those who sat more than 8 hours a day were at an increased risk of all cause cancer and non-cancer mortality. What is the exercise prescription for cancer survivors? Recommendations vary, but the American Cancer Society and ASCO recommend 150 minutes of moderate intensity aerobic activity or 75 minutes of vigorous intensity activity with strength training at least 2 days a week. But many forms of exercise can have clinical impact. One trial randomized patients with advanced lung cancer to aerobic exercise, Tai Chi, and a control group and then performed a 16-week intervention. Although the primary outcome was improved sleep quality, one of the secondary outcomes was one-year survival. 226 patients were randomized. And while both aerobic exercise and Tai Chi improve sleep, only the Tai Chi group experienced improved survival compared to controls.

Isotrretino clears acne, but does it keep acne away? Well, in most cases, yes. But the secret to acne remission might not be what you think. In a retrospective cohort study of commercial claims data from over 19,000 patients who took isotrretone for acne for 4 months or longer, 23% relapsed and needed another systemic acne treatment course like oral antibiotics or spiral lactone. 8% ended up needing a second round of isotininoan. Here's what's surprising. Acne relapse was not linked to how much isotrretininoan was taken per day, but the cumulative dose over time. Patients who received a higher cumulative dose had fewer relapses and fewer repeat trials of isotin. Daily doses did not seem to make a difference once patients received a cumulative dose above 120 milligs per kilogram. Female patients were more likely to relapse but were less likely to retry isotin. Why? It could be due to differences in the natural history of acne, access to orosperolactone or I pledge requirements for patients who can get pregnant. So what does this mean for you? Instead of focusing on a strict daily dose, clinicians can consider personalizing the isotrean dose to match the patients goal, preferences, and tolerance.

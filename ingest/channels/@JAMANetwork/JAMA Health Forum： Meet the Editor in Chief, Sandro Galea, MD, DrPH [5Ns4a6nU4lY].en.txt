I've spoken before about transformative moment for me in my life, which is when I was working with Médecins Sans Frontières in Somalia. And it was a moment in time when I was doing acute clinical work in a place of acute need. And it both reinforced for me the critical importance of excellent medicine, excellent clinical care, but also the importance of investing in approaches that removes the need for acute clinical care, meaning that we create societies so that people are as healthy as possible for as long as possible. JAMA Health Forum in the first 5 years of existence has done an outstanding job of becoming the leading journal in publishing health policy scholarship. I think one of the most exciting things about this journal is that it is not only JAMA Health Forum as a journal, but it is also part of the JAMA network family of journals. So the scholarship that we feature in JAMA Health Forum has the advantage of being a part of scholarship in a range of other journals. My hope for the journal is that it can become the premier journal for publications around policies that intersect with health for the US and globally. That means thinking about health policy expansively, thinking, yes, about policies that affect access to and the delivery of health care, but also that means thinking about policies that affect other structures that affect all health—that affect housing, that affect transportation, that affect income and income distributions. All these policies we know intersect with health care delivery, but also intersect with our health more directly. I want to encourage anyone who is interested in this mission to join us, to be a part, not only of submitting papers, but also to be a part of the conversation we aim to curate with JAMA Health Forum.

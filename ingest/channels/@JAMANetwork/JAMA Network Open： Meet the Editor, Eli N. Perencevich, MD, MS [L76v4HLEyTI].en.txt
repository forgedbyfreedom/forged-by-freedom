I'm Eli Perencevich. I'm a physician, health services researcher, and the next Editor of JAMA Network Open. I'm an infectious disease physician and when I was asked to join JAMA Network Open as an Associate Editor, I immediately jumped at it. Over the past decade or so, there's been a global call for open access scientific publications. We want taxpayers who pay for our science to be able to read it free, anytime, anywhere. And JAMA Network Open was started in 2018 to meet that call. Because JAMA Network Open is an open access journal, we reach all parts of the globe. I want to continue to expand our reach to get the best science from across the globe to be submitted to JAMA Network. I try to put myself in the shoes of a clinician in an area that typically hasn't had access to journal publications that are published behind a paywall. I imagine myself working in a rural clinic in a remote country. The real power of JAMA Network Open is delivering that science directly to them, to their phone, wherever they are. As we saw during the COVID-19 pandemic, the entire scientific and medical publication infrastructure shifted to open access publication —everything was open access. And that showed me that JAMA Network Open was on the right path. We don't just have epidemics of infectious diseases —we know globally there are epidemics in obesity, cardiovascular disease, and cancer —and we need to continue to deliver science in an open way, so that clinicians and the general public can access this important information in real time without delay. I truly believe there's no journal like JAMA Network Open with over 30 million page views a year and over 70 000 media mentions. If I was an author, and I am an author, I would want to submit my science to JAMA Network Open. And that's one of the reasons I'm incredibly honored to be the next editor of JAMA Network Open.

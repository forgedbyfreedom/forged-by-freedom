A recent study published in JAMAMA Surgery examined almost 850,000 patients undergoing either laparoscopic or robotic assisted colistctomy. This was a retrospective cohort study of adult patients in the United States with commercial insurance who underwent surgery between 2016 and 2021. The primary outcome was rate of bileduct injuries and there was no difference between the two groups. But the researchers found other differences. Patients undergoing laparoscopic colistctomy were more likely to undergo an interoperative collangio and had a higher rate of surgical sight infections. Patients undergoing robotic colctomy were more likely to have major post-operative complications, postoperative drain placement and a longer hospital length of stay. In subgroup analyses, there was not a significant difference in the rate of major complications between laparoscopic and robotic assisted surgery in patients younger than 34 and in patients with a BMI of 40 or higher. One of the limitations in the study is the fact that robotic assisted colaccysteectctomy has been increasing in its use since 2016. There's a known learning curve.

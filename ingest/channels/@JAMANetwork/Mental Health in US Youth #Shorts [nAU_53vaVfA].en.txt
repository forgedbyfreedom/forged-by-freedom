Multiple research studies point at worsening mental health among kids and youth in the US, especially among girls. And the newest data on mental health hospitalizations at important findings. This chart from a study in JAMA shows the number of pediatric hospitalizations from mental health related diagnoses in the US. It's based on a large national database that reports inpatient pediatric discharges every 3 years. The number of mental health hospitalizations among US youth went up at every point between 2009 and 2019, increasing by more than 25% in that period. But in 2022, the most recent year for which this data is available, this number finally went down. But there's important nuance within these overall numbers. Here's the same data split up by sex. As you can see, the decline in 2022 was driven by a decrease among boys and male youth who experienced fewer hospitalizations than any other study year. But hospitalizations among girls and female youth went up in 2022, just as in previous years, and female youth experienced more than 2/3 of all mental health hospitalizations that year. And suicide and self harm related hospitalizations did not decrease in 2022. Instead, these hospitalizations increased both overall and for female youth. Importantly, the data used for these reports do not include psychiatric, long-term, or rehabilitation hospitals or emergency department visits. These findings underscore the need to continue to pay close attention to sex-based differences in the mental health of kids and youth.

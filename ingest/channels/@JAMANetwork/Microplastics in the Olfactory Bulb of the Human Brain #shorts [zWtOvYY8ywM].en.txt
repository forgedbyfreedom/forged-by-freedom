These are pictures of microplastics in the alactory bulb of the brain. A group of scientists from S. Paulo sampled alactory bulb tissue from 15 deceased individuals and they found microplastics in eight of those people's olfactory bulbs. The most common compound found was polyropylene, a widely used plastic that could be found in food containers, pipes, and clothing. So, how do microplastics get in the brain? It's possible that microplastics in the bloodstream end up crossing the bloodb brain barrier. Another possible route of exposure is through our alactory system or our smelling pathway. Microplastics are in the air we breathe and the alactory system is one place where neurons are open to the environment. We don't yet know how microplastics affect brain health and given how common plastics are in our lives, more research is needed to learn about this potential health concern.

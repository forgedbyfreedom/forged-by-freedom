Randomized clinical trials are the gold standard of medical evidence, but it's not always feasible to conduct an RCT. For some clinical questions, it might be possible to use observational data to answer the causal questions that we have in clinical medicine. And one approach to doing this is the target trial emulation. Target trial emulation is an advanced technique and an important one for medical scholarship. But until now, there has not been specific guidance for how to report target trial emulation studies. We now have that guidance in the target statement published in JAMAMA. Target is a consensusbased guideline for reporting on target trial emulation study results. So, if you're a researcher with a complex clinical question working on a target trial emulation study and you want to make sure you've considered the critical issues in reporting these study results, check out this new target statement. You might also want to read target if you are a peer reviewer or a clinician evaluating a study that uses a target trial emulation design. Go to this article to learn more.

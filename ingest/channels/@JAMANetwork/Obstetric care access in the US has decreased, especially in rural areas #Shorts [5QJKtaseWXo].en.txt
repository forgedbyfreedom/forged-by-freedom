Obstetric's care is becoming less accessible in the United States and rural communities are particularly affected. A study recently published by JAMAMA finds that between 2010 and 2022 there has been a steady nationwide increase in the number of hospitals that do not offer obsetric services. [music] This graph depicts time on the x-axis and number of hospitals without obstetric services on the y-axis. So a higher percentage on the yaxis means reduced availability of OB care. Rural areas had the largest percentage of hospitals without obstetric services. These regions started with fewer hospitals providing obstetrics care and also saw a larger decline in these services. In 2022, over half of all rural hospitals did not offer obstetrics care. Urban hospitals followed a similar trend. By 2022, 35.7% of urban hospitals did not have OB services. During this time frame, some hospitals did add obsessive services. However, the rate [music] of gain was outpaced by the rate of loss. Rural areas gained fewer hospital-based OB services than urban regions. Of the 138 hospitals nationwide that added obsessive services, 26 were in rural areas while 112 were in urban regions.

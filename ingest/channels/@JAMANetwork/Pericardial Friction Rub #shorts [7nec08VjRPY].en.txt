[clears throat] The paricardial friction rub is a specific finding for active paricarditis and is reported in about onethird of cases of acute paricarditis. Rubs have a scratching quality that is perceived as superficial in the chest and best heard with the diaphragm of the stethoscope. They are often eancent and may require repeated oscultation for detection. The rub is usually loudest at the left lower sternal border with the patient leaning forward. The rub is attributed to friction between the inflamed and thickened paricardial layers and is therefore best appreciated during phases of the cardiac cycle with the most motion. Classically the rub has three components corresponding to ventricular cy early diastily and atrial contraction. Although a rub may also contain only one or two of these components.

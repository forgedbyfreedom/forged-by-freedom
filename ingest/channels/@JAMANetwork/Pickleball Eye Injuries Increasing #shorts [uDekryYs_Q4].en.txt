In a new study published in JAMAMA opthalmology, researchers discovered an alarming increase in eye injuries, more than 1,200 cases last year alone. And what surprised them most was that they were caused for the most part by direct ball strikes, not falls. Also, that more than 70% of these eye injuries occurred in players older than 50 years. And these aren't just minor bumps or bruises. Emergency departments have been seeing retinal detachments, bleeding in the eye, and orbital fractures, injuries that can cause permanent vision loss. Of course, it's not just eyes that are at risk with pickle ball. One study found a sevenfold increase in overall pickle ball injuries, even though the number of players has only tripled. Younger players tend to experience soft tissue injuries such as sprains or tendinitis, while older players in their 60s and beyond face more serious fractures, ankles, wrists, and hips that can require surgery and longer recovery periods. So, why is this happening? Experts point to several factors. As we age, collagen production declines, bone density decreases, especially in post-menopausal women, and reaction time slows. This combined with the fact that pickle ball requires rapid lateral movement, quick stops and starts, and good hand eye coordination, it's a perfect combination for injury.

to perform lung ultrasound the chest is scanned in six anatomic areas right and left anterior at the mid clavicular line right and left lateral at the mid axillary line and right and left posterior and parasagital lines medial to the scapula the following ultrasound videos are all of a child with normal healthy lungs scanning is performed superiorly and inferiorly in each of the six anatomic areas these ultrasound images are captured in the sagittal view the left video panel shows normal lung with horizontal lines in the lung parenchyma that are parallel to the pleural line these are termed a-lines which represent healthy aerated lung the transverse view the right panel shows the ultrasound hallmark of pneumonia lung consolidation with sonographic air bronchograms this segment is of a four-year-old girl with down syndrome and tricuspid atresia repair presenting with fever and cough for two days see if you can identify the pneumonia on ultrasound this segment is of an eight-year-old girl with a past medical history of hydrocephalus and a vp shunt who presents to the emergency department with productive cough and posttussive emesis for one day normal lung is demonstrated on ultrasound with horizontal a-lines she had no fever or chills but was noted to have increased work of breathing with tachypnea to 24 breaths per minute and super sternal retractions a pneumonia is identified in her right lower lobe in the transverse view the pneumonia is then evaluated in the sagittal view to confirm the abnormality remember to systematically search for pneumonia you will need to scan both sagittal and transverse views for each of the six anatomic areas of the chest you

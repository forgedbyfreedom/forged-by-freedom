This is a guide to assessing lung fields for B-lines to identify interstitial syndromes such as pulmonary edema or pneumonia. Pulmonary B-lines are echogenic vertical lines caused by pulmonary edema extending perpendicularly from the pleural line at the top of the ultrasound image into the lung peranigma. The presence of at least three B-lines in at least two bilateral zones has a sensitivity of 93% and a specificity of 77% for identifying volume overload. The curvilinear or phasoray probe can be used. Be sure to select the lung preset on your machine. The patient can be supine or semi-recombant for this examination. We will demonstrate the 8-zone protocol dividing each hemithorax into four zones based on anterior and posterior axillary lines. Place the probe longitudinally with the marker cephalad in an intercostal space within each zone. Upper interior, Lower interior, Upper lateral, and Lower lateral. In this loop recording of an ultrasound of lung peranigma, a phasoray low-frequency probe is being used in the intercostal space just below the right clavicle to see the upper anterior zone of the lung. You can see the bright white line of the pleural sliding. Emanating from the pleural line, you see hyperacoic beams that extend to the end of the ultrasound screen. These hyperacoic lines that are moving back and forth across the field are pulmonary B-lines, indicating interstitial syndrome and pulmonary edema.

Previous studies have shown that the prevalence of diabetes has been increasing in the United States. The objective of this study was to estimate the recent prevalence in total diabetes, diagnosed diabetes, undiagnosed diabetes, and pre-diabetes in the U.S. We studied about 26,000 people participating in a series of National representative surveys of the United States population, called the National Health and Nutrition examination surveys. The first one is conducted between 1988 and 1994 and the most recent 1 was conducted between 2011 and 2012. We found 14% of the people have diabetes and this was even higher in Blacks, Asians, and Hispanics for around a 3rd of the total people with diabetes were undiagnosed and this was increased up to 50% of Asians and Hispanics were not aware they had the condition, and the entirety of the United States population about a 3rd have pre-diabetes which means about 50% of people in the U.S. have either diabetes or pre-diabetes. Between the first survey in 88 and 94 to the last survey in 2011 and 2012 the prevalence increased about 25% and it increased in every subgroup that we studied and each age group both sexes and each race group. The prevalence of diabetes is high in the United States and its increasing, and it's even higher among Blacks, Asians, and Hispanics. Many of these cases are undiagnosed around a 3rd in the total population and higher in Asians and Hispanics. Also it's increasing over time. Future research should focus on subgroups of Asian Americans. This is particularly interesting since they had a relatively high prevalence of diabetes and a high prevalence of undiagnosed diabetes.

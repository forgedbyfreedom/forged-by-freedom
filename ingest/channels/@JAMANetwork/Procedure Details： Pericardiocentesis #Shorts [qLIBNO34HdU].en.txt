This video shows an apical approach to paricardioentesis with ultrasound. A paricardial fusion is noted apical and lateral to the left ventricle with no intervening lung between the skin and paricardial fluid. A wire is inserted through a needle into the paricardial eusion with a trajectory directed away from the heart as much as possible. The wire can be directly visualized and during the paricardial eusion. A catheter is then inserted and left in the paricardial space to ensure complete drainage. Pericardioentesis is performed when there is concern for paricardial tampenod and is also often pursued when specific ideologies such as bacterial, tuberculus or malignant paricardial eusions are suspected.

What is the link between screen time and depression in children and adolesccents? A recent study in JAMAMA pediatrics aimed to find out and the answer may involve sleep and brain development. Researchers used data from the ABCD study to compare screen time, sleep, white matter organization and depression symptoms in children. They assessed children at 9 to 10 years old and again at 11 to 13 years old. They found each additional hour of daily screen time in the younger age group was linked to an increase in depressive symptoms in the older age group. Importantly, shorter sleep duration and worse white matter organization accounted for over 36% of the association. There's still a lot to learn about how screen time impacts sleep, but many studies have shown that loss of sleep can negatively affect mental health. These results emphasize the importance of promoting healthy habits and balancing screen time with adequate sleep. A related editorial recommends keeping TVs, phones, and computers out of the bedroom, having screenfree family meals, and setting time and content limits for screen use. Encouraging a balanced lifestyle at this critical junction where childhood and adolescence merge can have a positive effect on sleep and emotional well-being.

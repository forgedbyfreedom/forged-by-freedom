GLP-1 agonist could help treat alcohol use disorder. A recent study published in Jamama Psychiatry shows that lowd doseide helped reduce cravings and decrease some other alcohol consumption related outcomes. This phase 2 double blind randomized clinical trial enrolled 48 participants with alcohol use disorder who were not actively seeking treatment. The study intervention group received once weekly simaglletide starting at 0.25 millig. Over the next nine weeks, the dose was increased as tolerated to a maximum of 1 milligram per week. Participants then had their alcohol self-administration measured over two hours in the lab. People who had received simaglletide as part of the study consumed 48% less alcohol compared to the placebo group. Participants also tracked the amount of alcohol they consume at home. People taking simlletide consumed about 41% less alcohol on each day that they drank. The saclletide group also reported a 39% reduction in cravings. The number of days that people were drinking versus abstinent were similar between the two groups. participants had greater reduction in drinking in the second half of the study compared to the first. This suggests the benefits may become more profound as treatment dose and duration increases. GLP-1 agonists are promising medications for the treatment of alcohol use disorder. The results of this phase 2 trial are preliminary and they justify the need for further clinical trials.

Should children with appendicitis have surgery or not? A new study just published in Jamma Pediatrics compared two treatment options for uncomplicated appendicitis in children. Researchers performed a metaanalysis of seven randomized clinical trials involving almost 1500 pediatric patients. They evaluated the safety and effectiveness of nonoperative management treatment with antibiotics compared to apppendecttomy. The study found that while non-operative management offers a faster return to school and normal activities, it is associated with significantly higher treatment failure and major complication rates within a year compared to surgery. Specifically, the relative risk of treatment failure in the non-operative management group was almost five times higher and major complications were over 33 times more frequent. About one in five children in the non-operative management group had appendicitis come back. This new analysis is significant because the results challenge previous studies suggesting nonoperative management is not inferior to operative management and non-operative management has become more common in recent years.

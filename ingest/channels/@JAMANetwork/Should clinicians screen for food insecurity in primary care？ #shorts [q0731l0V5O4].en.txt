should Primary Care clinicians routinely screen all patients for food insecurity the US preventive Services Task Force or uspstf issued an eye statement indicating that there is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against screening for food insecurity in primary care I want to be really clear on this that does not mean that clinicians should not be talking about food insecurity with their patients and their patients families and addressing food insecurity when it comes up this is the first time that the US psdf has addressed a social determinant of Health as members of the task force point out in a Jama Viewpoint the uspstf's review raised the question of whether the task Force's established methods and analytic framework for screening can adequately address food insecurity or other social determinant of Health Food insecurity affects more than 10% of us households so this topic is relevant to every primary care clinician in the US

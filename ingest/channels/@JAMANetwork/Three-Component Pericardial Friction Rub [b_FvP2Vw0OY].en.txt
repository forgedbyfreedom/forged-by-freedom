Here is an audio clip of a 3-component  pericardial friction rub. The pericardial friction rub is a specific finding for active pericarditis and is reported in about one third of cases of acute pericarditis. Rubs have a scratching quality that is perceived as superficial in the chest. and best heard with the diaphragm of the stethoscope. They are often evanescent and may require repeated auscultation for detection. The rub is usually loudest at the left lower sternal border with the patient leaning forward. The rub is attributed to friction between the inflamed and thickened pericardial layers and is therefore best appreciated during phases of the cardiac cycle with the most motion. Classically, the rub has 3 components corresponding to ventricular systole, early diastole, and atrial contraction, although a rub may also contain only one or 2 of these components.

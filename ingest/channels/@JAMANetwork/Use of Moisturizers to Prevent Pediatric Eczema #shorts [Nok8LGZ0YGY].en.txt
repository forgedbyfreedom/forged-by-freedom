Is preventing eczema as simple as moisturizing? A recent clinical trial in Jamba Dermatology found that applying daily moisturizer to children before 9 weeks old through two years old can prevent the development of attopic dermatitis, also known as eczema. Eczema affects 10 to 20% of children worldwide, causing itchy, dry patches of skin. In this study, 1,200 families were recruited from 25 primary care clinics across the United States. This was a randomized trial, meaning that half of families were instructed to use a moisturizer daily over their infant's entire body and half were not. Both groups were given general information on skinincare and bathing. The authors found that infants who are randomly assigned to the moisturizer group had a 16% reduction in the development of eczema by 2 years of age. The use of moisturizers also appeared to be safe and feasible. Strengths of the study included the large size, diverse participants, and that it evaluated full body moisturizer use. While future studies are needed to replicate these findings and identify which specific moisturizers work best, the results of this randomized trial support daily moisturizer use as a promising strategy to prevent attopic dermatitis in infants.

I personally view being the editor of JAMA Neurology as an immense privilege. This is a journal that has really transformed over the last few decades, and being able to shepherd it for a brief period of time and improve the journal and make it more useful for our authors and for our readers and for the world, is something very special, and it's a privilege that we take very seriously in our editorial board. Since the time that my team took over the stewardship of this wonderful journal we've seen a massive increase in our impact factor. But we view that as only one measure of a journal’s success. We are equally interested in how our journal is disseminated to our readers and how helpful we can be to our authors, rapidly turning around decisions and making sure when their articles are published in JAMA Neurology that they have wide visibility. I think JAMA Neurology is distinguished in a few ways. First, it covers a wide set of issues related to neurologic disease and therapeutics across many neurologic specialties. It also has a wide reach, whether it be through press releases, our social media platforms, or our e-table of contents that are sent out to over 100 000 individuals each Monday. JAMA Neurology also is unique in that it has multiple different article types, original investigations, research, but also education, opinion pieces, meaning that authors have many choices as to where they can publish their papers within JAMA Neurology. In the coming years, neurology as a field will continue to expand into new areas, new advances, new technologies and new therapeutics, and JAMA Neurology is uniquely positioned, with our multiple article types, to be able to disseminate all of these types of information as they become important not only to researchers, but to our patients.

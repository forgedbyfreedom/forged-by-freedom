I'm a surgeon and I'm more likely to die from cancer. Let's talk about it. A recent study in JAMAMA Surgery explored the death certificates of more than 1 million people in the United States. They compared causes of death in surgeons with three other groups. Other physicians who are not surgeons, a cohort of similarly educated professionals, including lawyers, engineers, and scientists, and all other workers, and the general population. The authors noted that non-surggeon physicians had a lower rate of death than other highly educated professionals, but this mortality advantage was not experienced by surgeons. Also of note, deaths from tumors were higher among surgeons than any other group. And motor vehicle collisions were the fourth leading cause of death for us compared with the ninth leading cause of death [music] in the other groups. The next logical question is why? particularly given that all physicians, regardless of specialty, are likely to have the same access and knowledge of the health care system. The authors point out that the higher death rate among surgeons might be due to differences in our lifestyle, the frequency of extended work hours, and our work environment.

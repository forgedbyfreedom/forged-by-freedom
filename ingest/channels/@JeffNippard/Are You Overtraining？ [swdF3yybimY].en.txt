How hard would you need to work out to actually overtrain? Let's break it down. If you do too little in the gym, you won't grow. That's called undertraining. Do a bit more and you'll maintain. A bit more than that and you're in the sweet spot for gains. Keep pushing from there and you'll hit functional overreaching where performance drops for a few days, then rebounds above baseline once you recover. That's actually good. And sometimes I purposefully try to functionally overreach. But if you keep pushing more, you'll eventually hit true overtraining where your performance tanks for weeks or months. And even once you recover, no extra gains. But here's the thing. True overtraining is insanely hard to hit. One study had lifters do 25 sets every workout on heavy compound lifts 4 days a week. And they were all, ready for this? 3 to five rep maxes. They did that for 2 weeks. You'd expect them to burn out, but they didn't overtrain. Their strength actually went up. The only study I'm aware of that caused true overtraining should be in the Geneva Convention. They made people do 10 sets of one rep max squats six days a week. And they did that for 2 weeks. It absolutely wrecked them. Most people took at least a month or two just to recover and get back to baseline. So unless you're training like that, and you're not, you're probably fine.

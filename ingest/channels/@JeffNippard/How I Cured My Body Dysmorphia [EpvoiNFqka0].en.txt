Sometimes when I look in the mirror, I'd feel both fat and small at the same time, even if I was in great shape. Like most people in fitness, I've struggled with body dysmorphia. I wouldn't say I'm totally cured now, but I'm definitely in a healthier place. Over time, I've learned that if you have body dysmorphia, you're not going to cure it by getting shredded. Your brain will just trick you into thinking you're actually getting fatter or smaller, and you'll start projecting those emotions onto others. I measured a professional natural bodybuilder at 8% body fat. He'd been brutally dieting for months, and people online still said he looked kind of average. That's how warped our expectations have gotten and how damaging online comparisons can be. I used to think that if I could just get a bit more shredded, it'd make me happier. And it can, but not if your mindset isn't healthy. Nowadays, rather than fixating on how my body looks, I focus much more on how my body feels and performs. If you're making gains in the gym in a healthy and sustainable way, you're doing great. Be proud of that. And if you're really struggling, you're definitely not alone. It's okay to ask for help and talk to a professional.

The traditional approach to teaching jiu-jitsu is all about giving the answers. It's an answersbased approach. And giving the answers isn't wrong, but a games-based approach is all about presenting students with problems to solve. And there's a lot of advantages of this. One, it's way more fun. And two, we want students to become problem solvers. So, while giving the answers isn't quite wrong, it's more so, do we want to base our whole practice around the answers when the answers, the problems, they always change throughout jiu-jitsu. It's very variable. And this is why so many gyms are switching to a games-based approach as opposed to the traditional approach. My gym included.

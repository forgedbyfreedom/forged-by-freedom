This is a narrowing of the aorta or a co-archctation of the aorta after it leaves the ventricle. One common finding in patients with co-archctation of the aorta is ribbing. What basically happens is that because of the low pressure of the descending aorta, a pressure gradient is created between the internal thoracic artery and the descending aorta. Because the blood pressure in the internal thoracic artery is higher than the descending aorta, an increased collateral circulation takes place through the intercostal arteries. This can lead to the appearance of rib notching on the chest X-ray where the lower surface of the rib appears eroded.

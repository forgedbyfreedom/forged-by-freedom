okay so pharmacology is a difficult subject and this post had a ton of good tips to approach it better but if there's one advice that I didn't see here that really made a difference for me it was reverse learning you see the problem with Farm is that there are so many details that it's hard to sort out which ones are actually important like do you need to remember the mechanism of action the molecular weight the dose and all of the adverse effects of X drug or just a few of these details and which ones exactly being aware of which elements of the subject are important before even reading the subject is a superpower so before you start reading your pharmacology textbook like a madman ask yourself what do I actually need to know about this topic and one way to figure that out is by starting your study session not with a text but with a round of practice questions the idea here is that instead of using questions to evaluate your knowledge you can use them to pinpoint the details that are truly important so that you can focus on them afterwards

well uh the uh study really goes back several years and i think it probably began with a patient coming in to see their doctor uh with concerns of atrial fibrillation and a family history in fact indicated that that individual had many other relatives with atrial fibrillation and so that was really how the study got started it's based on a single family that where there were 11 relatives who had relatively early onset atrial fibrillation without any known risk factors and looking at the pedigree or the family tree structure it was clear that there was a genetic basis for their arrhythmia so really the study took off from there we collected blood samples extracted dna from those samples and then did what's called a genome-wide mapping study where we were able to scan the human genome and pinpoint the specific chromosome location of the gene which was responsible for their disease then ultimately we identified that particular gene and in collaboration with other investigators we were able to show that it made a an abnormal protein that circulated in these patients bloodstream and then in an animal model showed that it had an adverse effect on the electrical properties of the heart so really it was a study that took place over several years and involved collaborations with other research laboratories

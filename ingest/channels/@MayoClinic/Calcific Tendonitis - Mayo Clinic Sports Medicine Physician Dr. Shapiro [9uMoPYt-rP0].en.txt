I'm Dr Shane Shapiro a sports medicine physician in the department of orthopedic surgery at Mayo Clinic in Florida I have some information today on calcific tendonitis calcific tendonitis is a painful condition of tendons that is very similar to other forms of tendonitis except that it is caused by small calcium deposits that build up inside the tendon most commonly this occurs in the shoulder and in fact May account for 20% of all shoulder pain we have several ways of treating calcific tontis in the shoulder most commonly rest ice and anti-inflammatory medications can help relieve the pain when it does not patients will often undergo a course of physical therapy if these conservative measures fail there is an alternative to surgery which is an aspiration and needling technique to remove the calcium from from the tendon without surgery using an ultrasound machine to visualize the calcium deposits within the tendon we needle the tendon using saline and Lidocaine to minimize pain as best as possible after the calcium is broken up within the tendon we are then able to aspirate it with a syringe and inject some anti-inflammatory medication after the procedure patients will wear a sling for approximately 2 days they may need some very mild pain relievers we will usually involve a physical therapist for some exercise for a couple of weeks thereafter and patients May resume their regular activity within a couple of weeks in addition to calcific tendinitis of the shoulder this condition can also occur in in the hips as well as other tendons within the body for more information on calcific tontis please visit mayoclinic.org

device that bypasses damaged portions of the inner ear to transmit sound directly to the hearing nerve the device has three components the processor the receiver and the electrode the processor is a device that patients wear over their ear and on the scalp that helps capture sounds from the environment the processor sends the signal to the receiver which is implanted under the scalp the two are held together by magnets like this the receiver then sends signal to the electrode which is coiled within the cochlea or the snail-shaped inner ear organ and from there the electrical signal is sent to the hearing nerve and the Brain in order to determine if you are a candidate to receive a cochlear implant you will undergo a series of hearing and speech understanding tests with an audiologist you might also undergo a series of balance tests and a CT scan or an MRI to look closely at the inner ear if you are deemed a cochlear implant candidate you will work with your audiologist to choose a device and features of that device that best support your lifestyle many patients are under the impression that cochlear implant surgery is dangerous but over the last several decades improvements in the technique and Technology have made it very safe though it's performed under general anesthesia it's usually done on an outpatient basis an incision will be made behind the ear a small portion of the bone from behind the ear canal will be removed in order to provide direct access to the entrance to the cochlea the receiver will then be slid under the scalp and the electric will be inserted and wrapped into the cochlea the scalp incision is then close with stitches and a bandage is placed over the ear for one day the wound is given some time to heal after which the device is activated or turned on by an audiologist this can be done anywhere between a couple of days after surgery to a couple of weeks there are a few risks of surgery and they include loss of residual hearing infection and device failure with time complications of surgery are rare but can include temporary dizziness or altered sense of taste hearing Improvement after cochlear implantation is often not automatic the brain takes time and needs practice to adjust to the new sound if you receive a cochlear implant you will work closely with an audiologist to program and adjust it to optimize your hearing and

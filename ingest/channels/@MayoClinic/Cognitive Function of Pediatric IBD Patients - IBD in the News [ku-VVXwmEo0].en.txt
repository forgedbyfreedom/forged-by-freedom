last December this was a study by Dr maratkotsky and her colleagues at Boston Children's Hospital they were interested in studying the brains and cognitive function of children with IBD now cognitive function or cognition is how your brain produces thoughts processes information from your senses and creates memories in the first study 12 pre-teens and young teenagers with crohn's disease had MRI scans of their brain all of these patients were on steroids due to a flare of their Crohn's these MRI scans were also compared to healthy children that did not have IBD they also took tests of memory IQ emotion and attention span in patients with crohn's disease a portion of their brain called the cortex was a bit thinner than Healthy Children of the same age the areas of the brain that had a thinner cortex play a role in cognition and emotion those who are on higher doses of steroids and had higher inflammatory markers in their blood work were more likely to have this thinning of their cortex in the second study they interviewed 85 children with crohn's disease and compared them to healthy children they examined school records tests of memory and IQ the Crohn's patients in this study were more likely to have trouble with memory even if they were in remission they were also more likely to have issues at school including General School problems use of a 504 plan for academic reasons and the need for special education classes now some of these numbers may seem high that we've listed below and in the first study all these patients were on steroids what I would take away from these studies is this first if possible avoid steroids or discontinue them as soon as you can second work with your gastroenterologist to find the treatment regimen that will get you in remission or get you back in remission and third work with your school teachers to look for any emotional or learning issues for your child a formal test of their IQ and learning ability may or may not be

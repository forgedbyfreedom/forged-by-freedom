when i speak to patients about minimally invasive spine surgery i explain that most surgeries fall into one of three categories the first category is decompression surgery with decompression surgery we're making small incisions maybe using an endoscope or a slightly smaller tubular retractor to go down and remove tissue that's pressing on the spinal cord or nerve roots this may be bone bone spurs ligaments disc fragments or cysts another type of surgery that we do is called disc replacement surgery or arthroplasty surgery with these surgeries we also make small incisions we go down we remove a disc and we replace it with a device that maintains natural movement within that joint a third type of surgery that we do is called spinal fusion surgery with spinal fusion surgery we're trying to fix two or more bones together to do this we need to place most of the time some sort of instrumentation into the body that includes rods screws plates sometimes cages into the disk space we look at each patient and we look for the least invasive way to address their problem in some cases for example a patient that has a very severe spinal deformity there are limitations to what we can do with minimally invasive surgery so in those cases we do have to do traditional open surgery i think it's very helpful to see a couple of cases and get a better sense of what minimally invasive surgery is

In certain conditions, a more complex reconstruction is required known as an epidimo vasostomy or vasoepidostomy. The animation provides a brief overview of the relevant anatomy with the vast deference, prior vasectomy site, epidmus and testicle highlighted. This procedure begins in a similar manner to a vaso vesostomy. A midline scrotal incision is made after which the obstructed basal segment is removed and the fluid on the testicular side is sampled under microscopy. In this particular case, microscopic analysis does not demonstrate the presence of any sperm. In that scenario, we would proceed with an epidimo vasostomy. The scrotal midline incision is extended after which the testicle is delivered through the wound. This permits better visualization of the epidmus. Next, the epidmus is insized and an obstructed epidmal tubule is selected for anastmosis. The vast deference is brought down to the level of the epidmus and secured using some initial stitches. Two double-armed smaller sutures are then placed into the epidmal tubule after which the epidmal tubule is insized. The fluid is again sampled and this time demonstrates the presence of normal sperm. Once sperm are identified, the stitches are then placed inside the lumen of the vast deference and the vast deference is tied down to the epidmal tubule. Additional stitches are then placed to secure the vast deference to the epidmal sheath for additional strength. This final animation demonstrates the post-operative result with the attachment of the vast deference to the epidmus directly.

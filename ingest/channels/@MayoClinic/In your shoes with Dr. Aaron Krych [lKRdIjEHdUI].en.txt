[Dr. Krych] This is really a trip down memory lane for me. I played for St. John's in college, and I played running back for four years. The most significant injury I had, I had kind of chronic hip issues that did culminate in a hip surgery for me. Really, you don't know the experience until you walk in those shoes. So when patients are telling me about their injury, that really resonates with me, 'cause I walked in those shoes as well. Having that perspective as a former athlete helps me decide, with the athlete as a partner, what's the best path forward. Sometimes I may play the primary role as a surgeon, but other times, they may need one of our physical therapists, one of our non-operative physicians. We really need excellence in all those positions of the team to take the best care of athletes that we can. The most satisfying part about working with an athlete is the journey, and then just to witness that journey over time. But really seeing them then reach that top of the mountain. Going from that injury to back on the field is the most fulfilling part of what I do on a daily basis. (upbeat music)

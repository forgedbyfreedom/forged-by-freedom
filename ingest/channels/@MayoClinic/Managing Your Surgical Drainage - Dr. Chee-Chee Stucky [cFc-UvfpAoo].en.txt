multiple different types of procedures to help remove fluid from the surgical site and to give us a window into the wound this helps us assess the progression of healing drains are temporary in the vast majority of cases the length of time a drain is left in place is dependent upon your procedure and your healing progression for the drain to function it must remain on suction this is maintained by the bulb when the bulb is collapsed the drain is maintaining suction as intended in order to recreate this section remove the occluding plug at the top of the bulb squeeze the bulb and replace the plug while still squeezing the bulb it is important to record the output of your drain in the booklet that was provided to you to empty the bulb remove the occluding stop and pour the contents into a container that allows you to assess the volume place the drain back to suction using the steps described previously empty your drain two to three times per day or as needed unless otherwise instructed by your surgeon on occasion a drain may become clogged this is especially common when fluid stays stationary in the tubing to prevent this it is important to strip or milk the tubing firmly grasp the tubing close to the skin with one hand and then pinch and pull the tubing with your other hand towards the bulb keep repeating these steps as you gradually work your way towards the bulb the frequency of drain stripping will depend on the type of drain and your surgeons preference a special note do not flush the drain tubing with fluid unless specifically instructed to do so we do expect the output of your drain to change over time the exact expected output is dependent upon the procedure though the drain may have more output on some days than others it should be decreasing overall as your recovery progresses the color of your drain is also expected to change over time at first we expect the output to have a maroon color to the fluid however it should not look bright red like fresh blood you are also likely to note small stringy clots in your bulb at this time after approximately two to three days we expect the output to become lighter and more pink in color again you may note small blood clots at this time and this is completely normal over the next few days we expect the drain to become even lighter and character and yellow in color the stringy contents may become white now but blood clots may still be present as mentioned above it is important to keep track of the output of your drain if you notice that your drain is putting out much less than it was previously please ensure that you strip the tubing as demonstrated earlier if your drain still continues to have low output despite stripping please call your surgeon or medical provider similarly if your drain starts putting out much more than it did previously please do not hesitate to call your provider it is very important to remain vigilant for signs of infection these include redness warmth or firmness around the side of the tubing where it enters your skin other signs of infection include nausea vomiting fever or chills finally please watch for any thickened output with the consistency of pus from around the tubing where it enters your skin or in the bulb itself pus is a thick cloudy white yellow substance that may indicate infection if you notice any of the above signs and symptoms please call your provider immediately we hope this video address some of your questions and concerns regarding your drain as mentioned earlier this is meant as a compliment not a substitute to the instructions

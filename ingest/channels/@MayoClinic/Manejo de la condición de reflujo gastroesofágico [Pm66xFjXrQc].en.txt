Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) can cause reflux. Unfortunately, we still don't fully understand its exact cause. We have ideas about it, hypotheses, and a more advanced understanding, but until we manage to understand the precise cause, our therapies are aimed at suppressing or reducing the amount of reflux coming from the stomach, which is stomach acid, or non- acidic material coming from the duodenum into the esophagus. We know, for example, that foods such as citrus fruits, irritants like spicy foods (typical of Latin American culture, at least in some countries), pizzas, tomato juice, tomatoes themselves, alcohol, chocolate, and fatty foods, to mention the most common and frequent ones, as well as smoking, contribute to gastroesophageal reflux. Therefore, we generally recommend that the patient avoid all these types of foods. Furthermore, since reflux occurs more frequently when the patient is in a horizontal position, we suggest that the patient elevate the head of the bed to reduce the chances of reflux, simply due to gravity. We also ask that  Avoid eating before going to sleep; instead, wait at least 3 hours so that the stomach has emptied and there isn't so much food or stomach content that facilitates reflux. These are general measures; of course, for overweight patients, we suggest losing weight. All of this contributes to more effective reflux management.

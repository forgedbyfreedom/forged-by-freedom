The proteomics corps provides essential support to Mayo Clinic Cancer Center members with services in protein chemistry, protein separation, and mass spectrometry. This Q exactive tandem mass spectrometer profiles the proteomes in cell tissues and biologic fluids. Mass spectrometers identify proteomes in complex mixtures by fragmenting peptides into smaller ions. Accurate mass measurement of the fragment ions provides amino acid sequences of the peptides that are used to identify the proteins in proteomes. Core director Dr. Dr. Daniel McCormack says such analytical expertise is vital to the work of many cancer center members because proteins are essential for key cell functions involved in cell signaling, metabolism, DNA uh repair, gene expression and regulation of the genes and all of these processes are integral in the development and control of cancer. A broad diversity of cancer center programs have been supported by the proteomics corps including cell biology, developmental therapeutics, gene and virus therapy, cancer immunology and immunotherapy, hematlogic malignancies, neurooncology and women's cancer. Highresolution mass analysis of analytes on this agyant tooff mass spectrometer is used to confirm the mass and elemental composition of drugs, small molecules and products made in the core such as synthetic peptides that are often used for clinical trials and vaccines. Several vaccines that are undergoing trials right now are vaccines for human adnocarcinomomas and human melanoma. And we use this instrument to verify the vaccine cocktails and the formulations. The Proteomics Corps provides cancer center members with free consultations, study design, competitive rates, and timely prioritized services utilizing new in-house technologies and instrumentation that's not readily available elsewhere. Under Dr. McCormick's direction, the core has received strong institutional support and funding for more than 28 years.

i'm mary o'connor an orthopedic surgeon at the mayo clinic in florida where i also chair our orthopedic department and i have been honored to receive research funding from the society of women's health research i will be the principal investigator leading a study with other researchers at other institutions looking at whether we can uncover biological differences between men and women in terms of knee arthritis and the reason why this is so important is because knee arthritis is a huge problem for our patients and our nation and a lot of older patients get knee arthritis but unfortunately the burden of disease falls disproportionately to women so women as we age tend to get a lot more arthritis in our knees than men and the reasons for this are not understood so the study that we are undertaking is going to look at tissue from the knee that normally we would discard in the course of routine knee replacement surgery tissues like bone and cartilage and joint fluid and then send those tissues to labs in different parts of the country where my colleagues will be performing tests to see if we can uncover differences between men and women in terms of these tissues and the development of arthritis so we're really trying to focus in on is why do women get more arthritis in their knee than men and if they do if what can we do to try and prevent or reverse that by better understanding the potential biological differences between men and women so it's a very exciting i'm very honored that we were awarded this research grant i'm grateful to the society for women's health research for providing this funding and for all my co-investigators who will participate my co-investigators include dr karen berkeley from florida state dr david hart from the university of calgary and dr barbara boyan at georgia tech and emory so we have a wonderful team of people assembled and i'm very excited about this research opportunity for us thank you very much

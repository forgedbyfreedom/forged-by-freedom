Growing up, my father was a geriatrician in our community. After our Saturday morning basketball games, he would take my siblings and I on house calls to visit his patients. So I was exposed from a young age to the medical field, but it was really seeing his interaction with them that solidified that this is something that I wanted to do. One thing that really sticks out for me about Mayo Clinic is the emphasis on collaboration. It's really rewarding being able to work with these patients, but also work alongside my colleagues and learn from them. Recently I had an instance where a family friend was at an outside facility. They were really stumped by his presentation with some rare neurologic complications, and I looked at the Mayo Clinic website at the neurologists, just found someone that I thought may be able to help me out, and I cold emailed him just to see if he had any thoughts, and within 15 minutes he'd already written me back on a Friday night and was able to figure out what was going on with this patient and we were able to get him the care he needed from hundreds of miles away. It made me just really proud to be a part of Mayo Clinic in a place where someone on their weekend, in their free time was willing to help out a physician they've never met, as well as a patient they've never met.

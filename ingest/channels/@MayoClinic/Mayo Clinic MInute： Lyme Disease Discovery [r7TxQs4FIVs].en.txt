What started as routine testing ended with the discovery of a new organism. We had Lyme disease in affecting humans but caused by a different species of bacterium. A bacterium dubbed Borrellia mayoni in honor of the founders of Mayo Clinic. A bacterium that appears to be localized to the upper Midwest. Like the other known cause of Lyme disease, it's believed this organism is transferred to humans through the bite of an infected tick. Patients symptoms including fever, neck pain, and rash appear to be similar but not identical to other cases. Notably, they were more likely to have nausea and vomiting. And the rashes were more diffuse. So, it wasn't the classic bullseye rash that you see with Lyme disease. Traditional Lyme disease treatment methods have worked in cases caused by the newly discovered bacterium. And protection will sound familiar, too. Stay out of tall grasses, wear long-sleeved clothing, tuck your pants into your socks, and use an insect repellent such as DE to avoid your own discovery of Brellia Mayoni.

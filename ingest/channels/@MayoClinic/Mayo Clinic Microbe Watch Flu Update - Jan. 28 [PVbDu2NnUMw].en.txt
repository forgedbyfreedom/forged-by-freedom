During the third week of 2014, influenza activity remained high in the United States, with 41 states reporting widespread activity. The predominant strain continues to be the 2009/H1N1 virus that was a component of this year’s flu vaccine, and I’m confident that the excellent preparation that the CDC did in formulating this year’s vaccine has helped contribute to the mild-to-moderate influenza season that we’ve experienced. For the third straight week, the number of patients presenting to outpatient clinics with an influenza-like illness dropped in the United States, so this is an excellent indicator that flu season has peaked on a national scale. Secondary peaks in influenza activity are possible, so it’s important that patients and healthcare providers remain diligent to help prevent the spread of the flu over the next 4-6 weeks. In Minnesota, influenza activity remains categorized as ‘widespread’, but similar to national data, the percentage of persons being seen at outpatient clinics for influenza-like illness dropped last week, and this was the third consecutive week we’ve seen a decline. Interestingly, healthcare providers may notice that the total number and percentage of specimens testing positive for influenza increased last week. So why is there this discrepancy, with a declining rate of patients being seen for influenza but an increasing rate of samples testing positive? Well, it’s hard to know for sure, but most likely, this reflects a declining rate of testing in the general population. Instead, it’s not uncommon at this point in the influenza season for lab tests to be reserved for the sickest patients, such as those individuals that are hospitalized. Healthcare providers are likely limiting testing to those patients that have a higher likelihood of having the illness, and so the percentage of positive samples increases. Hopefully, we’re just a few weeks away from leaving the 2013-2014 influenza season behind us.

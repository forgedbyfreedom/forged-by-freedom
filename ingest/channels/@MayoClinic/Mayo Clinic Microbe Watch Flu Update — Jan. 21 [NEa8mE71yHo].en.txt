For the second consecutive week, the percentage of visits for influenza-like illness has dropped on a National level, so I’m cautiously optimistic that we have experienced the peak of influenza in the United States, and the overall number of cases will continue to decline. However, I should emphasize that we’re not out of the woods yet, as 40 states reported influenza activity to still be widespread during the second week of 2014. The predominant strain continues to be the influenza A 2009/H1N1 strain that was included in the vaccine for this year, and over 99% of strains tested by the CDC have been found to be susceptible to antiviral medications used to treat influenza. The Minnesota Department of Health listed influenza activity as Widespread during Week 2 of 2014; however, similar to national data, Minnesota experienced it’s second consecutive drop in the percent of individuals presenting to outpatient clinics with an influenza-like illness, so it appears that things are headed in the right direction. One of the pieces of data that I’ve found particularly interesting is the prevalence of influenza-like illness in Minnesota schools this season. We haven’t seen a significant number of outbreaks in schools compared to past seasons, and this may be due to the high vaccination rate among school age children, the effectiveness of this year’s vaccine, and parent’s diligence in regards to keeping their children home if they are sick. It’s important for healthcare providers and patients to know that other viruses that are capable of making patients very ill are also circulating in the population. One of those viruses, which is RSV, or Respiratory Syncytial Virus, has been detected in a fairly high number of samples over the past 3 or 4 weeks in the upper Midwest, including the states of Minnesota and Iowa, so providers should keep this virus in mind, especially in young children or any patient that is hospitalized.

As women age, they may wonder, "When can I stop using birth control?" Birth control is one of those things that women tend to start not worrying about a little too soon. Dr. Petra Casey says in your 40s is too soon. By the time a woman reaches the age of 55, she is 95% likely to have gone through menopause. Menopause is defined as one year of no periods. So, if you go 11 and a half months of no periods and then have a period, the clock starts all over again and you're still not in menopause. Until that time arrives, she should continue using contraception. Once she reaches age 55, regardless of what's happening, she is okay to stop because the chances of her getting pregnant are super low. But until then, Dr. Casey says women should talk to their healthcare providers about the type of contraception that's best for them. For the Mayo Clinic News Network, I'm Vivian Williams.

More people are dying from accidental prescription opioids than from heroin and cocaine combined. Mayo Clinic's Dr. Michael Hooton is talking about narcotic pain relievers such as oxycodone, hydrocodone, and methadone. These pain relievers may help in the short term. However, Mayo Clinic researchers found one in four patients prescribed opioid painkillers for the first time progressed to chronic use. They also found that people with histories of tobacco use and substance abuse were most likely to use opioid painkillers long term. Dr. Hooton says there are certain clinical characteristics that put some people at risk. Psychiatric comorbid problems including depression, anxiety, substance substance use are independent predictors of opioid misuse and even accidental opioid deaths. Dr. Dr. Hooton says finding alternative methods to manage pain, including nonopioid analesics or other non-medication approaches, may help reduce the risk of opioid addiction.

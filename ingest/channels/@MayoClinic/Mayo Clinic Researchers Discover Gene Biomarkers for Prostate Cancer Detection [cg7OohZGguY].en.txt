in our study we analyzed 14,000 495 genes in 238 prostate cancer tissues of patients which includes patients who had no disease recurrence after primary therapy and patients with advanced cancer who had tumor recurrence and metastatic progression so we found that DNA methylation changes that occur during the early stages of cancer development are almost identical in all patients irrespective of whether the patient has an indolent slow-growing tumor or an aggressive tumor so these DNA methylation changes can be used as biomarkers for detection of prostate cancer cancer starts developing when the tumor suppressor genes which acts as a brakes for cell proliferation are switched off by DNA methylation so DNA methylation is like an on-off switch on the genes to control cell growth and tumor development

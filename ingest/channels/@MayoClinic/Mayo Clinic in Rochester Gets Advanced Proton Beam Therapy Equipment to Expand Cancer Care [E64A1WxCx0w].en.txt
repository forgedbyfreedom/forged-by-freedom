This week marks a significant milestone in expanding Mayo Clinic's Proton Beam Therapy program. The largest and heaviest components of our new Proton gantry, two cylinders weighing over 15 tons each, have arrived and will now be carefully lowered into the Andersen Building by large cranes and prepared for installation. The gantry is a critical structure that houses the radiation delivery system. By rotating around the patient, it allows us to precisely target tumors from multiple different angles, allowing us to give high doses of radiation to the tumor while sparing healthy organs and tissues that are nearby. When fully assembled, each gantry will weigh over 220 tons. It will rotate 360 degrees around the patient and deliver the proton beam to within a millimeter of accuracy. Our center treats 40% more patients than any other proton facility in the country, a testament to our unwavering commitment to ensuring access for those who stand to benefit the most from this precision treatment. This new gantry and the Andersen expansion that surrounds it will expand our capacity from 1 ,300 patients a year to 2,200 patients. It will also position us for the future. The CT scanners attached to the gantry will provide real-time imaging so we can adapt each treatment daily to the changing size and shape of the tumors. As impressive as the gantry is, we must remember it represents more than just machinery. It's an engineering marvel that represents innovation collaboration and most of all hope it's the promise that every patient who walks through our doors will receive the most precise personalized and compassionate care available anywhere in the world

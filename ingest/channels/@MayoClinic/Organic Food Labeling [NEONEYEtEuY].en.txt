Have you ever wondered why some organic foods have the USDA organic seal like this one on the package, and other products with organic ingredients don't have the USDA organic seal. Knowing more about the organic labeling system helps consumers identify how organic a product really is. This fact sheet from the USDA is a great illustration explaining which foods would have the organic seal on their packaging. There are three main labels to look for. The first is 100% organic. This means that everything in the product is fully organic. No synthetic chemicals, pesticides, or genetically modified ingredients are allowed. You'll also see the USDA organic seal on these products. For products labeled simply as organic, at least 95% of the ingredients are organic. The remaining 5% can be non-organic, but only if they come from a strict list of approved substances. These products can still display the USDA organic seal. If a product has 70-95% organic content, It will say made with organic and then a specific ingredients that are organic. For example, made with organic oats. However, these products cannot use the USDA organic seal. Anything with less than 70% organic content can only mention organic ingredients in the ingredient list, not on the front of the package. So to summarize, 100% organic is the strictest labeling. Organic means 95% or more ingredients are organic and made with organic has at least 70% organic ingredients. Pay attention to these labels to know what you're buying.

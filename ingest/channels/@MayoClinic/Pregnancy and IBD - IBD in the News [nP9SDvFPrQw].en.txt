hello i'm dr sununda kane and i'm a gastroenterologist at the mayo clinic here in rochester minnesota i want to spend a couple of minutes just talking about a recent article that came out in one of our scientific journals in relation to the topic that i get asked about a lot which is pregnancy and inflammatory bowel disease and a lot of women are concerned because they are told because they have inflammatory bowel disease either crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis that they can't get pregnant or they shouldn't get pregnant and the the newest article that just came out was a systematic review of all the literature dating back to the 1940s or 50s up through about 2010 looking at the fertility rates in women who have inflammatory bowel disease who have not had surgery and it turns out that there is no decreased fertility rates in women who have inflammatory bowel disease if you have not had significant pelvic or abdominal surgery and so why is this important well it turns out that women who have ulcerative colitis who have undergone a j pouch procedure to have their colons removed are increased risk for infertility and there was a lot of confusion about well what if i have crohn's and i've had an operation to remove some of my intestines from my crohn's disease and as my fertility impaired and it turns out that no basically that if you have ulcerative colitis and have not had an operation your fertility is not impaired if you have crohn's disease and have had surgeries but just to remove parts of your small intestine and maybe parts of your colon but not your entire colon then you are your fertility is not impaired so the bottom line is that just because you have inflammatory bowel disease does not mean that you cannot have children or that it might be harder to get pregnant

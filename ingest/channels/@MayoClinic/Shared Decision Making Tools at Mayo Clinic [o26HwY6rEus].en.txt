well i use a couple of decision aids on a somewhat regular basis probably the one i like the most is statin choice it already stratifies by patient risk so the low risk patients and the intermediate risk patients and things like that what i like about those tools primarily is that they get the numbers in front of the patient so sometimes i'm not sure whether patients really like decision aids or whether it's the first time they've ever actually seen the numbers associated with the benefits of of the medications being discussed so probably a year ago i had a conversation with a patient who was arguing with her doctor about whether she should take statins and so they came to me as sort of a neutral second opinion kind of person and i said well let's just look at your numbers and even before looking at the statin choice decision aid we looked up her 10-year cardiovascular risk with and without statins and then we looked at her 10-year cardiovascular risk with and without adequate blood pressure control and really for her it made a lot more sense for her to focus on keeping her blood pressure under control than it is to really add a statin and it we could have driven her LDL into the ground and we still wouldn't really made a dent on her cardiovascular risk so just getting the numbers in front of the patient is often to me the most beneficial thing and then of course you can also talk about values and say to the patient what's really important to you here do you want to get that submission of extra benefit from taking the medication or not so that's probably just one example that i would use from using decision aids

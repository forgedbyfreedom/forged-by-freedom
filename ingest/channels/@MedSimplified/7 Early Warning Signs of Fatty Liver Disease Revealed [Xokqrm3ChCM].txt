 Liver is one of the most important organs of the body, performing numerous vital functions like digestion, metabolism, storage, detoxification, and production of various essential compounds required by the body. Fatty liver, scientifically known as hepatic steatosis, occurs when there is an abnormal accumulation of triglycerides and other fats within liver cells. A healthy, high-functioning liver contains a small amount of fat, which is normal. But fat buildup becomes a problem when it reaches over 5% of your liver's weight. Liver is a vital organ responsible for processing and metabolism of fats in the body. However, when the influx of fats exceeds the liver's capacity for metabolizing them, lipids accumulate within their liver cells, resulting in the development of a fatty liver. Fatty liver disease has become a global health concern, affecting over 25% of adults worldwide, making it one of the most prevalent liver conditions in the world. When fatty liver develops in someone who drinks a lot of alcohol, it's known as alcoholic fatty liver disease. When it develops in someone who doesn't drink a lot of alcohol, it's known as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease or NAFLD. Usually, both alcoholic fatty liver disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease present similarly. However, problems arise because fatty liver doesn't produce much symptoms in the early stages. So, by the time the patient usually develops symptoms, the condition has already progressed. That's why it becomes very important to stay alert and note the early signs of development of this condition. So, let's talk about 7 signs and symptoms that may indicate that you have an underlying fatty liver disease. 1. Wage Generalized Symptoms In the initial phases, a patient with fatty liver will develop generalized symptoms like feeling tired all the time, loss of appetite, nausea and abdominal pain. The liver is located on the right side of the abdomen and the discomfort arises as the liver swells up a bit with the accumulation of excess fat. This pain can vary from mild to more noticeable, serving as an early indication of potential liver distress. The liver plays a crucial role in digestion by producing bile, which aids in breaking down fats. But when the fatty liver disease sets in, this process can be disrupted, leading to loss of appetite. Also, the liver's inability to release sufficient bile hinders the absorption of essential nutrients like vitamins and antioxidants. This deficiency can leave individuals feeling fatigued and sluggish all the time. 2. Jaundice Jaundice is a condition characterized by the yellowing of the skin and the whites of the eyes known as sclera. This yellow discoloration results from the accumulation of bilirubin under the skin, a yellow pigment produced during the breakdown of red blood cells. Bilirubin is typically processed by the liver and extruded in the bile. In the presence of fatty liver disease, the liver's efficiency in handling bilirubin is compromised. This can be due to inflammation, cellular damage or impaired liver function associated with the accumulation of fat within the liver cells. 3. Dark Urine Like we discussed earlier, that liver is important for the breakdown and metabolism of bilirubin. So when the liver doesn't function properly due to fatty liver disease, the bilirubin that builds up in the blood also gets excreted in the urine, causing it to turn dark. This condition is called bilirubinuria and can manifest as urine that's dark brown, amber or even cola colored. However, it's crucial to remember that many other factors can cause dark urine, including most commonly dehydration, certain medications, vitamins, supplements and even some foods. So it's a very non-specific sign of fatty liver. But when present with other symptoms like jaundice or abdominal pain or itching, it can be a vital symptom in the diagnosis of a fatty liver. 4. Right Shoulder Pain If your right shoulder, neck, jaw or under the shoulder blade feels painful or tight, it might be linked to a fatty liver. Now like we discussed, the liver produces bile, a liquid that helps to digest fats from food like meat and olive oil. When the liver gets too fatty, the bile becomes thicker and trapped, causing the liver and gallbladder to swell and get inflamed. This swelling puts pressure on the phrenic nerve on the right side, leading to pain in the upper back and neck muscles. This is known as referred pain. Referred pain happens when the actual source of pain is in one area, but the discomfort is felt in another unrelated location. Understanding this connection shows how a fatty liver can be related to discomfort in other parts of the body. 5. Spider Angioma Individuals experiencing liver damage exhibit elevated estrogen levels, as the liver struggles to effectively filter out the surplus hormone. This heightened estrogen presence can contribute to vascular weakening throughout the body, leading to the formation of small spider veins on the skin. These spider veins typically display a central red dot surrounded by reddish extensions emanating from the middle. 6. Gynecomastia Press enlargement in men, also known as gynecomastia, occurs due to an imbalance between the sex hormones, estrogen and testosterone. As most of you know, the levels of hormone testosterone are much higher in men as compared to estrogen. But due to fatty liver disease, the liver is unable to metabolize estrogen, like we discussed earlier in this video. So due to this, the estrogen levels in the body rise and lead to many problems, and one of the most common of them is breast enlargement in men, also known as gynecomastia. Another reason why fatty liver disease leads to gynecomastia is that the liver is also responsible for the production of certain proteins known as glopurins, which keep these hormones in a bound state in the blood. So when the liver function gets down due to fatty liver disease, excess of these hormones become free, due to which they get activated. 7. Generalized Itching of Skin Itchy skin or pruritus is also associated with fatty liver disease. As the liver struggles to process fats efficiently, it can lead to a buildup of bile salts in the bloodstream. This accumulation along with the changes in the balance of certain chemicals contributes to itching sensations on the skin. The impaired liver function can cause bile to leach out and accumulate under the skin, triggering a histamine reaction that induces itching. The palms of hands and soles of the feet are the common areas that get affected by this problem. So these were seven signs that can indicate that you have an underlying fatty liver disease.

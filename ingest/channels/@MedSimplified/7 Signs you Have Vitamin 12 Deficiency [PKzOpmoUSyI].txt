 Millions of people are unknowingly suffering from vitamin B12 deficiency that can impact everything from their energy levels to their mental health. Are you one of them? This video will help you identify signs and symptoms that you have a vitamin B12 deficiency. Vitamin B12 or cobalamin is a vital nutrient with diverse functions in the body. It plays a crucial role in red blood cell formation, supporting oxygen transport, and maintaining a healthy nervous system. Vitamin B12 is essential for DNA synthesis, energy metabolism, mood regulation, and also supports heart and bone health. Here are seven signs you may have an underlying vitamin B12 deficiency. 1. Numbness and tingling sensations in hands and feet Numbness and tingling, medically referred to as peripheral neuropathy, are common neurological symptoms associated with vitamin B12 deficiency. Vitamin B12 is crucial for synthesis of myelin, a protective sheath that surrounds our nerves. When B12 is deficient, myelin production gets impaired, leading to nerve damage. This damage disrupts the normal functioning of nerves, resulting in abnormal sensations like numbness and tingling. Numbness and tingling are often felt in extremities, such as hands and feet. Patients may describe up-pins and needles sensations or a feeling of reduced sensitivity in these areas. The symptoms can vary in intensity, ranging from mild discomfort to more pronounced sensations. Patients may initially notice subtle sensations that progressively intensify. These sensations may not initially cause significant pain, but can be persistent and noticeable especially during moments of rest or reduced physical activity. In many cases, the numbness and tingling associated with B12 deficiency presents symmetrically, meaning it affects both sides of the body equally. This bilateral involvement is a characteristic feature that helps to distinguish it from other potential causes of peripheral neuropathy. 2. Feeling tired and fatigued all the time Feeling tired or fatigued is one of the most common signs of vitamin B12 deficiency. This occurs because vitamin B12 is necessary to produce an adequate amount of red blood cells that transport oxygen throughout your body. Without enough oxygen in your cells, you'll feel tired and weak, regardless of how much you sleep. It's a subtle symptom, but can significantly impact your daily life and productivity. The fatigue experienced isn't just the ordinary tiredness after a day's work or lack of sleep. It's a profound, chronic fatigue that tends to impede your ability to form everyday activities. You might find yourself needing to rest after simple tasks like walking upstairs or carrying groceries. 3. Pale or yellowish skin Like we discussed, vitamin B12 plays a vital role in the production of red blood cells. In case of its deficiency, immature and defective red blood cells are produced which are not efficient in transport of oxygen in the body. Properly oxygenated blood imparts a reddish hue to the skin, contributing to the healthy complexion, but when the oxygen transport in the blood is reduced, it gives the skin a pale look. Beyond causing paleness, severe vitamin B12 deficiency can lead to jaundice. Jaundice occurs when there is an accumulation of bilirubin, a yellow pigment that's a byproduct of the breakdown of old red blood cells. Normally, the liver processes bilirubin which is then excreted from the body. However, the ineffective and rapid breakdown of the abnormal immature red blood cells in B12 deficiency leads to an increased production of bilirubin. When the liver can't process this surplus bilirubin, it accumulates in the body leading to the yellowing of skin and whites of the eyes. 4. Mood Changes Depression, irritability and changes in mood can also be symptoms of vitamin B12 deficiency. While the exact mechanism isn't fully understood, it's believed that B12 plays a role in the synthesis of neurotransmitters such as serotonin and dopamine, which affects mood. Serotonin is often called the feel-good neurotransmitter contributing to the feelings of happiness and well-being, while dopamine is associated with the reward system of the brain, influencing motivation and pleasure. Deficiency in B12 may disrupt the balance of these neurotransmitters, leading to mood disturbances and even depression. Researchers in a 2005 article published in the Journal of Psychopharmacology reviewed studies of depression and low vitamin B12 status. They found substantial evidence that a decrease in the vitamin B12 serum levels correlates with an increase in the prevalence of depression. 5. Cognitive Problems Vitamin B12 deficiency can lead to significant cognitive difficulties, impacting the brain's functioning in various ways. Cognitive difficulties basically mean having trouble with the thinking process. It's like when your brain is finding it hard to do its job. This can include problems with remembering things, paying attention, learning new information, making decisions, understanding things correctly and solving problems. One of the most common cognitive symptoms associated with vitamin B12 deficiency is memory impairment. Individuals may find it challenging to remember recent events, appointments or even conversations. In severe and prolonged cases of vitamin B12 deficiency, there can be structural changes in the brain, leading to an increased risk of neurodegenerative diseases, including dementia. This lack of B12 can harm the protective coating of nerve cells, the myelin, and this coating, like we discussed, is very important for the fast and smooth conduction of messages in the nervous system. When it gets damaged, it can cause major problems with thinking and memory skills. 6. Vision Problems Since vitamin B12 is very important for the health and proper functioning of our nerves, people with B12 deficiency can experience vision problems as the nerves connecting the eyes to our brain get damaged over time. These nerves are known as optic nerves and the condition is known as optic neuropathy. It can lead to symptoms like blurred vision or even total loss of vision. There might be noticeable change in how colors are perceived, with the colors appearing less vibrant. Individuals may also experience a reduction in their field of vision, meaning they can't see as wide an area as they normally would. The vision changes caused by vitamin B12 deficiency can often be reversed with prompt treatment, especially if the deficiency is caught early. 7. Mouth Ulcers Mouth ulcers, also known as canker sores, are another common symptom of vitamin B12 deficiency. These are small, painful sores that can appear on the inside of your mouth, including the lips, cheeks, and at the base of the gums. The ulcers are typically round or oval with a white, yellow, or grey center and a red border. They can be quite painful, especially when talking, brushing teeth, or eating. Vitamin B12 is essential for the healthy formation and repair of cells found in the lining of our mouth. Without adequate levels of vitamin B12, these cells cannot regenerate effectively, leading to the formation of mouth ulcers. 6. Oral Supplements Or in more severe cases, intramuscular injections of B12 can rapidly improve these symptoms.

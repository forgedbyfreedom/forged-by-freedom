Seboric dermatitis is a chronic skin condition that causes redness, itching and flaking of the skin. It is a form of eczema that most often affects the scalp but it can also develop on other oily areas of the body like your face, upper chest and back. The sweat glands in these areas of the body are known as sebaceous glands and they differ from the rest of the sweat glands as they also secrete some special oily compounds like squalene, esters and cholesterol on the skin. It was earlier believed that seboric dermatitis develops due to the excessive oil production by the sebaceous glands. But later it was found to be untrue. As some of you may know, our skin has its own microflora. There are hundreds or even thousands of species of bacteria and fungi that live on our body and these organisms are vital for the skin. They do not harm us but instead they are very very important for our skin. One such species of fungus that lives on our skin is known as Malassezia. And earlier it was also believed that seboric dermatitis develops due to the overgrowth of this fungus. But later the research showed that this was also not true. Now the recent studies have shown that seboric dermatitis develops due to multiple factors. The most evident of all these factors was a change in the type of oily compounds that are secreted by the sebaceous glands. Chemical analysis of these lipids from patients with seboric dermatitis showed increased presence of unsaturated fatty acids. When these unsaturated fatty acids are broken down by fungi like Malassezia, it leads to the production of certain chemicals that cause inflammation of the skin. Due to inflammation, there is a strong immune reaction against the Malassezia fungus. And this further leads to more inflammation and further progression of seboric dermatitis. The research also showed some other factors that were linked to the development of seboric dermatitis, like abnormal shedding of skin cells and epidermal barrier disturbances associated with genetic factors. So yes, there is also a genetic component linked to seboric dermatitis. Seboric dermatitis develops in people of all ages and races. While it can appear at any time, it is most likely to begin during the beginning of adolescence or adulthood. When a baby has seboric dermatitis, it usually appears on the scalp only and is known as cradle cap. It is also more common in adolescence as the sebaceous glands are more active under the hormonal influence, but also common in adulthood, usually beginning in a person's thirties or later. Other factors that can contribute to the development of this condition include stress, hormonal changes, obesity, poor skin care, environmental factors like pollution, the use of certain skin care products, particularly those containing alcohol, and using harsh soaps and chemicals over your scalp and body. Cold, dry weather and weakened immune systems such as HIV has also been linked to seboric dermatitis. Now many people have this question whether seboric dermatitis and dandruff are the same things. Well, dandruff may be considered the mildest form of seboric dermatitis as it occurs over the scalp only. Unlike dandruff, seboric dermatitis may develop on more than scalp. It can appear on the ears, eyebrows, beard or the skin around the nostrils. Sometimes it also appears on the chest, especially in men. The two conditions are similar in that they both cause white to yellowish scales which may be dry or greasy. Both these conditions also cause mild to severe itching depending upon the severity.

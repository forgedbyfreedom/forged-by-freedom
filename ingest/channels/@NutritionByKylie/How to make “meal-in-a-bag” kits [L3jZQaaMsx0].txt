 Groceries are already expensive, and for a lot of families, it's about to get even harder. Millions of Americans are about to lose access to SNAP, which is a federal program that nearly 42 million people rely on to buy food. That is one in eight Americans and about 40% are children. So today, I took inspiration from Dollar Tree Dinners, and I'm making meal in a bag kits to my local community fridge here in Brooklyn. For this meal, we're making a southwestern black bean soup. Each bag includes canned black beans, corn, fire roasted tomatoes, diced green chiles, vegetable broth, and a seasoning packet. All you do is combine everything in a pot, simmer, and you have a warm, balanced meal. It's filling, nourishing, high in fiber, and everything is shelf-stable. I also printed recipe cards so whoever receives this can easily follow the recipe. As a dietitian, I talk a lot about the importance of nutrition, but nutrition starts with access. It's difficult to focus on eating healthy if you're worried about having enough food at all. Health isn't just about individual choices, it's also about having the opportunity to make those choices in the first place. Everyone deserves access to food and supporting that access, even in small ways, is part of supporting health.

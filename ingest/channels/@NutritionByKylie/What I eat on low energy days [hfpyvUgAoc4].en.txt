I'm a dietitian and there are so many days when I'm too lazy to cook for myself. So, this is what I do to prepare for those days. I'm making a dump and go beef broccoli where all I have to do is dump everything in a bag, pop it in the freezer, thaw it, and then dump it in a skillet when I'm ready to eat. It takes a few minutes to prep, but aside from thawing, dinner is done in a few minutes. For a long time, I felt like a bad dietician for not having the energy to cook every night. But then I realized that having the energy to cook these elaborate meals every day is not realistic. Sometimes you have executive dysfunction, long work days, kids, chronic illnesses, or low energy levels, and that makes it hard to even start a meal, let alone finish one. That's exactly why I included a freezer section in this cookbook that not only focuses on freezer meals, but also utilizes frozen ingredients like vegetables for ultimate convenience. And just because it's frozen doesn't mean it's any less nutritious. In fact, frozen veggies are often picked and flash frozen at peak ripess, which means they retain their nutrients just as well. Sometimes even better than fresh produce that's been sitting in your fridge all week. Sometimes it's not realistic to cook from scratch every night. But that doesn't mean you're not worthy of having a delicious, warm, home-cooked meal.

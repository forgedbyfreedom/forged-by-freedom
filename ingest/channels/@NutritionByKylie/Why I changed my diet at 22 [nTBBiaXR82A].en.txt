Colon cancer is now the leading cause of cancer related deaths in people under 50. And in honor of colon cancer awareness month, I wanted to share something I never thought I'd talk about publicly as a dietitian. I've had a long history of GI issues since I was in middle school and had my first colonoscopy right out of college. And though they did rule out conditions like IBD and celiac disease, they found and rected large precancerous polyps, which if not caught in time would have developed into colon cancer. I was only 22 years old. This is extremely unusual for someone that age. First of all, I feel lucky enough to have caught it when I did because colon cancer screenings usually start at 45. Second of all, there are so many reasons this could have happened. I have a family history of colon cancer and also sometimes these things just happen no matter what you eat or do. But in full transparency, I didn't eat well growing up or even throughout college. Though I knew how to eat healthy as a nutrition student, I was extremely stressed constantly and didn't prioritize it. So when I got this news at 22, it made me re-evaluate a lot. I scaled back on alcohol and processed meat, but most importantly, I focused on what I could add. And what I could add was a lot more fiber. Fiber isn't a foolproof solution to prevent any disease, including cancer. But from a nutrition perspective, it's the best tool we have, and it comes with a lot of other health benefits as well.

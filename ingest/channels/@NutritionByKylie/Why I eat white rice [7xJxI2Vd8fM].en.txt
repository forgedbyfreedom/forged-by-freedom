Whenever I make what I eat in a day videos, people always bring up the fact that I eat white rice and that I tend to eat a lot of it in my meals. And as a dietitian, I get it. White rice has gotten a bad rap in the nutrition world. And for a long time, I thought my diet wasn't healthy enough either. Growing up Asian and in Hawaii, rice was a staple at every meal. So, when I got to college and started studying nutrition, I was upset to learn that the foods that I grew up with were often labeled as unhealthy. I was taught that the Mediterranean diet was the gold standard. And while it's wonderful and absolutely shown to have numerous health benefits, I've come to realize that there's no single path to health. Yes, brown rice is a whole grain and does contain more fiber than white rice, but the difference isn't as major as people make it out to be. As much as I love extra fiber, I'll always love the fluffy texture of white rice more. And the truth is, I get so much fiber and whole grains from other areas of my diet. For a long time, nutrition advice has been eurocentric. And while the guidelines might work for some, they can be limiting for others. The definition of healthy has been shaped by a limited perspective, and I really wanted to change that. This cookbook includes a variety of recipes and flavors, including Mediterranean inspired dishes, but it also features white rice and Asian recipes. It might not look like your typical healthy cookbook, but that's the point. It still includes all of this information, but it also shows that health doesn't have to come at the cost of comfort or joy.

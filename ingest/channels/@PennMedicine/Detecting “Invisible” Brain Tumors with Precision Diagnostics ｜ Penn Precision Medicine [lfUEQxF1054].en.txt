(soft music) - Globally, nearly 190,000 people die each year as a result of brain cancer, with glioblastoma, or GBM, being the most common and most lethal form of primary brain cancer. Glioblastoma is very difficult to treat, and a cure is often not possible. The standard of care is surgery, followed by radiation therapy and chemotherapy. In more than 80% of cases, the cancer comes back after a period of remission. One of the major factors of glioblastoma's poor prognosis is its infiltrative growth. Like mixing black and white sand together, it makes differentiation from normal brain tissue extremely difficult. This infiltrative tumor growth is not usually visible on conventional imaging. What we can't see is what we can't treat. We believe that normal MRI techniques coupled with artificial intelligence can help in better detection of this invisible component of the tumor. We are utilizing state-of-the-art imaging techniques, using the strongest available clinical MRI scanner, the 7-Tesla MRI. Coupled with sophisticated post-processing machine learning algorithms to accurately delineate the extent of this invisible tumor, what we call precision diagnostics, which will be used for individualized treatment planning or personalized therapeutics. This will assist both the neurosurgeon performing resection of the tumor and the radiation oncologist designing and implementing the radiation plan. We believe that this transformative brain tumor imaging and treatment protocol has the potential to change the standard of care and improve the overall outcomes of this deadly disease. I am Doctor Suyash Mohan and this is precision medicine at Penn. (upbeat music)

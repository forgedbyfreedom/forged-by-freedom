it's long been known that excessive alcohol consumption can damage one's health including effects on the brain and liver new research led by a team at Penn medicine discovered a surprising pathway that shows alcohol byproducts traveled to the brain to promote addiction memory after consuming alcohol the liver begins to break down the alcohol converting it into byproducts consisting mainly of a chemical group called acetate acetate which circulates in our blood travels up from the liver to a key brain region involved in learning and memory acetate then binds to proteins packaging the DNA at important genes in neuronal cells once there the team showed in a mouse model that the acetate governs how these genes are expressed and ultimately how behavior is effective when given environmental cues to consume alcohol blocking this pathway could be beneficial to selectively lower unwanted addiction memory in a therapeutic setting this new potential target could provide physicians novel ways to fight alcoholism and addiction you

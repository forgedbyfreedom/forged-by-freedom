I was interested in neurosurgery because I really liked the combination of helping patients to improve their outcome, but also the innovation and the novelty of our field in neurosurgery where we still improve techniques every day. Hi, my name is Jan Burkhardt, and I'm one of the vascular neurosurgeons here at Penn Neurosurgery. The main focus of my clinical work here at Penn is vascular neurosurgery. That means I'm treating patients with vessel pathologies in the brain, but also in the neck, and also the spine. These diseases may be, for example, an aneurysm, or vascular malformation, or vessel stenosis, or occlusion, or stroke. Most of my patients actually come through the ER with stroke symptoms, so for example, weakness or difficulty to speak, and the other portion of my patients come to the elective clinic, and these patients, most of the time, don't even have symptoms. The reason why I think patients with these very rare diseases should be treated here at Penn is that we are a center for rare diseases and we see these patients quite often. It's a world-class institution for research and also patient care and education. This is the unique place to combine all three things. For me, it's important to really involve the patient into their current situation and to make sure that they understand every step on the way and that they can ask all the questions they have. The best part of my job is to come in in the morning and really help patients to get better, but also improving surgical techniques and thrive in excellency.

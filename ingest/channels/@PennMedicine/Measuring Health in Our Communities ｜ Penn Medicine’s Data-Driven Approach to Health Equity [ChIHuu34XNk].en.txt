At Penn Medicine, we're committed to uplifting the communities we serve. To truly improve health outcomes, we need to understand our communities on a deeper level. Philadelphia faces significant challenges. As one of the poorest big cities in the US, nearly 20% of residents live in poverty. What's striking is how geography impacts health. Life expectancy can vary by up to 15 years from one zip code to another. But it's also a city with tremendous vitality and connected communities. We want to build on that strength while helping to address the challenges our communities face. To address these disparities, we developed a comprehensive approach to measure health in our communities. We focused on five key indicators. Poverty rates, food insecurity rates, hypertension diagnosis and control, colurectal and breast cancer screening, and immunization rates for respiratory infections. We measured each indicator at the zip code level for Penn Medicine's downtown Philadelphia catchment area, comparing results to city, state, and national rates. The findings revealed significant opportunities. High poverty and food insecurity alongside gaps in preventive care. Most importantly, communities with higher poverty consistently had worse outcomes across multiple health metrics. But we're not just identifying problems, we're implementing solutions. Pen Medicine has been running a remote hypertension management program for nearly a decade with proven results. I work at Pen Medicine and live in North Philadelphia. The program gave me a blood pressure cuff and sends text messages to check my readings. A nurse and a doctor review my results and help me find the right medications to control my blood pressure. We've also invested in a mobile mimography unit to increase screening access for our most vulnerable patients. This work is about understanding our community's needs so we can respond with precision and care. By identifying where disparities exist, we can focus our resources where they'll have the greatest impact. At Penn Medicine, we believe all patients deserve a long, healthy life, regardless of their zip code. We're using these insights to reduce disparities and build healthier communities across Philadelphia.

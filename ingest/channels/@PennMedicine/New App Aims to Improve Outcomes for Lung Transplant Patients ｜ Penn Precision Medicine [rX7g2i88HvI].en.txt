(soft music) - Over 2300 patients undergo lung transplantation in the United States every year. With improvements in medical and surgical care, plus recent changes in lung allocation algorithms, older and sicker patients are prioritized for transplant each year. This can lead to increased length of stay, increased disability, and a development of frailty after lung transplantation, which can lead to a significant decline in health-related quality of life. Additionally, 60% of lung transplant patients are readmitted within 30 days after transplant. This can be physically and emotionally devastating for a patient who has been suffering for months or years prior to transplantation. What if there was a way to harness the power of our smartphones to improve physical function and prevent or reverse frailty after lung transplant? In collaboration with colleagues at the University of San Francisco, California and the medical application company AidCube, and with support from the Penn Center for Precision Medicine, we are utilizing a targeted, individualized, customizable physical therapy mobile health app to mitigate frailty, decrease hospital readmission rate, decrease healthcare expenditures, and improve health-related quality of life. I'm Dr. Joshua Diamond, and this is Precision Medicine at Penn.

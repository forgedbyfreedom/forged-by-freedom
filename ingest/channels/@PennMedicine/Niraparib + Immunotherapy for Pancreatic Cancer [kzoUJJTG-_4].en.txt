(soft music) - 53,000 people will be diagnosed with Pancreatic Cancer this year. For most of them long term chemotherapy is the only option we have to keep their cancer in check. Over time, the side effects of chemotherapy can make treatment progressively difficult or even impossible, but is there a better way? What if we could use a combination of clinical data, genetic sequencing and imaging to predict which patients might be able to come off chemotherapy and switch to a lower toxicity maintenance treatment. In this phase two clinical trial patients with Pancreatic Cancer whose cancer has not grown after at least four months of platinum based chemotherapy will be offered a novel combination of Nuroparith, a PARP inhibitor, plus immunotherapy to treat their disease. In collaboration with a team of scientists from the Abramson Cancer Center and with support from the Penn Center for Precision Medicine cutting-edge imaging and genetic sequencing will be performed so that we can learn to predict who would benefit most from this strategy. I'm Dr. Kim Reiss Binder and this is Precision Medicine at Penn.

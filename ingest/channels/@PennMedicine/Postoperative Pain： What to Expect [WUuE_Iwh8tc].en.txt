The purpose of this video is to give you information about pain control and, let you know what to expect on your day of surgery It is normal to be worried about pain after surgery The good news is that the pain you will experience after surgery is almost always temporary and will get better over the first 3 days after most operations Your doctors and nurses will do everything they can to safely make you as comfortable as possible after surgery Knowing what to expect after surgery in terms of pain control is an important step in your recovery It is normal that you will experience some amount of discomfort after surgery Know that pain is experienced in different ways by different people The goal of post-surgery pain care is to do as much as possible to reduce your pain so you are able to do the things you need to do to get well Having a realistic idea of what to expect after surgery will aid in your healing process It is important to understand that pain treatment options can have some risks and there are limits to what can be safely offered Your body has been through a lot during an operation, and it is not likely that you will have no pain afterwards Postoperative pain control is designed to make you comfortable, but not to eliminate your pain 100% After surgery, you may be given several medications, either opioid, an opioid alternative, or both, to help treat your pain Our goal is to help improve your physical function after surgery to help you get better If you are given opioid pain medications, it is important to note that these do come with some risks Some people have nausea or vomiting after taking these medications, as well as itching and increased sleepiness There is great concern that use of opioid medications, may increase the risk of abuse, misuse, and addiction especially with extended use after surgery Whenever possible use over-the-counter medication for pain relief As a result, we work hard to prescribe opioid medications only when absolutely necessary and at the lowest dose for the shortest amount of time If prior to surgery you were regularly taking opioids for a chronic pain condition or to treat an opioid use disorder (addiction) it is likely that you will have developed some tolerance to the pain-relieving effects of opioids and they may be less effective in reducing your pain We will be sure to address your pain by using the lowest possible, yet still effective dose of opioids along with other types of pain medication Our goal is to make this experience as comfortable as possible for you Knowing what to expect is an important first step to managing your symptoms after surgery Your physicians and nurses have your best interest in mind and will work with you to achieve adequate control of your postoperative symptoms If you have any questions or concerns about pain management after surgery please remember to speak with your physician when you meet them in the preoperative area on your day of surgery.

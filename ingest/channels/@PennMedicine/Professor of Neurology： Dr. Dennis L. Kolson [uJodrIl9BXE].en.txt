- I grew up in an environment where my family was very dependent upon physicians for health care. My only sister was severely mentally retarded. And from the time I was very little, I thought that I would maybe be able to cure people like her. And that's how I first thought about doing medicine. My name is Dennis Kolson, I'm a professor of neurology at Penn Medicine. The focus of my work is in two areas, one is the basic research that I do in the nervous system, And the second is the clinical practice that I have. And my ultimate goal is to combine the two in terms of discovering new strategies to protect the brain. My approach to patient care is one of service. I see physicians as service providers, much like I see police officers, teachers, firefighters, military. I'm going to do my best, I'm going to treat you like you were a member of my family, like I would my wife or brothers or father, mother, children or how I would want to treated, that's how I look at it. The best part of my job is seeing somebody down the road so many years later, feeling like they have a pretty good life after all. There's nothing better than that feeling, that keeps me going.

- Hi. My name is Patrick Kim. I'm a trauma surgeon and the trauma program medical director at Penn Medicine's Level 1 Trauma Center. One of the most challenging aspects of trauma care is managing patients who have multiple life-threatening injuries. I'd like to talk about how we do this here at Penn. Many trauma patients have multiple injuries, each of which alone might require emergent treatment. When the injuries are combined, the care is particularly challenging. It becomes necessary to prioritize treatments and organize care that may be provided by several different specialists. Some of the most challenging patients in trauma care are those with what we call competing or conflicting priorities. In other words, the treatment for one injury may be contradictory or harmful to treating another injury. An example of this is a patient who suffers a fall from height. The patient is found to have a severe traumatic spinal cord injury with paralysis and a thoracic aortic injury. The best treatment for the spinal cord injury is to maintain a high blood pressure to make sure the spinal cord receives enough oxygen. However, the best short-term treatment for the aortic injury is to keep the blood pressure as low as safely possible to prevent rupture which could be immediately fatal. So these are conflicting priorities. Here the trauma surgeon works with the surgical intensivist, neurosurgeon, vascular surgeon, and anesthesiologist to create a comprehensive plan of care that includes an acceptable short-term goal for blood pressure management and coordination of the optimal timing of the aortic operation. Patients with competing priorities highlight that trauma care is a team sport, and I'm proud to be on the team at Penn.

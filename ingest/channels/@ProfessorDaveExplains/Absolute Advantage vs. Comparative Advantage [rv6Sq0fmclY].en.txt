In the previous tutorial, we learned about\h specialization, which is when an individual,\h\h company, or country specializes in producing\h one thing or one part of a task, and relies on\h\h others to produce everything else or complete the\h other parts of the task. We also learned about why\h\h specialization is so important. This is\h because it leads to more efficiency, higher\h\h productivity and higher standards of living. To better illustrate this, in this tutorial we are\h\h going to look at the difference between absolute\h advantage and comparative advantage. Absolute\h\h advantage means a person or nation can produce\h more of something than another person or nation\h\h using the same amount of resources. Comparative\h advantage, on the other hand, means a person or\h\h nation can produce something most efficiently\h given all the products it could potentially\h\h produce. This considers the opportunity cost of\h producing one good over another. In other words,\h\h comparative advantage is the circumstance in which\h an entity gives up comparatively less, or has a\h\h lower opportunity cost, than some other entity,\h when making the same amount of the same product.\h First, let’s look more closely at absolute\h advantage. Say you have two people,\h\h Harry and Lloyd. They are both carpenters, and to\h keep things simple at first, let’s say they both\h\h build rocking chairs. If Harry can build two\h chairs in a day and Lloyd can only build one,\h\h it is certainly the case that Harry\h has an absolute advantage over Lloyd.\h\h Of course in the real world things are rarely this\h simple. So now to make things a little trickier,\h\h let’s say Harry and Lloyd are both carpenters\h who build tables and birdhouses. In one day,\h\h Harry can make either three birdhouses or one\h table. Lloyd can make either two birdhouses\h\h or one table per day. Based on this information,\h we know that Harry is more productive than Lloyd\h\h in making birdhouses. In economic terms, time is\h the limited resource, and Harry has an absolute\h\h advantage over Lloyd, because he can make one\h more birdhouse given the same amount of time.\h Now, let’s look more closely at comparative\h advantage with the same example. Again,\h\h Harry can make three birdhouses or one table.\h Lloyd can make two birdhouses or one table. For\h\h comparative advantage, we need to figure out what\h makes more sense for Harry and Lloyd to produce.\h\h In order to do that, it is useful to compare\h opportunity costs. Harry sacrifices three\h\h birdhouses for every table he produces. So, the\h opportunity cost of making one table is three\h\h birdhouses, the three birdhouses he could have\h built in the time it takes him to build one table.\h\h And the opportunity cost of making one birdhouse\h is 1/3 of a table. Now let’s look at Lloyd.\h\h The opportunity cost of Lloyd making one table is\h just two birdhouses, while the opportunity cost\h\h of Lloyd making one birdhouse is 1/2 of a table.\h According to the law of comparative advantage,\h\h each person should produce the good for\h which there is a lower opportunity cost\h\h than other producers. Harry’s opportunity cost\h for making one birdhouse, one third of a table,\h\h is lower than Lloyd’s opportunity cost for making\h one birdhouse, one half of a table. Therefore, it\h\h makes more sense for Harry to make birdhouses. And\h this works out well for Lloyd, too. It looks like\h\h Lloyd’s opportunity cost for making one table, two\h birdhouses, is lower than Harry’s opportunity cost\h\h for making one table, three birdhouses. Therefore,\h it makes more sense for Lloyd to make tables.\h\h If Harry focuses on birdhouses and Lloyd makes\h tables, they will end up with three birdhouses\h\h and a table at the end of the day, as opposed to\h two birdhouses and one table if they were to swap.\h Now with this very simplistic example understood,\h let’s apply absolute advantage and comparative\h\h advantage to entire countries. Suppose\h two countries, Country A and Country B,\h\h are able to produce both coffee and sugar.\h However, Country A’s climate and soil are such\h\h that it can more easily produce coffee, making two\h tons of coffee per hour as opposed to only one ton\h\h of sugar per hour. Country B’s climate and soil\h are such that it can more easily produce sugar,\h\h making three tons of sugar per hour as opposed\h to just one ton of coffee per hour. Therefore,\h\h we can say that Country A has an\h absolute advantage producing coffee,\h\h and Country B has an absolute advantage producing\h sugar. But now, let’s look at a more accurate\h\h way to conceptualize specialization, which is\h through their relative comparative advantage.\h For Country A, the opportunity cost of one ton of\h coffee is a half a ton of sugar. For Country B,\h\h the opportunity cost of one ton of coffee is\h three tons of sugar. Since Country A has a\h\h lower opportunity cost than Country B, Country A\h has a comparative advantage in producing coffee.\h\h Now, let’s look at producing sugar. For Country\h A, the opportunity cost of one ton of sugar\h\h is two tons of coffee. For Country B,\h the opportunity cost of one ton of sugar\h\h is one third of a ton of coffee. Country B\h has the lower opportunity cost, and therefore\h\h has the comparative advantage in producing\h sugar. So hypothetically, Country A should\h\h be exporting coffee to Country B, and Country\h B should be exporting sugar to Country A.\h What we have just demonstrated is an economic\h concept known as interdependence. Interdependence\h\h is the shared need of countries for the resources,\h goods, services, labor, and knowledge supplied by\h\h other countries. Because of interdependence,\h a drought in Guatemala that diminishes their\h\h coffee crop might mean more expensive coffee the\h next time you visit your favorite artisanal coffee\h\h shop. Perhaps this shop would turn to another\h country that produces coffee, like Brazil,\h\h to get its coffee beans in order to avoid raising\h prices. In any case, this is just one example of\h\h interdependence, which illustrates how many of the\h economies in the world are intimately connected.

For some time we have been discussing fungi\h within the phylum Basidiomycota, the club fungi.\h\h Within this phylum, let us now move\h further into the class Agaricomycetes,\h\h which consists of many of the mushroom-forming\h species of fungi, and more specifically,\h\h the order Agaricales, which consists of many of\h the gilled mushroom species. In this tutorial, we\h\h will be introducing a number of new types of these\h fungi, including the families Cortinariaceae,\h\h Nidulariaceae, and Psathyrellaceae, along\h with the genera Conocybe and Laccaria.\h Let’s begin by discussing the Cortinariaceae\h family. Generally, this family describes fungi\h\h in the genus, Cortinarius, which is the\h largest genus of mushroom-forming species,\h\h estimated to have over 2000 thousand species\h worldwide. The notable structural characteristic\h\h of this genus is the presence of a cortina,\h a web-like partial veil that covers the gills\h\h of the mushroom when young. As the mushroom ages,\h the remnants of the cortina may form a ring on the\h\h stipe or along the margin of the cap. The gills\h produce a rusty-brown spore print, so the gills\h\h of Cortinarius mushrooms are generally rusty-brown\h as well. These mushrooms are not purely saprobic,\h\h so throughout their life cycle, they do not always\h need to break down organic matter to survive.\h\h Instead, they form mutually benefitting\h relationships called mycorrhiza, “myco” meaning\h\h fungi and “rhiza” meaning root. So, mycorrhizal\h relationships form between fungi and plant roots.\h\h A number of fungi form mycorrhizal relationships\h of different types. However, when fungi in\h\h the phylum Basidiomycota form mycorrhiza, they\h specifically form ectomycorrhiza. Structurally,\h\h ectomycorrhiza have the root system completely\h surrounded by a layer of hyphae called a mantle.\h\h Some of the hyphae penetrate the outer layer of\h the root forming a network called the Hartig net.\h\h The Hartig net allows the fungus and the\h plant to become closely connected, allowing\h\h for the exchange of nutrients such as sugars,\h nitrogen-containing compounds, and amino acids.\h\h Many other mushroom genera we will\h discuss throughout this series\h\h form ectomycorrhizal relationships. Our next family is quite fascinating. This is\h\h Nidulariaceae, commonly known as the Bird’s\h Nest fungi, because their fruiting bodies\h\h look like tiny egg-filled bird’s nests. The\h name comes from the latin root “nidus” meaning\h\h nest. These fungi are saprobic on decaying wood,\h so you are likely to find these adorable fruiting\h\h bodies on wood chips or mulch. Fungi in the\h family Nidulariacae have unique structures.\h\h We previously discussed the gasteroid puffballs\h of Agaricaceae. Bird’s nest fungi are also\h\h gasteroid because they develop their spores\h inside those small egg-like structures called\h\h peridioles. These peridioles have their basidia\h and basidiospores surrounded by a hardened wall.\h\h The nest-like structure that houses the peridioles\h is called the peridia. All Bird’s Nest fungi have\h\h an interesting method of dispersing their\h peridioles, and therefore their spores. The\h\h nest-like peridia act as splash cups. When a\h raindrop hits the peridia slightly off-center,\h\h the raindrop expels the peridioles up to a meter\h away. Two out of the five genera in the family\h\h possess tendrils called funiculi that are attached\h to the peridioles. When the peridioles are\h\h expelled from the peridia, the funiculi can wrap\h around a plant nearby. It is hypothesized that\h\h herbivores will eat the plant and the attached\h peridiole, assisting in further spore dispersal.\h Now let us move to a family that has the general\h structure of a gilled mushroom with a cap,\h\h gills, and a stipe, the family Psathyrellaceae.\h We will discuss three genera in this family:\h\h Coprinellus, Coprinopsis, and Psathyrella. Fungi\h in Psathyrellaceae are almost always saprobic.\h\h You can find these fungi on soil, dung,\h decaying wood, and many other substrates.\h\h The fruiting bodies are generally small\h and fragile with dark-colored spores.\h Let’s begin our discussion of Psathyrellaceae\h by discussing the similar genera,\h\h Coprinellus and Coprinopsis, commonly known as\h the ink caps. The common name originates from a\h\h unique property of these mushrooms. As they age,\h the gills partially liquify or deliquesce into a\h\h black goopy and ink-like substance. This process\h is already quite captivating, but it is another\h\h demonstration of the genius of evolution. The\h caps of these mushrooms liquify from the edges in,\h\h so the maturing spores at the inner parts\h of the cap are in prime position to catch\h\h wind currents to disperse. Identification on the\h species level is quite difficult for two reasons.\h\h The first is that these mushrooms are extremely\h fragile and ephemeral. Over a single day,\h\h some species will fruit, deliquesce, and reduce\h to almost nothing. So it is quite difficult to\h\h gather the mushrooms as specimens. Second, the\h separation of Coprinopsis and Coprinellus species\h\h may require microscopic and genetic analysis.\h Coprinopsis atramentaria, or the common ink cap,\h\h is known to be edible, but contains a molecule\h called coprine that causes poisoning when\h\h consumed within a two to three day time period of\h drinking any form of alcohol. They\h produce black to brown spores and do not\h\h liquify like the aforementioned genera. The stipes\h are whitish to yellow and hollow on the inside,\h\h allowing them to have a satisfying snap. The caps\h are generally brown and often times hygrophanous,\h\h meaning the caps change color as they lose\h moisture. Identification on the species level\h\h generally requires microscopy. In Haitian\h tradition, species of Psathyrella are highly\h\h valued as a delicacy. Called Djon-Djon, the caps\h of these mushrooms are served at important events\h\h such as baptisms and weddings. Djon-Djon is\h generally boiled and strained. The Djon-Djon\h\h water is used to flavor cooked rice. Because\h these mushrooms can be quite expensive and rare,\h\h djon-djon flavored bouillon cubes are commonly\h used as a replacement for these dishes.\h Now we will move away from Psathyrellaceae\h to discuss the genus Conocybe, commonly\h\h known as the dunce-caps due to their conical\h or bell-shaped caps. A common species of Conocybe\h\h is Conocybe apala, a mushroom that notably fruits\h in grass lawns. The mushroom is toxic but not\h\h lethal, so keep your pets and children away. It\h has a pale brown color, and you will find the\h\h mushroom when the weather is hot and humid. The last genus we will discuss is Laccaria.\h\h These mushrooms are easy to recognize as they have\h thick and waxy looking gills that are attached to\h\h the stipe. They form white spore prints, and under\h a microscope, these spores are round with tiny\h\h spine-like projections. All Laccaria species form\h ectomycorrhizal relationships that are generally\h\h associated with tree roots in the order Fagales,\h which houses oak and beech trees.

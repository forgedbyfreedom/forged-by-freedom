While the history of some ancient societies is\h charted by the evolution of their architecture,\h\h or the amount of territory they occupied, the\h long course of ancient Egyptian history has been\h\h charted for more than two thousand years by its\h rulers. Our earliest archeological evidence, from\h\h the middle of the 4th millennium BCE, describes\h a country that did not have any centralized\h\h authority, but was made up of independent local\h communities that were coalescing into two groups.\h\h First there was Upper Egypt, located somewhat\h unintuitively in the south of the country, named\h\h as such because the Nile flows downward from south\h to north. Then there was Lower Egypt, around the\h\h Nile Delta in the north. Communities in both areas\h developed along the banks of the river, which was\h\h the only place where the land was suitable for\h farming, and they depended on its annual flooding\h\h to provide enough water for a good harvest. Upper\h and Lower Egypt fought one another until they were\h\h unified under a single king around 3100 BCE.\h This king, called Narmer, was the first to wear\h\h the double crown, the white crown of the south,\h and the red crown of the north, which symbolized\h\h the unification of the two regions under a single\h ruler. Narmer is therefore considered the earliest\h\h king of the First Dynasty. It was during his time,\h and that of his successors, that the type of tomb\h\h which would later develop into the pyramid was\h first built, and a simple form of hieroglyphic\h\h writing appeared. It may also have been in that\h era that Egyptian kings started to take on the\h\h divine qualities that would later define them. The beginning of the Old Kingdom is associated\h\h with the Third Dynasty of the 27th century\h BCE. Its first king, Djoser, moved the capital\h\h to the city of Inebu-hedj, or Memphis in Greek,\h just south of modern Cairo. His chief official,\h\h Imhotep, built in the town of Saqqara a\h step pyramid, which some historians say\h\h was an example of Sumerian influence on Egyptian\h art, architecture, and design beginning around\h\h the middle of the 4th millennium BCE, when objects\h like cylinder seals first appeared in Egypt. There\h\h was also the parallel development of Egyptian\h hieroglyphics alongside Sumerian cuneiform,\h\h although there is no strong evidence of\h one influencing the other. Both systems\h\h began as simple drawings that represented the\h objects they depicted, and evolved into characters\h\h that could either represent the syllables of\h words, or symbolize entire ideas. But cuneiform\h\h is the older of the two systems, and while it\h took centuries to develop in Mesopotamia, the\h\h appearance of hieroglyphics at the end of the 4th\h millennium was relatively sudden. Our information\h\h about the early kings and their activities comes\h from the inscriptions carved on the walls of their\h\h tombs and palaces, as there are no other written\h records from this period of Egyptian history.\h Djoser’s step pyramid is the oldest known\h stone structure in Egypt, and it was in the\h\h 26th century, during the Fourth Dynasty, that\h the technique of building pyramids with smooth\h\h sides was first developed. The earliest of these\h was built by King Sneferu, followed by the three\h\h famous ones at Giza, near Cairo, that were built\h by his descendants Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure.\h\h One or more of these kings also built the Great\h Sphinx, but it is most often associated with\h\h Khafre, whose face it seems to resemble. While\h the pyramids were made from limestone and granite\h\h blocks carved at the site and assembled there, the\h Sphinx was a solid piece carved out of a section\h\h of bedrock that roughly resembled its shape. It was also during the Fourth Dynasty that\h\h the country was divided into a number of\h administrative districts that the Greeks later\h\h called nomes, and military expeditions were sent\h to Canaan in the north, to Nubia in the south,\h\h and to Libya in the west. A sea trade for luxury\h goods like precious metals and ivory developed,\h\h for which the Egyptians built their own ships;\h and they imported wood, which was scarce,\h\h from Lebanon on the Mediterranean coast.\h They also traded down the eastern coast\h\h of Africa with the kingdom they called Punt. It was during this period that the government\h\h of Egypt became centered almost entirely on the\h king, and it was often administered by the members\h\h of his family. When they died, they were buried\h close to his own tomb, and the inscriptions on\h\h their graves are one of our main sources for the\h life of the royal court. The country’s lands were\h\h the king’s personal possession, and could\h be temporarily granted to those he favored;\h\h while the people who farmed those lands\h were bound to them like medieval serfs,\h\h and had to pay the government a percentage of\h their crop. The highly developed and centralized\h\h bureaucracy that was typical of ancient Egypt also\h developed around this time, along with the use of\h\h papyrus for keeping administrative records. This\h was a writing material which was readily available\h\h because it was made from the pith of the reeds\h that grew along the Nile. The script used for\h\h these records, which first appeared around 3000\h BCE, was a cursive form of hieroglyphics adapted\h\h for writing with black or red ink. It was known\h as Hieratic, which essentially means “priestly”,\h\h because it was used to write down religious texts. The divine power and authority of the king,\h\h which first appeared in the Old Kingdom, was\h specifically identified with the god Horus,\h\h whose symbol, the falcon, was linked with\h a special royal name on inscriptions. This\h\h “Horus-name” was a title, different for each\h king, that was placed within a symbolic rectangle,\h\h above which perched the falcon. The king was\h also called a “son of Ra”, the god of the sun;\h\h and several of the kings of the Fifth Dynasty\h built temples for the worship of Ra. While the\h\h king was considered both a god and human, it was\h the case that only he could appeal directly to the\h\h gods to ensure the well-being of the people,\h which was tied to the flooding of the Nile.\h\h The priests who served the cults of these gods\h were also crucial in ensuring their benevolence,\h\h which they did by bringing offerings, burning\h incense, and praying to them; though they did\h\h not minister directly to the people themselves. Our knowledge of the religious ideas of this\h\h period comes from the “pyramid texts” that were\h carved on the inner walls of the tombs built in\h\h the town of Saqqara, near Memphis, starting in\h the Fifth Dynasty. They are called funerary texts\h\h because their contents, which described what\h would happen to the deceased after they died,\h\h were intended to accompany them, like the\h objects placed in the tomb, into the afterlife.\h From the Fifth Dynasty, we find more and more of\h the tombs of governors of nomes who were unrelated\h\h to the king, and were buried in an increasingly\h impressive style. This trend continued into the\h\h Sixth Dynasty of the 24th to 22nd centuries BCE,\h as these governors became independent of Memphis\h\h to the point that they started their own local\h dynasties, and took over the duties of the other\h\h local officials who had been appointed by the\h king. The king’s power during the fifth and sixth\h\h dynasties also suffered from the fact that a great\h deal of the government’s wealth had been spent on\h\h building pyramids, while the priesthood of Ra,\h which was exempt from taxation and therefore\h\h able to amass a lot of wealth, was growing\h in influence. Then, starting around 2200 BCE,\h\h the entire region suffered years of drought,\h which was particularly hard on Egypt because\h\h it was so dependent on the annual flooding\h of the Nile to irrigate its farmlands.\h A series of civil wars began after the\h death of Pepi II in 2184 BCE. This period,\h\h in contrast with the nearly century-long reign\h of Pepi, saw 18 kings in the span of twenty\h\h years. After the end of the Eighth Dynasty in\h the mid-22nd century, power was split between\h\h two families that founded dynasties in the city\h of Waset or Thebes, on the Nile in central Egypt,\h\h and in Heracleopolis, which was between Thebes and\h Memphis, as well as other smaller dynasties.

With the Old Kingdom of Egypt covered, it’s\h time to move on the the Middle Kingdom. This\h\h term was invented by Egyptologists to describe\h the 300 or so years that followed the end of the\h\h First Intermediate Period, around 2040 BCE. During the Eleventh Dynasty, trade was resumed\h\h with Punt and the Red Sea region, and new\h defenses were built; while during the Twelfth,\h\h a standing army was formed for the first time,\h though it was mainly defensive and was not sent\h\h out to acquire territory as the armies of the\h New Kingdom later were. The Middle Kingdom also\h\h developed a new system of administration which\h was led by the vizier, a later Arabic term that\h\h is often used to describe a king’s chief official.\h There were sometimes two viziers at the same time,\h\h one in charge of the north and the other the\h south; and there was a period of nearly a\h\h century when the office was held by members of\h the same family. There was also an Overseer of\h\h Sealed Goods, who was in charge of the treasury,\h and a Scribe of the Royal Document; and as with\h\h many governments centered around monarchies, the\h king’s personal servants came to take on much\h\h broader administrative responsibilities. There\h are also references to a head of the military,\h\h and to a number of other officials whose\h titles first appear in this period. We know\h\h about them because the individuals who served\h the king were, like him, buried in impressive,\h\h though not nearly as impressive, tombs with stone\h markers that illustrated what they looked like,\h\h and described their family and their career. The transition from the Eleventh to the Twelfth\h\h Dynasty, around 1990 BCE, may have been the\h result of a battle between King Mentuhotep\h\h IV and his vizier Amenemhat, since the latter,\h who was not a member of the royal family, became\h\h king in his place, and possibly while he was\h still alive.

The first era of Indian civilization that\h left a written record behind is known as\h\h “Vedic” because it produced the Vedas, which is\h Sanskrit for “knowledge”. These are the earliest\h\h works composed in Sanskrit and the oldest texts of\h Hinduism. The four Vedas, the oldest of which is\h\h the Rig Veda, were written between 1300 and\h 900 BCE, and they combine religious hymns,\h\h instructions for carrying out various ceremonies,\h and philosophical discussions called Upanishads.\h\h The concept of a “Vedic period” refers both\h to the time when these works were written,\h\h and to the culture and society they describe,\h which is sometimes defined more broadly as having\h\h flourished between 1500 and 500 BCE. This makes\h “Vedic” a modern concept that defines a historical\h\h period in terms of a work of literature rather\h than a series of events, in the same way that\h\h scholars will talk about a “Homeric period” or a\h “Biblical period”; though in all three of these\h\h cases, our literary sources have been augmented\h by archeological evidence from the same period.\h\h The Vedas were recited, and eventually written\h down, in an older form of Sanskrit, which once\h\h again was an Indo-Aryan language. This means that\h the ancestors of the people who produced them\h\h had originally lived in Eurasia, near the Ural\h Mountains, but migrated around 2000 BCE to Iran,\h\h and from there to northwest India. The same\h people were also the ancestors of the Hittites,\h\h who worshipped gods mentioned in the Vedas such as\h Indra, Varuna, and Mitra. People in both northern\h\h India and Iran referred to themselves as Aryas\h or Aryans, and they distinguished themselves from\h\h the other cultures in the region. In fact, the\h Rig Veda mentions conflicts between the Aryas,\h\h the people for whom it was written, and\h others called dasas whom it describes as\h\h uncivilized and disrespectful toward the\h Aryan gods. During the early Vedic period,\h\h the Aryas were organized into nomadic tribes or\h clans that herded animals and rode horses. They\h\h spent a lot of their time in battle, and the Rig\h Veda describes the wars that were fought between\h\h them, such as the Battle of Ten Kings, which\h ended in the foundation of the Kuru kingdom.\h The clans were led by chiefs called rajan, and by\h popular assemblies called samitis or sabhas. It\h\h has been suggested, based on a few statements\h in the Vedas, that these chiefs were actually\h\h chosen by the assemblies, as opposed to later\h Vedic kings who had a hereditary right to rule;\h\h but as most of the Vedas’ descriptions\h of these assemblies refer to the gods,\h\h it’s not clear how we should apply them to actual\h institutions, if at all. In fact, in the Vedas it\h\h is the gods who are described as kings, and\h who lend their authority to human leaders.\h The later Vedic period is distinguished from the\h earlier one in two ways. First, the sources that\h\h describe it are drawn not from the Rig Veda\h but from the three Vedas that followed it,\h\h and from the Upanishads. And second, during\h this period, which began around 1000 BCE,\h\h Indians started using iron tools, they cleared\h land near the Ganges River that had been forest,\h\h they settled into permanent communities, and\h became farmers. It was also at this time that\h\h the members of rural clans, who continued to live\h as they had in the past, began to be incorporated\h\h into the social structure of the cities. They also developed a system of social\h\h organization that would later form the basis\h of the caste system. Differences in social\h\h status also played a part in the law, in that\h the severity of punishments depended on the rank\h\h of the person who had been killed or injured. The Kuru kingdom, which was the first to form\h\h from the independent tribes of the Ganges region,\h was founded circa 1200 BCE by Parikshit and his\h\h successor Janamejaya.

During the history of ancient India, there were\h two states that are commonly described as empires\h\h because they acquired, through treaty or conquest,\h almost all of the subcontinent, and governed it\h\h through elaborate systems of administration and\h control. These states were the Mauryan empire\h\h of the 4th through 2nd centuries BCE, which we\h discussed in the previous tutorial, and the Gupta\h\h empire of the 3rd through 6th centuries CE. But\h these were the exception rather than the rule,\h\h and in the years between and after them, India\h generally consisted of many small states,\h\h which were ruled by family dynasties or tribal\h leaders. In fact, it was only in the 16th century\h\h that the country was once again unified, when\h it came under the rule of the Mughal empire.\h The last king of the Maurya family, Brihadratha,\h was assassinated in 185 BCE by a general named\h\h Pushyamitra Shunga, who founded his own dynasty,\h which is now known as the Shunga Empire. Though\h\h this state continued to be ruled from the\h city of Patna, it occupied only the central\h\h and eastern parts of Mauryan territory, as the\h western part was invaded around the same time by\h\h the Bactrian kingdom that had split off from the\h Greek Seleucid empire around 250 BCE. Until 180,\h\h the Bactrians had occupied what is now\h Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, and Tajikistan;\h\h but their expansion into Indian territory\h resulted in the creation of an Indo-Greek kingdom\h\h in Central Asia and the northwest part of the\h subcontinent which lasted for the next 200 years,\h\h and fought at times with the Shungas and with\h Kalinga, which was once again an independent\h\h state. Sources like the Mahabharata do not specify\h when or how these conflicts may have occurred;\h\h though our classical sources suggest that an\h Indo-Greek force may have tried to conquer the\h\h capital of Patna. We do have a copy of a treaty\h from around 110 BCE that established diplomatic\h\h relations between the two states. Though the\h Indo-Greeks were ethnically and culturally a\h\h mixture of Greek and Indian, our Indian sources\h call them Yavanas after the name “Ionian”,\h\h which referred to the Greeks of Asia Minor. According to our Buddhist sources, Pushyamitra\h\h was a Brahman follower of Vedic traditions who\h was opposed to the Mauryan support of Buddhism.\h\h He was said to have burned down monasteries and\h torn down stupas, the mounds of earth built as\h\h places of meditation. He also turned many Buddhist\h temples into Hindu shrines. There are reports that\h\h Pushyamitra had millions of Buddhists executed,\h though this may reflect the tendency of our\h\h pro-Buddhist sources to demonize him. In either\h case, some Buddhist sites were improved later\h\h in the 2nd century, which could reflect either a\h change in policy under Pushyamitra’s successors,\h\h or the fact that more and more of the empire’s\h territories were breaking away from it. The\h\h Shungas were known for their patronage of\h scholarship and the arts. It was during\h\h this time that the Bhagavad Gita, the dialogue\h between the god Krishna and the prince Arjuna,\h\h was composed, which became the centerpiece of\h the Mahabharata. Another work from this time\h\h was the Mahabhasya, an important work\h about the rules of Sanskrit grammar.\h Pushyamitra ruled for about 35 years,\h and was followed by his son Agnimitra,\h\h who ruled for eight years. Judging by the\h inscriptions from the period after his death,\h\h and from the coins minted by new dynasties, it\h was then that the empire’s possessions began to\h\h secede and create their own independent states.\h The last Shunga king, Devabhuti, was killed in 75\h\h BCE by one of his ministers, Vasudeva Kanva,\h who established his own dynasty. He and his\h\h family ruled more or less the same area as the\h Shungas, for a total of 45 years. At that point\h\h they were displaced by the Andhra dynasty, which\h had already been in control of the Deccan Plateau\h\h in southwestern India; though the Magadhan state\h itself, where the capital of Patna was located,\h\h came under the rule of the Mitra dynasty\h which may have been related to the Shungas.\h The Andhra dynasty continued to rule the southern\h and western parts of the subcontinent until the\h\h 3rd century CE, while northern India and Central\h Asia came under the control of the Kushan Empire.\h\h This was a state that originated in\h Central Asia as the territory of an\h\h Indo-European people that Chinese historical\h accounts, which are our main source for them,\h\h call the Yuezhi. These were a group of tribes that\h moved into the Bactrian territory of the Yavanas\h\h in the later 2nd century BCE. They became\h united under a king in the 1st century BCE,\h\h and from there they took control first of\h Gandhara, which was on the border between the\h\h Greek and Asian worlds, and then all of northern\h India. This gave them a territory that bordered,\h\h in the 2nd century CE, Persia to the west, China\h to the north, and the Bay of Bengal to the east,\h\h making the Kushans a point of contact between\h the economic spheres of the Roman Empire,\h\h China, and India, and a participant in the\h growing trade between them. Their leaders\h\h portrayed themselves as Hellenistic kings on\h the model of the Yavanas they had supplanted,\h\h and like them they used the Greek alphabet\h and minted coins with their images. They\h\h also adopted a wide array of Asian cultural\h elements. They were adherents of Buddhism,\h\h which they helped spread to China via trade along\h the Silk Road, and by building monasteries along\h\h the way. One of their kings, Kanishka, held a\h Buddhist council, and he came to be considered\h\h a great patron of the faith. The Kushans were also\h interested in the dualist faith of Zoroastrianism,\h\h judging by the fact that they featured\h the good god Ahura Mazda on their coinage.\h In the early 3rd century CE, the Kushan\h Empire split into eastern and western halves,\h\h and within a few years the Sasanian rulers of\h Persia had conquered the western half and replaced\h\h its kings with a dynasty loyal to them. At the\h same time, the eastern half was disintegrating\h\h into a group of small kingdoms, which were\h conquered in the middle of the 4th century\h\h CE by Samudragupta, the second king of the Gupta\h dynasty which either displaced the Kushans,\h\h or took advantage of their disappearance. The Guptas were a family from the north of\h\h the country, whose kingdom had been small until\h their third king, Chandragupta, married a princess\h\h of the Licchavi state around 325 CE, and began\h to call himself “King of great kings” rather\h\h than simply maharaja or “great king”. The titles,\h conquests, and activities of the Guptas are based\h\h primarily on their honorific inscriptions and the\h gold coins they issued. The inscriptions tell us\h\h about their military victories, while the coins\h show us how they wanted to portray themselves,\h\h and the language and imagery they preferred to\h use. The places where the coins of particular\h\h rulers have been found in large numbers also\h give us some idea about the borders of the\h\h many kingdoms, small and large, that dotted\h the subcontinent in the first centuries CE.\h Around 350 CE, Chandragupta’s son Samudragupta\h used his forces to expand the boundaries of the\h\h empire first into northern and then central\h India. The Gupta army was different from\h\h previous Indian armies in that it had heavy\h armed cavalry soldiers and archers, as well\h\h as archers on horseback of the sort the Persians\h had. It also had chariots and a fleet of ships.\h Samudragupta also made the kings and tribal chiefs\h of the areas he did not conquer into his vassals,\h\h according to the inscriptions that celebrate his\h accomplishments; and he performed the Ashvamedha\h\h ceremony in which a horse was allowed to wander\h free for a year and everywhere it went was claimed\h\h as part of the king’s domains. Samudragupta’s\h son, who was also named Chandragupta,\h\h continued his father’s campaigns in the years\h between 375 and 415, expanding the empire’s\h\h borders from Pakistan in the west to Bengal\h in the east and the Arabian Sea in the south.\h We know from the inscriptions of the time that\h the Gupta empire was divided into 26 provinces,\h\h which were themselves divided into districts\h governed by a chief official together with a\h\h council of four. Though the men in charge of\h the provinces were members of the royal family,\h\h the administration of towns and villages was\h left to local officials who were generally\h\h free of interference from the capital. We also have some information about\h\h Gupta administration from the account of a\h Chinese Buddhist named Faxian who visited\h\h India at the beginning of the 6th century.\h He describes a safe and secure environment\h\h in which people traveled freely and were not\h oppressed by the authorities. Lawbreakers were\h\h penalized with fines rather than rhe corporal\h punishments sometimes used by the Mauryans.\h The system of taxation was also less burdensome\h than it had been in the past. Only people who\h\h farmed state land were required to give a part\h of their crop, and craftsmen were taxed less than\h\h they had been before. Revenue from farmland\h was the Gupta state’s main source of income;\h\h and in principle, it owned all of the empire’s\h farmlands, though it rented them out under a\h\h variety of arrangements which gave the farmer\h total or partial rights over the property and its\h\h income. It also increased the stock of available\h farmland by enhancing the country’s irrigation\h\h system, and farmers who reclaimed barren land were\h allowed to keep it without having to pay any rent.\h Though Chandragupta’s coins portray him as a\h worshipper of the traditional Hindu deity Vishnu,\h\h he tolerated Buddhists and Jains as well. In\h fact, both of these groups became famous for the\h\h religious sculptures and paintings they created\h during this period. The Buddhist art of the time,\h\h which had been influenced by the Greek visual\h arts in the Indo-Greek region of Gandharal,\h\h went on to shape the art of\h central, east, and southeast Asia.\h Chandragupta’s reign, and the Gupta period in\h general, are considered a time when literature,\h\h scholarship, and the arts reached a peak that\h has influenced Indian culture ever since,\h\h and its painting, sculpture, and architecture were\h important for the development of artistic styles\h\h in places like Burma, Malaysia, and Thailand,\h which as the Roman Empire was coming to an end,\h\h were becoming important markets for\h fine Indian crafts like textiles of\h\h various kinds, leather, ivory, and metalwork. Chandragupta’s court hosted a group of men\h\h known as the Navaratna or “nine jewels”. They were\h mathematicians, scientists, and poets. Among them\h\h was Aryabhata, a mathematician and astronomer who\h wrote about subjects like algebra, trigonometry,\h\h and quadratic equations. He also designed a system\h of notation that used the syllabic characters of\h\h Sanskrit to represent even very large numbers\h by substituting consonants for the numbers 1\h\h through 25 and vowels for powers of ten, so that\h each syllable would stand for a single digit\h\h and indicate which column it belonged in.\h Another of the jewels was the poet and\h\h playwright Kalidasa, who like the playwrights\h of classical Greece, based his work on ancient\h\h myths like the Vedas and the Mahabharata. Chandragupta’s son Kumaragupta ruled from\h\h 415 to 455. It was during his reign that the\h empire began to be threatened by the invasions\h\h of tribes of Huns from Central Asia, whom he\h was able to defeat. He also founded a Buddhist\h\h university in the city of Nalanda, which was a\h center of learning for 700 years. In the decades\h\h after his death there were more attacks, and the\h western part of the empire was lost after a large\h\h battle in 510 CE. During the 60 years that they\h occupied this region, the Huns destroyed many of\h\h the Guptas’ institutions, and the region's\h cities declined in importance. They also\h\h attacked the country’s Buddhist infrastructure. At around the same time, the Gupta empire’s\h\h eastern half was reduced to a group of small\h independent states, each with its own dynasty;\h\h but since the Huns were never able to overrun the\h east, it’s not clear what caused Gupta authority\h\h there to shrink until it was limited to its\h heartland in Magadha. It’s been suggested that\h\h the Huns cut off the Guptas’ access to trade with\h the West, which severely reduced their income;\h\h and that they were faced with competition\h from other local dynasties. In any event,\h\h this period has come to be seen as India’s\h “golden age”, just as classical Athens and\h\h Republican Rome were seen in Europe as the height\h of political power and cultural achievement.

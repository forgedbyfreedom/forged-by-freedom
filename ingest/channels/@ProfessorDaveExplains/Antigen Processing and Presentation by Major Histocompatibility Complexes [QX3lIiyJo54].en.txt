Adaptive immune responses rely on antigen\h processing and presentation pathways for\h\h their effector functions. These pathways\h ensure that the glycoproteins encoded by\h\h the major histocompatibility complex, MHC,\h are loaded with their appropriate ligands.\h\h Only such MHC molecules expressed on the surface\h of antigen-presenting cells can activate T cells,\h\h which can then execute effector functions, such\h as cytotoxicity, assisting B cells, and inducing\h\h cytokine production. These molecules allow the\h adaptive immune system to survey the host proteome\h\h and its modifications. Any undesirable\h pathogens, such as viruses and bacteria,\h\h or mutations that contribute to malignancy,\h can be detected. Thus, defining the concept\h\h of tolerance and “self” plays a key role in not\h only thymic T cell development and selection,\h\h but also in homeostasis. The structure of MHC\h molecules determines which antigens are presented.\h\h The entire process of antigen processing and\h presentation can be divided into six distinct\h\h steps, for both MHC class I and II molecules.\h First the antigens are acquired. Second,\h\h antigens are tagged for proteolysis into peptides.\h Third, proteolysis. Fourth, delivery of antigen\h\h to MHC molecules. Fifth, antigen loading. And\h sixth, display of the antigens on cell surfaces.\h MHC class I molecules are heterodimers of a heavy\h chain type I integral membrane glycoprotein,\h\h and β2-microglobulin or β2M soluble protein.\h These heterodimers are arranged in a specific\h\h pattern. The extracellular regions of the heavy\h chains fold into three domains (α1, α2, and α3);\h\h whereas β2M contributes as the fourth domain.\h The α1 and α2 domains are crucial as they form\h\h the main peptide-binding site. This groove\h is located on the upper surface of the MHC\h\h class I molecule and is essential for binding\h to various antigenic peptides that are 8 to 10\h\h amino acids in length. It was thought that the\h majority of peptides presented by MHC class I\h\h molecules are discarded old proteins. However,\h most of the peptides presented by MHC class I\h\h molecules are derived from defective ribosomal\h translation products. These proteins are degraded\h\h by the multisubunit proteasome complex in the\h cytosol. This process is central to the MHC\h\h class I function as it allows the MHC class I\h pathway to sample and test a diverse array of\h\h proteins as soon as they are synthesized. This\h allows the CD8+ T cells to immediately detect\h\h the presence of a virus in an infected cell. A multitude of proteins are processed by the\h\h proteasome in the cytosol. These proteasomes\h generate peptides which are then translocated into\h\h the endoplasmic reticulum lumen by transporter\h associated with antigen processing, or TAP,\h\h protein. Next, MHC class I molecules, heavy chain\h and associated β2M, fold and assemble in the ER\h\h lumen. This process requires the assistance\h of ER chaperones. The MHC class I molecule,\h\h chaperone complex, associates with TAP,\h abridging the molecules, then facilitates\h\h peptide binding. Loaded MHC class I molecules\h then dissociate from TAP, selectively recruited\h\h into cargo vesicles for transport to the Golgi\h apparatus, and are finally transported through\h\h the secretory pathway to the plasma membrane. MHC-II molecules bind peptides that are slightly\h\h larger than the average of those peptides bound\h by MHC-I molecules. They are generally 10 to 15\h\h amino acid residues long and are known to interact\h with bigger peptides as well. Unlike class I MHC,\h\h which is expressed in all nucleated cells,\h class II MHC is exclusively expressed in\h\h professional APCs, such as macrophages,\h dendritic cells, and B cells, and can also\h\h be found in the thymic epithelial cells. Structurally, class II MHC is formed by\h\h two heavy chains that are divided into two α\h domains, α1 and α2, and two β domains, β1 and\h\h β2. The 3-D binding grooves are similar to those\h observed in the class I MHC, but are more open,\h\h thus allowing the binding and anchoring of\h larger peptides. Class II MHC mainly presents\h\h extracellular antigens that are processed in\h endosomes. Next, vesicles with external cargo are\h\h processed by acid proteases present in the early\h endosomes. This pre-processed content in the early\h\h endosomes is transferred to the late endosome. MHC class II molecules associate with the\h\h invariant chain Ii in the endoplasmic\h reticulum and travel to the cell surface\h\h via the Golgi apparatus. From there, the complex\h is delivered to endosomal compartments or reaches\h\h the cell surface, where it may then undergo\h internalization and delivery to antigen-processing\h\h compartments. With a better understanding\h of antigen processing and presentation,\h\h we are now ready to move forward and learn\h about the resulting T cell activity next.

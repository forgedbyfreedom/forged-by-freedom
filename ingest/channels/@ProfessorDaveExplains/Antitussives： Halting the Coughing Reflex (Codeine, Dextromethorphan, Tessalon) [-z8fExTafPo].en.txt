Over the past few tutorials we have been\h discussing respiratory pharmacology,\h\h so let’s continue by examining antitussives, which\h are drugs that halt the coughing reflex. Tussis,\h\h or coughing, is a complicated physiological\h reflex that can be somewhat simplified by breaking\h\h it down into three connected neurological\h pathways. These are the afferent pathway,\h\h the central pathway, and the efferent pathway. The afferent pathway is triggered by cough\h\h receptors, also known as rapidly adapting irritant\h receptors, located in the trachea and pharynx.\h\h These receptors are not well-understood but what\h is known is that they respond to both mechanical\h\h stretching and chemical irritants. Following\h stimulation, an increased frequency of action\h\h potentials are conducted via the laryngeal\h nerves toward the vagus nerve, then arriving\h\h in the medulla of the brain. Unlike the clearly\h defined region of the medulla responsible for the\h\h mediation of swallowing, we currently aren’t aware\h of a clearly defined cough center in the medulla.\h\h Following processing in the medulla, the cough\h reflex enters the efferent pathway via the vagus\h\h nerve where impulses innervate the diaphragm and\h external intercostal muscles in between the ribs,\h\h causing them to contract and establishing\h negative pressure around the lungs.\h\h Due to this newly established pressure\h gradient, air rushes into the lungs via\h\h rapid inhalation to ablate the negative pressure\h which closes the glottis. Expiratory and abdominal\h\h muscles contract, which increases the pressure\h inside the lungs. Due to the increased pressure,\h\h the glottis opens and air is rapidly released\h in the form of a cough. With this process,\h\h the physiological aim is to remove irritants\h in the lining of the respiratory system.\h Causes of cough that may be treated by antitussive\h drugs are most commonly respiratory infections,\h\h followed by chronic obstructive pulmonary\h disease and lung cancer. It is worth noting that\h\h antitussive drugs do not treat the cause of the\h cough, they only offer symptomatic relief for the\h\h cough, and may be used in combination with other\h drugs that treat the root cause where appropriate. Now that we understand the basics\h regarding the mechanism behind coughing,\h\h and the situations where antitussive drugs may\h be used, we can discuss mechanisms of action\h\h behind antitussive drugs. The most commonly used\h antitussives are codeine, a mu opioid receptor\h\h agonist, dextromethorphan, a drug with a\h very promiscuous pharmacodynamic profile,\h\h and benzonatate, better known by its brand\h name, Tessalon. This drug’s mechanism of\h\h action is through its ability to induce\h blockage of voltage gated sodium channels. Because of the ambiguity of the cough reflex\h in general, it is difficult to be definitive\h\h on the mechanism of action of antitussives, but\h we will discuss some mechanisms and pharmacology\h\h which are most likely to be contributing to\h efficacy. Let’s first discuss codeine. Codeine\h\h is a prodrug that requires hepatic metabolism via\h UDP-Glucuronosyltransferase-2B7 and cytochrome\h\h P450 enzyme CYP2D6, to form the major active\h metabolites codeine-6-glucuronide and morphine.\h\h Following metabolism, codeine-6-glucuronide and\h morphine mediate their effects through agonism\h\h of the mu opioid receptor, or μOPR. The mu opioid\h receptor is a G protein coupled receptor which\h\h signals through Gαi. The receptor is widely\h expressed in the body, but especially present\h\h on neurons in the GI tract. Here it has a\h wide range of effects including euphoria,\h analgesia, constipation, and respiratory\h suppression, but most relevant to this\h\h tutorial is cough suppression. The other\h pharmacodynamic effects we just mentioned\h\h will be expanded upon in future tutorials,\h namely those about drug abuse and analgesia. Cough suppression is likely achieved\h through hyperpolarization of neurons\h\h through activation of potassium ion channels, via\h the βγ G protein subunit following dissociation\h\h from the Gαi/βγ heterotrimer following receptor\h activation. This causes an efflux of positively\h\h charged potassium ions. Hyperpolarization of the\h pre-synaptic neurons in the cough reflex reduces\h\h neurotransmission and inhibits action potential\h generation in this reflex arc, likely both in the\h\h peripheral and central nervous system in the\h medulla. To add to the antitussive mechanisms\h\h mediated through the mu opioid receptor, there is\h likely a role for its coupling to Gαi, and that is\h\h inhibition of adenylate cyclase and subsequent\h reduction of intracellular cAMP concentration,\h\h but little is understood about the relative\h contributions of cellular signalling and\h\h antitussive phenotypes. It’s also worth noting\h that due to the high adverse effect profile of\h\h codeine, including respiratory suppression and\h addiction liability, prescriptions to treat cough\h\h are rare, and in general are saved for the most\h extreme cases where the pharmacological benefits\h\h of cough suppression outweigh the risks the side\h effects present. There is a lot more to be said\h\h about codeine and other opioids, but this is\h outside the scope of this tutorial. Opioids\h\h will be discussed at great length in future\h tutorials regarding analgesia and drug abuse. Dextromethorphan, or DXM, is an over-the-counter\h antitussive that has a very promiscuous\h\h pharmacological profile. Due to this profile, it\h is difficult to anticipate what the mechanism of\h\h action for the drug is, with regard to cough\h suppression, and it is not known what the\h\h main mechanism of cough suppression is. The\h mechanism is likely related to sigma opioid\h\h receptor agonism, which has the same canonical\h signaling as codeine since the σOPr is also a Gαi\h\h coupled GPCR. However, its mechanism may also\h be related to its N-Methyl-D-Aspartate, or NMDA,\h\h receptor antagonism. NMDA is a ligand-gated cation\h channel that responds endogenously to glutamate.\h\h When DXM is present it inhibits glutamate binding,\h hence reducing the neuronal depolarization that\h\h an influx of cations would cause, those being\h sodium ions and to a lesser extent calcium ions.\h\h This overall inhibition of neuronal\h transmission in the cough centers\h\h could contribute to the antitussive\h effect seen with DXM. It is worth\h\h mentioning at this stage that like codeine,\h DXM has abuse potential as a dissociative,\h\h hallucinogen, and sedative, due to its NMDA\h receptor antagonism. Other NMDA receptor\h\h antagonists will be discussed in future tutorials\h concerning analgesia and antidepressants. Finally, we will briefly discuss benzonatate,\h better known as Tessalon. Unlike DXM and codeine,\h\h benzonatate is a non-narcotic antitussive\h drug. The mechanism of action for benzonatate\h\h is mediated through its blockage\h of voltage gated sodium channels.\h\h Voltage gated sodium channels are important\h in propagating action potentials down neurons,\h\h as we recall from the biopsychology tutorial\h on neural conduction and synaptic transmission.\h\h Following oral admission and\h distribution of benzonatate,\h\h the molecule blocks voltage gated sodium channels\h in the bronchi and alveoli of the airways\h\h reducing action potential frequency output\h from the afferent cough pathway via the vagal\h\h fibers. Benzonatate is also able to have some\h impact on voltage gated sodium channels at the\h\h level of the medulla, which is thought to impact\h central processing of the cough reflex response. To summarize, we have just explored\h compounds that inhibit the cough reflex.\h\h Although the mechanism of the cough\h reflex as well as the mechanism of\h\h action of some of these drugs can be\h unclear, their effects are reproducible\h\h and highlight an area of active mechanistic\h and neuro-physiological research.

Continuing with our earlier analysis of\h the Classical Greek period, Aristotle,\h\h who lived in the 4th century BCE, continued\h the direct line of tutelage from Socrates\h\h and Plato. By most accounts, he was the first\h “scientist” as we understand the term today.\h\h Biology, astronomy, geology, and many\h other fields – if he did not invent it,\h\h he established the major foundations for it to\h thrive. Much like his predecessors, Aristotle did\h\h not have a clear separation between philosophy,\h science, politics or any branch of knowledge:\h\h everything was interwoven. His method of research,\h however, was much more focused on the senses and\h\h observation. He was “empirical”, from the Greek\h empeirikos meaning practice and experience,\h\h in contrast with other abstract thought. He would\h observe animals and plants in order to analyze\h\h them, and attempt to understand their behavior\h and characteristics through this observation.\h\h Afterwards, all explanations must be adequate\h and follow a rigorous format in order to maintain\h\h objectivity. He was very interested in language,\h with extensive writings in rhetoric, the art of\h\h guiding public emotions through words intended\h to convince others. Dialectics, its counterpart,\h\h is a more private and academic manner of debating\h based on developing an argument with premises that\h\h are based on the concessions of another. And\h finally, logic, which is a tool for analyzing\h\h discourse in a thorough and quasi-mathematical\h manner, where a conclusion must, by necessity,\h\h be a direct result of its premises. As we already\h saw, this was an essential tool for discourse\h\h clarification in every science, and this was the\h first time it was established explicitly. Logic\h\h allowed us to see beyond any flair or trick of\h language to understand the core of what is being\h\h said. We’ll delve further in this creation of his\h in the next tutorial, but it was undeniably a game\h\h changer for any future scientific endeavor\h – a system so reliable that it went without\h\h major overhaul until the early 20th century. However, being such an early scientist also\h\h meant treading through uncharted territory, so\h Aristotle held many false beliefs. He believed\h\h life could spring from nothing, due to observing\h eels lacking gonads simply appearing in mud,\h\h unaware that they’d only develop them in a journey\h very far from there. Still in the realm of gonads,\h\h he theorized that snakes do not have two legs\h because they do not have penises – twice wrong\h\h on a single affirmation, as they do have penises,\h and the attributes are clearly unrelated. He also\h\h believed women had fewer teeth, colder blood,\h and that enslavement could be natural to some\h\h people lacking rational skills, among other\h bad ideas. But we can see a pattern here:\h\h Aristotle was very concerned with the purpose\h of things. He posited the existence of a single\h\h primary substance from which everything arose,\h taking different Forms-with-a-capital-F. But\h\h unlike Plato we can properly understand them\h with experience – a direct criticism of Plato,\h\h as according to Aristotle, Plato’s theory of Forms\h could not account for creation and modification.\h\h His physics postulate that forms cannot change\h without interference, even if non-intentional.\h\h Thus, everything has a purpose, or a “Telos”\h in Greek, as the heart beats for pumping blood,\h\h the lungs function for breathing air, and so\h on. This\h\h biological perspective connected to a purpose,\h which we can call a “teleological” perspective,\h\h would dominate biology until Darwin, who would be\h the first to develop a system for understanding\h\h how physical characteristics rise from adaptation\h and selection rather than proper function.\h Humans, on the other hand, are social\h animals with a natural desire for knowledge,\h\h according to Aristotle. They thrive on several\h levels of communities, from their close family\h\h to the entire city they live in, and they do\h so by communicating. Thus, the function of\h\h humans is rational discourse, or reason. To\h act in a good manner, then, is to rationally\h\h analyze how a certain action can be good or not.\h Following his criticism of the platonic forms,\h\h what is “good” is too ever-changing, and related\h to every individual context it appears in.\h For Aristotle, prudence is the virtue that should\h lead one’s moral life: to deliberate prudently is\h\h to better judge which action is appropriate in\h given situation. For example, if you saw someone\h\h attempting to kill another person, what would you\h do? For Aristotle, vice, the opposite of virtue,\h\h would be characterized by the extremes, either\h a lack or excess of virtue. In this example,\h\h running away with no intention to look back is\h a lack of courage, which we can call cowardice,\h\h while trying to overpower or kill the attacker\h would be an excess of courage, or rashness. To\h\h act virtuously would be to inform your courage\h with prudence. You could attempt to notify the\h\h authorities, or perhaps disarm or immobilize the\h attacker. This could be viewed as acting with the\h\h right balance of courage and prudence. Yet, the\h correct path would never be a single one, and also\h\h never the same, because the virtuous act depends\h heavily on the context. There is ambiguity halfway\h\h between actions, as running away to survive or to\h attempt to notify authorities are both closer to\h\h simply abandoning the victim than attempting\h to overpower a dangerous person. There’s no\h\h easy path to virtuous life, but habit is the key:\h the exercise of rational prudence then, without\h\h neglecting your emotions, would lead to virtuous\h actions that help the agent reach its telos,\h\h or its goal as a person – which is to flourish\h as a human being and achieve true happiness. This\h\h brand of virtue ethics, focusing on improving\h human action rather than identifying objective,\h\h immutable principles of what is “good” or “bad”,\h is still very strong in current philosophical\h\h perspectives, surviving many iterations and\h different interpretations, to be one of the\h\h three major ethical schools still being studied. Adapting Plato both in method and in form,\h\h Aristotle then constructed his political theory\h based on these previous conceptions of humans\h\h and their actions. The best governments and\h political systems would be the ones which\h\h provide the best environment for happiness and\h flourishing.

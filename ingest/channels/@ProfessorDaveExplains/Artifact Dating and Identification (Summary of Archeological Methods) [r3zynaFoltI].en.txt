Archeological methodology refers to the\h methods archeologists use to perform\h\h scientific investigations of material remains.\h Artifact dating and identification obtained by\h\h survey and excavation is the fundamental\h framework of methodology we will discuss.\h\h In this tutorial we will discuss several\h dating methods used by archeologists.\h Dating artifacts and features is critical\h in discerning human past. Dating is used\h\h at archeological sites to understand a site's\h chronology. Chronology is the order of events at\h\h a site that can be inferred by material remains\h found there. A basic chronology of a site might\h\h look something like this: Stone tools are made,\h a temple is constructed, a village is built,\h\h the village is burnt, the site is abandoned.\h Being able to date artifacts and features\h\h lets archeologists place their construction\h before or after other events. In this example,\h\h through archeological dating we know that a\h village was built around an existing temple,\h\h rather than the temple being built in an existing\h village. This might help us build a hypothesis\h\h about the people who lived there. Dating methods\h used to know the order of events at a site are\h\h known as relative dating. Relative dating methods\h will be able to tell you in what order events took\h\h place at a site, but not exact dates for\h those events. This is still very helpful,\h\h as the chronology for the site wouldn’t change\h if the site was built 10,000 years ago or 500\h\h years ago. However, a site's chronology can’t\h be compared to another site without actual\h\h dates. Dating methods utilized to find actual\h dates are called absolute dating. Different\h\h calendar systems are used throughout the world,\h so scientists need to agree on a reference date\h\h to set absolute dates. Therefore, archeologists\h often use BP to discuss absolute dates. This means\h\h “years before present”. Specifically, “present”\h is set to 1950, as that is when radiocarbon\h\h dating was first utilized. The year 2500 BCE\h would therefore be 4450 BP. However, if the\h\h dates are in the Common Era, or CE, these dates\h will often be used. We covered some methods of\h\h absolute dating in the geology series, but let's\h look at the ones that are specific to archeology.\h Radiometric dating is one of the most commonly\h used absolute dating methods in geology. It is\h\h also very helpful in dating archeological sites.\h However, there are some major problems that many\h\h radiometric dating methods have when dating\h archeological remains. Suppose we have an\h\h artifact, an obsidian spearpoint. Recall from the\h geology series that obsidian is an igneous rock,\h\h so radiometric dating could be used to estimate an\h age for the obsidian. This is the main problem. We\h\h don’t want to know how old the obsidian is,\h we want to know when it was shaped by human\h\h hands. There is no method of radiometric dating\h to tell us when something was altered by humans,\h\h as opposed to when it was formed. So to use\h radiometric dating, archeologists would need\h\h an element that humans produce which decays in\h such a way that radiometric dating is applicable.\h Fortunately, nature has given us this\h element. Carbon is the element of life,\h\h and humans create and utilize it in a multitude\h of ways. An isotope of carbon, carbon-14,\h\h exhibits radioactive decay that makes it\h applicable for radiometric dating. In the\h\h general chemistry series we go over the details\h of this type of dating and how it works, so let’s\h\h discuss its application. Several artifact types\h have carbon components to them. Unfortunately,\h\h most carbon materials decompose after a few years.\h However, there are ways for carbon to be retained\h\h by an artifact for millennia. The most common\h way is burning. This can include burned food,\h\h animal bones, and most frequently, wood.\h Charcoal created by burning wood has been\h\h produced by humans for hundreds of thousands of\h years, therefore it is the most common artifact\h\h to be utilized by C14 dating. These burned\h materials can survive millions of years,\h\h retaining the half-life cycle of the carbon that\h was created when the organism was born. However,\h\h other ways exist of obtaining preserved carbon\h artifacts. Organic artifacts sustained in\h\h low-humidity environments have a much better\h chance of surviving the passage of time.\h\h This can occur in a variety of ways, such as\h being frozen in ice, trapped in anoxic mud,\h\h or placed in caves or deserts. This is how\h mummies can still have flesh after thousands\h\h of years. However, the artifact, or a piece\h of it, must be destroyed in performing the\h\h C14 analysis. This is why artifacts with little\h cultural value, such as charcoal, are preferred.\h Finally, a simple form of absolute dating in\h archeology is examining artifacts that have\h\h dates already inscribed on them in some way. One\h of the most common forms this takes is through\h\h coins. For example, Roman coins commonly have\h the current emperor’s head engraved into them.\h\h Since we know through historical texts the date\h ranges for the reign of each emperor, we can date\h\h the coin easily. Other forms of absolute dating\h can be even more cut and dry. Many Mayan stelae\h\h have a date inscribed on them indicating when the\h statue was finished. In this case, all we need\h\h to do is find a date on the Mayan calendar that\h corresponds with our current calendar, which is\h\h easy since Mayan people still use their calendar\h today. Other methods of absolute dating do exist,\h\h but they are less frequently utilized and outside\h the scope of this course. One of these dating\h\h methods is dendrochronology, which we learned\h about in the botany series. While absolute dating\h\h methods are useful, all of them require some\h specific condition for the material remains.\h\h Relative dating methods, on the other hand, are\h more broadly applicable and therefore the most\h\h commonly used dating methods.

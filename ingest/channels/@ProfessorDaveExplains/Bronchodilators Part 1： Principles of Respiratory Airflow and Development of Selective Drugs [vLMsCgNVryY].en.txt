Broncho-dilating drugs, or bronchodilators,\h\h are used in instances of increased airway\h resistance, which occurs when there is\h\h luminal narrowing of the bronchus and bronchioles.\h Airway resistance is modelled using a law called\h\h “Poiseuille’s law” published by Jean Léonard\h Marie Poiseuille in 1838. This law accurately\h\h models the flow of a fluid, such as a liquid or\h gas, through a tube. This law is generally used\h\h in physics applications concerning fluid dynamics.\h However, we can also use this law to understand\h\h air flowing through the respiratory system, and\h for later reference, blood flowing through the\h\h cardiovascular system, which will be relevant in\h later tutorials discussing antihypertensive drugs.\h The law states that: Q = ΔPπr4/8ηl, where Q\h is the volume flow rate, ΔP is the pressure\h\h gradient along the tube or pipe, r is the radius\h of the pipe, η is the viscosity of the fluid,\h\h and l is the length of the tube or pipe. The\h general goal of bronchodilators is to decrease\h\h airway resistance and improve the flow of air in\h and out of the respiratory system. This is done by\h\h influencing the ‘r’ variable, the luminal radius\h of the bronchus and bronchioles. From glancing at\h\h the equation, it can be appreciated how much\h the radius of the pipe influences flow rate,\h\h given that the radius is raised to\h the fourth power. In other words,\h\h relatively small increases to the radius of these\h vessels dramatically increase air flow rate, Q.\h Bronchodilation is primarily achieved\h through the relaxation of smooth muscle\h\h cells located in the wall of the bronchus and\h bronchioles. As the smooth muscle cells relax,\h\h the lumen of the airway increases in radius and\h resistance is reduced. The luminal radius can be\h\h increased via bronchodilation pharmacologically\h using three main mechanisms. Those are beta-2\h\h adrenoceptor agonism, muscarinic receptor\h antagonism, and glucocorticoids to reduce\h\h inflammation. This tutorial will focus\h on the first two of these, since the\h\h anti-inflammatory action of glucocorticoids\h was already covered in an earlier tutorial.\h Let’s start with beta-2 adrenoceptor agonists. The\h beta-2 adrenoceptor is part of a general family\h\h of receptors known as adrenoceptors. These\h receptors are widespread, and play multiple\h\h roles in physiology. They are activated by\h endogenous agonists adrenaline and noradrenaline.\h\h Although adrenaline and noradrenaline are\h effective bronchodilators, adrenaline even\h\h being used clinically for anaphylaxis, they are\h unselective across adrenoceptors. Due to this\h\h lack of selectivity, they can activate both types\h and all subtypes of the adrenoceptor family. This\h\h leads to promiscuous pharmacology causing multiple\h side effects as a consequence of activating\h\h the whole family of receptors, rather than one\h specific target, making this an undesirable course\h\h of action. With one specific pharmacological\h target, fewer adverse effects will be prevalent.\h It’s worth bearing in mind that certain receptors\h for adrenaline regulate different biological\h\h processes and are drug targets for different\h purposes.

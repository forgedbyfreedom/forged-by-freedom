CD4+\h T cells express CD4, or cluster of differentiation\h\h 4, receptors, which is a glycoprotein serving as\h a co-receptor for the T-cell receptor (TCR). These\h\h cells play a central role in providing immune\h protection against pathogens. Their functions\h\h include assisting B cells to produce antibodies,\h activating macrophages, as well as recruiting\h\h neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils to sites\h of infection and inflammation. They also produce a\h\h variety of cytokines and chemokines that regulate\h various downstream immune responses, including\h\h immune suppression. Naive CD4+ T cells are\h activated once they interact with the antigen-MHC\h\h complex, after which they differentiate into\h specific subtypes depending mainly on the cytokine\h\h milieu of the existing microenvironment. These\h cells exist in various forms based on function\h\h and cytokine profile, those being: T-helper 1\h or Th1, T-helper 2 or Th2, T-helper 17 or Th17,\h\h regulatory T cells or Tregs, follicular\h helper T cells or Tfh, and T-helper 9 or Th9.\h The differentiation of naive T cells begins with\h the antigenic stimulation that is a result of an\h\h interaction between TCR and CD4. They play a role\h as co-receptors with the antigen-MHC II complex\h\h which is presented by professional antigen\h presenting cells, or APCs. TCR coupled with\h\h CD3 activation consequently induces a network of\h downstream signaling pathways. The CD3 antigen is\h\h a surface structure associated with the\h TCR. These steps result in a cascade of\h\h signal transduction that eventually leads to\h naive T cell proliferation and differentiation\h\h into specific effector cells. The differentiation\h outcome is governed predominantly by the cytokines\h\h present in the microenvironment, and to some\h extent, by the strength of the interaction of\h\h the T cell antigen receptor with antigen. Th1 cells are created when cytokines IL12\h\h and IFN-γ initiate the downstream signaling\h cascade. IL-12 is secreted into the environment\h\h by the APCs after they are activated through\h pattern recognition receptors. Additionally,\h\h various important transcription factors\h are involved in the differentiation of the\h\h Th1 cells. Notably, the master regulator for Th1\h differentiation is the T-box transcription factor,\h\h T-bet. T-bet expression relies on signal\h transducer and activator of transcription 1,\h\h or STAT1, expression, which in turn is\h activated by IFN-γ. IL12-induced STAT4 is\h\h another important transcription factor involved\h in Th1 cell differentiation. Other notable TFs\h\h that participate in Th1 differentiation are\h Runx3, Eomes or eomesodermin, and Hlx. Th1\h\h cells are involved with the elimination of\h intracellular pathogens and are associated\h\h with organ-specific autoimmunity. They mainly\h secrete IFN-γ, lymphotoxin α, or Lfα, and IL-2.\h For Th2 differentiation, the key players\h are cytokines IL-4 and IL-2 and the major\h\h transcription GATA-binding protein 3, GATA3.\h Th2 cells mount immune response to extracellular\h\h parasites, including helminths, and play\h a major role in induction and persistence\h\h of asthma as well as other allergy-related\h diseases. The key effector-cytokines are IL4,\h\h IL5, IL9, IL13, IL10, IL25, and amphiregulin. Th9 cells are relatively newly classified helper\h\h T cells, and since these cells preferentially\h produce IL-9, these cells are termed Th9 cells.\h\h These were initially characterized as a subset of\h Th2 cells; however, now they are classified as a\h\h distinct subset of CD4+ T cells. In comparison\h to other characterized Th cell subsets,\h\h differentiation of Th9 cells is poorly described\h at this time. The main cytokine that influences\h\h the differentiation of Th2 cells to Th9 cells is\h TGF-β. Additionally, IL-4 also directly induces\h\h Th9 differentiation. The expression of PU.1,\h a key transcription factor in Th9 development,\h\h is induced by TGF-β signaling and is expressed\h at higher levels in Th9 cells compared to\h\h other T cell subsets. Th9 cells mount immune\h response in response to allergic inflammation,\h\h autoimmune disease, and tumor immunity. The\h key effector-cytokines are IL9 and IL10.\h IL-6, IL-21, IL-23, and TGF-β are the major\h signaling cytokines involved in Th17 cell\h\h differentiation. Retinoic acid receptor-related\h orphan receptor gamma-T, or RORγt, is the master\h\h regulator responsible for directing Th17\h differentiation. The differentiation process\h\h can be classified into 3 stages that enhance the\h Th17 differentiation process: the initiation stage\h\h by TGF-β and IL6, the self-amplification stage by\h IL-21, and the stabilization stage by IL23. STAT3,\h\h RORα, Runx1, Batf, IRF4, and AHR are other\h transcription factors responsible for\h\h promoting Th17 differentiation. Tregs are a unique subset of T cells\h\h as they suppress several autoreactive responses\h and maintain self-tolerance in the immune system.\h\h Functionally, Tregs suppress the activation,\h proliferation, and cytokine production of CD4+\h\h T cells, as well as CD8+ T cells. After interacting\h with IL4, Th17 can differentiate into Th2 subset.\h\h Treg also showed a tendency to convert to Th17 and\h Tfh.

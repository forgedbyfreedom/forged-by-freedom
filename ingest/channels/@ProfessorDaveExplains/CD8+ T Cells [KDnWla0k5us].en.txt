We just learned all about CD4+ T cells,\h such AST helper cells, regulatory T cells,\h\h and more. Now let’s learn about the other class\h of T cells, CD8+ T cells. Cytotoxic T lymphocytes,\h\h or CTLs, are CD8+ T cells that recognize their\h targets via the interaction of rearranged T-cell\h\h receptors and the class 1 major histocompatibility\h complex. As we learned in an earlier tutorial,\h\h MHC class I molecules are expressed on\h virtually all cells in the body. This\h\h feature allows CTLs to thoroughly scan\h the tissues for cells expressing foreign\h\h or cancer-associated peptides. Specifically,\h CTLs are specialized in efficiently detecting\h\h cancerous cells or those infected with\h intracellular pathogens, such as viruses. CD8+ T lymphocytes develop in the thymus,\h where they are selected for their ability to\h\h recognize nonself peptides in the context of MHC\h class I molecules. After they exit the thymus,\h\h they exist in a quiescent state and are referred\h to as naïve cells. Naïve CD8+ T cells circulate\h\h between peripheral lymphoid organs, such as\h the spleen and lymph nodes, via the arterial\h\h and lymphatic systems. The tissue distribution of\h lymphocytes is determined by targeting proteins,\h\h which can be divided into three categories:\h selectins, chemokine receptors, and integrins.\h\h Naïve and activated CD8+ T cells display distinct\h sets of these targeting proteins that direct the\h\h differential homing abilities of these cells.\h Naïve CD8+ T cells express high levels of the\h\h lymph node homing receptor L-selectin, or CD62L,\h and CCR7, a chemokine receptor that recognizes\h\h other chemokines produced in the T cell areas\h of secondary lymphoid organs. At this location,\h\h naïve T cells interact with antigen-presenting\h cells. In case a naïve CD8 T cell does not\h\h encounter its specific antigen, it leaves\h the lymph node. The CTL response to an acute\h\h infection consists of three phases: first, the\h initial activation and proliferation of the CTL;\h\h second, the contraction of effector populations;\h and third, the long-term maintenance of memory\h\h cells. When a CD8+ T cell encounters the\h correctly presented peptide–MHC I complex,\h\h its localization and homing properties are\h altered in response. They undergo multiple\h\h rounds of proliferation to become activated CTLs,\h after which they move to the non-lymphoid sites,\h\h tether to endothelial cells, and extravasate\h into target tissue. Importantly, a number of\h\h the effector memory CTLs are retained in these\h nonlymphoid tissues, where they are poised to\h\h respond rapidly should the antigen be encountered\h again. The CTL memory compartment is composed of\h\h three cell types: effector memory or TEM cells,\h central memory or TCM cells, and tissue-resident\h\h memory or TRM cells. These subsets differ in their\h surface molecule expression and in their ability\h\h to exhibit effector functions. TCMs are found\h in the lymph nodes, spleen and blood; TEMs are\h\h located in the non-lymphoid tissues and spleen;\h while TRMs are found in non-lymphoid tissues. Notably, only direct interaction between an APC\h and appropriate co-stimulation leads to full CTL\h\h activity. There are two distinct mechanisms\h by which CTLs encounter peptide–MHC class I\h\h complexes. If the APC expresses an antigen,\h for example a virus, the endogenous MHC class\h\h I pathway is used for presentation.\h In case the antigen is not expressed,\h\h the APC acquires and processes the antigen\h via a process termed cross-presentation. In\h\h this process, cross-presentation is\h initiated by the capture of foreign\h\h or exogenous antigens by phagosomes. The\h antigens are then processed by a different\h\h mechanism that directs the peptides to the\h MHC class I pathway and presentation on the\h\h cell surface. When a CTL encounters such an\h antigen, the pathway is termed cross-priming. Antigen-stimulated CTLs most notably\h produce IFN-γ and TNF. These potent\h\h inflammatory cytokines activate macrophages and\h lymphocytes at the site of infection. IFN-γ helps\h\h establish a T-helper cell-1 or Th1 response,\h and further stimulates differentiated CTLs. CTLs\h\h also secrete a number of chemokines including\h chemokine ligand 3, or CCL3, CCL4, and CCL5,\h\h that aid in recruitment of additional\h lymphocytes into the immune reaction. Cytotoxic T cells kill their targets\h via two major pathways: First,\h\h there is perforin/granzyme–mediated lysis. For\h this first approach, CTL produces perforin,\h\h which is a membrane-disrupting protein.\h Along with a family of serine proteases,\h\h or granzymes, together they form the bulk of\h lytic granules within the cells. Then perforin\h\h forms a pore that disrupts the target cell\h membrane, and once inside the target cell,\h\h granzymes initiate cell death. The mechanism\h of action of granzymes is direct cleavage of\h\h substrates, such as nuclear proteins and DNA,\h or indirect action via a protease cascade. The second pathway is death receptor–induced\h apoptosis. This second approach is a\h\h receptor-based system that induces apoptosis\h of target cells, and it utilizes members of\h\h the tumor necrosis factor receptor, or TNFR,\h superfamily that are expressed on the target\h\h cells. These TNFR have a death domain that\h is responsible for recruiting molecules that\h\h transduce the death signal, such as FADD,\h or Fas-associated death domain. The two most\h\h prominent apoptosis-inducing TNFR family members\h are Fas, or CD95, and TRAIL, or TNF-related\h\h apoptosis–inducing ligands. Furthermore, Fas is\h expressed on a wide variety of tissues, whereas\h\h Fas ligand or FasL expression is restricted\h to activated CTLs and NK cells. The Fas/FasL\h\h pathway is very important in controlling T-cell\h numbers through activation-induced cell death,\h\h or AICD, as well as in the rejection of some\h tumors. Cytotoxic cells also express TRAIL,\h\h which upon binding to TRAIL receptors, induces\h apoptosis in a wider selection of targets and\h\h are particularly effective on tumor cells.\h With some understanding of CD8+ T cells,\h\h let’s move forward and check out still other\h T cell types and learn what those do as well.

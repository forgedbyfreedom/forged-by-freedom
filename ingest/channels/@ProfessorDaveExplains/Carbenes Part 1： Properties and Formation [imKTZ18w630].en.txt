First we learned about carbocations.\h\h These occur when a tetravalent carbon atom loses\h a bond and therefore makes only three bonds,\h\h for a total of six electrons in its outer\h shell. Contributing only three electrons\h\h to the Lewis structure instead of four, the\h carbon must bear a formal positive charge.\h\h Carbocations are therefore electrophilic, and\h seek to coordinate to some electron density,\h\h so as to fill their outermost shell and neutralize\h the formal charge. If this doesn’t happen,\h\h elimination is likely to occur, in order to\h stabilize via alkene formation, giving that carbon\h\h a fourth bond. Contributing five electrons\h\h carbon must bear a formal negative charge.\h\h Carbanions are therefore nucleophilic, and will\h seek to coordinate to some electron deficiency,\h\h so as to share the lone pair with some other\h atom and neutralize itself. Then we learned about\h\h carbon radicals. These have seven electrons in\h their outermost shell, as one of them is unpaired.\h\h Contributing four electrons to the Lewis\h structure, the carbon atom is of neutral charge,\h\h but because it doesn’t have a full octet,\h it is still a form of electron deficiency.\h\h Carbon radicals will seek to gain a single\h electron from something else, so as to\h\h complete its octet, usually through propagation\h steps that produce other, more stable radicals.\h Now it is time to learn about one more unstable\h carbon species, and that is the carbene. Carbenes,\h\h like carbocations, have only six\h electrons in their outermost shell,\h\h but in the form of two covalent bonds and\h two additional electrons. Because four of\h\h these electrons belong to the carbon atom\h itself, the carbon is of neutral charge,\h\h though it does not possess a full octet.\h Carbenes tend to react with neutral partners\h\h to form two new bonds, in order to achieve a full\h octet and stabilize. Much like carbon radicals,\h\h carbenes are very reactive, and they come\h in two forms. There are triplet carbenes\h\h and singlet carbenes. Here we can see a triplet\h carbene.

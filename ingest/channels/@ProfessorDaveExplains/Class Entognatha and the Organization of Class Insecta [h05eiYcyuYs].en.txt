Most animal\h\h life forms on our planet are hexapods, and they\h are found globally in almost all environments,\h\h though very few inhabit the oceans and polar\h regions. They are so biodiverse that over half\h\h of all described eukaryotic species, including\h all plants, animals, and fungi, are hexapods.\h The clade Hexapoda includes just two classes,\h Insecta, or Ectognatha, which contains over\h\h 1,000,000 species, and Entognatha, which contains\h a little less than 11,000 described species.\h\h Entognathans have entognathous mouthparts, meaning\h they are below the surface of the head. They are\h\h all wingless and ametabolist, meaning they\h have little or no metamorphosis, and instead\h\h gradually increase in size from juvenile to\h adult. Entognatha traditionally is split into\h\h three orders: Collembola, Diplura, and Protura,\h though this grouping is currently disputed.\h Collembola, commonly known as springtails or\h snowfleas, includes over 3,500 species that are\h\h normally very small, lack compound eyes, and are\h quite abundant in moist soils and the surface of\h\h small bodies of water around the world. There they\h feed upon decaying material and microbes. They\h\h are noted for their ability to “jump” or backflip\h using their furcula even while standing on water.\h Likewise, the 300 or so species of Protura,\h sometimes known as coneheads or proturans,\h\h are also small, about one millimeter.\h These are soil-dwelling animals that\h\h lack eyes and pigmentation. They are\h most commonly found in soils, mosses,\h\h and leaf litter, and are often less common\h than the bristletails. They are cylindrical,\h\h slow-moving, and elongated. They sense their\h surroundings using the first of their three\h\h pairs of legs and walk on just four legs. Diplura contains about 800 species of\h\h two-pronged bristletails, most of which are\h larger than the springtails and coneheads,\h\h though they are still quite small at less than\h 10 millimeters. They are also pale, eyeless,\h\h and most commonly found in the soil and leaf\h litter with their cousins, the springtails and\h\h coneheads. They are easily distinguished from\h the former clades by their pair of cerci that\h\h project backwards and resemble a tail. Moving on, class Insecta, or Ectognatha,\h\h is further broken down into the three clades\h Archaeognatha, Zygentoma, and Pterygota. The\h\h 500 members of Order Archaeognatha are often\h called “jumping bristletails.” They are small\h\h insects with elongated bodies and arched backs.\h They are characterized by their large compound\h\h eyes that meet at the top of the head with\h three ocelli and their two lateral cerci and\h\h medial filaments, which when viewed together,\h resemble a three-pronged tail which they can\h\h use to spring into the air if disturbed. The silverfish were so named due to\h their silvery scales, while the firebrats were\h\h named for their tolerance to high temperatures\h and can sometimes infest bakeries or homes and\h\h form nests near boilers and furnaces.

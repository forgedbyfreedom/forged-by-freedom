In the previous tutorial we learned about how\h rocks melt, differentiate, and crystallize.\h\h Now we will discuss the different types of\h igneous rocks and how they are classified.\h\h Say you are given an igneous rock and asked what\h it is. Where would you begin? Your first step\h\h might be determining whether it was extrusive\h or intrusive. Intrusive is a word describing\h\h an igneous rock that cooled slowly at depth,\h and extrusive is a word describing a rock that\h\h cooled quickly at the surface, usually during a\h volcanic eruption. Intrusive rocks are also called\h\h plutonic, and extrusive rocks may be described as\h volcanic. It is usually pretty easy to distinguish\h\h a plutonic rock from a volcanic rock simply by\h analyzing the size of the mineral grains. Plutonic\h\h rocks, which cool slowly, have ample time to\h grow large crystals, while volcanic rocks, which\h\h crystallize rapidly, are composed of tiny grains\h that usually can’t be seen by the naked eye.\h Let’s first discuss volcanic rocks,\h since they are simply named after the\h\h magma from which they are derived. Both are\h classified based on their silica content,\h\h and are as follows: with rhyolite, silica\h content is greater than 69%, dacite, 62-69%,\h\h andesite, 52-62%, basalt, 45-52%, and picrite,\h which contains less than 45% silica. The term\h\h ultramafic is commonly used to describe picritic\h rocks, mafic for basaltic rocks, intermediate for\h\h andesitic rocks, and felsic for dacitic and\h rhyolitic rocks. Rhyolite and basalt are very\h\h different rocks, and it is typical for them not to\h have any common minerals. For example, rhyolite is\h\h rich in alkali feldspar, quartz, and sodium-rich\h plagioclase with minor amounts of amphibole,\h\h while basalt is mainly composed of pyroxene,\h olivine, and calcium-rich plagioclase with minor\h\h amounts of iron and titanium oxides. Andesites\h and dacites can have both felsic and mafic\h\h minerals but are typically mostly plagioclase with\h lesser amounts of pyroxene, amphibole, and quartz.\h Plutonic igneous rocks have an entirely different\h naming convention than volcanic rocks, which are\h\h classified based on their composition; instead\h being named based on their three most abundant\h\h minerals. This is because the minerals in plutonic\h rocks can be easily identified in hand sample,\h\h whereas volcanic minerals are typically\h too small. For felsic plutonic rocks,\h\h names are based on the relative amounts of\h quartz, plagioclase, and alkali feldspar,\h\h which are placed at the corners of a triangular,\h or ternary diagram, called a QAP diagram.\h\h Plagioclase-rich rocks are called diorite,\h quartz-rich rocks are called granitoid,\h\h and alkali feldspar-rich rocks are called syenite.\h Rocks of intermediate composition are called\h\h granite if they are quartz-rich and monzonite if\h they are feldspar-rich. On the other hand, mafic\h\h plutonic rocks are named based on their relative\h abundances of olivine, pyroxene, and plagioclase,\h\h and again are plotted on ternary diagrams. Gabbro,\h which is the plutonic version of basalt, contains\h\h roughly equal parts plagioclase and clinopyroxene.\h Likewise, ultramafic rocks can be classified\h\h using the minerals olivine, clinopyroxene, and\h orthopyroxene. These rocks, like harzburgite and\h\h lherzolite, are representative of the upper\h mantle and are rarely found at the surface.\h So, what types of igneous rocks are found at\h the surface? That depends on where you look.\h\h For instance, the lava that erupts from midocean\h ridges is exclusively basaltic, with gabbro\h\h forming deeper beneath the surface, which is\h basically just basalt with big crystals. Hotspots\h\h are also capable of producing massive amounts of\h basaltic lava, especially when they are beneath\h\h oceanic crust. Subduction zones tend to produce\h andesitic magma, but can be much more variable,\h\h also being influenced by crustal thickness.\h Island arcs, which are subduction zones where the\h\h overriding plate is oceanic crust, tend to produce\h basaltic and andesitic magma, while continental\h\h arcs, like the volcanoes of the Pacific Northwest,\h tend to erupt rhyolite and dacite.

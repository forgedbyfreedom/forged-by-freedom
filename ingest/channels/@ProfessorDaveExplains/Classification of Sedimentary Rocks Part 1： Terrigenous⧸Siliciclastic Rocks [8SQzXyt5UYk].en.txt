Now that we better understand the nature\h of sedimentary rocks, we are ready to\h\h start classifying them. Sedimentary rocks\h are grouped into three primary categories:\h\h Terrigenous or siliciclastic, biogenic, and\h chemogenic. Terrigenous, which literally\h\h means “originating from Earth” from the Latin\h terra, refers to materials derived from the\h\h erosion of preexisting rocks. Siliciclastic\h refers to materials composed of clasts,\h\h or fragments of silicate minerals. Terrigenous\h and siliciclastic are used synonymously since most\h\h terrigenous sediments are derived from the erosion\h of silicate rocks. Biogenic, or biochemical rocks,\h\h are derived from living organisms, both\h directly and indirectly. Chemogenic,\h\h or chemical sedimentary rocks are derived from\h the precipitation of minerals from natural waters,\h\h usually involving evaporation in arid climates. Let’s start with the siliciclastic sedimentary\h\h rocks, since they are the most abundant,\h accounting for roughly three-fourths of\h\h all sedimentary rocks. In our study of igneous\h rocks, we learned that one of the first steps in\h\h analyzing a hand sample is to determine the grain\h size, and the same holds true for sedimentary\h\h rocks. In fact, the main way that geologists\h classify siliciclastic rocks is based on grain\h\h size. Recall from earlier that the grain size of\h sediments is broken down into gravel, sand, silt,\h\h and clay. So, a sandstone is a stone composed of\h mainly sand-sized particles, while a claystone is\h\h a stone composed mainly of clay-sized particles.\h What about a rock that consists of a mix of sand,\h\h silt, and clay? Geologists often use\h ternary diagrams to systematically classify\h\h rocks. Ternary diagrams represent the relative\h proportions of three components, in this case,\h\h different grain sizes. For example, this\h ternary diagram has the components sand,\h\h silt, and clay, with each corner of the triangle\h representing 100% of one component. For example,\h\h if you were to determine that a sedimentary\h rock contains 60% sand, 20% silt, and 20% clay,\h\h it would be classified as a muddy sandstone. It\h is important to choose the appropriate ternary\h\h diagram when classifying a rock. For example, you\h would not use a ternary diagram with gravel as\h\h a component for the aforementioned rock, since\h it does not contain gravel. Furthermore, it is\h\h important to recognize that the classification of\h a rock as a sandstone, siltstone, or otherwise,\h\h says nothing about its mineralogy, though\h it is implied to be siliciclastic, mainly\h\h because the biochemical and chemical rocks\h follow a different classification scheme.\h Before we explore the main types of siliciclastic\h rocks, we must cover some terminology so we\h\h can better describe them. Let’s use a typical\h sandstone as an example. Sandstones consist mainly\h\h of sand-sized grains of silicate minerals, often\h quartz, which make up the framework portion of the\h\h rock. Imagine you are building a stone wall, using\h only the randomly shaped rocks found in a stream.\h\h How would they fit together? Obviously, there will\h be a good amount of empty space between the rocks,\h\h owing to the fact that random shapes do not\h perfectly fit together. The same applies to\h\h the sand in a sandstone, there is an amount of\h empty space between the sand grains due to the\h\h way they fit together. This is known as a rock’s\h pore space, and it is often filled with materials\h\h to an extent. In some cases, it’s filled with\h fine-grained muds, which is referred to as the\h\h matrix portion of a rock. The matrix is commonly\h composed of clay minerals, such as illite and\h\h kaolinite. Other times, pores get filled in with\h minerals that precipitate out of groundwater,\h\h called cement. The cement helps glue the rock\h together, making it harder and more cohesive.\h\h The most common cement-forming minerals are\h quartz and calcite, with lesser occurrences of\h\h the iron oxide minerals and gypsum or anhydrite.\h Cementation along with compaction are together\h\h known as lithification, which is the process\h of converting sediments into sedimentary rock.\h We now have sufficient knowledge to start looking\h at siliciclastic rocks in more detail. Let’s start\h\h with sandstones. We already know that sandstone\h is a rock composed mainly of sand-sized grains,\h\h which make up the rock’s framework. The most\h common framework minerals are quartz, followed\h\h by feldspar. Another common framework constituent\h are pieces of weathered and eroded rock, called\h\h lithic fragments. For example, the sedimentary\h rocks of the Appalachian Basin contain abundant\h\h fragments of metamorphic rock derived from the\h erosion of uplifted metamorphic terrane during the\h\h Acadian Orogeny. Geologists classify sandstones\h by combining two properties: framework mineralogy,\h\h and the proportion of framework to matrix. A\h quaternary diagram can be used to illustrate\h\h the different types of sandstone. Let’s start\h by assuming the amount of matrix is zero; these\h\h are known as “arenites”. Sandstones that are very\h rich in quartz are called quartz arenites. If the\h\h framework is composed mainly of quartz and lithic\h fragments, it is called a lithic arenite, if it\h\h is instead quartz and feldspar, it would be an\h arkosic, or feldspathic, arenite. Sandstones with\h\h more than 15% matrix are called wackes (wack-ees),\h so if the framework was dominated by quartz and\h\h lithic fragments, it would be classified as a\h lithic wacke, and so forth. Sedimentary rocks with\h\h more than 50% matrix are considered mudstones. Recall from the previous tutorial that feldspar\h\h is unstable in weathering environments and alters\h to clay over time. The relative concentration\h\h of stable materials such as quartz, to unstable\h materials such as feldspar, is used in conjunction\h\h with the degree of rounding, sorting, and amount\h of matrix to determine a sandstone’s “maturity”.\h\h More specifically, maturity is broken down into\h two categories, textural and compositional.\h\h Compositionally mature sandstones are depleted in\h materials that are thermodynamically unstable in\h\h weathering environments, including most igneous\h and metamorphic minerals except for quartz.\h\h Consequently, they tend to be quartz arenites and\h quartz wackes, though they can occasionally be\h\h lithic arenites or lithic wackes if the lithic\h fragments are quartz-rich, such as chert or\h\h quartzite. Compositional maturity indicates that a\h rock’s sediment has spent a great deal of time in\h\h a weathering environment prior to deposition and\h burial, sufficient to chemically alter unstable\h\h sediments. We will talk more about chemical\h weathering in a later tutorial. Texturally mature\h\h sandstones have well-rounded and well-sorted\h grains with little to no matrix mud, indicating\h\h great transport distance from source to burial. Let’s move on to the coarsest-grained sedimentary\h\h rocks, conglomerates. Conglomerates are\h composed of at least 30% gravel-sized\h\h particles. True conglomerates have\h well to sub-rounded clasts, whereas the\h\h term “breccia” describes a conglomeritic\h rock that is composed of angular clasts.\h\h Since it is relatively uncommon for rocks to\h contain mineral grains larger than 2 millimeters,\h\h most conglomerate gravels are composed of lithic\h fragments, rather than monocrystalline grains,\h\h as is typically the case for quartz sandstones.\h Conglomerates are classified based on the\h\h proportion of framework to matrix, similar to\h sandstones. True conglomerates contain very\h\h little matrix and are framework-supported, meaning\h that gravel-sized clasts are touching. When a\h\h conglomerate is matrix-supported, it is called\h a diamictite, with diamictite meaning “mixed\h\h rock”. Diamictite is a general term for extremely\h poorly sorted geologic material in a muddy matrix\h\h and is sometimes used to describe glacial till.\h To summarize, conglomerate typically refers to a\h\h clast-supported rock composed of rounded gravel.\h Breccia also refers to a clast-supported rock,\h\h but composed of angular gravel. And diamictite\h refers to any matrix-supported rock that\h\h contains gravel-sized clasts. The compositional\h classification of conglomerates can get a bit\h\h complicated but is generally based on the type\h of lithic fragments that comprise the framework\h\h fraction, mainly distinguishing sedimentary,\h metamorphic, and igneous clasts. Additionally,\h\h they can be classified based on clast stability,\h similar to sandstone maturity. Conglomerates with\h\h greater than 90% stable clasts are said to\h be “quartzose”, while those with less than\h\h 90% stable clasts are said to be “petromict”. Last but not least are the mudstones and shales,\h\h which are the finest-grained variety of\h siliciclastic rocks. More than half of\h\h all sedimentary rocks are mudstones, compared\h to about 20% for sandstones and only 1% for\h\h conglomerates. Recall that mud is a general\h term for silt and clay-sized grains. Hence,\h\h mudstones are composed of more than 50%\h clay-sized grains, with silt-sized grains\h\h making up most of the rest. Conglomerates, with\h their very coarse grains, are deposited in the\h\h highest energy depositional environments, like\h rocky beaches and braided streams. At the other\h\h end of the spectrum are mudstones, which can\h only be deposited in low energy environments,\h\h where fine-grained sediments are able to settle\h out of suspension. The largest mudstone deposits\h\h form in the deep, calm waters of lakes and oceans. It is not uncommon for clay, silt, and fine sand\h\h to be simultaneously present in a mudstone,\h elevating the importance of proper grain size\h\h classification. This can be done accurately with\h a petrographic microscope, though the clay-sized\h\h grains will be too small to resolve. Field\h geologists have a few tricks for determining\h\h grain size. Mud can be easily distinguished from\h sand based on whether grains are visible to the\h\h naked eye, since anything smaller than fine\h sand is too small to be seen. So, if we have\h\h a sedimentary rock without visible grains, it\h would be classified as a mudstone. Furthermore,\h\h the relative proportions of clay to silt can be\h determined by rubbing a small sample over your\h\h teeth to determine the amount of grit present.\h If little to no grit is detected, it is probably\h\h nearly pure clay and would be classified in the\h field as a claystone. If moderate grit is present,\h\h that will suffice to classify it as a general\h mudstone. If a mudstone contains a fraction\h\h of visible fine-grained material, it\h would be classified as sandy mudstone.\h\h Siltstone is a more specific type of mudstone,\h being mainly composed of silt-sized particles.\h Compositionally, mudstones are primarily composed\h of clay minerals, with moderate amounts of\h\h silt-sized quartz and feldspar. There are several\h types of clay minerals including illite, chlorite,\h\h and kaolinite. They are a product of the chemical\h weathering of primary silicate minerals, such as\h\h feldspar, amphibole, and pyroxene. Mudstones can\h be quite complex mineralogically, containing minor\h\h amounts of several other minerals, especially\h the carbonate, sulfide, and oxide/hydroxide\h\h groups. They can also be quite rich in organic\h carbon, making up an enormous fraction of global\h\h source rocks for oil and gas extraction. The classification of mudstones isn’t as\h\h straightforward as that of sandstones, which can\h be easily classified by plotting compositional\h\h and textural properties on ternary diagrams.\h Classifying mudstones involves stringing together\h\h descriptive terms. For example, a mudstone that is\h rich in organic carbon with abundant fossils would\h\h be classified as a fossiliferous carbonaceous\h mudstone. Mudstones rich in organic carbon\h\h tend to be black in color and often contain\h pyrite, or FeS2. If a mudstone is fissile,\h\h meaning it easily breaks into planar sheets, it\h is called a shale, the term most nongeologists\h\h probably know rather than mudstone. If\h a mudstone contains very thin layers,\h\h called laminae, it is said to be laminated. Another interesting property of mudstone is\h\h that it’s the most radioactive of the sedimentary\h rocks. There are a few reasons for this. First,\h\h some clay minerals are rich in potassium,\h and 0.01% of potassium is the radioactive\h\h isotope potassium-40. Second, several clay\h minerals contain trace amounts of thorium,\h\h which is also radioactive. And three,\h organic materials help concentrate uranium.\h\h The association between organic-rich mudstones\h and radioactivity is exploited by oil and gas\h\h prospectors, who measure the gamma ray intensity\h throughout boreholes to identify likely targets.\h\h Homes built in areas with mudstone bedrock are\h often afflicted with high levels of radon gas,\h\h which is part of the decay chain of uranium\h and thorium.

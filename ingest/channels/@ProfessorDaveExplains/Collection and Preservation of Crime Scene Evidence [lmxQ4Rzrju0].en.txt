We just learned about protection of a crime scene,\h as well as recognition, search, and documentation\h\h of evidence. Once the evidence is located\h after searching, and it is properly documented,\h\h it is marked for collection. As we have learned,\h documentation is done after marking the evidence.\h\h Why is marking evidence so important? Why not\h directly collect and package it? Well, for one,\h\h a CS is a complicated mess, and you don’t want\h any investigator to get confused by evidence\h\h of similar nature. Two strands of hair at two\h different places look similar, so what’s the\h\h harm in collecting them together? Because they are\h always to be presumed as belonging to different\h\h sources until they are analyzed in the laboratory.\h Remember, crime investigation is not solely\h\h limited to the CS, but it is a continuous process\h until the evidence is presented in the courtroom.\h\h Also, evidence from different crime scenes is\h often sent to the same laboratory for analysis.\h\h If this evidence is not properly marked, it will\h lead to more confusion ahead. Just imagine the\h\h results of a blood test from one crime getting\h forwarded to another case! This could lead to\h\h incorrect identification of the suspect, which\h will have the biggest ramification on the age-old\h\h adage of criminal law, known as Blackstone’s\h ratio. “It is better that ten guilty persons\h\h escape than that one innocent suffer”. Therefore,\h it is very important to unambiguously identify the\h\h evidence at all stages of the investigation. Evidence differs in its nature, size,\h\h and shape. The investigator must have a pragmatic\h approach for deciding how and where to mark the\h\h evidence. They should be able to judge which\h part of the evidence is essential for further\h\h processing and which is not. Throughout all stages\h of investigation, knowledge and experience of the\h\h investigator play the most important role. Marking\h should not be done in such a way so as to damage\h\h the essential details of the evidence. Otherwise,\h the principle of analysis, which emphasizes the\h\h purity of evidence, will be jeopardized. It is\h comparatively easier to mark bigger evidence than\h\h smaller evidence. If the evidence is so small that\h marking is no longer possible, it is documented\h\h using a separate sketch or a close-up photograph\h and marked accordingly. However, a better approach\h\h will be to directly mark the packaging itself.\h This will save precious time involved in sketching\h\h and photography. Details such as the serial\h number of evidence recognized, nature of evidence\h\h such as blood or hair, name of the investigating\h officer, location of evidence, date and time of\h\h recognition, are included in the marking. In cases\h where clothes are involved, such as sexual assault\h\h or a firearm injury, a tag can be attached to\h them for marking. If tagging is not feasible, the\h\h clothes are marked from the inside with permanent\h marker. Utmost precautions should be taken so that\h\h parts of the clothes containing bullet holes,\h bloodstain impressions, and marks of wear and\h\h tear are not disturbed. This applies to any such\h evidence of similar nature. If the evidence is\h\h fragile, such as a glass object, it must be marked\h with a “handle with care” tag. The same goes for\h\h evidence that might injure the investigator. A\h particular system can be followed for marking\h\h evidence according to the nature of the CS. For\h example, if the CS is indoors, the evidence can\h\h be marked according to the room it was found in,\h such as living room-1, living room-2, bedroom-1,\h\h bedroom-2, and so forth. Otherwise the evidence\h can be directly numbered according to their order\h\h of search and recognition. Whatever system is\h adopted, it should be consistent for all evidence\h\h recognized at the CS. All stakeholders benefiting\h from this investigation, starting from the first\h\h responding officers to the lawyers and jury in the\h courtroom should be able to understand the details\h\h of evidence from the marking itself. Finally,\h as we have already emphasized, the investigator\h\h must be able to relive the investigation just\h by going through the documentation and marking.\h After all the evidence is marked, they are\h documented with notes and photography. It\h\h is to be ascertained by the investigator that\h each and every corner surrounding the evidence\h\h is documented thoroughly before collection.\h This is followed by the actual collection\h\h of evidence. The material or equipment needed for\h collection include a scalpel, tweezers, scissors,\h\h other hand tools, cardboard boxes, wrapping\h paper, adhesive tape, zip bags, hand gloves,\h\h air-tight containers, and envelopes. There is\h no common procedure to collecting evidence,\h\h and it will depend on different circumstances\h surrounding the CS and the evidence. For example,\h\h is the CS indoors or outdoors? Is the evidence\h fixed on the CS or movable? Is it fragile? Is\h\h the evidence of a biological nature? What is the\h size of the evidence? Usually, large and obvious\h\h evidence are collected first, while smaller\h evidence are collected later. Among the smaller\h\h evidence, fragile evidence are collected first.\h Bigger evidence can be simply taken by hand and\h\h packaged, while smaller trace evidence such as\h hair can be collected using techniques such as\h\h vacuuming. A scalpel can be used to dig out\h trace evidence such as hair or nail clippings\h\h from unreachable places such as window sills\h and corners of the room. Subsequently, they are\h\h collected using tweezers. If blood is identified\h as evidence, it is essential to know if it is a\h\h wet stain or a dry stain. Whether it is found on\h a porous substrate such as fabric or wood, or a\h\h non-porous surface such as the floor or glass. Is\h the blood contaminated with other minute evidence\h\h such as glass particles, gun powder residue,\h or hair? If similar evidence is present in\h\h different locations, each of them is collected and\h packaged separately. There is a specific protocol\h\h of collection for each of these conditions, for\h every category of evidence. Whenever possible,\h\h control samples of the substrate around the\h evidence are collected separately for comparison.\h\h If a piece of fabric contains a bloodstain,\h a portion of the unstained fabric around the\h\h bloodstain is collected separately. In upcoming\h tutorials of this series, we will learn about\h\h these collection and preservation techniques in\h detail while discussing each category of evidence,\h\h including impression evidence, biological fluids\h such as blood and semen, as well as physical\h\h evidence such as paint, glass, and soil. But the\h only fundamental rule to follow during collection\h\h is that the respective collection techniques\h should not alter or contaminate the evidence. For\h\h this, the collectors must be covered with suitable\h gear from head to toe. This will also ensure that\h\h the collectors don’t get injuries related to\h evidence such as glass, or by the toxicity of\h\h certain biological evidence or chemicals present\h on the CS. Following the collection of all marked\h\h evidence, a secondary survey of the CS is carried\h out, to ensure that no evidence is left behind.\h The next stage after collection is the packaging\h of the evidence. The three main objectives served\h\h by packaging are: 1) To avoid contamination\h of the evidence. If the evidence collected is\h\h not packaged separately, its integrity will\h be heavily compromised. For example, if two\h\h different bloodstains are packaged together,\h there is a chance of false identification.\h\h 2) To preserve the evidence from decay and loss:\h Evidence can decay if not packaged correctly. An\h\h example is bodily fluids packaged in air-tight\h containers. They will decay due to microbial\h\h action as there will be no space for the moisture\h to dissipate. Therefore, the correct protocol in\h\h packaging wet bodily fluids is to dry them at\h room temperature and package them in porous\h\h envelopes that will allow the moisture to escape.\h Certain preservatives are also added to slow down\h\h the process of decay. By contrast, in the case\h of volatile liquids, air-tight containers are\h\h used for packaging to prevent the escape of such\h fluids. 3) To protect the evidence from the rigors\h\h of transportation: Once packaged, evidence\h is to be transported to the laboratory for\h\h further analysis. It is unlikely that the forensic\h laboratory will be a short distance away from the\h\h CS. Time and distance required, condition\h of the road, perishability of the samples,\h\h these are some of the factors to be considered\h for efficient transportation. Sometimes,\h\h brittle evidence can get shattered if not packaged\h properly. Exchange of material between evidence\h\h can also occur due to evidence falling on each\h other during transport. Proper packaging, mounting\h\h of packages, and cushioning, are paramount\h to prevent such a scenario. If the transport\h\h container is not secured properly, evidence might\h fall off the vehicle in the process. This is why\h\h the careful attention of the investigator is\h needed in each stage of the investigation,\h\h as even a minor lapse in the protocol can\h lead to complete investigation failure.\h Successful transportation of evidence to\h the laboratory marks the end of crime scene\h\h investigation. Let us briefly review all the\h stages of CSI. CSI starts with protection of\h\h the CS, recognition of evidence, searching for\h evidence, marking and documentation, collection\h\h of evidence, finally ending with packaging\h and transportation.

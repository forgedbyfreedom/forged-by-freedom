In the previous tutorial we examined two\h methods of documenting a crime scene,\h\h those being note-taking and sketching. Let’s\h now check out two more. The third method of CS\h\h documentation is photography. This is used to\h provide a pictorial legal record of the CS and\h\h the evidence. We must remember here that however\h capable the investigator might be at illustrating\h\h and describing the CS through sketches and\h notes, it fails to replicate the real-world\h\h impact of photographs. Observing photographs of\h the initial conditions at the CS and the evidence,\h\h in conjunction with the sketches, can provide\h excellent visualization of the CS. Often,\h\h judgements get influenced by CS photographs. They\h can create an impact in the courtroom by proving\h\h evidence prima facie. Sometimes, graphic\h photographs can even lead to an emotional\h\h influence on the jury. Photographs are often\h easier to grasp and understand than the notes\h\h and sketches. They serve as a permanent record\h to look at the CS and the evidence in detail. The\h\h photographs captured are of examination quality\h and intricate details can be easily recorded,\h\h which is not possible using any other technique.\h A photograph taken of a piece of evidence such\h\h as a fingerprint, a bullet, or a bloodstain\h can easily be used for comparison. However,\h\h like all techniques, photography is a\h complementary technique, and never used as\h\h a standalone method. This is because photographs\h may deliver a false sense of perspective. They do\h\h not record the measurements and other dimensions\h at the CS as accurately as a sketch. Nevertheless,\h\h there is a new technique called photogrammetry,\h which can be used in accurately mapping the scene\h\h by a utilizing a string of photographs.\h But it is still under development,\h\h and currently not extensively used in CSI. Photography of the CS is always done in a\h\h systematic and organized manner. It is\h started as soon as the investigators\h\h arrive at the CS. General photography is done\h first, which includes overall photos of the\h\h CS and its outside environment, followed by\h specific photography, which depicts the CS,\h\h the evidence, and their spatial relationship\h in a close-up view. Recall the TV show Friends.\h\h There is always a wide-angle shot of the\h beautiful New York skyline and the apartment,\h\h before transitioning to the interiors. This is\h known as establishing a scene. Coming back to CSI,\h\h many times close-up photographs of evidence are\h taken out of context. The overall wide-angle\h\h photographs of the outside of the CS and of the\h actual CS help in establishing the scene first,\h\h before moving on to the actual evidence.\h Photographs taken outside of the CS are\h\h essential to capture landmarks such as surrounding\h buildings, streetlights, street names and numbers,\h\h mailboxes, and fixed objects like statues and\h fountains. These help in identifying the location\h\h of the CS. In many cases, the perpetrator visits\h the CS after committing the crime, to observe the\h\h investigation. For this reason, photographs of the\h spectators outside the CS should always be taken.\h\h Sometimes, they can also be used to identify\h witnesses reluctant to testify. Inside the CS,\h\h photographs of every room are taken, including the\h ones with seemingly no relation to the crime. Four\h\h corners of a room are taken as fixed points from\h which overall photographs are taken. These help\h\h in creating an overlapping view of the scene from\h all angles. If appropriate equipment is available,\h\h photographs can also be taken from the height of\h the ceiling. This provides a fresh perspective\h\h to the CS, as many details can go unnoticed if\h viewed from the ground. Next in the sequence is\h\h midrange photography, which is used to focus on\h the evidence located within the overall CS. Then,\h\h close-up photographs of each piece of\h evidence are taken, from all sides.\h\h The rule of thumb is to capture the evidence\h in such a way that it totally fills the camera\h\h frame. This helps in recording every detail of\h the evidence such as its surface characteristics,\h\h markings, and defects. They are always taken\h parallel to the surface of the evidence to\h\h avoid a distorted view. A scale, or two scales\h perpendicular to each other, are placed beside\h\h the evidence to give an idea of its dimensions.\h Photographs are taken both with and without scale.\h\h As we have learned, the marking of evidence\h is done after a successful search. However,\h\h photographs of the evidence are taken before,\h as well as after placing the evidence markers.\h The photographer adjusts their camera settings\h according to the light conditions, indoors/outdoor\h\h location, nature of evidence, reflection, and so\h forth. Sometimes, a single piece of evidence is\h\h captured several times under different lighting\h conditions. The question is, how many photographs\h\h at the CS are enough? Well, there is no such\h predetermined number. The photographer takes\h\h as many photographs as they can. If there is any\h doubt whether to take a photo or not, it should be\h\h taken. This is the only opportunity to visualize\h the scene later in the easiest manner. The only\h\h requirement is that a photography log should\h be thoroughly maintained. This log maintains\h\h a record of details such as time and date for\h each photograph, roll number, exposure number,\h\h f-stop, shutter speed, approximate distance\h from a fixed point, type of photograph, and\h\h a brief description of the captured photograph. The fourth method of documentation is videography\h\h of the CS. This technique provides an overall\h view of the CS, including its exterior. Some\h\h of the advantages are similar to photography.\h However, the major advantage of videotaping is\h\h that it helps to establish a real-time perspective\h of the CS as if the viewer is present at the\h\h scene. After the preliminary survey, the\h investigator has an overall idea of the\h\h CS. The videographer records the CS tracing the\h same path the investigator followed. Therefore,\h\h this technique is often implemented immediately\h after note-making, although it can be employed a\h\h second time after the evidence has been searched.\h It is one continuous and uninterrupted video until\h\h all the points of entry, diverging pathways,\h and points of exit are completely covered,\h\h along with the location of evidence. Videography\h begins with a close-up shot of a placard on\h\h the screen. This placard includes information\h related to the scene, such as the date, time,\h\h and type of crime. The process and precautions\h to be followed are similar to photography. First\h\h overall shots are taken, followed by mid-range and\h close-up shots. The shots should be steady, and a\h\h tripod is preferred whenever possible. The video\h should not include any bystander or member of\h\h the investigating team. A shot can be taken from\h the position of the victim as well, to understand\h\h how they viewed the CS when the crime occurred. Videography is seldom used in courts as evidence.\h\h But this does not mean that it is not needed. In\h fact, videography is the most effective technique\h\h in certain circumstances. If the investigation\h is to be handed to some other investigator who\h\h never visited the original CS, the video\h assists in providing them with the most\h\h accurate overall perspective. The same goes\h for a laboratory scientist analyzing evidence\h\h far from the chaos at the scene. So why not use\h other techniques for memory-refreshing? Because\h\h photographs might be distorted, the dimensions not\h accurately recorded, and it is a tiresome process\h\h to get a complete and continuous point of view\h by stitching them together. Sketches do provide\h\h an overall perspective and accurate dimensions,\h however they are complex to understand and have\h\h less visual appeal. While note-taking is like a\h book, visualizing the scene is completely left to\h\h your imagination. Videography, on the other hand,\h is the most understandable of all techniques.\h In summary, the most important thing to understand\h about these four CS documentation techniques is\h\h that all of them should complement each other. One\h is never enough, and each one of them has its own\h\h distinct advantage. There is no upper limit to the\h number of times one can carry out documentation,\h\h and each of them can be repeated at every\h stage of the investigation.

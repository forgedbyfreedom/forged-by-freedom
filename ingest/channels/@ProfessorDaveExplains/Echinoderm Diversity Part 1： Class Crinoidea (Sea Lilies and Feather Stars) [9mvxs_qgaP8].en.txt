After discussing the general anatomy and\h characteristics of phylum Echinodermata,\h\h it’s time to get a sense of the diversity of this\h clade, which will take several tutorials. Though\h\h the echinoderm phylum is still large, diverse,\h and ecologically significant, there was a time\h\h when at least 20 classes existed on Earth,\h as is evidenced by the fossil record. Today,\h\h the five extant echinoderm classes are Crinoidea,\h Asteroidea, Ophiuroidea, Echinoidea, and\h\h Holothuroidea. It’s worth mentioning that although\h there is widespread consensus that these 5 classes\h\h are distinct, the exact number and relationship\h between orders within a class, and even the total\h\h number of orders is still a subject of ongoing\h research. In this tutorial we will cover the least\h\h diverse of the echinoderm classes, the crinoids. The stalked members of class Crinoidea are known\h\h as sea lilies, while the unstalked members are\h known as feather stars. This class contains four\h\h subclasses, those being Articulata, Flexibilia,\h Camerata, and Disparida. Of these, only the\h\h members of subclass Articulata are extant, the\h other three are all extinct. Though there are only\h\h about 700 described species of extant crinoids,\h the class was once much more abundant and diverse.\h They are unique among the echinoderms in that\h their mouths are located on their upper surface,\h\h surrounded by feeding arms, and linked to\h a U-shaped digestive system with the anus\h\h located near the mouth on the oral disk. They\h still maintain the characteristic echinoderm\h\h fivefold symmetry, but the individual arms are\h often subdivided into ten or more. Their basic\h\h anatomy involves a crown and stalk, though\h the feather stars lack this stalk as adults.\h At the center of their stalk is the calyx, or oral\h disk, that is covered by a leathery skin or tegmen\h\h which contains the boney, calcareous plates.\h The arms radiate out from the calyx, and lateral\h\h protrusions known as pinnules, which resemble\h barbs on a feather, are used in feeding. The long\h\h stalk is made up of a column, sometimes simply\h called the “stalk” itself or the “columnal”,\h\h which may or may not bear a great deal of cirri.\h The stalk ends in a holdfast comprised of cirri\h\h that the crinoid uses to grip the substrate. On the oral surface there is a series of\h\h five ambulacral grooves that branch out into\h additional arms. The margins of the grooves\h\h contain tube feet that run the length of the arms\h and transport food into the dorsal mouth. As such,\h\h the crinoids are passive filter feeders that\h obtain nutrients from the surrounding water column\h\h using their pinnules to capture food and bring it\h into the ambulacral grooves. They are dioecious,\h\h and their barrel-shaped doliolaria larvae are\h free-swimming for a time until they become\h\h attached to the substrate and metamorphosize. Extant crinoids of Articulata are generally\h\h accepted to be split into four orders, those\h being Comatulida, Cyrtocrinida, Hyocrinida,\h\h and Isocrinida. Members of order Comatulida\h are known as feather stars, and primarily do\h\h not have stalks as adults. They are common on the\h seabed and on reefs, mainly in tropical waters,\h\h and are capable of swimming as adults. They\h are the more diverse extant crinoid order,\h\h and are sometimes referred to as “modern”\h crinoids. They are the only order that contains\h\h the feather stars. The remaining three orders\h are different types of sea lilies. For example,\h\h order Cyrtocrinida contains sea lilies that\h have stalks made of very few skeletal units,\h\h and an occasionally asymmetrical calyx. They\h have no cirri and instead attach directly to\h\h the substrate. Meanwhile, order Hyocrinida\h contains sea lilies with long slender\h\h stalks, no cirri, and a basal disk that\h attaches directly to the substrate. They\h\h are often found in the deep ocean on seamounts\h and the basin. Finally, the sea lilies of order\h\h Isocrinida have a long stalk with a series of\h cirri, and a holdfast used for attachment to\h\h the substrate. Though the sea lilies are much\h less mobile as adults than the feather stars,\h\h many are able to abandon a site if threatened,\h though often quite slowly. And that covers just\h\h one of the extant classes of Echinodermata. Let’s\h move forward and check out some other classes.

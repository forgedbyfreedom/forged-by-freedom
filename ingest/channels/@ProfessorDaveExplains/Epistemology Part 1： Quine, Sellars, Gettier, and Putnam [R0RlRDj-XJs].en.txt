This becomes an endless vertical,\h\h ever-returning endeavor without much progress,\h as all knowledge comes back to the basic,\h\h unquestionable and unchanging truths. Quine,\h however, influenced by Dewey and realizing\h\h the importance of the social context and our\h imperfect reasoning in the scientific endeavor,\h\h proposed a coherentist approach to knowledge\h construction: cyclical as the foundationalists,\h\h however oriented toward a self-correcting\h relationship between our worldview and the logical\h\h foundations of our criteria for truth. His paper,\h “Two Dogmas of Empiricism”, is probably one of\h\h the most influential philosophy texts of the past\h century. In his perspective, we must dialogue with\h\h cognitive psychology to understand the limitations\h of our information input through our senses, and\h\h how we socially develop scientific knowledge to\h explain it toward our logic models of the world,\h\h which would alter given new information, and also\h provide an enhancement to our perception of the\h\h world, in an endless cycle where the manner\h in which we acquire knowledge enhances how we\h\h understand reality. This version of philosophical\h naturalism would be groundbreaking and highly\h\h influential even outside of epistemology. American philosopher Wilfrid Sellars,\h\h son of evolutionary naturalist philosopher Roy\h Sellars, also attacked this foundationalist\h\h perspective referring to it as the “myth of the\h given”, where the semantics of a word would be\h\h a given, an obvious connection to its sound or\h symbols, as knowledge would have an inherent and\h\h necessary network of meaning according to this\h tradition. Very similarly to Quine’s position,\h\h Sellars focuses his criticism on the fact\h that the knowledge we apprehend cannot be\h\h different in substance from the physiological\h mechanisms involved in their input process – a\h\h direct attack to both the logical positivists and\h previous schools of thought like rationalism and\h\h idealism. This would also be his criticism of\h Quine, as he denies even that there would be a\h\h separate process of experience evaluation\h of our cognitive framework, as both would\h\h be inherently indissoluble. This is a clear and\h deliberate influence of continental phenomenology,\h\h given its focus on the subject’s perception\h in and of itself for a theory of knowledge,\h\h as even though he was a prominent analytic,\h he often cited authors from both traditions.\h In 1963, within this ever-shifting landscape\h of philosophy, and especially epistemology and\h\h language, American philosopher Edmund Gettier\h wrote a short three-page paper with a simple,\h\h yet very effective argument, which utterly\h re-directed the discourse in those areas and\h\h is still very relevant. By then, the standard\h epistemological definition of “knowledge”,\h\h tracing all the way back to ancient Greece,\h and in particular Plato’s dialogue Teeteto,\h\h was associated with a “true justified belief”,\h which means a person has a belief in something\h\h about the world which not only is true, but also\h has and is aware of the reasons for assessing\h\h its truth. Gettier criticizes this traditional\h position on knowledge by pointing out a systemic\h\h flaw within this perspective where a person can\h have a true justified belief but have no actual\h\h knowledge. For example, let’s say you saw, among\h several other people, me, Dave, walking down the\h\h street in front of your house one afternoon. You\h were sure it was me, and thus you had the belief\h\h that you saw me walking by your house in the\h afternoon. However, you discover later by watching\h\h security footage and following me on social media\h that I had recently cut my hair differently and\h\h so I was not that person you thought was me. But\h you also discover that, around that time, I myself\h\h actually had passed in front of your house, and\h you saw me but did not recognize me. Clearly,\h\h you had a true and justified belief of the event,\h but could you claim that, before watching the\h\h security camera footage, you had true knowledge?\h Of course not. This develops the epistemological\h\h debate toward new horizons of attempting to define\h what knowledge would configure while also setting\h\h the final nail in the coffin for metaphysical\h theories which relied on this definition. Since\h\h then, philosophers have debated if and how this\h definition holds up, and whether we can save it\h\h by adding another clause, that is, strengthening\h either the conditions of justification, belief,\h\h or knowledge. We should give Gettier some credit\h for shaking two millennia of epistemological\h\h tradition in just three short pages. In this context, American philosopher\h\h Hilary Putnam, one of Quine’s most famous\h students, attuned much of his work with the\h\h second Wittgenstein and the current discussions on\h epistemology toward what he called the “death” of\h\h ontology, or of metaphysical theories of language\h and knowledge. Going beyond the coherentist,\h\h anti-foundationalist Quinean perspective of\h truth and meaning, Putnam brings mathematics\h\h and physics to thoroughly criticize Heidegger\h and metaphysicians in general. He does so by\h\h claiming that any concept can be associated\h with other concepts as freely as we want,\h\h as long as we can communicate with others using\h them. Any concept, then, broadly speaking,\h\h can represent any association of things in\h the world without requiring a metaphysical\h\h anchor to support it. If you take a shoe and the\h Eiffel tower, both share concepts such as “left”,\h\h “solid” and others, even though they seem utterly\h different. Thus, meaning does not need to have\h\h any sort of ontological foundations, and it is\h a useless tool which is dead, according to his\h\h interpretation and succeeding in the original\h intent of Mach and the Vienna Circle. He even\h\h criticized some formulations of Quine’s philosophy\h of mathematics as having a metaphysical approach,\h\h however faintly. This anti-metaphysical linguistic\h perspective of epistemology is what is called\h\h “conceptual relativity”, where optional languages\h describe objects by convention of the given group\h\h dealing with it in specific contexts. And again\h similarly to Quine, this process would develop in\h\h a coherentist manner as our conceptual resources\h would interact with our capability of describing\h\h the world toward enhancing each other and thus\h allowing for greater knowledge of the world.\h\h Objectivity is then maintained – contrary to the\h first impression of the use of “relativity” –\h\h since it must occur within public discourse,\h and apparently contradicting definitions can\h\h also be sorted out by the same method. Mind, knowledge, and language become\h\h increasingly difficult to treat separately\h as the linguistic turn reaches widespread\h\h theories within – and even beyond – the analytic\h tradition.

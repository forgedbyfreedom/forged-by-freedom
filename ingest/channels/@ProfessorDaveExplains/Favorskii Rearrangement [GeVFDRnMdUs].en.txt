Next up in our survey of name reactions\h is the Favorskii rearrangement,\h\h not to be confused with the Favorskii reaction,\h which is actually something completely different.\h\h As applied to ring systems, it\h is a classical reaction inducing\h\h ring contraction, meaning that\h a ring is getting smaller.\h The reaction was invented by Russian chemist\h Alexei Favorskii in 1894. It applies to enolizable\h\h alpha-chloro ketones, including acyclic\h substrates, but it is used in synthesis\h\h mainly as a ring contraction tool.\h Let’s take a look at how this works now.\h To begin, we see formation of an enolate at the\h carbon opposite the halogen. Then the nucleophilic\h\h carbon from the enolate attacks the carbon that\h bears the chloro group, thus producing the very\h\h strained cyclopropanone intermediate. This\h is immediately attacked by the hydroxide base\h\h at the carbonyl carbon, and when these\h electrons come back down to reform the carbonyl,\h\h the new three-membered ring pops open, and the\h electrons in this bond remain with this carbon.\h\h This leaves a carboxylic acid with a carbanion\h moiety next door. Rapid proton transfer from\h\h the carboxyl group to the carbanion gives the\h following product. A key element of regiochemistry\h\h we must understand is that in a case such as\h this when the system is not symmetrical, opening\h\h of the ring usually occurs to yield the less\h substituted and therefore more stable carbanion.\h This mechanism is consistent with the fact\h that isomeric alpha-haloketones yield the\h\h same carboxylate, such as here where the\h structural difference between these two\h\h starting materials doesn’t matter, because\h enolate attack onto the bromide gives the\h\h same cyclopropanone in both cases, although it\h is too strained and unstable to be isolated.\h\h This scheme illustrates two other facts.\h First, the reaction works for alpha-bromo\h\h ketones as well as chloro ketones, although\h generally in lower yield. And second, when\h\h anhydrous alkoxides are used as bases, esters\h are obtained instead of the carboxylate salt.\h An elegant way to confirm that a symmetrical\h intermediate is formed is shown in the following\h\h scheme. Favorskii rearrangement gave the\h cyclopentanecarboxylate, in which the label\h\h was then found equally distributed in the\h alpha and the beta positions, confirming\h\h the intermediacy of the cyclopropanone, which\h can be opened both ways with equal probability.\h Applications for this chemistry in synthesis are\h plentiful, especially when a larger ring size is\h\h easy to make, and ring-contraction\h leads to rather strained systems.\h\h In Eaton’s famous synthesis\h of the very strained cubane,\h\h the final cube structure was established\h with a Favorskii ring contraction. The last\h\h step was a simple thermal decarboxylation,\h which yielded the target cubic structure.\h Another ring-contraction was applied to the lab\h synthesis of the poisonous alkaloid epibatidine,\h\h found in frogs. This is alpha-brominated to yield\h a mixture of exo and endo alpha-bromides.\h\h Ring contraction using methoxide yields the\h desired 2.2.1 skeleton from the 3.2.1 precursor.\h\h Rather standard chemistry then converts the ester\h to the desired target epibatidine as a racemate.\h With many ring-contraction strategies as\h applications, the Favorskii rearrangement\h\h continues to be a useful tool in the synthesis of\h natural products and other structures of interest.

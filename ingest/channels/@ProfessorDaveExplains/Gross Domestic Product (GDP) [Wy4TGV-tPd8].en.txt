In macroeconomics, the most common way economists\h and policymakers measure the health of an overall\h\h economy is by examining gross domestic product,\h or GDP. Gross means total, domestic means within\h\h a country’s borders, and product means the value\h of all goods and services produced. However,\h\h that’s a bit of an oversimplification,\h so let’s look at the complete definition.\h\h Gross domestic product is the value of all\h final goods and services produced within a\h\h country’s borders in a given year. Note the\h emphasis on “final” and “in a given year.”\h Final goods are also called “finished goods.” A\h final good is one that will not be sold again as\h\h part of some other good. If a baker buys flour,\h sugar, and butter, we don’t count those as final\h\h goods, because the baker will use those goods to\h make a cake. In other words, they are goods that\h\h aren’t finished yet, they are intermediate goods.\h Now, the cake the baker made using that flour,\h\h sugar, and butter, is a final good since the cake\h is the final product, and its final destination is\h\h the consumer. In other words, a customer will buy\h it and consume it. There are also goods that are\h\h used to make other goods but are still considered\h final goods. These are called “capital goods.”\h\h For example, if a farmer buys a combine to harvest\h his crops, the combine is considered a final good.\h\h Even though the combine is used to produce other\h goods, it won’t be sold again as part of another\h\h good. There are also capital goods used to provide\h services counted as final goods. For example, if a\h\h landscaper buys a chainsaw in order to take down\h trees, the chainsaw is considered a final good.\h GDP also only counts production in a given\h year. If an old car is sold this year,\h\h economists don’t count it as part of GDP since\h the car wasn’t produced this year. They only\h\h count new cars sold this year as part of GDP.\h Also, production is only counted if the good or\h\h service is produced within a country’s borders.\h For example, if you are in the United States and\h\h buy a shirt imported from Vietnam, that adds to\h Vietnam’s GDP, not the United States GDP. But a\h\h movie made in the United States that is shown in\h theaters in Vietnam adds to the United States GDP.\h It’s helpful to think of the entire economy\h of a country as one big hypermarket store,\h\h meaning they sell pretty much everything you could\h possibly ever want to buy. Not just products like\h\h food and clothing, but also services like pet\h grooming and tax preparation. Every time a\h\h consumer buys a final good or service, we record\h the cost, just like at a cash register. All the\h\h cost combined over a given year is the GDP. Government economists often divide final\h\h goods and services into four categories. Consumer\h goods and services, business goods and services,\h\h government goods and services, and net exports.\h Net exports are found by simply adding up exports\h\h and subtracting imports. Government economists can\h also calculate GDP using another method. Instead\h\h of adding up all the stuff that consumers,\h businesses, and the government buys, it can\h\h add up all the incomes in the economy. There is\h also nominal GDP and real GDP. Nominal GDP is\h\h measured in current prices. Real GDP is measured\h in constant, or unchanging prices. In other words,\h\h real GDP is nominal GDP but adjusted for\h inflation. Real GDP is a more accurate way\h\h to measure economic growth, since inflation can\h distort the actual value of goods and services.\h However, GDP is not a perfect way to assess the\h health of a country’s economy. First of all, GDP\h\h doesn’t take into account many things that could\h be considered part of an economy. For example,\h\h it doesn’t measure non-market activities, or goods\h and services that people make or do themselves,\h\h like mowing their own lawn or washing their own\h car. It doesn’t measure the underground economy,\h\h like in the black market, or market for illegal\h goods, as well as transactions that are legal\h\h but simply not reported. GDP also doesn’t measure\h unintended negative externalities. For example,\h\h a power plant that creates air pollution by\h emitting dust and smoke into the air is not\h\h subtracted from the GDP. GDP makes no adjustment\h for leisure time. For example, if workers get\h\h more time off in one country yet produce the\h same amount, they are not rewarded for it.\h\h GDP counts goods and services even when\h those goods and services are created after\h\h disasters. For example, if a tornado wipes out\h a building, GDP automatically increases when a\h\h new building takes its place. And finally, GDP\h doesn’t account for how goods and services are\h\h distributed. It doesn’t affect the GDP if lots\h is produced and most of it only goes to a small\h\h minority of the wealthiest individuals. One way economists attempt to calculate a\h\h slightly more accurate measure of a country’s\h economy is by figuring out its GDP per capita.\h\h Like the name suggests, to get GDP per capita\h you just take a country’s GDP and divide by\h\h its population. To quickly illustrate this\h difference, let’s look at Germany and China.\h\h In 2020, China’s GDP was 17.7 trillion USD and\h Germany's GDP was 3.8 trillion USD. However,\h\h Germany’s GDP per capita was 45,724 dollars and\h China’s GDP per capita was just 10,511 dollars.\h Some economists have called for alternatives to\h GDP, in order to more accurately assess the health\h\h of a country’s economy with a general emphasis\h on quality of life. Some of these alternatives\h\h include the Human Development Index, Inclusive\h Wealth Index, and Genuine Progress Indicator.\h\h Regardless, gross domestic product continues\h to be the go-to metric government policymakers\h\h rely on when planning for their country’s economic\h future, because institutions are better equipped\h\h to measure its components, and the clarity of\h its definition reduces the likelihood of infusing\h\h bias. In this way, GDP remains the primary way\h to quickly measure the health of an economy.

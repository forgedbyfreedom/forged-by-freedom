Professor Dave here, let’s revisit Grover Cleveland. As we have already learned, Grover Cleveland was the only president to serve two non-consecutive terms. For this reason, we regard him as both the 22nd and 24th president. The end of his first term saw Benjamin Harrison taking office, and while in office, Harrison passed the McKinley Tariff and the Sherman Silver Purchase Act. Cleveland claimed these were dangerous to the nation’s financial livelihood. He refrained from criticism at first, but by 1891 Cleveland felt compelled to speak out in an open letter to a meeting of reformers in New York. This “silver letter” put Cleveland back in the arena just as the 1892 election was approaching. In the rematch against Harrison, Cleveland triumphed and returned to the White House, just as his wife had predicted. Yet, just as Cleveland’s second term began, the Panic of 1893 produced a severe national depression, caused by Harrison’s disastrous monetary policies, exactly as Cleveland had predicted. Unfortunately, he was unable to reverse the crash and the Democrats were blamed for the ongoing depression. When Cleveland declined to run for a third term, the Democrats ran the agrarian populist William Jennings Bryan against the Republican William McKinley, who won in a landslide with a political realignment that ushered in the Progressive Era. Cleveland was a competent policymaker, but critics complained he had little imagination and seemed overwhelmed by the nation’s economic disasters, the depressions, and strikes in his second term. Even so, his reputation for honesty and his good character survived the troubles of his second term and he is generally ranked among the second tier of American presidents.

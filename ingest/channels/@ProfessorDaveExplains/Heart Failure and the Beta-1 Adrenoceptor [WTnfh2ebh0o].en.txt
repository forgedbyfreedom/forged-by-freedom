Activation of\h\h the beta-1 adrenoceptor increases both\h heart rate, or positive chronotropy,\h\h and force of contraction, or positive inotropy. Like the beta-2\h\h receptor, the beta-1 adrenoceptor couples to Gs,\h which dissociates from its G heterotrimer and\h\h activates the enzyme adenylate cyclase. Here there\h is enhanced conversion of ATP to cAMP which causes\h\h increased intracellular cAMP concentrations. cAMP\h increases the opening of the hyperpolarization\h\h cyclic nucleotide channels, as discussed in the\h previous tutorial, to increase heart rate. cAMP\h\h also activates protein kinase A, which leads\h to increased calcium release from intracellular\h\h stores such as from the endoplasmic reticulum.\h Protein kinase A also phosphorylates myosin, which\h\h combined with increased intracellular calcium,\h increases cardiac muscle contractility force.\h In the context of acting as antiarrhythmic\h agents, antagonists at the beta-1 receptor\h\h aim to slow down the heart rate by removing\h the basal innervation by endogenous adrenaline\h\h and noradrenaline, in an effort to re-establish\h normal coordinated rhythmic contractions of the\h\h heart. However, they also have profound medical\h benefit in reducing the effects of adrenaline and\h\h noradrenaline in order to reduce the overall work\h of the heart in treating chronic heart failure.\h Heart failure is characterized by the cardiac\h output of the heart being insufficient to supply\h\h the rest of the body with its demands\h of oxygenated blood. In other words,\h\h there is a gap between cardiac output and the\h demands of the rest of the body. Chronic heart\h\h failure is often managed with diuretics\h and vasodilators to reduce blood pressure,\h\h and as a consequence of the Frank-Starling law,\h reduces cardiac pre-load to reduce the work\h\h done by the heart and treat heart failure.\h It can be appreciated that cardiovascular\h\h pharmacology must be understood in a holistic\h sense while considering the various mechanisms\h\h of different drugs actions that are often used\h in tandem. For more information on diuretics\h\h and antihypertensives, please check out\h the previous tutorials on these drugs.\h However, relevant to this tutorial, beta\h blockers are also an important tool in managing\h\h long term heart failure. By reducing the tonic\h innervation of the sympathetic nervous system,\h\h beta blockers reduce the often upregulated\h sympathetic innervation of the heart and\h\h lead to reduced cardiac contractility\h force and overall heart rate. Critically,\h\h this translates to reduced metabolic demands of\h the heart, and as a result aids the treatment of\h\h vasodilator drugs in treating angina as well as\h chronic heart failure. Generally, stroke volume\h\h is unchanged by beta blockers since slowing the\h heart rate through beta-1 adrenoceptor antagonism\h\h increases diastolic filling time, which by the\h Frank-Starling law, maintains stroke volume.\h Conversely, acute heart failure usually requires\h an alternative approach, where cardiac output\h\h needs to be rapidly increased in order to reach\h sufficient supply of oxygenated blood to critical\h\h organs. Dobutamine\h is often used in cardiogenic shock and severe\h\h acute heart failure, and is a somewhat selective,\h high efficacy agonist for beta-1 adrenoceptors,\h\h and signals through the Gs coupled beta-1\h adrenoceptor via the mechanisms discussed\h\h earlier to cause both increased\h heart rate and contractility.

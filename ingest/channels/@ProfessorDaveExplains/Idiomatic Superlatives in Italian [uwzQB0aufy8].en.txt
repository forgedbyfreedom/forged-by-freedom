It’s Professor Dave, let’s learn more superlatives. We’ve learned a few ways to express superlatives, which allow us to state the extreme version of a certain quality. In the past we have done this either by using the adverb molto, or by adding the suffix “issimo”. For example, if something is very expensive, we could say “molto costoso”. If something is very sweet we could say dolcissimo. These are equivalently valid ways to form a superlative. Now let’s look at a couple other ways to form superlatives that are a little less literal and more idiomatic, as well as expressions for superlatives that are strictly idiomatic. These are more creative and are excellent ways to sound like a native speaker. For one example, we can simply repeat a word twice. If we have some bread that is extremely fresh, we could refer to it as: pane fresco fresco. This means not just fresh, but very fresh. We could also say pane bello fresco. This is kind of like “nice and fresh”. Sometimes we use special prefixes to form the superlative. And then beyond this, we can use fun and colorful idioms that may not always make so much sense when translated literally, but of course every language has such expressions. And with that, we now have a lot of ways to use superlatives. See if you can work them into your next Italian conversation!

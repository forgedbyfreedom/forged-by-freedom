Now let’s check out the only\h\h other group of extant jawless fish, the lampreys.\h Lampreys are found in both freshwater and\h\h saltwater environments throughout the world, and\h they are important ecologically as both predators\h\h and prey. Though they are most infamously known\h for their parasitic feeding habits, in which they\h\h attach themselves to other fish using their\h circular mouth and sharp teeth and then feed\h\h on their bodily fluids, only about 18 lamprey\h species are parasitic or carnivorous. The other\h\h 20 or so known species are filter feeders that\h consume small particles of food from the water.\h\h They are all members of class Petromyzontida,\h or more accurately, order Petromyzontiformes. In\h\h order to examine these animals, let’s check out\h their anatomy, life cycle, and classification.\h Lampreys have a long, cylindrical body that lacks\h true scales and is instead covered in a thin layer\h\h of skin. They have a cartilaginous skeleton that\h is reinforced by some bony elements, including the\h\h skull and several arches that support the gills.\h Instead of true vertebrae, they have a series of\h\h cartilaginous structures called arcualia arranged\h above the notochord. The mouth is surrounded by a\h\h sucker-like disc that is used to create a seal\h against the host fish in parasitic species,\h\h allowing the lamprey to feed. Their circular\h mouth is also equipped with a rasping tongue-like\h\h structure called the buccal funnel, which is\h used to rasp away flesh and create a wound\h\h on the host fish so that the lamprey can\h drain blood and other fluids. Meanwhile,\h\h non-carnivorous species use their teeth to scrape\h algae from rocks. Adult lampreys have large eyes,\h\h a single nostril atop their head, and seven gill\h pores on each side of the head. Juvenile forms,\h\h however, look entirely different. In addition to their anatomy, lampreys\h\h are well known for their life history which\h includes a radical shift in morphology,\h\h and diet, with respect to carnivorous species. All\h known species of lamprey swim against the current\h\h up freshwater rivers to breed. While freshwater\h lampreys, many of which are also non-parasitic,\h\h may live in the same stream their whole lives,\h marine lampreys are anadromous, meaning they leave\h\h the sea where they spend their adult lives and\h migrate up freshwater streams to spawn. In North\h\h America, lamprey spawn in the spring or early\h summer. Males arrive first and begin building\h\h a nest to later be joined by females. Mating is\h external and males fertilize sticky eggs as they\h\h are released, which then adhere to the pebbles\h of the nest and become lightly covered in sand.\h Adults die shortly after spawning and eggs\h hatch in about two weeks into tiny larvae\h\h known as ammocoetes, which closely resemble the\h cephalochordate amphioxus. In fact, they look\h\h so different from their parents that when they\h were first discovered they were thought to be\h\h an entirely different species. Like amphioxus,\h the ammocoetes display all ancestral chordate\h\h characteristics, and filter feed while wedged\h down in the sediment, much like cephalochordates.\h As they feed, they grow, and eventually after\h reaching a size of about one centimeter,\h\h they leave the gravel nest and drift downstream\h to burrow into a sandy area where they continue\h\h to filter organic material and slowly grow\h for another 3 to 7 years or more, until they\h\h rapidly metamorphosize into adults. This change\h is dramatic and involves the formation of eyes,\h\h a buccal funnel and keratinized teeth,\h enlargement of fins, modification of the\h\h gill openings and digestive system, and\h the maturation of gonads. At this point,\h\h some nonparasitic species cease feeding entirely,\h while others continue to feed on algae. Some\h\h parasitic species may remain in the stream, while\h others migrate to the sea and attach themselves\h\h to the body of a fish with their sucker like\h mouths, and rasp through their flesh to consume\h\h body fluids. Some species remain at this stage\h for up to three years before they begin their\h\h migration back upstream to their birthing grounds. Phylogenetically, lampreys are considered the\h\h sister group to the hagfish, and those clades\h combined are considered to be the sister group\h\h to all other vertebrates, namely those of clade\h Gnathostomata, the jawed vertebrates. There are\h\h only 10 living lamprey genera, and their phylogeny\h was recently reworked in 2023. The pouched\h\h lampreys and southern topeyed lampreys have\h long been considered basal to all other species.\h\h Pouched lampreys are often locally abundant and\h reach sizes of about 60 centimeters as adults, and\h\h are parasitic before becoming reproductive adults.\h Topeyed lampreys are often much rarer but have a\h\h similar life history. Of the remaining species,\h perhaps the most well known, in the Northern\h\h Hemisphere at least, is the 1-meter-long sea\h lamprey, also known as the “vampire fish.” It is\h\h infamous as an invasive species in the great lakes\h region where it feeds upon trout, damaging local\h\h food webs, and disrupting fishing industries. An\h individual lamprey has the potential of killing\h\h 40 pounds of fish through its 12–18 month feeding\h period. However, not all lampreys are parasitic\h\h carnivores that destroy fish stalks. Many, like\h the American brook lamprey, are harmless filter\h\h feeders for their entire lives, using their\h specialized oral hood to capture and filter\h\h food particles from the water.

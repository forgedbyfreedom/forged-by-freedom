Rheumatoid arthritis, or RA, is an autoimmune\h disease that occurs when a person’s immune system\h\h mistakenly attacks normal proteins\h within the body. The resulting chronic\h\h inflammation primarily affects the synovium,\h or lining of the joints, leading to swelling,\h\h stiffness, pain and deformation of the\h joints. In about 40% of individuals,\h\h symptoms may also occur within the skin, lungs,\h muscles, and cardiovascular system. RA affects\h\h about 1-1.5% of people within North America and\h is 3 times more prevalent in women than men.\h\h According to CDC, the annual medical costs\h and earnings losses associated with arthritis\h\h came in at 303.5 billion dollars in 2013, an\h amount equivalent to 1% of the gross domestic\h\h product of the United States that year. The\h need for anti-rheumatic drugs is immense,\h\h with a market size of 57 billion dollars in 2019\h which is expected to grow to 63 billion by 2027.\h Earlier in this series, we learned about NSAIDs,\h or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. These\h\h drugs are common treatments for RA symptoms.\h However, they do not slow the progression of\h\h the disease or limit joint deformation. Many\h patients, especially with moderate to severe RA,\h\h are prescribed disease-modifying antirheumatic\h drugs, or DMARDs. These drugs limit the\h\h underlying inflammation that causes RA, which\h not only decreases the pain associated with RA,\h\h but also prevents further damage to\h the joints, preserving their function.\h DMARDs consist of two different drug modalities:\h small molecule, and biologic. Small molecule drugs\h\h are small compounds which bind to and either\h activate or deactivate a protein of interest.\h\h They can be administered and readily\h absorbed through various routes that\h\h we discussed earlier in the series. On the\h other hand, biologic DMARDs, or bDMARDs,\h\h are antibodies, which are protein complexes that\h are significantly larger than small molecules.\h\h While antibodies are normally generated by our\h immune system to guard us from invading organisms,\h\h pharmacologists can hijack\h this biological response\h\h to generate antibodies which target select\h proteins of interest with incredible specificity.\h These two modalities differ greatly in\h their production, cost, administration,\h\h and side effects. As proteins, biologic\h DMARDs must be made using biological processes\h\h rather than by the significantly\h simpler and cheaper chemical processes\h\h for small molecules. This also complicates their\h administration as large proteins can’t be absorbed\h\h by normal routes of administration, and must\h be given by injection as often as once a week.\h\h Production, storage, and dosing difficulties\h contribute to the high expense of bDMARDs.\h\h As an example, Remicade, also known as infliximab,\h costs between 19 and 22 thousand dollars per year\h\h for treatment. Conversely, a treatment regimen of\h 1 to 2 small molecule DMARDs can achieve disease\h\h remission in many RA patients. Therefore,\h due to dosing difficulties and high costs,\h\h biologic DMARDs are only prescribed for moderate\h to severe RA in cases when non-biologic DMARDs\h\h fail to sufficiently relieve disease symptoms.\h Both modalities in this drug class are commonly\h\h combined with NSAIDs for the treatment\h of the pain associated with inflammation.\h Small molecule DMARDs are associated\h with a range of side effects.\h\h Due to the highly specific nature of the antibody\h interaction with proteins, the side effects are\h\h generally lower in this class however they have\h been associated with uncommon adverse events\h\h including decreased white or red blood cell\h counts, lymphoma, demyelinating disease,\h\h and heart failure. Both of these classes, however,\h are immunosuppressive and lead to an increased\h\h risk for various opportunistic infections\h by bacterial, fungal, or viral agents that\h\h would not cause infection in an individual with\h normal, uncompromised immune function. Though\h\h DMARDs were originally classified for the\h treatment of RA, their immunosuppressive effects\h\h make them efficacious treatments for many other\h immune disorders, including plaque psoriasis,\h\h ankylosing spondylitis, Crohn’s disease, and\h ulcerative colitis.

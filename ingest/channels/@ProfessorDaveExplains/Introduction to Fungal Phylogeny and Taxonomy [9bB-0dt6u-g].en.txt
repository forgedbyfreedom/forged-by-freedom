Our planet is a dynamic system that is home\h to an unthinkable number of living organisms.\h\h Within the vast expanse of diverse life on\h Earth, where do fungi fall into place? There are\h\h more than 120,000 species of fungi that have been\h classified by mycologists. In order to understand\h\h how they make sense of all of these species, it\h is essential to understand the modern system of\h\h biological classification called taxonomy. We have\h covered this in some detail in the biology series,\h\h so be sure to check out that tutorial if it\h sounds unfamiliar. Otherwise, to briefly review,\h\h there are generally eight or more levels that go\h into classifying an organism. In the direction of\h\h increasing specificity, these are domain, kingdom,\h phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species,\h\h and we refer to each of these levels as a taxon.\h Let’s work our way down these taxa in order to\h\h classify a particularly notorious mushroom, the\h fly agaric, or Amanita muscaria. So let’s start\h\h by zooming out as far as possible, considering\h the totality of all the living organisms on the\h\h planet. The broadest category of classification\h is a domain. There are three of these, which are\h\h Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. All fungi have\h nucleated cells, and therefore are eukaryotes,\h\h in contrast with the other two domains, which\h contain unicellular prokaryotic organisms.\h\h Thus, fungi fall into the Eukarya domain. Plants\h and animals are also eukaryotes, and there is a\h\h common misconception that plants and fungi are\h highly related in the tree of life. However,\h\h fungi are actually more related to animals\h than plants. So, scientists have classified\h\h animals and fungi in their own taxonomic lineage\h called opisthokonts. Within the opisthokonts,\h\h we can reach the next level of classification,\h and that is the kingdom which is encompassed by\h\h this series, the Fungi kingdom. Any organism\h within this kingdom is considered a fungus.\h\h Now from here, mycologists use structural\h patterns and genetic factors to classify\h\h each fungal species into their respective taxa. The taxon below the kingdom is called the phylum.\h\h The precise number of fungal phyla and their names\h has changed quite a bit over the past few decades,\h\h as mycology is a young and dynamic field, and\h there are some differing opinions as to which\h\h groups should qualify as phyla and which should\h be referred to merely as subphyla. In general,\h\h phyla will have the suffix “mycota” and\h subphyla will have the suffix “mycotina”,\h\h and in this series we will consider eight fungal\h phyla. These are Basidiomycota, Ascomycota,\h\h Chytridiomycota, Blastocladiomycota, Mucoromycota,\h Zoopagomycota, Glomeromycota, and Cryptomycota.\h There are individual characteristics within each\h of these phyla that allow us to differentiate\h\h between them, and we will discuss these as we\h move through the series. The Chytridiomycota,\h\h Blastocladiomycota, and Cryptomycota\h contain all the zoosporic fungi,\h\h or single-celled fungi which possess a flagellum\h at some point during their life cycles.\h\h The phyla Mucoromycota, Zoopagomycota,\h and Glomeromycota contain fungi which\h\h have lost the flagellum but have developed\h the ability to grow with filamentous hyphae.\h\h Finally, and most notably, the two phyla\h Basidiomycota and Ascomycota are often\h\h grouped together as the subkingdom Dikarya,\h also known as the higher fungi. This name\h\h refers to the pair of nuclei in the hyphae of a\h mycelium which has DNA from two individual fungi,\h\h and they are known as the higher fungi because\h these phyla produce the macroscopic mushrooms\h\h that you can see with the naked eye. So, most\h of the well-known fungi fall into the Dikarya\h\h subkingdom. As it is a multicellular fungus,\h the fly agaric falls into the subkingdom Dikarya\h\h and, more specifically, is placed into the\h phylum Basidiomycota. The main difference between\h\h mushrooms within the Basidiomycota and Ascomycota\h are the spore-producing structures, or hymenium,\h\h on their respective sporocarps or fruiting bodies.\h The sporocarps from the phylum Ascomycota are\h\h known as ascocarps and from Basidiomycota, they\h are known as basidiocarps. The ascocarps produce\h\h sacs of spores called asci and are therefore\h commonly known as sac fungi. The basidiocarps\h\h form club-like structures that produce spores\h called basidia and are therefore commonly known as\h\h club fungi. The fly agaric is a club fungus, so we\h classify it further into the phylum Basidiomycota.\h The taxon below a phylum is known as a class. Any\h class within the kingdom of fungi has the ending\h\h “mycete”. There are three subphyla within the\h phylum Basidiomycota, and within each of these,\h\h there are numerous classes. As we go down each\h taxon, the amount of specificity to classify\h\h needs to increase, which leads to innumerable\h divisions. We do not have time to cover each\h\h of these divisions in this tutorial, though many\h of them will be discussed as we move through the\h\h series. Regardless, we need to finish classifying\h our fly agaric, Amanita muscaria. This falls into\h\h the class Agaricomycetes and then is part of the\h order Agaricales. Any order will have the ending\h\h “ales”. Generally, the order Agaricales\h describes any mushroom with gills or lamellae.\h\h Within the order, the fly agaric is further\h classified into the Amanitaceae family. Whenever\h\h the taxon name ends with “aceae”, that name\h describes a taxonomic family. Knowing the family,\h\h we finally can reach the genus Amanita, and after\h that, the species muscaria. Just as when writing\h\h the official name of any other organism, for this\h fungus we include both the mushroom’s genus and\h\h species, italicizing both taxa, and capitalizing\h only the genus. This naming scheme is known as\h\h binomial nomenclature. So, the full scientific\h name of the fly agaric is Amanita muscaria.\h Any other mushroom will be classified just as we\h did with Amanita muscaria. Each division down the\h\h tree of life reveals the fundamental nature and\h properties of every studied organism. Within the\h\h kingdom of fungi, there are still millions\h of species that have yet to be classified.\h\h Only with the help of professional and\h amateur mycologists can we further this\h\h endeavor. Mycology still has so many mysteries\h within its branch on the ever-expanding tree of\h\h life. Let’s keep moving forward, so that we may\h gather the ability to unravel those mysteries.

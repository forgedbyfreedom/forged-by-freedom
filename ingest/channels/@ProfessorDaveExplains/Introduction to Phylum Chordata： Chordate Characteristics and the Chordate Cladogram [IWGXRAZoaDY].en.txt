At this point in the series, we have covered every\h single phylum in the animal kingdom except one.\h\h The last phylum left to go is Chordata, and this\h is the one that contains the animals that are the\h\h most familiar to us, as well as humans ourselves.\h There are about 81,600 species of chordates, which\h\h is quite a bit more than some of the minor phyla,\h the ones that we covered in a single tutorial,\h\h but nowhere near the diversity of the arthropods.\h They are also not the most numerous animals on\h\h our planet, as they are far outnumbered by\h the nematodes, nor are they the heaviest,\h\h since the weight of all crustaceans alone outweigh\h all vertebrates. Nevertheless, there is tremendous\h\h diversity in this phylum. Chordates range in\h form from sea squirts to monitor lizards, from\h\h eels to primates, and from sharks to birds. Their\h ecological footprint is massive, present in nearly\h\h every habitat on earth, from the deep seas to\h the polar regions, and even outer space, given\h\h the existence of astronauts. They are prominent in\h the land, sea, and air, and they have changed our\h\h world to an astonishing degree. In order to study\h them, instead of continuing with the cladogram for\h\h the entire animal kingdom, from this point forward\h we will use the chordate cladogram, so that we\h\h can better visualize the diversity within. It is\h generally accepted that there are five chordate\h\h characteristics, that when taken together, set\h the chordates apart from all other phyla. The\h\h five chordate characteristics, also known as the\h five chordate synapomorphies, are the notochord,\h\h dorsal tubular nerve cord, pharyngeal pouches\h or gill slits, endostyle, and post-anal tail.\h The notochord is a flexible rod that extends\h through the length of the body. It is a major\h\h location for muscle attachment, and since\h it is flexible, it allows for undulatory\h\h movements. In all vertebrates, it is replaced\h by series of cartilaginous or bony vertebrae\h\h that form the characteristic vertebral column.\h The dorsal tubular nerve cord is dorsal to the\h\h digestive tract and begins as a hollow tube. In\h vertebrates, the anterior end becomes enlarged\h\h to form the brain and is protected by the skull.\h The tube itself becomes the spinal cord, which\h\h passes through the protective neural arches of the\h vertebrae. The pharyngeal pouches are also known\h\h as gill slits, which lead from the outside of the\h body. It evolved as a filter-feeding apparatus\h\h and is easily visible in the lancelets. In the\h vertebrates they have developed into the jaws and\h\h jaw support structures. The endostyle secretes\h mucus that traps food particles brought into\h\h the pharyngeal cavity. In most chordates, like\h the vertebrates, it has been modified into the\h\h thyroid gland. The postanal tail provides motility\h for many larval tunicates and lancelets. Since\h\h it is a structure added to the body behind the\h digestive tract, it likely evolved specifically\h\h for propulsion in water. In many aquatic forms,\h such as the fish, cetaceans, and crocodilians,\h\h it has been highly modified for increased\h propulsion. In some terrestrial forms, like cats\h\h and squirrels, it is used for balance. In others,\h like many primates, such as spider monkeys,\h\h it can be used to grasp objects almost like a\h fifth limb. In still others, such as the birds,\h\h it aids in flight. In humans it is only visible as\h a vestige known as the coccyx, a series of small\h\h vertebrae near the end of the vertebral column. Now let’s familiarize ourselves a bit with the\h\h chordate cladogram. All the clades shown here\h are members of the phylum Chordata, so they all\h\h share the five chordate characteristics. These\h members, the tunicates and vertebrates, are all\h\h considered to be members of Olfactores, due to\h their advanced olfactory system. The vertebrates,\h\h or craniates, include both the jawless fish, such\h as lampreys and hagfish, and all the gnathostomes.\h\h Gnathostomata includes the jawed vertebrates.\h The gnathostomes are further broken down\h\h into the cartilaginous fish, Chondrichthyes,\h including sharks and rays, and the boney fish,\h\h Osteichthyes. The Osteichthyes include all other\h fish, and the tetrapods. Members of Osteichthyes\h\h have skeletons composed of mineralized bone\h tissue, while the Chondrichthyes have skeletons\h\h primarily composed of cartilage. Osteichthyes\h is further broken down into Actinopterygii,\h\h the ray-finned fish, which is the vast majority\h of animals we think of as fish, and Sarcopterygii\h\h which are the lobe-finned fish: which are the\h coelacanths, lungfish, and tetrapods. These are\h\h characterized by having fleshy, lobed, paired\h fins which are joined to the body by a single\h\h bone. These appendages are highly modified by the\h tetrapods into four limbs. The tetrapods are then\h\h split into the amphibians and the amniotes. The\h amniotes are characterized by the development\h\h of three extraembryonic membranes, and they are\h further split into Synapsida, whose only surviving\h\h members are the mammals, and Sauropsida which\h includes the non-avian reptiles and the birds.\h This cladogram highlights evolutionary\h relationships, but it may be a bit confusing.\h\h For example, all the jawed vertebrates are\h more closely related to each other than to\h\h the jawless vertebrates, which makes sense, but\h this also means that all bony jawed vertebrates\h\h are technically “bony fish.” What we normally\h think of as “fish” are actually the ray-finned\h\h fish of Actinopterygii. In addition, some\h animals have secondarily lost ancestral\h\h characteristics. A good example are the snakes of\h Lepidosauromorpha. Modern snakes have no limbs,\h\h and they kind of look like eels. Eels are\h members of Actinopterygii, and they kind of\h\h look like lampreys, but the lampreys are\h jawless. So then, snakes are tetrapods,\h\h eels are ray-finned fish, and lampreys aren’t even\h considered to be jawed fish. How is this possible?\h It all relates to evolution and common ancestry.\h The fossil record, DNA sequencing, and comparative\h\h morphological analyses all indicate that the\h ancestors of snakes had four limbs. Some modern\h\h snakes still have the remnants of their hind\h limbs, known as spurs. Snakes are amniotes,\h\h some of which give birth to live young. They also\h breathe air, and have dry, scaly skin. Meanwhile,\h\h the eels are highly adapted ray-finned fish\h that have gills, live and breathe in the water,\h\h and lay eggs. Lampreys, meanwhile, have no\h jaws, no mineralized skeleton, and external\h\h gill slits. Both are distantly related to\h the snakes. Snakes are more closely related\h\h to primates and dogs than they are to eels, even\h though eels and snakes and lampreys look somewhat\h\h similar. This is due to convergent evolution.\h Snakes, eels, and lampreys have similar forms,\h\h but different evolutionary pathways. These\h are known as analogous characteristics,\h\h like how birds, bats, and insects all fly, but\h the underlying structures are different. The\h\h converse of this is homologous structures,\h which may vary in appearance, while their\h\h underlying structures suggest common ancestry. Remember that in a cladogram, the more shared\h\h characteristics, the more related the organisms\h are. For example, snakes and humans are both\h\h chordates, Olfactores, vertebrates,\h Osteichthyes, Sarcopterygii, tetrapods,\h\h and amniotes. Meanwhile, snakes and eels are\h both chordates, Olfactores, vertebrates, and\h\h Osteichthyes. If you know where an organism sits\h on the chordate cladogram, you can figure out how\h\h closely or distantly related it is to others.

There are fewer than 35 species of extant\h\h cephalochordates, all of which are found in the\h class Leptocardii, and the order Amphioxiformes.\h\h They are commonly known as “lancelets” or\h “amphioxi”, the singular being amphioxus.\h\h Extant forms are small, only 3 to 7 centimeters\h in length, slender, laterally compressed,\h\h transluscent, and found in sandy sediments\h of coastal waters around the world. Let’s go over their anatomy\h\h and physiology, ecology, and phylogeny. The anatomy of a lancelet is well-known,\h\h and often used as a model when learning\h about the five chordate characteristics.\h\h These characteristics are evident in an adult\h lancelet, and its body plan is considered to\h\h be ancestral for all chordates. Lancelets swim\h using their segmented muscles called myomeres,\h\h which attach directly to the notochord. Their\h movement is helped along by long flaps known as\h\h metapleural folds, but they lack the paired fins\h that are characteristic of the jawed vertebrates.\h\h In order to feed, lancelets keep themselves\h partially buried in fine sediments, with only\h\h their anterior end exposed. Water enters the\h mouth, driven by cilia in the buccal cavity and\h\h pharynx, and passes through the pharyngeal gill\h slits where food is trapped in mucus secreted by\h\h the endostyle, and then moved into the gut where\h food particles are separated from mucus and passed\h\h into the hepatic cecum where they are digested. Lancelets have a closed circulatory system,\h\h but lack a heart and pigmentation. They also lack\h a respiratory system, and instead exchange gases\h\h directly through their skin. Thus, the pharyngeal\h gill slits function in food capture rather than\h\h respiration. The lancelet nervous system is\h centered around their dorsal hollow nerve chord\h\h that lies above the notochord. This notochord also\h extends into the head where it is only slightly\h\h larger than in the rest of the body. Though the\h sensory system of the lancelets is often touted\h\h as simple, they do have numerous photosensitive\h organs, which include a single anterior ocellus,\h\h or frontal eye, and other cells and organs\h that line the body. Curiously, lancelets also\h\h express green fluorescent proteins in their oral\h tentacles, and near the frontal eye that may play\h\h a role in attracting planktonic organisms.\h Lancelets are dioecious, and fertilization\h\h takes place externally in the water column.\h Fertilized eggs become ciliated neurula larvae\h\h that elongate and metamorphosize into adults. As adults, lancelets are benthic filter feeders\h\h that can swim when threatened or to find a\h better feeding location. They can be quite\h\h numerous locally, and when disturbed can quickly\h leave their burrow, swim a short distance, then\h\h rapidly burrow again, posterior end first, into\h the substrate. Though as adults they primarily\h\h feed on plankton, as larvae, they themselves\h are part of the planktonic community and may\h\h drift for great distances before settling into\h the substrate.

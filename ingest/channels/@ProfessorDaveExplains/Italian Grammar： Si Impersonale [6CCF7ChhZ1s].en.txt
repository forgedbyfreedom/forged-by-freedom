It’s Professor Dave, let’s learn the impersonal pronoun. The word “si” can be very confusing, because Italian has multiple uses for this word, and they are very different from one another. Here are the ways we have already seen this word. Now we are going to add two more uses for this word, the first of which is the impersonal pronoun, or si impersonale. This pronoun is used when there is no precise subject, just a generic one, you, we, people, or everybody. It is used especially with intransitive verbs, reflexives, with adjectives, or transitive verbs with no direct object expressed. In the past tense the auxiliary will always be essere. This has many translations. Or identically, do you go by car, where you is the general non-specific you. Or even, do people go by car? The most common expressions that use si impersonale are as follows. Impersonal construction may include potere, dovere, or volere, followed by an infinitive. Notice that in this context, “you” does not mean you in the normal sense, it is the general you, as if to actually say “one must speak softly”, as a rule that applies to everyone. Again, the question is not how you specifically say it, it’s more like “how does one say”. Indeed you must sleep well, because anyone must sleep well in such a comfortable bed. You always know when you leave, but never when you arrive. Universal wisdom, indeed. Now we must remark that when an impersonal construction is used with a reflexive verb, the pronoun “ci” replaces “si” in order to avoid using the pronoun “si” twice in succession.

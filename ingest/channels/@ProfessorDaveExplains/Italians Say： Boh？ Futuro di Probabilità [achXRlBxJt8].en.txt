It’s Professor Dave, let’s discuss Italian probability. We’ve looked at two future tenses, the simple future and the future perfect. These tenses descibe actions that happen at some point in the future. But the Italian language also has a different type of future, which is completely idiomatic, and does not have any corresponding tense in English. This is called the future of probability, because rather than a future reality, it expresses a speculation or supposition. English uses many different tenses and expressions in order to convey speculations. All of these expressions clearly indicate that the person speaking doesn’t know the answer with certainty, but still wants to give a speculative answer. In Italian, the use of the future tense in this context, together with some classic Italian facial expressions, can do exactly the same thing. Often times, especially in a colloquial environment, one can also add the interjection: BOH? This word alone, which is rather colloquial, implies that the statement that follows is strictly hypothetical. Of course it’s fun to include the word boh, but we don’t necessarily have to. This kind of speculation can also be in the past, in which case the future perfect is substituted. Don’t forget, a great way to sound very Italian very fast is to use words like boh along with their accompanying facial expressions and gesticulations. A well timed boh will allow you to blend right in. A similar word that can be used to express a lack of knowledge, or almost a begrudging admission of ignorance, would be “mah?”. This is kind of like saying “mmMMmm” whilst shrugging ones shoulders. You’re starting to sound pretty Italian, so let’s move on to some more vocabulary.

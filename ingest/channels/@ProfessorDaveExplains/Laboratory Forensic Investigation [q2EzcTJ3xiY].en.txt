We’ve spent several tutorials going over the\h details of crime scene investigation. While CSI\h\h dealt with the recovery of evidence, laboratory\h investigation deals with the actual examination\h\h of evidence. The responsibility of the crime scene\h investigator ends once it reaches the laboratory\h\h safely. At this stage, it is the laboratory’s\h duty to ensure the evidence is immediately\h\h preserved for analysis to avoid degradation and\h contamination. The first step is to identify all\h\h the evidence according to the labels on their\h packaging. Then, they are sorted and forwarded\h\h to their respective divisions in the laboratory.\h For example, biological evidence is sent to the\h\h biology and serology division, fingerprints are\h sent to the fingerprint examination division,\h\h handwriting evidence is sent to the questioned\h document division, firearms and gunpowder evidence\h\h is sent to the ballistics division. Once the\h packaged evidence reaches their respective\h\h divisions, they are opened and labeled a second\h time. Every division in the laboratory has its\h\h own system of labeling. The main reason for\h this is to avoid confusion with the evidence\h\h belonging to other divisions. The next step is\h to determine a protocol of analysis. For this,\h\h the laboratory investigator communicates with the\h CS investigator to understand the observations\h\h related to the case. This is where the notes,\h photographs, sketches, and video play an important\h\h role in placing the evidence to be analyzed in\h the context of the crime. Along with this, the CS\h\h investigator will discuss what kind of information\h is relevant or irrelevant for the investigation.\h\h So the plan of action by the laboratory\h will depend on facts surrounding the case,\h\h the nature of the crime, and information relevant\h to the crime including witness statements. The CS\h\h investigator communicates a list of questions\h and doubts regarding the particular evidence.\h\h They know best how the evidence might be helpful\h for further investigation. Here, the investigator\h\h should be as inquisitive as possible, posing\h questions which may sound very obvious. However,\h\h this is not the same for professionals in\h the courtroom. Officials including judges,\h\h jury, the prosecutor, and the defense counsel\h depend on evidence analysis to make a judgement\h\h or an argument because they often lack basic\h knowledge related to the evidence. So, they need\h\h to be thoroughly informed about the evidence. Now, let us understand what kinds of questions\h\h are posed by the investigator to the laboratory.\h If a piece of bone is submitted for analysis to\h\h the biology division, the list of questions will\h include: 1) Is the material a bone? 2) If yes,\h\h does it belong to a human or non-human? 3) If it\h is human, does it belong to a male or a female? 4)\h\h What is the approximate age of the person? 5)\h What is the estimated stature of the body? 6)\h\h Are there any marks of injury on the bone? 7) Are\h the injuries pathological, caused due to disease,\h\h or mechanical, inflicted by impact? 8)\h If there is an injury, what could be the\h\h age of the injury? 9) What could be the time\h since death based on the decaying of the bone?\h Broadly speaking, evidence analysis includes three\h stages: 1) identification, 2) individualization,\h\h and 3) reconstruction. We need to understand the\h characteristics of the evidence before learning\h\h about these stages. When we are analyzing\h evidence, we’re looking for two types of\h\h characteristics which will help us in further\h stages of the investigation. The first type\h\h is class characteristics. These are the features\h of the substance which confirm that the evidence\h\h belongs to a particular class of evidence. The\h second type of characteristics is individual\h\h characteristics. Just as the name implies, these\h characteristics include certain features that\h\h will make the evidence unique from all other\h substances belonging to that particular class.\h\h Their utility in investigation depends only on our\h technological limitations. Recall one of the most\h\h important principles of forensic science, the law\h of individuality. All types of evidence possess\h\h some characteristics that make them unique. These\h characteristics help in identifying the source of\h\h the evidence, thus pointing towards the individual\h to whom the evidence belongs. Let us consider a\h\h piece of fiber recovered as evidence and sent\h to the laboratory. It is identified as a cotton\h\h fiber upon microscopic examination because of\h certain structural characteristics similar to\h\h cotton. However, is that information enough to\h identify which shirt it came from? Obviously,\h\h not. For that, we will have to conduct some more\h rigorous analysis, such as extraction of the dye\h\h from the fiber, identifying the nature of the\h dye by chromatography, and so on. If these dye\h\h characteristics are found to be similar to those\h of the fibers recovered from the suspect’s shirt,\h\h it will be a case of positive identification.\h Therefore, in this example, the characteristics\h\h that identify the fiber as cotton are class\h characteristics, while the specific dye\h\h characteristics identifying the source of the\h fiber are known as individual characteristics.\h Now, let's go back to the stages of laboratory\h investigation, as we will be able to understand\h\h them better. The first stage is identification,\h which is identifying evidence by examination\h\h of class characteristics. The second stage is\h individualization, which is the identification\h\h of the source of evidence by examination\h of individual characteristics and their\h\h comparison with control samples. Both stages\h should be carried out one after the other.\h\h Though mere identification of the evidence\h will not help in determining its exact source,\h\h it definitely does assist in excluding other\h substances of origin. Thus, identification is\h\h essential for individualization as it limits\h the individual characteristics to a particular\h\h type of evidence. In cases involving the\h identification of drugs and poisons on different\h\h materials or human viscera, identification of\h evidence is enough to incriminate a suspect,\h\h and individualization is not needed. The third stage of evidence analysis\h\h is reconstruction. Here, by analyzing\h all the evidence from all the divisions,\h\h the investigator forms a theory regarding\h how the crime must have transpired. However,\h\h like identification and individualization,\h this stage does not restrict itself within the\h\h confines of time and place. It is not necessary\h at all for reconstruction to start only after\h\h identification and individualization, nor\h is it necessary for reconstruction to be\h\h conducted only in the laboratory. Many types\h of reasoning, logic, and different stages of\h\h investigation will be involved while\h reconstructing a crime. Reconstruction\h\h itself consists of a number of steps.

Homo naledi is a very strange hominin due\h\h to its odd morphology and behavior, to the\h degree that when it was initially unearthed,\h\h prior to the dating of the fossils, it was thought\h to be a very early member of genus Homo. Homo\h\h naledi presents a mosaic of basal and derived\h features. The body proportions are derived,\h\h looking much like modern humans, as is the\h morphology of the feet. The hands display a\h\h long thumb, like that seen in late genus Homo, and\h the shape of the skull is similarly derived. The\h\h arms are primitive and long, with adaptations for\h climbing maintained, and the fingers are curved.\h\h This hominin also isn’t very sexually dimorphic,\h a more derived condition. Its teeth are totally unique,\h with notched incisors and a small overall size.\h So far we have a mosaic hominin: a bipedal\h creature that maintains climbing ability,\h\h with an Australopith-sized brain evidently wired\h like genus Homo. But it gets even weirder. Homo\h\h naledi is found in a cave system with virtually no\h other animals, which led researchers to initially\h\h propose that it may be a burial site rather\h than a predator cache. Recently the team at\h\h Rising Star has released pictures of old hearths\h in the cave, which in accordance with the solitary\h\h presence of Homo naledi at the site, suggests\h that this hominin was using fire in the caves.\h\h This work has not yet been peer-reviewed, but it\h would lend credence to the idea that Homo naledi\h\h may have behaved much like a member of genus\h Homo indeed, despite its primitive adaptations.\h On the other side of the world from 190-50\h thousand years ago we have Homo floresiensis.\h\h This hominin was discovered in Liang Bua cave\h on the Indonesian island of Flores in 2003,\h\h and oddly enough is also known from\h 15 individuals. Homo floresiensis, like Homo naledi, has a\h\h mosaic morphology. Its small size means\h it also had a small brain: just 380 cc’s,\h\h within the range of both Australopiths and\h chimpanzees.

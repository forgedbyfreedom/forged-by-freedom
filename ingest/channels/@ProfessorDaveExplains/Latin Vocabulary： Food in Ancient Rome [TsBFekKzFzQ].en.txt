According to Petronius,\h wealthy Romans would enjoy three main meals.\h Jēntāculum-ī: This was a meal similar to\h our breakfast, except that the Romans,\h\h like today’s Italians, would not eat too much\h at this hour. This light meal would mainly\h\h include some type of wheat bread, perhaps\h accompanied by fresh fruit or dry dates.\h Prandium-iī: This was a midday\h meal, usually cold, sometimes\h\h involving the leftovers from the previous day. Cēna-ae: This was definitely the main meal of the\h\h day. For the rich it could be a long entertaining\h meal, lasting until late at night. It would have\h\h many courses, consumed in the triclīnium-iī of\h their wealthy house, lying on couches often built\h\h from cement but made comfortable with pillows and\h mattresses filled with feathers. Since the Romans\h\h didn’t have utensils, and ate with their fingers,\h slaves would constantly come around to wash their\h\h master’s hands. This meal would also include\h regular wine, vīnum-ī, or a sweet wine made\h\h with raisins, passum-ī. On the menu you would find\h appetizers - gustātiōnēs-um - like boiled eggs,\h\h salad, or small bites of fish, often accompanied\h by mulsum-ī, a wine flavored with honey. Among\h\h the vegetables we have mentions of\h carrots - carōta-ae, in several colors\h\h like purple or white but not orange,\h cabbage - brassica-ae, onions - caepa-ae,\h\h kales and broccoli, all from the brassica family,\h asparagus - asparagus-ī , and beet leaves rather\h\h than roots - bēta-ae. Many of these items are\h evident in paintings at Pompeii. But for the very\h\h rich the main attraction was meat. Unlike Greece,\h in ancient Rome beef was not too common. It was\h\h often replaced by pork - caro suilla, including\h sausages, lamb - agnus-ī, venison - dāma-ae,\h\h goat - capra-ae, boar - aper-rī, suckling\h pig, poultry, like chicken - pullus-ī,\h\h duck - ānas-ātis, and goose - ānser-eris. Seafood was also very\h\h important. Wealthy Romans would eat red mullet\h - mullus-ī, oysters - ostrea-ae, and all kinds\h\h of shellfish, as well as lobster - cammarus-ī.\h Then we get to fruits. We are fortunate enough to read this information\h\h about Roman meals in Apicius, a collection\h of Roman recipes bearing the name of a Roman\h\h foodie called Marcus Gavius Apicius. Another\h great source, as we mentioned previously,\h\h is the Latin author Petronius, author of\h Satyricon. Hard boiled\h\h eggs were known in Roman times as appetizers.\h Fava beans - faba-ae, lentils - lēnticula-ae, and\h\h chickpeas - cicer-eris in several varieties would\h provide inexpensive protein.

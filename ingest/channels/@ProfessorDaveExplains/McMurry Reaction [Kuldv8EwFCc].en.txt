Olefins, also known as alkenes,\h can of course be made by a number of routes\h\h which do not involve skeletal changes, but simply\h refunctionalizations, like elimination reactions,\h\h or redox reactions. Here, however, we will\h be talking about olefinations which involve\h\h skeletal construction steps. We will likely recall that the product,\h\h in general, will exist as a mixture of E and\h Z alkenes, and indeed one important theme in\h\h this area of research is E or Z selectivity: how\h to achieve it, and which parameters control it.\h We will begin with a reaction that is relatively\h recent, at least compared with many of the\h\h reactions we have been discussing in this series.\h This is the McMurry reaction, invented in 1974\h\h by American chemist John McMurry of Cornell\h University in Ithaca, NY. In this particular case,\h\h with reference to the scheme previously\h discussed, both X and Y are oxygen atoms,\h\h and the McMurry reaction is the reductive coupling\h of two carbonyl compounds to yield an olefin. The\h\h reagent is usually a low-valent titanium species,\h which is generated in situ by some reducing agent.\h The first step indeed involves reducing titanium\h trichloride or titanium tetrachloride in an ether\h\h solvent like THF and under scrupulously anhydrous\h and oxygen-free conditions. The reducing agent\h\h initially used by McMurry was lithium aluminum\h hydride, but recently other chemists have\h\h introduced lithium, sodium, potassium, magnesium,\h zinc, zinc-copper couple, and other metals.\h\h It is not clear which reduced species of titanium\h is the active one. In the case of LiAlH4,\h\h a species like HTiCl, complexed with THF, was\h isolated and shown to promote the coupling,\h\h but it is not clear that in every case the\h active species is a titanium(II) reagent.\h\h Thus, the mechanism that is usually written\h is rather vague. The latter then\h\h eliminate to give E and Z olefins and some complex\h titanium species by an unspecified mechanism.\h From what we have seen so far with this\h reaction, it is clear that it may be limited to\h\h homo-coupling reactions, as two different\h ketones would yield a mixture of four\h\h products with little hope of controlling the\h product distribution. It is also clear that the\h\h problem of E/Z isomerism is a major one, like in\h every other olefination reaction we will explore.\h\h Due to these difficulties, the McMurry reaction\h has found application primarily in intramolecular\h\h reactions, where it has been used in complex\h natural product synthesis. In such a situation,\h\h it is the intramolecular nature, and therefore the\h more favorable entropy of activation, that leads\h\h preferentially to the desired hetero-coupling\h product, with a double bond geometry\h\h corresponding to the less strained isomer. As an example, let us highlight the synthesis\h\h of Compactin, a natural cholesterol-lowering\h agent, carried out in the group of Professor\h\h Derrick Clive at the University of Alberta,\h Canada. This is a complex bicyclic substance\h\h featuring seven stereogenic centers, and\h it involves much sophisticated chemistry.\h\h The key step is an intramolecular McMurry\h reaction creating the bicyclic dienic structure.\h\h Clive’s group carefully optimized the\h reaction to achieve a very good 85% yield,\h\h and found that potassium on graphite was the\h ideal reducing agent for titanium trichloride.\h\h The diene product was then elaborated\h to Compactin through five simple steps.\h The McMurry reaction is not limited to ketones\h and aldehydes. Other carbonyl compounds,\h\h such as esters and amides, can in some\h cases undergo the McMurry reaction,\h\h allowing for the synthesis of heterocycles.\h For example, the reaction is especially useful\h\h for building hindered indoles in good yields using\h the standard titanium trichloride/zinc protocol.\h\h Similarly, benzofurans can also be made this\h way, as ketoesters are also good substrates.\h One detail that should also be mentioned is that\h the McMurry reaction is uniquely suitable for\h\h the preparation of very strained olefins, meaning\h olefins which have four very bulky substituents.\h\h These are particularly difficult to prepare by\h other means. For example, olefins 1-3 have been\h\h prepared quite easily via the McMurry reaction.\h Compound 4, however, tetra tert-butyl ethylene,\h\h has so far resisted preparation by this route or\h by any other means. It is an extremely strained\h\h product that perhaps may never be prepared. Due to the wide availability and versatility\h\h of carbonyl compounds, their coupling to form\h olefins via the McMurry reaction has found\h\h extensive use in organic synthesis, and continues\h to enjoy synthetic extensions, as well as thorough\h\h physico-chemical studies to clarify the details\h of its intriguing and very complex mechanism.

The Nef reaction was reported by Swiss\h chemist Johann Nef in 1894. It involves\h\h the hydrolysis of a nitronate salt to yield\h a carbonyl compound and nitrous oxide.\h\h The reaction is general for primary nitronates,\h which yield aldehydes, and secondary ones, which\h\h yield ketones. And in fact, the first reported\h example involved nitroethane to form acetaldehyde.\h Although this is essentially a degradative\h reaction and not a skeleton-building reaction,\h\h there is a reactivity aspect which\h makes the Nef reaction extremely useful.\h\h Due to the high carbon acidity and\h nucleophilic reactivity of nitroalkanes,\h\h the reaction can be incorporated\h into a strategy called “Umpolung”,\h\h a German word introduced by Professor Dieter\h Seebach of ETH Zurich and U.S. Nobelist\h\h E. J. Corey of Harvard University to designate\h a “reverse of polarity”. We will introduce the\h\h Umpolung concept in this tutorial, to further\h elucidate the importance of the Nef reaction.\h The accepted mechanism for the Nef reaction\h is as follows. We must preface this by stating\h\h that alkyl nitro derivatives are rather acidic\h carbon acids. Their pKa of around 10 makes them\h\h comparable to common oxygen acids like phenols\h or nitrogen acids like trialkyl ammonium ions.\h\h This means we can make their salts with a strong\h base, like sodium hydroxide, and these salts are\h\h even isolable. These are called nitronates and\h they are the starting point of the Nef reaction.\h\h In a strongly acidic medium like 4 normal\h sulfuric acid, kinetic protonation occurs,\h\h not at carbon to yield the starting nitroalkane\h again, but rather at the oxygen, yielding the aci\h\h form, which is a tautomer of the nitroalkane in\h which a C-H bond has been replaced by an O-H bond.\h\h This form is immediately protonated\h again at the other oxygen atom to yield\h\h the conjugate acid of the nitroalkane.\h This looks a lot like an iminium salt,\h\h which you may recall are easily hydrolyzed\h in water because they are very electrophilic.\h\h Here, water is the only nucleophile, and\h attack of water over two steps leads to\h\h loss of the nitrogen-containing fragment, which\h ultimately yields nitrous oxide gas and water.\h We should be aware that the major side product\h in the Nef reaction is usually a hydroxamic acid,\h\h which is an amide with a hydroxyl on the nitrogen.\h This is especially the case if more concentrated\h\h sulfuric acid is used, around 10 molar. It\h appears likely that under these conditions,\h\h the nitroalkane is doubly protonated, which means\h the nitronate is triply protonated, which leads\h\h to a different pathway. Loss of a proton can\h occur from the carbon atom next to the nitrogen,\h\h which yields a hydroxy nitrilium ion. This is\h rapidly hydrated to give the hydroxamic acid.\h\h If conditions are extreme, this can even be\h hydrolyzed to give the carboxylic acid. Thus,\h\h the Nef reaction must be run under well-calibrated\h acidic conditions to avoid side reactions.\h Now let us get back to some important terminology.\h In the Nef reaction, nitroalkanes are Umpolung\h\h reagents. An Umpolung reagent is one which\h allows us to carry out synthetic operations\h\h through a reversal of polarity. For example,\h an aldehyde contains a carbonyl group which\h\h is electrophilic and reacts rapidly with strong\h nucleophiles, like an enolate. After protonation\h\h we get the aldol product, which should be\h very familiar from earlier in the series.\h\h Thus, a carbonyl can act as an electrophile,\h whereas the carbon atom next to it is deprotonated\h\h by strong bases and acts as a nucleophile.\h This is the normal, expected polarity.\h Another operation, where the carbonyl carbon\h acts as a nucleophile would be called “reverse\h\h polarity” and would be very hard to do directly.\h This is because it is quite difficult to produce\h\h acyl anions and use them synthetically. If\h this were possible, the following reactions\h\h could occur. These would result in either an\h alpha-hydroxycarbonyl or gamma-keto dicarbonyl\h\h compound, as opposed to a beta-hydroxycarbonyl or\h delta-keto dicarbonyl compound which is obtained\h\h using a normal-polarity reaction like the\h aldol condensation or Michael addition.\h\h This is due to the fact that it would\h be the carbonyl carbon attacking\h\h rather than the alpha carbon, and if this specific\h spatial arrangement for these functional groups\h\h were desirable for a particular synthesis,\h it would be tremendously useful to be able\h\h to achieve such a transformation. We can indeed\h perform transformations resembling these ones\h\h through an Umpolung approach. This is\h what we mean by reversal of polarity.\h\h By somehow causing the carbonyl carbon, which\h is usually electron-deficient and electrophilic,\h\h to become electron-rich and nucleophilic,\h that would change the types of chemistry\h\h it would be capable of doing and thus\h the types of products that could form.\h Let us illustrate the Umpolung concept with\h the Michael addition of nitronates to enones.\h\h An example is shown in the synthesis of\h cyclopentenones. Under basic conditions the\h\h nitro compound forms the nitronate, which reacts\h with the enone, undergoing a Michael addition,\h\h just as though this had been a regular enolate.\h The product can be treated with base to again\h\h obtain the nitronate, which undergoes the Nef\h reaction under acidic conditions to give the\h\h dione. Notice the resulting spatial relationship\h of these carbonyls, which is not what we would\h\h expect had we used a ketone or aldehyde enolate\h rather than the nitronate. Now the 1,4-dicarbonyl\h\h system can cyclize to give a cyclopentenone\h via an intramolecular aldol reaction.\h\h Note that deprotonation will occur at the more\h acidic benzylic position due to resonance, and\h\h this carbon will then attack the other carbonyl.\h After the condensation step and loss of water,\h\h we are left with the cyclopentenone. This strategy, coupled with others which\h\h attain similar transformations, are very valuable,\h because it is not easy to obtain a 1,4-dicarbonyl\h\h compound, due to the irregular separation of\h the functional groups involved. Therefore the\h\h Nef reaction, coupled with the nucleophilicity\h of the nitronate anion, offers easy access into\h\h this class of compounds, and is therefore a\h valuable tool in modern organic synthesis.

Professor Dave again, let’s get negative. You may remember when your English teacher repeatedly told you that only one negative is allowed in an English sentence. If your reply was “that don’t make no sense”, then you weren’t listening. But either way, this is your chance for revenge, because the Italian language actually requires double negatives. In certain cases, you may see a triple or even a quadruple negative in one sentence. Some clauses, however, will use just one negative. These are sentences stating simply “not”, or ones starting with the word nessuno, or niente, which mean no one, and nothing. But with these examples aside, it’s time to enter the wonderful world of multiple negatives. The word “mica” is a Latin word meaning “crumb”, so if you say something like “non ho mica fame”, you are literally saying: I don’t even have a crumb of a hunger, which means I am not at all hungry. This one has four negatives in one sentence. Just a few more now. Let’s mention one last thing. Italian has a negative adjective, and it’s nessuno, which is different from the pronoun nessuno. This word literally means “not even one”, and it has different forms which follow the indefinite article.

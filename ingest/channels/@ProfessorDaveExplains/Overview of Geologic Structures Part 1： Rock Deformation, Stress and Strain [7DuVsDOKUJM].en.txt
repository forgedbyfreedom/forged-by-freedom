The term viscosity relates\h\h to a material’s resistance to flow, and it is\h a very important concept in structural geology.\h Now, to better understand the mechanical\h behavior of rocks, we need to understand the\h\h concepts of stress and strain. Stress describes\h the quantity of the forces that cause deformation\h\h and is expressed in terms of force per unit area,\h or Pascals. Stress is equivalent to the pressure\h\h of a solid. There are three normal components of\h stress, with normal meaning acting perpendicular\h\h to a surface, and each one describes the\h amount of stress in a particular direction\h\h in three-dimensional space. If a force pulls on\h an object in an outward direction, causing the\h\h object to stretch, that is called tensile stress.\h Conversely, if a force pushes inward on an object,\h\h causing it to squash, that is called compressive\h stress. Both compressive and tensile stresses are\h\h normal stresses. Forces that act parallel\h to a surface are called shear stress.\h\h If an object experiences sufficient stress to\h cause deformation, whether it is stretched,\h\h squashed, or sheared, it undergoes\h strain. Strain is a unitless measure\h\h of the change in shape or volume of an object. The relationship between stress and strain, or\h\h how a material responds to stress, is represented\h by a value called Young’s modulus. Young’s modulus\h\h is dependent on the physical properties of a\h material and is determined in the laboratory by\h\h subjecting it to a series of different pressures\h and measuring the accompanying strain. These\h\h values are recorded and used to create a graph\h for the material’s stress-strain relationship.\h\h The slope of the best-fitting line through\h the data is the material’s Young’s modulus.\h\h Since minerals are the building blocks of rocks\h and each mineral has a specific value for Young’s\h\h modulus, the mineralogy of a given rock will exert\h a strong control on how that rock will deform.\h\h The relative tendency of a rock\h to strain in response to stress\h\h is called its competence, which can also\h be defined as the relative viscosity of a\h\h rock. Incompetent rocks are composed\h of soft, weak minerals like clay,\h\h while competent rocks are composed of strong,\h rigid minerals like garnet or feldspar.\h\h Other than competence, the manner in which a rock\h responds to stress is controlled by the conditions\h\h that it is under, mainly temperature and pressure,\h but other factors like the deformation rate and\h\h presence of fluids are also important. As the\h pressure and temperature of a rock are increased,\h\h its strength, or viscosity decreases,\h making it more willing to deform.\h We now understand that rocks respond to stress by\h becoming strained. The precise response to stress\h\h is described by four different mechanisms:\h brittle deformation, elastic deformation, plastic\h\h deformation, and viscous deformation. Viscous\h deformation mainly describes the deformation\h\h of fluids. Rocks that deform in a brittle manner\h break instead of bend, and this behavior is most\h\h common at the surface. If the force is increased,\h\h the material experiences more strain, and once\h the force is removed, the material reverts back\h\h to its original shape when the atomic bonds relax.\h However, it is possible to exert a force so great,\h\h that the rock fails and forms a fracture.

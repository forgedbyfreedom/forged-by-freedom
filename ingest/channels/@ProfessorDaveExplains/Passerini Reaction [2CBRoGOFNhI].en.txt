Next up in our survey of name reactions is the\h Passerini reaction. To delve into this reaction,\h\h we first have to introduce an interesting new\h class of reactive compounds, the isonitriles,\h\h also known as isocyanides. Be careful not to\h mix these up with isocyanates, which we have\h\h discussed in other tutorials. Isonitriles\h are zwitterionic, with a positive charge\h\h on the nitrogen and a negative charge on the\h adjacent carbon, and a triple bond between them.\h Now, when reacting one equivalent of a primary\h formamide with a good dehydrating agent in the\h\h presence of excess base, something interesting\h happens. First, the dehydrating agent, let’s say\h\h an aryl sulfonyl chloride, reacts at the more\h basic center of the amide, the carbonyl oxygen,\h\h and forms an imidoyl sulfonate. The nitrogen that\h initiated this step with its lone pair now has a\h\h positive charge, so pyridine or a similar amine\h will act as a proton sponge to neutralize the\h\h nitrogen, which is why this reaction requires\h a primary formamide rather than secondary, so\h\h that a proton is present for this step to occur.\h The sulfonate is an excellent leaving group due\h\h to resonance stabilization, so the reaction will\h proceed further. Just as with the previous step,\h\h this nitrogen will push its lone pair onto\h carbon, and this time the sulfonate will leave,\h\h leading to a protonated isonitrile. Given that\h pyridine is present in excess, this intermediate\h\h is also deprotonated to yield the isonitrile. Now, isonitriles are the less stable structural\h\h isomers of nitriles. As we can see, in nitriles,\h the CN group is bound to an R group through the\h\h carbon atom. But in an isonitrile, the R group\h is bound via the nitrogen atom, so this totally\h\h changes things in terms of electron distribution.\h Isonitriles can be represented two different ways.\h\h Starting with the zwitterion, if one of the pi\h bonds in the triple bond is given to the nitrogen,\h\h this will neutralize both atoms, leaving\h us with a carbene. Since these are both\h\h simply resonance structures, recall that\h the reality lies somewhere in between.\h Two questions may arise if you have been paying\h attention. Number one: If the isonitrile is so\h\h much less stable than the nitrile, why does\h it not convert? The answer is that it does,\h\h and the reaction has been studied both\h kinetically and thermodynamically.\h\h We will not go over these studies\h in detail. Suffice it to say that,\h\h although the reaction is spontaneous, meaning\h it has a negative delta G, the activation energy\h\h for the process is very high. The R group has to\h effect a 1,2-migration, and this is accompanied by\h\h severe distortions in the transition state. Thus,\h if you can form isonitriles at room temperature,\h\h as with the above reaction, they are quite\h stable kinetically. They can even be isolated,\h\h although they are well-known among\h chemists for their incredibly foul odor,\h\h and thus are often used in situ, meaning formed\h and then used immediately without purification.\h The stability of these unusual compounds\h illustrates the principle that,\h\h for a reaction to occur readily, it must have\h both a favorable delta G and a low delta G of\h\h activation. Due to their high delta G of\h activation, isonitriles are quite stable,\h\h do not easily convert to nitriles, and\h display their own quite fascinating chemistry.\h\h Indeed, this might be the second question: Do\h isonitriles react as carbenes, or as carbanions\h\h from the zwitterionic form? Well, the answer\h is both. Their carbene nature affords them many\h\h applications in cycloaddition chemistry, and they\h are important ligands in organometallic chemistry,\h\h like all other carbenes. Here, however, we will\h deal with their reactivity as mild nucleophiles.\h The first reaction of isonitriles we will discuss\h was invented by Italian chemist Mario Passerini in\h\h 1921. It is an example of a “multicomponent\h reaction”, in this case a reaction in which\h\h three reactants combine to form a single product,\h an alpha-acyloxy amide. The reactants involved\h\h are a carbonyl compound, such as a ketone or an\h aldehyde, a carboxylic acid, and an isonitrile.\h\h Now let’s take a look at precisely how this works. The mechanism is most likely stepwise and first\h\h involves the nucleophilic attack of the\h isonitrile onto the carbonyl compound,\h\h the ketone in this case, which is first activated\h by protonation from the carboxylic acid.\h\h This leads to a very electrophilic nitrilium\h ion, and even this carboxylate that results\h\h from the previous proton transfer is nucleophilic\h enough to attack it. So here we see the oxyanion\h\h attacking this carbon, which pushes a pi\h bond up to the nitrogen to neutralize it.\h\h Now here is the interesting\h part. This intermediate undergoes\h\h a 1,3-intramolecular shift of the acyl group\h to give the more stable alpha-acyloxy amide,\h\h so in the end this is thermodynamically\h driven, and there is our product.\h So what can we do with this kind of chemistry?\h The Passerini reaction is very popular in an area\h\h of chemistry which deals with library synthesis.\h Often chemists make libraries of many compounds,\h\h perhaps a few hundred or a few thousand,\h and screen them to see if, by sheer luck,\h\h some of them have some desired chemical or\h biological properties. With current technologies,\h\h a few thousand compounds can be screened in less\h than a day. In this reaction, one can vary four\h\h substituent groups, two on the ketone, one on\h the carboxylic acid, and one on the isonitrile,\h\h and it is therefore possible to reach a high\h level of diversity utilizing a single reaction.\h\h For example, if one chooses ten readily\h available ketones and ten carboxylic acids,\h\h and also makes ten isonitriles, all\h the possible combinations together\h\h afford 1,000 compounds as potential products. Now, looking again at our generalized example,\h\h if you are observant, you will notice that\h the reaction leads to a chiral product.\h\h With the carbonyl group, if R1 does not\h equal R2, it is considered prochiral,\h\h so the product has a stereogenic center, this\h one here that used to be the carbonyl carbon.\h\h And since the reaction requires no activator\h or catalyst, the product will be racemic. It\h\h turns out that for the synthesis of libraries,\h this is not a major concern.

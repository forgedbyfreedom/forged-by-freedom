Moving right along from the pnictogens, we\h reach Group 16, which is the oxygen group,\h\h also known as the chalcogens. In addition\h to oxygen this group includes sulfur,\h\h selenium, tellurium, polonium, and\h livermorium. Elements in this group\h\h have electron configurations ending in ns2np4, and\h it is here where metals are finally the minority.\h\h While metallic character increases going down the\h group, oxygen and sulfur are strict nonmetals.\h\h Selenium is characterized as a nonmetal but\h along with tellurium, the metalloid below,\h\h these two elements have semiconducting allotropes.\h Polonium is also characterized as a metalloid\h\h though it is distinctly metallic in some ways. It\h is highly radioactive and has no stable isotopes.\h\h Livermorium is yet another synthetic and\h exceptionally radioactive transactinide element,\h\h first created in 2000, whose most\h stable isotope has a half-life of just\h\h 53 milliseconds. With this group we finally\h see a strong tendency to complete the outermost\h\h shell of electrons, thus -2 oxidation states\h will be very common, along with the +4 and\h\h +6 that we would expect from utilization of\h all p electrons, or all valence electrons.\h So where can we find these elements? Oxygen is\h no problem, being that it is the most abundant\h\h element in earth’s crust at about 50% by mass.\h It also makes up 89% of all the oceans by mass.\h\h Additionally, the atmosphere is 21% molecular\h oxygen. This can be isolated by fractional\h\h distillation of liquified air, as it boils at a\h temperature just above that of molecular nitrogen.\h\h Sulfur has historically been found in natural\h deposits near volcanoes and geothermal vents,\h\h and is easy to mine. Sulfur is also found\h under geological salt domes, where sulfates are\h\h converted to solid sulfur by bacteria. This can be\h melted by steam and pumped to the surface through\h\h pipes, which is called the Frasch process, and\h it yields extremely pure sulfur. More commonly,\h\h we can get sulfur from hydrogen sulfide in natural\h gas. This can be reacted with oxygen to get sulfur\h\h dioxide and water, and sulfur dioxide can react\h further with hydrogen sulfide to produce elemental\h\h sulfur and water. Selenium is obtained from\h selenide impurities in sulfide ores of copper,\h\h silver, and iron, and is mainly recovered from\h the sludge of electrolytic copper purification.\h\h It can also be obtained as an impurity in sulfuric\h acid production from elemental sulfur. Tellurium,\h\h on the other hand, is one of the\h rarest elements in Earth’s crust,\h\h more so than gold. This is obtained primarily from\h anode sludges of electrolytic copper purification,\h\h much like selenium, and also from dust\h produced during blast furnace refining of lead.\h\h The biggest producers of tellurium are the\h United States, Peru, Japan, and Canada. Polonium,\h\h as we mentioned, is highly radioactive,\h forming naturally from uranium in uranium ores,\h\h and can also be made artificially by\h bombarding bismuth-209 with neutrons.\h\h Livermorium, again, is strictly synthetic and\h too radioactive to serve any practical purpose.\h In terms of properties, oxygen has two major\h allotropes. Diatomic oxygen is a colorless,\h\h odorless, tasteless gas, and it is by far\h the more common. This has both a singlet\h\h and triplet state, which differ in\h whether the two highest-energy electrons\h\h are paired in the same orbital with opposite spin,\h or alone in separate orbitals with spin aligned,\h\h the latter of which is more\h common, and is the ground state.\h\h The other allotrope is ozone, or O3. This is\h found in trace amounts in the upper atmosphere,\h\h and shields Earth’s surface from ultraviolet\h radiation. Sulfur also has several allotropes, the\h\h most common of which is the crown-shaped S8. This\h can take on rhombic or monoclinic arrangements.\h\h Sulfur can also form highly viscous coils of\h indefinite length, which take on plastic-like\h\h properties. And when sulfur boils, it produces\h varying amounts of S8, S6, S4, and S2. Selenium\h\h also most commonly exists as Se8, which has a red\h color, formed from the reduction of H2SeO3. But it\h\h can also form long, gray, spiral chains. Tellurium\h will also tend to make long spiral chains.\h Now let’s talk about applications. Oxygen has\h the most obvious application of any element on\h\h the periodic table, as without molecular\h oxygen to breathe, we die in minutes,\h\h just like all other aerobic life. But we have\h many industrial applications for oxygen as\h\h well. The chemical industry utilizes an enormous\h variety of oxygen-containing oxidizing agents.\h\h Many rockets use liquid oxygen as fuel. Oxygen\h can be injected into iron ores to remove\h\h impurities of carbon and sulfur. Sulfur is\h added to soil to lower its pH. Carbon disulfide is\h\h needed to make cellophane and rayon. Sulfites have\h utility in preserving fruit and wine.

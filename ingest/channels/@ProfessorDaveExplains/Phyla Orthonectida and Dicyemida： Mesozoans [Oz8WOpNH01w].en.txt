As we move along in our investigation of Spiralia,\h we arrive at two interesting phyla, Orthonectida\h\h and Dicyemida. Animals in these phyla are\h invertebrate parasites in the sense that they\h\h are invertebrates themselves, and they parasitize\h other invertebrates. Animals in these phyla are\h\h also referred to as mesozoans. The name Mesozoa\h comes from the Greek for mesos, or middle, and\h\h zōion, or animal. They were originally considered\h to be a missing link between the unicellular\h\h eukaryotes and all other animals, and as such\h they were considered to be part protozoa and\h\h part metazoa. The mesozoans were once proposed as\h a subkingdom closely related to the animals, but\h\h not quite animals. In the all-animal phylogeny,\h they are considered to be part of spiralia,\h\h but their relationship to all other spiralian\h phyla is unclear. Hence, they form a polytomy.\h Currently, all members of Orthonectida\h and Dicyemida are considered to be highly\h\h degenerate metazoans, meaning they are animals\h that have lost one or more of the characteristics\h\h shared by all other animals. However, genomic\h analyses of dicyemids have revealed that they\h\h contain spiralian peptides, and orthonectids\h were found to contain HOX genes, which are\h\h found only in animals and no other organisms. The Dicyemids, also known as rhombozoans,\h\h live exclusively within the excretory systems,\h more specifically the renal sac, of benthic\h\h cephalopods, that is, organisms such as the\h common octopus that are found living only on the\h\h sea floor. These organisms have both a sexual and\h asexual mode of reproduction. But unlike all other\h\h animals, they lack tissues, organs, symmetry,\h gastrulation, and even an extracellular matrix,\h\h which is a three-dimensional network of\h macromolecules, a characteristic shared by nearly\h\h all multicellular organisms. They do, however,\h demonstrate spiral development, a characteristic\h\h found only in the spiralians. In short, it’s\h not hard to see why they’re such a mystery.\h Adults in this phylum, sometimes called\h vermiforms, or nematogens, are long and worm-like.\h\h They are composed of a singular axial cell\h surrounded by just a handful of somatic cells.\h\h Some individuals can grow to be\h up to seven millimeters in length,\h\h though most are a fraction of a millimeter.\h Within their bodies, reproductive cells develop\h\h into vermiform embryos which can exit the parent\h into the cephalopod renal duct, and then develop\h\h into adults. Vermiforms will reproduce asexually\h in conditions of low crowding, but if the area\h\h becomes too crowded the reproductive cells\h of some adults develop into hermaphroditic\h\h gonads that produce fertilized eggs. These eggs\h develop into an infusoriform embryo that in turn\h\h develops into an infusoriform larva. These\h larvae contain more cells and are more complex\h\h in body organization than adults. They have been\h documented being expelled as waste from the hosts,\h\h but their method of development into adults and\h their colonizing of a new host is still unknown.\h Orthonectids spend most of their lives as\h trophic plasmodium within the tissues of\h\h their host. In this stage they are little more\h than a multinucleated mass of naked protoplasm.\h\h They essentially resemble slime molds, but instead\h of living on the forest floor, they live within\h\h the tissues of their host. Within the body of the organism, a few cells\h\h develop into worm-like male and female adults that\h leave the host and swim free searching for mates.\h These short-lived adults lack digestive,\h circulatory, respiratory, and excretory systems.\h\h They are little more than gametes and a reduced\h nervous system surrounded by contractile muscle\h\h cells and a layer of cilia. If a one millimeter\h long female meets a much smaller dwarf male of\h\h the same species, they reproduce by uniting their\h genital pores. The fertilized eggs develop into\h\h ciliated larvae that exit the female and search\h for a host. If a larva makes it into a suitable\h\h host’s tissue, it disintegrates and scatters its\h cells within the host’s tissue in order to become\h\h new infective cells known as agametes that develop\h into plasmodium and restart their life cycle.\h So, the orthonectids, like the dicyemids\h break almost all the rules of Kingdom\h\h Animalia. Their unique life cycle, cryptic\h nature, and morphology have both fascinated\h\h and confounded researchers since their discovery.\h There is still much to learn about them,\h\h but we have many other phyla to cover, so let’s\h continue onwards in our investigation of Spiralia.

With four of the five major classes of phlyum\h Cnidaria covered, there is just one left,\h\h Anthozoa. Anthozoans, or “flower animals”, are\h all Cnidarian polyps that lack a medusa stage.\h\h There are three subclasses of Anthozoans;\h Hexacorallia, which includes over 4,300 species\h\h of sea anemones, hard corals, and many others,\h Octocorallia which includes the horny corals,\h\h sea fans, sea pens, and soft corals, and\h Ceriantharia, which includes the tube anemones.\h At first glance, the tube anemones\h look very similar to the sea anemones,\h\h but the two are actually quite different. Unlike\h sea anemones, the tube anemones have completely\h\h unpaired septa and can withdraw into the fibrous\h tubes made of mucus and sticky substances excreted\h\h by their glutinant type nematocysts. In addition,\h the tube anemones have two crowns of tentacles,\h\h an outer crown of long extended tentacles and\h an inner crown of smaller erect tentacles.\h\h These Cnidarians have notoriously\h cryptic life histories and some,\h\h like Isarachnanthus nocturnus have extensive\h larval stages that closely resemble medusae.\h Meanwhile, all members of Octocorallia are\h colonial, connected by a system of gastrodermal\h\h tubes known as solenia. Each polyp has exactly\h eight tentacles arranged around the mouth\h\h and eight septa arranged within the gastrovascular\h cavity. Though different species of octocorallian\h\h polyps may look similar, the structures they\h form are incredibly diverse. Compare for example,\h\h the venus fan, to the feathery sea pen. Or\h compare the bamboo corals, to the blue coral.\h\h Collectively, these animals are sometimes called\h “soft corals” because they lack the distinctive\h\h stony skeleton of the reef-building corals. The sea pens, named for their resemblance to\h\h old fashioned “quill pens,” are unique\h among the anthozoans in that instead of\h\h having relatively morphologically uniform\h polyps, a sea pen’s polyps are specialized.\h\h A single polyp develops into a rigid, erect\h stalk, or rachis, loses its tentacles, and\h\h forms a bulbous root-like structure, or peduncle,\h at its base. This polyp then reproduces asexually\h\h to produce branching polyps from its central\h stalk that form siphonozooids, for water intake\h\h and respiration, autozooids with nematocysts for\h feeding, and gonozooids for reproduction. The\h\h entire colony is fortified by calcium carbonate\h in the form of spicules and a central axial rod.\h\h Some sea pens, like the orange sea pen, survive\h best at depths between 14 and 225 meters,\h\h while others, like the Umbellulas can survive\h at depths of about 200 meters to over 6,100\h\h meters below sea level, where they\h feed on planktonic organisms. Others,\h\h like the strikingly colored and bioluminescent sea\h pansy are found only in shallow coastal waters.\h Now, unlike members of Octocorallia which have\h eight-fold symmetry, members of the subclass\h\h Hexacorallia have six-fold symmetry. They\h are further divided into five extant orders:\h\h Actiniaria, the sea anemones, Antipatharia, the\h black corals, Corallimorpharia, the false corals,\h\h Scleractinia, the stony corals, and\h Zoantharia, also referred to as Zoanthidea\h\h or Zoanthiniaria, which includes the zoanthids. The sea anemones of order Antipatharia are named\h\h after the terrestrial flower of the same name.\h They range in size from 5 to 200 millimeters,\h\h though some, like Mertens’ carpet sea anemone, can\h grow up to 1 meter in diameter. Though anemones\h\h are more common in warm water, they are also found\h on substrate in coastal seas all over the world.\h In general, sea anemones are cylindrical polyps\h that are larger, heavier, and more muscular than\h\h hydra polyps. Their small slit-like mouths\h are positioned at the top of their oral disk\h\h and surrounded by tentacles. Their mouths\h lead into a pharynx which empties into the\h\h gastrovascular cavity. Their gastrovascular\h cavities are further divided by complete septa\h\h into six radial chambers, and each chamber\h is partially divided by smaller, incomplete\h\h septa that further increase the surface area of\h the gastrovascular cavity to aid in digestion.\h Sea anemones are some of the most\h muscular Cnidarians. Their well-defined,\h\h longitudinal retractor muscles stretch from\h their pedal disc, which is used for attachment,\h\h to their oral disk. Many species also have\h longitudinal epidermal fibers within the tentacles\h\h and oral disk. These muscles allow them to\h actively expand and contract their tentacles\h\h when searching for prey, which they paralyze\h with nematocysts and drag into their mouths\h\h for digestion. Their musculature also\h allows them to glide along the substrate,\h\h contract and withdraw their tentacles and\h oral disks, and even swim, more or less,\h\h by utilizing rhythmic bending movements. Anemones are perhaps most famous for\h\h their symbiotic relationship with\h anemonefish, in particular the clown\h\h fish. Anemonefish and their hosts have a\h long history of mutualistic coevolution.\h\h The sea anemone protects the anemonefish from\h predators, as well as providing food through the\h\h scraps left from the anemone’s meals. In addition,\h occasional dead anemone tentacles function as a\h\h safe nest site. In return, the anemonefish defend\h the anemone from its predators and parasites.\h\h Anemonefish are highly host-specific, and\h different species prefer different anemones.\h\h The most common anemones to host anemonefish are\h the carpet anemones, the magnificent sea anemones,\h\h and the bubble-tip anemone. The specific mucus\h that the anemonefish secrete does not cause host\h\h anemone nematocysts to discharge. Anemonefish\h that had their mucus removed experimentally\h\h were killed by their host anemones. Other\h mutualisms include the anemone shrimp,\h\h the boxer crab, and the anemone hermit crab which\h appear to pick and choose specific anemones.\h Some anemones are monoecious, that is they are\h male and female at the same time, while others are\h\h dioecious, meaning either male or female.\h The monoecious anemones are also protandrous,\h\h they develop first as males that produce\h sperm, and later as females that produce eggs.\h\h Their gonads are arranged within their septa where\h they are fertilized externally by species that\h\h spawn eggs, and internally by species that allow\h fertilization to occur within the gastrovascular\h\h cavity. Fertilized eggs develop into ciliated\h zygotes that swim or drift to suitable substrate.\h\h Many sea anemones can also reproduce\h asexually by budding, longitudinal fission,\h\h transverse fission, or pedal laceration, where\h small pieces of the pedal disk break off as the\h\h animal moves and regenerate into new anemones. Though the anemones play important ecological\h\h roles, they do not rival the ecosystem building\h capacity of the hexacorallian corals of Order\h\h Scleractinia. These include the “true,” or\h “stony,” corals also known as hermatypic,\h\h or reef-building corals. A close-up of an\h individual coral polyp resembles a miniature\h\h sea anemone. And like the sea anemones, their\h gastrovascular cavity is divided into multiples\h\h of six. However, unlike sea anemones, they have no\h pedal disk and instead live in secreted calcareous\h\h cups that project into the polyp between its true\h septa. Superficially, stony corals resemble the\h\h soft corals. But they are unique in their calcium\h carbonate skeletons, rather than chitinous, which\h\h give them the capacity to build some of the most\h productive of all ecosystems, the coral reefs.\h\h In many corals, the secreted calcareous skeleton\h can become massive, and individual coral polyps\h\h form a living, connected tissue\h sheet that covers the surface.\h The ectodermal cells of hermatypic corals\h contain high concentrations of symbiotic,\h\h unicellular, photosynthetic dinoflagellates\h known as zooxanthellae, up to 5 million per\h\h square centimeter in fact. Up to 50% of the\h organic compounds produced through photosynthesis\h\h by the dinoflagellates are utilized as food\h for the coral polyps. The dinoflagellates also\h\h supplement their hosts with oxygen as a byproduct\h of photosynthesis. This oxygen, combined with\h\h additional energy derived from sugars produced\h by zooxanthellae enables these hermatypic\h\h corals to grow at a rate that is up to three times\h faster than similar species without symbionts.\h The black corals, or horn corals, from the\h order Antipatharia, are deep-water soft corals\h\h generally distinguished from other corals\h by their polyp-filled black or dark-brown\h\h skeletons lined with small spines. Black\h corals are found around the world from subtidal\h\h to abyssal depths, though they are most common\h in tropical and subtropical waters at depths\h\h greater than 50 meters, where competition for\h space is less fierce. Within suitable habitats,\h\h sessile organisms must compete for space, and as\h such, have evolved a wide range of adaptations\h\h to hold off invaders. Black corals, on the\h other hand, escape such competition by occupying\h\h deep-sea habitats with strong currents that are\h generally avoided by other sessile organisms.\h\h They survive at such depths as suspension feeders,\h with strong holdfasts, that mainly prey upon\h\h passing zooplankton they capture with their\h nematocysts. In addition, deep-sea black corals,\h\h in particular, may be some of the oldest colonial\h organisms on the planet. At least one sample of\h\h Leiopathes glaberrima found at 450 meters\h below sea level, was over 4,200 years old.\h The false corals, commonly known as the mushroom\h anemones of order Corallimorpharia, look similar\h\h to anemones since they have no skeletons. However,\h they are more closely related to the stony corals.\h\h Though the false corals do not build reefs,\h they are often found among stony corals where\h\h they sometimes overgrow reefs in a carpet-like\h formation. Most false corals grow as colonies\h\h that resemble encrusting corals, like the elephant\h ear anemone. Others like the strawberry anemone\h\h are solitary. Most false corals are, like the\h black corals, suspension feeders that prey on\h\h tiny planktonic organisms common in coral\h reefs, though some, like Ricordea yuma,\h\h supplement their diet with symbiotic\h zooxanthellae, much like the stony corals.\h Finally, the zoanthids, of order Zoantharia,\h sometimes called Zoanthidea or Zoanthiniaria,\h\h are some of the most commonly collected corals\h used by hobbyists in reef aquaria due to their\h\h high tolerance and ease of reproduction\h in captivity. Many also fluoresce under\h\h a black light, and others have been bred to\h produce a wide range of distinctive colors.\h\h They generally grow in small colonies and are\h distinguished from other anthozoans by their\h\h exclusively marginal tentacles and ability\h to incorporate sand and other small pieces\h\h of material into their tissues to help make\h their structure. Like all anthozoans, they\h\h are capable of asexual reproduction, though they\h generally remain attached to the original polyp.\h\h Like the false corals, zoanthids are often found\h living among the reef-building corals, where they\h\h feed by capturing plankton and supplementing\h their diet with symbiotic zooxanthellae.\h And that’s it for the general characteristics\h and phylogeny of class Anthozoa.

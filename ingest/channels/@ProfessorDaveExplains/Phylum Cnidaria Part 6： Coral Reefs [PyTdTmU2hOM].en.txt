Coral reefs are some\h\h of the most productive ecosystems in the world.\h However, unlike most highly productive ecosystems,\h\h their primary producers are not plants,\h but the zooxanthellae that live within\h\h the tissues of hermatypic coral polyps, and the\h coralline algae that create calcareous deposits.\h Coralline algae are red algae in the order\h Corallinales. Their importance is sometimes\h\h overlooked, but their excretion of calcium\h carbonate not only adds to the total mass of\h\h the reef, but also aids in holding everything\h together. Their calcium deposits are usually\h\h pink or red but can also vary in color from\h purple to blue, and yellow to white or green.\h\h In some parts of the world, most notably\h the Mediterranean, coralline algae are\h\h primary reef builders since they can survive\h in areas inhospitable to the hermatypic corals.\h In addition to coralline algae, many other\h Cnidarians, like the octocorallian corals\h\h and the hydrozoan fire corals,\h also contribute to the calcareous\h\h structure of the reef. An enormous variety\h of other animals also aid in reef-building.\h\h However, they are all often seen more as\h “supplemental” to the hermatypic corals,\h\h which are generally considered to be the primary\h reef builders of tropical coral reefs. Most\h\h hermatypic corals require warmth, sunlight, and\h the salinity of undiluted saltwater. As a result,\h\h they are limited to shallow waters between 30\h degrees north and 30 degrees south latitude.\h\h They are also very sensitive\h to pollution and sedimentation\h\h since hermatypic corals rely on their mutualistic\h dinoflagellates, known as zooxanthellae.\h It’s important to note that not all hermatypic\h corals contain zooxanthellae. Some, like Lophelia,\h\h are cold-water corals that survive in deep\h waters throughout the North Atlantic. These\h\h deep-water stony corals, like black\h corals, soft corals, and sea fans,\h\h provide habitats for other organisms, but rarely\h form large coral reefs like the tropical corals.\h Stony corals, like all anthozoans, can reproduce\h sexually and asexually. When an egg cell is\h\h fertilized by a sperm cell, the resulting zygote\h develops into a free-swimming planula. If this\h\h planula successfully settles into the substrate,\h it will metamorphosize into a primary polyp.\h\h This primary polyp will then form the basis of\h its calcareous skeleton through excretions of\h\h the lower epidermis. This initially forms\h a tiny cup for the young polyp. The polyp\h\h then secretes septa in the characteristic\h six-fold pattern of all hexacorallians.\h\h Then, the polyp will repeatedly reproduce\h asexually to give rise to an entire colony.\h A colony of coral polyps are connected by\h horizontal sheets of tissue known as coenosarcs\h\h that extend over the outer surface of the calcium\h carbonate skeleton and completely cover it. These\h\h sheets not only connect polyp bodies, but are\h also extensions of the gastrovascular cavity,\h\h meaning that nutrients and water can be\h exchanged between members of the colony.\h\h These colonies undergo sexual reproduction\h once annually. Cued in intricate ways by\h\h the lunar cycle and water temperature, they\h simultaneously release eggs and sperm into\h\h the water. If an egg cell is fertilized\h by a sperm cell of the same species,\h\h they form a free-swimming planula which can start\h a new colony elsewhere. Once in a while the sperm\h\h can fertilize eggs of other coral species, but\h the zygote usually dies before forming a planula.\h\h Less frequently such a hybrid zygote will form\h a planula, but this typically dies very quickly.\h\h Even less frequently the hybrid planula can\h settle to form a polyp and even a juvenile coral,\h\h but there are no recorded cases of these\h hybrid corals living past the juvenile stage.\h Getting back on track, before a colony of coral\h polyps can begin to form, the polyp needs to\h\h obtain their photosynthetic zooxanthellae. This\h can happen through either a direct or indirect\h\h transfer. In direct transfer, the mother\h coral polyp allows her own zooxanthellae\h\h to enter developing eggs, or the eggs themselves\h absorb zooxanthellae before they are released.\h\h In indirect transfer, developing planula\h and polyps absorb their zooxanthellae\h\h from the fecal matter of fish or by\h capturing free-swimming dinoflagellates.\h Most individual coral polyps are\h tiny, about 1-3 millimeters in size,\h\h but the skeletons they construct can\h be massive. Not only that, but since\h\h corals grow at a steady rate and are extremely\h sensitive to conditions in the world’s oceans,\h\h coral reefs, like tree rings, provide a type of\h climatological proxy data. Like coring a tree,\h\h something we discussed at length in\h the botany series, taking a slice of\h\h a coral skeleton allows researchers access to\h deposits that were excreted slowly over time.\h Though corals don’t generate rings like trees do,\h there are still numerous ways to date coral reefs.\h\h One common protocol for dating deep sea corals\h involves a combination of radiocarbon dating,\h\h a method of dating comparing the current carbon-14\h to carbon-12 ratio with that of a past atmosphere,\h\h and uranium-thorium dating, a method\h of radiometric dating that determines\h\h the age of calcium carbonate, the\h dominant material in coral deposits.\h\h A second method is a combination\h of magnetostratigraphy,\h\h which is a geophysical correlation\h technique, and strontium isotope analysis,\h\h which are used in conjunction to determine the\h earliest evidence of the great barrier reef\h\h at around 600,000 years, though its most recent\h form is only about 6,000 – 8,000 years old.\h Despite the incredible diversity of reefs, the\h most common are fringing reefs, barrier reefs, and\h\h atolls. Fringing reefs form close to land masses,\h while barrier reefs run parallel to the shore and\h\h form wide and deep lagoons. Atolls are ring-shaped\h reefs that form lagoons without land masses.\h\h The Great Barrier Reef is, contrary to its name,\h actually a complex of many different reef types.\h Large reefs generally have distinguishable zones.\h The side facing the sea is known as the reef\h\h front, which runs parallel to the shore and slopes\h downward into the water. In many regions around\h\h the globe, coral reef ecosystems exhibit a similar\h depth zonation of dominant organisms. That is,\h\h shallow waters, less than 50 meters in depth, are\h dominated by coralline algae and zooxanthellae\h\h containing hermatypic corals. These organisms\h then give way to black corals, false corals,\h\h and other Cnidarians that do not contain\h zooxanthellae and dominate from below the\h\h transition zones to areas of the abyssal plains. Back close to shore, in shallow water, the reef\h\h forms a reef crest that gently slopes into a reef\h flat, which slopes down further into a lagoon\h\h that is protected from the waves. An astounding\h number of species make their homes in different\h\h areas of the reef and form unique and fragile\h habitats with strong ecological connections.\h Globally, these connections, and hermatypic\h corals in particular, are under stress due to\h\h climate change and ocean acidification. Global\h warming has been correlated to coral bleaching\h\h which occurs when corals lose their zooxanthellae\h and become white and brittle. As the water warms,\h\h it damages part of the photosynthetic\h mechanism in the zooxanthellae,\h\h which leads to a buildup of harmful oxidants\h which diffuse into the polyp tissues and begin\h\h to harm or kill the corals. In an attempt to\h survive, many corals expel the zooxanthellae.\h\h In some cases, bleached corals can\h then uptake new zooxanthellae later,\h\h but in others the corals become too weak. These\h brittle corals are then colonized by algae\h\h that outcompete the weakened polyps and\h colonize the skeleton, killing the coral.\h Corals are also under threat due to ocean\h acidification, where carbon dioxide entering\h\h seawater reacts to form carbonic acid, causing\h an increase in acidity that makes it harder,\h\h and sometimes impossible, for corals to form\h their calcium carbonate structure. In many parts\h\h of the world, corals are also under threat due to\h physical damage, sedimentation from urban run-off,\h\h nutrient pollution from farms, pathogen infection\h from slugs, toxic substances from sunscreen,\h\h microplastics from stormwater run-off, destructive\h fishing practices, and coral harvesting.\h Though all these problems add up\h to major issues for the corals,\h\h it’s worth noting the increase in local and\h global efforts to save coral reefs.

We just covered phylum Hemichordata, so let’s\h move on to Echinodermata. The echinoderms,\h\h along with the hemichordates, make up the\h clade Ambulacraria. All members of this clade\h\h share a three-part coelom, and similar larval\h forms. The name of the phylum, Echinodermata,\h\h comes from the Greek ἐχῖνος (ekhînos) meaning\h hedgehog, and δέρμα (dérma) meaning skin,\h\h though they are more commonly called the “spiny\h skin animals” or simply “the echinoderms.”\h They are found in all oceans around the world and\h at all depths, from shallow intertidal regions and\h\h tide pools to the abyssal regions of the deep sea.\h Though they are locally abundant in some areas,\h\h they are often found dispersed throughout\h the ecosystem, with the exception of the\h\h abyssal zone and “biotic desert” of the deep sea,\h where they are some of the most abundant animals,\h\h like the deep-sea crinoids and sea pigs, where\h they fill a range of ecological niches, from\h\h deposit feeders like the sea cucumbers, to filter\h feeders like the sea lilies, to scavengers like\h\h the feather stars, to active predators like many\h starfish. Though many are slow moving, a great\h\h number of echinoderms are predatory, and some,\h like the sunflower sea stars, which can grow to a\h\h size of more than a meter and have 16 to 24 limbs,\h are top predators that feed on other echinoderms\h\h and slow-moving or sessile mollusks. Many\h species are considered to be keystone species,\h\h meaning they have a disproportionally large impact\h on their respective marine communities. In order\h\h to examine these organisms, let’s go over their\h general and unique characteristics, external\h\h characteristics, development, regeneration,\h ecology, and finally, classification.\h The echinoderms are triploblastic, entirely\h marine, primarily dioecious deuterostomes with\h\h an extensive coelom, no brain and no excretory\h system. All species are free-living or commensal,\h\h meaning none are parasitic, though many other\h animals parasitize or otherwise live on or\h\h in the bodies of many echinoderms. As adults,\h most are unsegmented with pentaradial symmetry,\h\h though their larvae are bilaterally symmetrical\h and their common ancestor was also bilaterally\h\h symmetrical. As such, the echinoderms go through a\h series of developmental stages that vary by class.\h\h In addition, echinoderms are capable of\h autonomy and regeneration of lost parts.\h The five unique echinoderm characteristics that\h separate them from all other animals are as\h\h follows. First, an endoskeleton of large plates or\h small scattered ossicles. Second, a water-vascular\h\h system that is comprised of a network of fluid\h filled channels and extends into the tube feet.\h\h Third, pedicellariae which are unique wrench- or\h claw-shaped appendages with movable jaws called\h\h valves, found only in some echinoderms. Fourth,\h papulae, also known as dermal branchiae or skin\h\h gills, which can be extended or withdrawn. And\h fifth, internal pentaradial, or five-pointed\h\h symmetry in adults. External pentaradial symmetry\h is evident in some adults, like the sea-stars,\h\h but even the echinoderms which have become\h secondarily bilateral are pentaradial at\h\h some point in their lives. It’s important to note\h that even in species with more than 5 limbs, like\h\h the Antarctic sun starfish with 50 arms, or the\h variable bushy feather star with over 100 arms,\h\h they are still symmetrical in multiples of 5. Echinoderm external features vary widely by\h\h class. For example, the sea stars, brittle\h stars, and sea urchins all have a mouth on\h\h their ventral end and an anus on their dorsal\h end. Meanwhile, the crinoids have both their\h\h mouth and their anus on the dorsal sides, while\h the sea cucumbers have mouths on their anterior\h\h end and anus on their posterior. In order to\h generalize a bit, we will examine members of\h\h Asteroidea for external and internal features. Many echinoderms have an aboral surface,\h\h meaning the side opposite the mouth, that is rough\h and spiny, though in some species these spines are\h\h flattened so that the surface appears smooth.\h Around the bases of the spines are the minute,\h\h pincer-like pedicellariae, which are\h manipulated by muscles, and function in\h\h keeping the surface clean, aid in food capture,\h like in the Velcro stars, and in some species,\h\h protect papulae against predators, like in many\h urchins such as the venomous flower sea urchin,\h\h and the collector urchin which can actually\h eject its pedicellariae. Among the spines,\h\h pedicellariae, and generally rough skin, there\h are numerous extensions of the coelomic cavity\h\h that extend through spaces between the skeletal\h ossicles and aid in respiration and waste removal.\h\h These are known as papulae or “skin gills.” Another extension to the surrounding environment\h\h is the circular madreporite, which is located\h aborally in the sea stars and orally in the\h\h brittle stars. It is a calcareous sieve plate that\h leads to the echinoderm water vascular system,\h\h which extends into the tube feet, or podia, that\h are often protected by movable spines. These\h\h tube feet regularly function in locomotion\h and many have sensory structures that aid\h\h in chemoreception, pressure reception, and\h photoreception. Many species of urchins have\h\h light-sensitive cells in their tube feet all over\h their bodies, essentially acting as one giant eye.\h\h Meanwhile, many sea stars have light-sensitive\h sensory tentacles on the tips of their tube feet\h\h that also act as eyes. Between the tube feet,\h there is an ambulacrum or ambulacral area,\h\h sometimes called a food groove, that runs\h from the mouth on the oral side to the\h\h tips of each arm. This is essentially\h a pathway to carry food into the mouth,\h\h and it is extremely pronounced in the crinoids. Echinoderm development is unique in that although\h\h adult echinoderms possess pentaradial symmetry,\h their larvae are ciliated, free-swimming organisms\h\h with bilateral symmetry. Echinoderm larvae vary\h widely within echinoderm classes, and even orders\h\h within the same class. In addition, the process of\h metamorphosis is often quite complex and involves\h\h numerous larval stages before the echinoderm\h settles to the ocean floor as a sexually\h\h immature juvenile. The larvae of some species are\h plankton feeders, while many others are initially\h\h yolk-feeders that may or may not pass through a\h plankton feeding stage before becoming juveniles.\h\h Metamorphosis involves a dramatic reorganization\h of the bilateral larvae into radial juveniles,\h\h which may involve the complete loss of the\h original mouth and anus, and the growth of an\h\h entirely new mouth and anus, like in the starfish. Echinoderms are notable for their ability of\h\h regeneration, in that they can survive and\h regenerate even if up to three fourths of\h\h their bodies are removed. Many species\h of echinoderms are not only capable of\h\h regenerating lost limbs, but sometimes, lost\h limbs are capable of regenerating whole bodies,\h\h as long as part of the central disk is\h present. However, most lost limbs die,\h\h and do not regenerate. In addition, echinoderms\h that are in danger or diseased can discharge parts\h\h of their bodies and internal organs, and then\h regenerate those limbs and organs over a period\h\h of several months. For example, many starfish\h will remove limbs that have become infected,\h\h and many sea cucumbers can expel their digestive\h and respiratory organs through the process of\h\h evisceration if they sense they are in danger. Ecologically, echinoderms are numerous\h\h invertebrates whose adults play an important role\h in benthic ecosystems, while the larvae are a\h\h major component of the planktonic invertebrates.\h Many sea urchins are grazers that keep down algae\h\h populations. Others, like the heart urchins,\h process sediment. And still others, like the sea\h\h cucumbers and sea pigs, are deposit feeders, while\h crinoids are suspension feeders. Some sea urchins\h\h can bore into solid rock, destabilizing rock faces\h and releasing nutrients into the ocean. Others,\h\h like many starfish are top predators that keep\h populations of prey species in check. While still\h\h others, like the purple urchins, can explode in\h population and cause overgrazing of kelp forests,\h\h resulting in an alga-denuded "urchin barren."\h In addition, since they have a calcium rich\h\h endoskeleton, the echinoderms collectively\h sequester over 0.1 gigatons of carbon dioxide\h\h per year as calcium carbonate. Unfortunately,\h many species of echinoderms are in decline\h\h globally. Some species of sea urchins and\h sea cucumbers are also harvested for food,\h\h which has led to further population declines. In terms of classification, there are five\h\h extant classes of echinoderms. Those are\h Crinoidea, which includes the feather stars\h\h and sea lilies, Asteroidea, commonly known as sea\h stars or starfish, Ophiuroidea, commonly known as\h\h the brittle stars or serpent stars, Echinoidea,\h which includes the sea urchins, and Holothuroidea,\h\h which includes the sea cucumbers.

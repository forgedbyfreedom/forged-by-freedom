In the previous tutorial we went over some general\h information regarding phylum Echinodermata. Now\h\h let’s get into some more detail by examining\h echinoderm body systems. Echinoderms generally\h\h have an endoskeleton, coelom, water-vascular\h system, digestive system, hemal system,\h\h nervous system, and reproductive system. Echinoderms, like the chordates,\h\h have an endoskeleton, but their endoskeleton\h is very different from ours. The echinoderm\h\h endoskeleton is directly beneath the epidermis and\h is comprised of small calcium rich plates known as\h\h ossicles that are bound together by a unique form\h of connective tissue known as catch collagen, that\h\h the echinoderm can neurologically change from a\h liquid to a sold state, allowing the echinoderm to\h\h assume different postures without muscular effort.\h Spines and tubercles project from the ossicles to\h\h form the echinoderm’s rough and spiny surface. The coelom of an echinoderm is a fluid-filled\h\h chamber that circulates fluids around the body\h cavity and into the papulae for gas exchange\h\h and waste excretion. It also contains amoebocytes\h that have a protective function against infection.\h Meanwhile, the echinoderm water-vascular system\h comprises a second coelomic compartment made\h\h up of a series of canals and numerous tube feet,\h that when paired with the dermal ossicles of the\h\h endoskeleton, and often the madreporite, create\h a hydraulic system that aids in locomotion and\h\h feeding. In some species, like the sea stars, it\h is the primary mode of locomotion, while others,\h\h like the brittle stars, have tube feet on their\h underside, but lack suction cups, so they are not\h\h used for locomotion. When sea stars walk, they do\h so upon their hydraulic tube feet. They do this\h\h by using muscular pressure to push coelomic fluid\h into the tube feet to stiffen them for walking,\h\h and the internal ampulla that connects to each\h foot acts as fluid reservoir. Contraction of\h\h muscles in the ampulla forces water into\h the tubes, and valves prevent backflow.\h Echinoderm feeding strategies vary widely by\h class. For example, many sea stars are carnivorous\h\h and feed by everting their stomachs, beginning to\h digest their prey externally. Others, like many\h\h brittle stars, eat prey whole, while many urchins\h eat plant matter with self-sharpening teeth,\h\h and crinoids are mostly filter feeders. Regardless\h of the feeding strategy, food enters the digestive\h\h system through the mouth and moves through a short\h esophagus to a large stomach often located in the\h\h central disk. From there, a short intestine leads\h to the intestinal ceca and any undigested food\h\h passes out through the anus. In the sea cucumbers,\h the intestine is long and runs most of the length\h\h of the body, ending in a large anus which is\h sometimes inhabited by other animals, while some\h\h sea stars lack intestines and anuses entirely. The echinoderm hemal or circulatory system is\h\h open and greatly reduced. It usually consists\h of a central ring and five radial vessels. There\h\h is no true heart, and the blood often lacks any\h respiratory pigment. Gas exchange and respiration\h\h instead occurs through the tube feet and papulae\h in starfish, genital bursae in brittle stars,\h\h peristominal gills in sea urchins,\h and cloacal trees in sea cucumbers.\h The echinoderm nervous system is radial\h and consists of a modified nerve net of\h\h interconnecting neurons and no central\h brain. Instead, nerves radiate from the\h\h nerve ring around the mouth, into each arm,\h or along the body wall. The nervous system\h\h attaches to sensory structures for chemoreception,\h photoreception, and touch. However, this varies\h\h by taxon. Sea stars generally have eye-spots\h at the tips of their arms, but some brittle\h\h stars have very few sensory structures, if any. Though some sea stars can reproduce asexually,\h\h either through parthenogenesis or by cleaning\h their ventral disk, most echinoderms reproduce\h\h sexually. Sexes are separate and the reproductive\h system generally consists of large gonads that\h\h are often held in each arm. Fertilization is\h external. Sperm and eggs are generally released\h\h into the water in mass spawnings. However, some\h cold-water echinoderms do brood their eggs in\h\h special pouches which they carry until the larvae\h hatch, metamorphosize, and become juveniles. Still\h\h others are viviparous and give birth to\h live young that burst out of their mother.\h And that wraps up the body systems.\h However, we’re not quite done discussing\h\h the echinoderms.

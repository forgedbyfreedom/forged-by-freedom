The gastrotrichs, or “hairybellies” get their\h name from the Greek gaster or gastros meaning\h\h stomach or belly, and thrix or trichos, meaning\h hair. This name comes from the fact that their\h\h entire ventral surface, or belly, and only\h their belly, is often covered in visible cilia.\h\h These are tiny bilaterally symmetrical animals\h usually less than one millimeter in length,\h\h with a transparent bowling pin-shaped or\h strap-shaped body, that is dorsally arched\h\h and ventrally flattened. They are divided into two\h clades, the Macrodasyida which are marine animals\h\h except for two species, and the Chaetonotida,\h some of which are marine and some freshwater.\h Like the flatworms of phylum Platyhelminthes,\h all hairybellies of phylum Gastrotricha\h\h are triploblastic, meaning they\h have three unique germ cell layers,\h\h and they are also unsegmented acoelomates, meaning\h they lack a true body cavity and segmentation.\h\h Unlike the flatworms, however, the gastrotrichs\h have distinctive paired extensions on their\h\h posterior ends called “feet” or “furca” which\h contain glands that aid in adhesion. Like the\h\h flatworms, many gastrotrichs have a type of\h duo-gland system, where one gland secretes an\h\h adhesive agent that allows the animals\h to attach themselves to the substrate,\h\h and a second gland secretes a de-adhesive\h agent that breaks down the first adhesive.\h In addition, gastrotrichs have a well-developed\h cuticle that may form plates, scales, and spines,\h\h though it does not contain chitin and is not\h molted at any time throughout their lives. They\h\h have a complete gut that begins with a terminal\h anterior mouth and ends with a ventral posterior\h\h anus, in many species. Gastrotrichs eat detritus,\h algae, bacteria, and other organic material which\h\h they pump into their mouths through suction\h from a muscular pharynx, which has a Y-shaped or\h\h inverted Y-shaped lumen. From there, the\h food passes into their midgut and cylindrical\h\h intestine, which is lined with digestive cells.\h Undigested material passes out of the anus.\h Their excretory system consists of one or more\h pairs of protonephridia, which are equipped\h\h with solenocytes instead of the flatworm “flame\h cells”, and function primarily for osmoregulation.\h\h Their muscular system is unique and comprised\h of a system of separate longitudinal,\h\h circular, and helicoidal muscles. Their nervous\h system includes a bilobed cerebral ganglion\h\h at the head end and one or more pairs of nerve\h cords that extend to the tail. Sensory and motor\h\h neurons project off the brain and nerve cords.\h Their sensory organs are comprised of ciliated\h\h receptor cells. Some receptors line the body\h and function as mechanoreceptors that respond\h\h to pressure, while others form ciliated pits\h on the head that function as chemoreceptors\h\h and respond to various chemicals. A few species\h also have red or black-pigmented eyespots.\h\h Like the flatworms, they lack any specialized\h circulatory or respiratory systems.\h Gastrotrichs are considered to be cosmopolitan\h in distribution, meaning they are found all over\h\h the world, though their patterns of distribution\h are not yet fully understood. They appear to be\h\h locally abundant, sometimes reaching densities of\h more than 2.6 million per square meter. As one can\h\h imagine, it’s a pretty small area for 2.6 million\h of any type of animal to live in, but gastrotrichs\h\h are able to do it because their incredibly small\h size allows them to live in the spaces between\h\h grains of substrate. Since the gastrotrichs\h are so small, they often make up an important\h\h part of the meiofuana, or meiobenthos of marine\h and freshwater environments. Some species live\h\h attached to plants and animals where they form\h an important part of the periphyton, which is a\h\h collection of small animals, algae, cyanobacteria,\h microbes, and organic matter that adheres to many\h\h submerged surfaces in aquatic environments,\h including live plants and animal shells.\h Gastrotrichs are important detritivores, as the\h fifth most abundant group of sediment-dwelling\h\h invertebrates on Earth. They are often an\h important part of the diet of free-living\h\h flatworms and other slightly larger\h animals. In terms of defenses,\h\h when threatened by something large enough\h to penetrate their outer covering, they can\h\h withdraw their body and inch backwards using a\h combination of muscle contraction and adhesion.\h In general, gastrotrichs are hermaphroditic, or\h monoecious, meaning they contain both male and\h\h female reproductive organs. Most marine species\h are simultaneously hermaphroditic, meaning they\h\h are born with both male and female reproductive\h organs and retain them throughout their lives.\h\h Most simultaneous hermaphrodites take part\h in internal mutual reciprocal sperm transfer\h\h where both partners are fertilized in unison. Most freshwater species have a sort of biphasic\h\h form of reproduction, wherein they are born as\h parthenogens, meaning they can produce mitotic,\h\h not meiotic eggs, in the absence of sexual\h reproduction. These eggs always hatch as females\h\h and are genetic clones of the mother. After\h a period of time, these same parthenogenetic\h\h mothers and daughters will become simultaneous\h hermaphrodites and develop sperm and meiotic eggs.\h\h No one has ever witnessed copulation and\h sperm exchange between two such gastrotrichs.\h Most gastrotrichs are oviparous, meaning they\h lay eggs. Some species can produce two different\h\h kinds of eggs; thick-shelled dormant eggs that\h can survive in harsh environments for years\h\h until conditions become more favorable,\h and thin-shelled eggs that hatch rapidly.\h\h Newly hatched gastrotrichs also develop extremely\h rapidly and often reach sexual maturity within\h\h three days. Some species survive for up to\h forty days, while many die much younger.\h Since gastrotrichs somewhat resemble a cross\h between a flatworm and a rotifer, the latter of\h\h which we will discuss later in the series, their\h classification has been in flux for some time.\h\h At various points since their discovery,\h they have classified with the deuterostomes,\h\h the rotifers, the nematodes, and the flatworms.\h The most recent analyses place them within the\h\h clade Rouphozoa, under Platytrochozoa, making\h them the sister clade of the flatworms.\h\h However it must be made clear that even this most\h recent classification is disputed by some studies,\h\h and could very well change as research continues.\h Regardless, the phylum Gastrotricha itself\h\h is considered to be monophyletic, and\h consists of about 840 described species\h\h as of the recording of this tutorial.

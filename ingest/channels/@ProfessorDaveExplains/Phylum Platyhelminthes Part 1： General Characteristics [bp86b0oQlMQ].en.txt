As any worm enthusiast will quickly realize, there\h are many so-called “flat worms” in the animal\h\h kingdom, but the animals of phylum Platyhelminthes\h were the first to earn that distinction. The name\h\h Platyhelminthes comes from the Greek words platy,\h meaning flat, and helminth, meaning worm. Thus,\h\h they are commonly referred to as flatworms. Though\h most of them are free-living, meaning they survive\h\h in nature without a host, others are parasitic. All members of phylum Platyhelminthes\h\h are triploblastic, meaning they have three\h unique cell layers. They are acoelomates,\h\h meaning they lack any true body cavities. And they\h are bilaterally symmetrical, meaning their bodies\h\h can be divided along a plane that splits the\h animal into a “left” and “right” side that are\h\h approximate mirror images of each other. They all\h have incomplete guts, meaning they lack an anus,\h\h and they demonstrate cephalization,\h meaning they have some type of head\h\h with a centralized nervous system. This\h nervous system is comprised of an anterior\h\h cerebral ganglion, somewhat like a “basal animal\h brain”, and longitudinal nerve cords connected by\h\h transverse nerves. The head region of many\h flatworms also contains light-sensitive ocelli,\h\h also known as light-sensitive eyespots, and a\h high concentration of chemoreceptors. Parasitic\h\h flatworms also have abundant sensory organs\h around their oral suckers and holdfast organs.\h Though flatworms have a nervous system and a\h complex reproductive system, they lack skeletal,\h\h respiratory, and circulatory systems. This might\h seem bizarre at first, since they are animals\h\h with three cell layers that lack blood. However,\h large flatworms have extremely flattened bodies,\h\h which means that cells can more easily exchange\h molecules between each other and the surrounding\h\h environment. The larger species also have a\h branched digestive system which helps with\h\h circulation of material throughout the body.\h Essentially, all of their cells are only a short\h\h distance from either the external environment,\h digestive system, or excretory system, allowing\h\h nutrients to exchange through diffusion. This\h eliminates the need for a complicated circulatory\h\h system, but restricts them to environments where\h dehydration is unlikely, such as seawater or\h\h freshwater, moist terrestrial environments such\h as leaf litter or between grains of soil, and as\h\h parasites within other animals. The majority\h of free-living flatworm species are small,\h\h around one millimeter in length, and cylindrical. Most free-living flatworms have a ciliated\h\h epidermis that sits on top of their body wall\h musculature, which has outer circular and inner\h\h longitudinal muscles. Rod-shaped rhabdites that\h form a protective mucus sheath around the body\h\h are produced by glands within the meshwork of\h their parenchyma cells, which are made up of\h\h cells that developed from the mesoderm and fill\h the spaces between muscles and visceral organs. In\h\h addition to these fixed cells, the parenchyma, or\h mesenchyme, also contains pluripotent stem cells,\h\h which can transform into any other type of\h cell, and are used in regenerating tissues\h\h after injury or asexual reproduction. Meanwhile, the surface of the epidermis\h\h of many free-living flatworms\h contains single-cell mucous glands\h\h along with dual-gland adhesive organs that are\h made of three cell types. These include adhesive,\h\h or viscid glands, which allow the animals to\h chemically attach themselves to the substrate,\h\h releaser gland cells that dissolve their adhesive\h and allow them to quickly detach their bodies,\h\h and anchor cells that hold the apparatus in place. Most platyhelminthes have a digestive system that\h\h begins at the mouth or pharynx, and terminates\h at the gastrovascular cavity or intestine, though\h\h some, like tapeworms, digest food directly across\h their body walls and lack any form of a digestive\h\h system. In many species, like fresh-water dwelling\h triclads, often called planarians, the mouth and\h\h pharynx are not located in the anterior region,\h like in most bilaterally symmetrical animals.\h\h They are instead located on the ventral side of\h the posterior region. In addition, the pharynx can\h\h often extend out through the mouth, allowing the\h flatworm to feed and still move its head freely.\h Platyhelminths have no anus and regurgitate\h undigested material through the mouth. Digestion\h\h normally begins extracellularly, that is, outside\h of the organism’s cells, due to the secretion of\h\h proteolytic enzymes that catalyze the breakdown of\h protein by cleaving peptide bonds. As the food is\h\h slowly broken down, the flatworm will generally\h suck small pieces into the intestine, often\h\h through a pharynx, where phagocytic cells will\h complete the digestion process intracellularly.\h In addition to a digestive and nervous system,\h parasitic and freshwater flatworms also possess\h\h excretory and osmoregulatory systems that are\h dually controlled by their protonephridia. The\h\h invertebrate nephridium occurs in pairs and\h functions similarly to the vertebrate kidney.\h\h That is, they remove metabolic\h waste from an animal’s body.\h\h The protonephridium of the flatworms is a network\h of dead-end tubules that lack internal openings.\h\h The dead-ends are called flame cells, which are\h cup-shaped and filled with beating flagella that\h\h resemble a flickering flame. The beating of\h the flagella moves fluid through the animal,\h\h excretes waste, and allows for osmoregulation. Almost all flatworms are monoecious,\h\h or hermaphroditic, meaning they have\h both male and female reproductive organs.\h\h And many are capable of both sexual and asexual\h reproduction. We will cover all of that in a\h\h later tutorial, since it is quite different\h for parasitic and nonparasitic flatworms.\h\h Traditionally, phylum Platyhelminthes was\h divided into the classes Turbellaria, Trematoda,\h\h Monogenea, and Cestoda. However, this\h classification is now obsolete since the parasitic\h\h trematodes, monogeneans, and cestodes are known\h to form a monophyletic group related to one of the\h\h groups previously classified within Turbellaria.\h This means that the parasitic groups evolved from\h\h a free-living ancestor. The four-class division\h of the Platyhelminthes is still often used in\h\h many textbooks due to its simplicity, but\h it is now widely recognized as artificial.\h Modern cladistics of Platyhelminthes reveal\h that the phylum is split into two sister clades:\h\h Catenulida and Rhabditophora. Members of\h Catenulida have protonephridium and testis\h\h unpaired, and nonmobile sperm. Members of\h Rhabditophora contain all parasitic flatworms\h\h and the majority of the species once\h considered to be part of Turbellaria.\h\h These flatworms are characterized by the ancestral\h presence of rod-like rhabdites that are absent in\h\h Catenulida, and their dual-gland adhesive organs.\h Rhabditophora contains at least ten orders\h\h of free-living flatworms and several parasitic\h orders.

The cestodes are a clade\h\h of parasitic flatworms, the most infamous\h of which are those of subclass Eucestoda,\h\h commonly referred to as tapeworms. These have long\h flattened bodies that vary dramatically in length.\h\h The smallest sexually mature specimens\h are about half a millimeter in length,\h\h while the largest can grow to over thirty meters,\h or almost 100 feet, and inhabit sperm whales.\h Their bodies begin with a headlike scolex,\h or holdfast, with two attachments called\h\h bothria, or four suckers that\h it uses to attach to its host.\h\h These scolexes are often also armed with\h hooks or spiny tentacles. Behind the scolex\h\h is the strobila, the tapeworm’s main body.\h The strobila begins with a germinative zone,\h\h sometimes referred to as the “neck” that forms\h new proglottids through mitotic division.\h\h This body plan is unique among the flatworms, not\h only for the apparent proglotization, but also\h\h because of the lack of any head region. Though\h the scolex superficially resembles a head, it\h\h is actually derived from the posterior end of the\h larva. These larval forms are known as oncospheres\h\h and have six hooks on their anterior end. However,\h some research suggests that as the larva grows\h\h these hooks are detached, and on the opposite\h end, the posterior end, the scolex develops.\h\h This would mean that tapeworms completely change\h the orientation of their bodies as they grow.\h In addition, tapeworms do not have intestines,\h and all nutrients are absorbed by a specialized\h\h surface layer of cells called neodermis, covered\h by unique appendages called microtriches, that are\h\h found only in tapeworms and no other animals. Almost all tapeworms are hermaphrodites,\h\h meaning they are both male and female at the\h same time, and each proglottid contains an\h\h independent reproductive complex as well as\h a chain of excretory canals. In addition,\h\h two nerve cords extend from the nerve ring in\h the scolex and run back through each proglottid.\h\h Like the flukes, the tapeworm reproductive system\h takes up the majority of their bodies and includes\h\h mostly numerous testes, seminal vesicles, sperm\h ducts, a copulatory organ called a cirrus, a\h\h single lobed or unlobed ovary, vagina, and uterus\h with eggs and unique vitellarium or multiple\h\h vitelline follicles, which produce vitelline\h cells that are included in the formation of eggs.\h\h Both male and female parts share a common\h external opening known as the genital pore,\h\h which is situated at the surface opening of\h the cup-shaped atrium. The uterus, meanwhile,\h\h is separated by the uterine pore on the ventral\h side of the proglottid. These genital complexes\h\h can also multiply within a single proglottid\h to two or even more sets of complexes,\h\h maximizing the reproduction potential of the\h tapeworm. Often individual tapeworms will\h\h cross-fertilize with other tapeworms in mutual\h exchange of sperm, however, if no other tapeworms\h\h are present, individual proglottids can instead\h fertilize other proglottids or self-fertilize.\h\h Since these worms are unable to synthesize\h lipids, which they need for reproduction, they are\h\h entirely dependent upon the diet of their host,\h whose food they absorb through their body walls.\h As the individual proglottids mature after\h fertilization they become gravid with eggs\h\h and continue to grow as new proglottids\h are added from the tapeworm neck.\h\h Eventually, they will break off or even crawl out\h of the anal cavity. The cow’s digestive system\h\h dissolves their shells, and they burrow through\h the intestinal wall, eventually reaching the\h\h muscle where they encrust themselves. Here they\h remain dormant in this stage called cysticercosis\h\h unless the undercooked meat, such as rare burgers,\h steak, or barbeque, is consumed by a human.\h\h The beef tapeworm is found around the world and\h is especially common in countries with a heavy\h\h beef diet, such as the United States, where a\h voluntary study that represented over 28.6 million\h\h cows found the infection rate to be between\h 10.8 and 18.4%. However, these parasites are\h\h easily identified by routine veterinary\h control in Europe and the United States.\h Other human tapeworms include the pork tapeworm,\h Taenia solium, and the broadfish tapeworm,\h\h Dibothriocephalus latus. Both of these make\h their way into their final hosts through the\h\h consumption of undercooked pork or fish.\h The pork tapeworm in particular can cause\h\h cysticercosis. In addition, there are some\h cases where pork tapeworms migrate to other\h\h parts of the body and cause damage to the liver,\h eyes, heart, and brain, or even induce epilepsy.\h If you’re feeling a little worried right now, we\h should note that it is always more dangerous to be\h\h an intermediate host of the tapeworm, rather than\h the final host. In total, less than 1,000 people\h\h are treated for tapeworm infections annually\h in the United States, but the total number\h\h of infections is probably much higher since they\h cause almost no discernable damage to their final\h\h host. Tapeworms normally cause considerably less\h damage to their final hosts than the trematodes,\h\h and many humans, especially those that regularly\h consume beef, may already house a tapeworm without\h\h knowing it. Usually, the only symptom in adults\h is the presence of proglottids in bowel movements,\h\h but unless the host is unhealthy, or paranoid,\h such proglottids are difficult to identify.\h For intermediate hosts, however, it’s a different\h story. Intermediate hosts generally suffer quite\h\h a bit more than the final hosts because the\h purpose of a secondary host, from the tapeworm’s\h\h perspective, is to help get the tapeworm into\h its final host.

Wrapping things up with phylum Rotifera, we\h reach the acanthocephalans. These are obligate\h\h endoparasites, meaning they are only found within\h the bodies of other animals, as they cannot\h\h survive outside of their host for long. The clade\h was once considered the sister phylum to Rotifera,\h\h but more recent analyses have placed them instead\h as a major clade within the rotifer phylum, though\h\h their exact relationship to other rotifer clades\h is still contested. As of 2022, modern cladograms\h\h place acanthocephalans as a subclass or clade\h alongside the bdelloid and seisonid rotifers.\h Acanthocephala get their namesake from their most\h distinctive feature, a cylindrical, eversible\h\h proboscis that bears rows of curved spines. This\h proboscis, when everted, meaning turned inside\h\h out, is used to anchor the parasite to the host’s\h intestinal wall. Like the free-living rotifers,\h\h some spiny-headed worms are considered to be\h cosmopolitan, meaning they can be found all\h\h over the world, and they include between 1,100\h and 1,500 described species. Their total species\h\h count is currently contested as they are the\h subject of numerous ongoing phylogenic studies.\h Species range in size from less than 2 millimeters\h to up to 1 meter in length. Females are generally\h\h slightly larger than males and both sexes are\h bilaterally flattened with numerous wrinkles\h\h throughout the body that aid in increasing the\h animal’s surface area. Since acanthocephalans\h\h completely lack a mouth, they absorb nutrients\h from their host directly across their body walls.\h\h Up to 80% of their mass is devoted to their\h unique lacunar system of fluid-filled canals.\h\h These animals have no heart, respiratory,\h digestive, or true circulatory system, but the\h\h muscles of the body wall form tubes that connect\h to the lacunar system. This forms a network of\h\h contractible tubes so that the body-wall muscles\h collectively function similarly to a heart.\h\h The animal is then able to pump lacunar fluid\h throughout the body by squeezing these muscles.\h\h This system essentially acts as a rudimentary\h circulatory system. Paired with an excretory\h\h system comprised of a pair of flame cells\h containing protonephridia, it is responsible for\h\h moving fluids into, within, and out of the animal. The acanthocephalan nervous system consists of a\h\h central ganglion within the proboscis receptacle\h and nerves through the proboscis and body. Though\h\h they do have sensory endings in the proboscis and\h genital area, the majority of the nervous system\h\h and sensory organs are greatly reduced\h as is common in obligate endoparasites.\h All known acanthocephalans are dioecious, meaning\h they possess either male or female reproductive\h\h organs. However, complete life cycles have\h only been worked out for a handful of species,\h\h as there are currently over 1,000 species\h only known from their adult phase.\h Adult spiny-headed worms mate within their final\h vertebrate host. Once an egg is fertilized, it\h\h matures and subsequently leaves the female's body\h through her oviduct, where it then passes into the\h\h host’s alimentary canal and is expelled from the\h host within the feces. In order for the egg to\h\h develop from this stage, it must be consumed by\h an arthropod, usually a crustacean. Inside the\h\h intermediate host, the egg develops into an\h acanthella. It then penetrates the gut wall,\h\h moves into the body cavity, encysts, and\h begins transformation into the infective\h\h cystacanth stage. This form has all the organs\h of the adult except the reproductive ones. The\h\h parasite is released when the first intermediate\h host is ingested. If this new host is a suitable\h\h final host, such as a vertebrate, the cystacanth\h develops into a mature adult. However, if the\h\h host is unsuitable, like a larger crustacean, the\h parasite will instead reform a cyst. It then feeds\h\h within the final host, where it grows and develops\h its sexual organs. Mating will then occur.\h Luckily, human infection by spiny-headed\h worms is extremely rare. The species\h\h Moniliformis moniliformis is the most common\h parasitic acanthocephalan found in humans,\h\h but since it requires its primary host to\h consume raw infested beetles or cockroaches,\h\h it very rarely passes to humans. Another\h potential human parasite are individuals\h\h of the genus Macracanthorhynchus, otherwise known\h as the “giant thorny-headed worm of swine” which\h\h occur throughout the world in the small intestine\h of pigs and very rarely in humans. In most cases,\h\h there is very little inflammation,\h and the parasite can co-exist within\h\h its host peacefully. However, in rare cases\h infection can cause great pain, lacerations,\h\h ulcers, and even death due to multiple\h infections.

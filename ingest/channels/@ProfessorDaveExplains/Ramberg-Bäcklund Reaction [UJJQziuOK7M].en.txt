For the last few tutorials, we have\h been discussing olefination reactions.\h\h A rather unique and intriguing approach to olefins\h is the Ramberg-Bäcklund reaction, described\h\h for the first time by Swedish chemist Ludwig\h Ramberg and his student Birger Bäcklund in 1940.\h\h The reaction starts with alpha-halo sulfones,\h where X is most commonly chlorine or bromine,\h\h and it induces extrusion of SO2 using a strong\h base. The product is an olefin, usually a mixture\h\h of E and Z isomers, unless the sulfone is cyclic. Although this reaction may appear rather bizarre\h\h in comparison to other, more linear olefin\h syntheses, it has been used rather frequently.\h\h Its advantages are that alpha-halo sulfones\h are available through a variety of methods,\h\h as well as the fact that highly substituted\h olefins can be made, and especially strained\h\h ones, which would be more problematic to\h synthesize through an intermolecular process.\h The mechanism of the reaction is rather\h straightforward. The first step is the formation\h\h of a carbanion through the agency of the base.\h Keep in mind that the sulfone group is highly\h\h electron-withdrawing and capable of stabilizing\h a negative charge on the adjacent carbon,\h\h a little bit like a carbonyl. Thus, common\h bases like hydroxide or tert-butoxide ions\h\h can form the carbanion. Once formed, the carbanion\h finds a leaving group in the same molecule in the\h\h form of the halogen just two atoms away. Thus,\h an SN2-like intramolecular reaction follows,\h\h with formation of a strained three-membered ring. A three-membered ring with two carbons and a\h\h sulfur is called a thiirane. Since\h the sulfur is completely oxidized,\h\h we called this intermediate thiirane dioxide.\h These rings have a tendency to fragment.\h\h The reaction of extrusion of SO2 is an example\h of a cheletropic reaction. We have introduced\h\h pericyclic reactions in previous tutorials, and a\h cheletropic reaction is a subset within pericyclic\h\h reactions. In the most classical example that we\h have discussed at great length, the Diels-Alder\h\h reaction, two new bonds are made simultaneously\h between two different sets of carbon pairs, so\h\h four carbons are involved in the formation of new\h sigma bonds. Of course, in the retro-Diels-Alder,\h\h two bonds are being broken between two different\h pairs of carbons, which is the precise opposite.\h\h In a cheletropic reaction, on the other hand, two\h bonds are being formed or broken to the same atom.\h\h In this case the sulfur loses two S-C bonds\h in one step. This reaction is concerted,\h\h and therefore whatever the stereochemistry\h we had in the thiirane dioxide,\h\h that will be retained through to the olefin. Of course, it is not trivial to make pure\h\h E or Z 1,2-disubstituted thiirane dioxides.\h Simple alpha-halo sulfones yield a mixture\h\h in which the Z isomer predominates, for\h reasons which are not fully understood.\h\h E-olefins are accessible usually if excess\h base is used, because the thiirane dioxide\h\h can equilibrate to the more stable E isomer, and\h fragments stereospecifically to the E-olefin.\h In terms of the starting material, there\h are many methods for making the required\h\h alpha-halo sulfone. The most common\h one is alpha-halogenation of a sulfide\h\h with N-chlorosuccinimide, followed by oxidation\h of the alpha-chloro sulfide with a peracid.\h\h Another involves the alpha-chlorination of an\h alpha-lithiosulfone, but there are many others.\h Given the lack of complete stereoselectivity of\h the reaction, it is often used in cyclic systems,\h\h where the Z-geometry of the double bond\h is ensured by the excessive strain of\h\h the hypothetical E-olefin. For example,\h the synthesis of cyclobutenes is very difficult,\h\h and few olefin syntheses can match the success\h of the Ramberg-Bäcklund approach in this case.\h There are many modifications of the\h Ramberg-Bäcklund reaction, in which a wide\h\h variety of leaving groups are used. An example is\h the epoxy-Ramberg-Bäcklund reaction, in which the\h\h leaving group is an epoxide. This leads to allylic\h alcohols, often in very good E selectivity.\h\h Due to its versatility, the Ramberg-Bäcklund\h olefin synthesis continues to find widespread\h\h use in synthesis, and 80 years after its discovery\h it is still receiving new creative applications.

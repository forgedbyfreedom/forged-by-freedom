Professor Dave here, let’s talk about reciprocal verbs. The word reciprocal contains two Latin prefixes. Re means back, and pro means forward. These suggest the idea of alternating, or going back and forth between two people. That’s what you do when you reciprocate something, like kindness, or a gift. You receive one, and you give one. This concept is also used in grammar with some specific reflexive verbs in which the action doesn’t quite turn back to the subject, but rather is exchanged between two or more different people. Here, the action is performed on one another. The reciprocal verbs will act just like reflexive verbs, and they will use the same reflexive pronouns. Let’s see how this works, using the verb abbracciarsi. This means “to hug”, but you can’t really hug yourself. A second person is needed. For this reason, we won’t look at singular forms, only the plural forms for each person, which are noi, voi, and loro. Let’s go through a list of some other common reciprocal verbs now, starting with the first conjugation, which will be the most numerous. Now a few from the second conjugation. And finally, just one from the third conjugation. In the past tense, just like for the reflexive, reciprocals will always use the auxiliary essere, and the past participle will need to agree with the subject.

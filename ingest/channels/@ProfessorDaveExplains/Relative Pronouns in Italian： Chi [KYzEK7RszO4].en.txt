Professor Dave again, let’s discuss chi. We know about the relative pronouns che, cui, and il quale, in all its forms. To this list let’s add just one more pronoun, chi. This can be confusing as it is close to the other relative pronoun, che, and also looks identical to the interrogative pronoun “chi” which means “who”, but the relative pronoun has a slightly different meaning that will make sense in context. First, to understand its meaning, let’s realize that chi is technically an invariable double pronoun that combines “colui” (to use the most common form) and “che” to get “the one who”. But we will also use this in place of a number of phrases such as the following: As we said, colui or colei che, which means he who or she who, can be written as chi. The same goes for the plural, coloro che, which means those who. Then qualsiasi persona che, or whoever, uno che, or the one who, and la persona che, or the person who. All of these can be shortened to simply “chi”. As we said, this pronoun is invariable, does not require an antecedent, and it can be accompanied by a preposition or not.

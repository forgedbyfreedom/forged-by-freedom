In\h most animals, the cells of the blastula then begin\h\h to shift and differentiate into cell groups that\h will become specialized for different functions\h\h within the animal. These layers\h\h are separated by a primarily noncellular,\h water-filled tissue layer called the mesoglea,\h\h which we will discuss in more detail later in\h the series. The ectoderm develops into epidermis,\h\h the outer layer of the body wall. The endoderm,\h meanwhile, gives rise to the gastrodermis, the\h\h tissues that line the gut cavity. The mesoglea is\h not considered to be a true cell layer because any\h\h cells found within it arise from the endoderm or\h the ectoderm. The cells within each tissue layer\h\h are functionally interdependent. The gastrodermis\h consists of digestive and muscular cells, and the\h\h epidermis contains epithelial and muscular cells.\h The feeding and general movements of a jellyfish,\h\h for example, are only possible when groups\h of cells from both tissue layers cooperate.\h Now, unlike the Cnidarians,\h most animals are triploblastic,\h\h meaning they have a third embryological layer\h sandwiched between the ectoderm and the endoderm\h\h called the mesoderm.

This contains all the flying\h\h insects, of which there are many orders,\h so let’s go through them one at a time.\h Over 3,000 species of order Ephemeroptera are\h collectively known as mayflies, shadflies,\h\h or fishflies. The name Ephemeroptera is a\h reference to their “ephemeral” or short lives as\h\h adults that can sometimes aggregate in tremendous\h numbers during the breeding season. Though the\h\h adults do not feed, have vestigial mouthparts,\h and are indeed short-lived, their immature fresh\h\h water forms known as naiads or nymphs survive\h as freshwater hunters that eventually molt into\h\h a subimago and finally an imago, or adult form.\h The adults are characterized by their long tails,\h\h and wings that do not fold flat over the\h abdomen. Their ancestors are thought to\h\h be some of the first flying insects. Closely related to the mayflies are the\h\h dragonflies and damsel flies of order Odonata.\h The dragonflies of suborder Epiprocta are usually\h\h larger, with eyes together and wings up or out at\h rest, while the damselflies of suborder Zygoptera,\h\h are usually smaller, with eyes placed apart and\h wings along the body at rest. Like the mayflies,\h\h they have aquatic larvae called nymphs or naiads,\h all of which are carnivorous and some of which\h\h are large enough to eat small vertebrates\h like fish and tadpoles. By some estimates,\h\h they are among the most successful hunters\h on the planet, with over 95% of dragonfly\h\h hunts ending in success, compared to the\h measly 25% chance of a successful lion hunt.\h The order Orthoptera includes the familiar\h grasshoppers, locusts, crickets, katydids, and\h\h wētā, the giant flight-less crickets. It is split\h into two suborders: Caelifera, which includes the\h\h grasshoppers, locusts, and close relatives; and\h Ensifera, the true crickets, camel crickets,\h\h katydids, wētā, and cooloola monsters. There are\h more than 24,000 described orthopteran species,\h\h all of which, like the mayflies and odonatas,\h display incomplete metamorphosis. They are\h\h generally capable of producing sounds through\h stridulation by rubbing parts of their bodies\h\h against corrugated bumps on other parts of their\h bodies. Though many play an important ecosystem\h\h role, some species, like the locusts can cause\h extreme crop damage during their swarming phase.\h The lesser-known members of order Neuroptera are\h collectively referred to as “net-winged insects”,\h\h includes the lacewings, mantidflies, antlions,\h snakeflies, alderflies, and owlflies. There\h\h are over 6,000 described species of Neuropterans,\h adults of which have four large membranous wings,\h\h and all of which are about the same size with\h numerous visible veins. They undergo complete\h\h metamorphosis and have a larval stage. Larvae\h of most families are predators that prey upon\h\h aphids and pest insects, and have even been\h used as biological controls. Juvenile antlions\h\h are particularly well known for their ability\h to create sand pit traps and eat any insects,\h\h especially ants, that get trapped within it. The 3,000 or so described species of order\h\h Phasmatodea are commonly known as stick insects,\h stick-bugs, walking sticks, or stick animals.\h\h Members of the family Phylliidae are also commonly\h known as “leaf insects”, not to be confused with\h\h orthopteran katydids. Some members of this order\h can grow to be quite large, over 63 centimeters or\h\h 2 feet in length. They are generally characterized\h by their elongated, thin bodies, and are\h\h herbivores. The more than 400 described species\h\h of footspinners are generally similar in\h appearance, with long, flexible bodies,\h\h and short legs. They are sexually dimorphic\h and are found mainly in tropical regions.\h Members of order Notoptera are all wingless\h and have no common name. They\h include the ice-crawlers and the gladiators.\h The more than 3,500 described species of order\h Plecoptera are commonly known as “stoneflies.”\h\h They go through incomplete metamorphosis with\h an aquatic, predatory nymph. They have\h simple mouthparts with chewing mandibles,\h\h long, multiple-segmented antennae, large\h compound eyes, and two or three ocelli.\h\h They are often distinguished by the long,\h paired cerci that project from the tips of\h\h the abdomen in both nymphs and adults. Earwigs are largely nocturnal,\h\h and often hide in small, moist crevices during\h the day, and are active at night, feeding on a\h\h wide variety of insects and plants. Though they\h are common near humans, they transmit\h\h no diseases and are considered harmless. They are\h\h all predatory with elongated bodies and\h raptorial front legs, triangular heads,\h\h large compound eyes, and flexible necks.\h They have a range of antipredator defenses,\h\h including camouflage and threat displays. They\h have had a range of cultural impacts on humans,\h\h from martial arts to mythology, art, and\h religion, and have been used as biological\h\h controls against pest insects.

Continuing along with our journey through\h phylum Arthropoda, we reach the clade Crustacea.\h\h Traditionally, Crustacea is considered one of the\h five arthropod subphyla. In order to best study this group,\h let’s go over their general characteristics,\h\h external anatomy, internal anatomy,\h reproduction and life cycle, and ecology.\h Crustaceans vary widely in their body plans.\h They include animals as diverse as decapods,\h\h seed shrimp, branchiopods, fish lice, krill,\h remipedes, tongue worms, isopods, barnacles,\h\h copepods, amphipods and lobsters. They range in\h size from microscopic tantulocarids all the way\h\h up to the Japanese spider crab with a leg span\h of over 12 feet and a mass of 44 pounds. Most\h\h crustaceans are marine, but there are quite a\h few freshwater and terrestrial species as well,\h\h like the water fleas and isopods respectively. Crustaceans, like all arthropods, are segmented,\h\h bilaterally symmetrical ecdysozoans, with jointed\h appendages, and an open circulatory system\h\h that makes use of a haemocoel. They are often\h distinguished from other arthropods due to their\h\h ancestral condition of two pairs of antennae,\h combined with a single pair of mandibles,\h\h and two pairs of maxillae on the head, often\h followed by a pair of appendages on each body\h\h segment. Most crustaceans have between 16 and\h 20 individual segments, but some species may\h\h have 60 or more, like the remipedes. The major\h tagmata of most crustaceans is the head, thorax,\h\h and abdomen, though in many crustaceans,\h one or more thoracic segments are fused\h\h with the head to form the cephalothorax,\h with is a common feature in decapods such\h\h as crayfish and shrimp. In addition, in many\h crustaceans, the dorsal cuticle of the head\h\h may extend posteriorly and around the sides of the\h animal, forming a structure known as a carapace.\h For external anatomy, we will refer to a large\h crayfish as a model organism. Keep in mind,\h\h not all of these characteristics apply to all\h crustaceans, especially parasitic and sessile\h\h forms like the barnacles. However, this does\h apply to a great deal of the members of class\h\h Malacostraca, including the decapods. The\h crustacean cuticle is composed of chitin,\h\h protein, and a calcareous material, and the\h harder, heavier plates of larger crustaceans are\h\h often particularly high in calcareous deposits.\h The cuticle is often soft and thin at the joints,\h\h thus permitting movement. All segments posterior\h to the carapace, if present, are covered by\h\h a dorsal plate known as a tergum. The abdomen\h terminates in the telson, flanked by the uropod.\h Members of classes Malacostraca and Remipedia\h typically have a pair of jointed appendages\h\h on each segment, though many other classes lack\h appendages on their abdomen. Looking at a typical\h\h crayfish, we begin with the cephalothorax, at the\h rostrum, followed by the stalked compound eye that\h\h is typical in decapods, the first antennae, the\h second antennae, then a single pair of mandibles,\h\h two pairs of maxillae, three pairs of maxillipeds,\h followed by the cheliped, or first leg that often\h\h bears claws in the malacostracans,\h and then four pairs of walking legs,\h\h for a total of 10 walking legs in the decapods,\h hence the name “decapoda”. The second and third\h\h maxilliped and all walking legs also bare gills\h located within the chitinous exoskeleton. Next,\h\h each segment of the abdomen contains a pair\h of swimmerets that are used in locomotion.\h Let’s look a bit closer at a biramous, meaning\h two main branches, crustacean appendage, in this\h\h case the third maxilliped. The basal portion,\h which includes the gills, attachment tissue,\h\h and coxa, makes up the protopod. The protopod\h bears an exopod and an endopod. This ancestral\h\h biramous structure has been modified in many\h walking legs to make them secondarily uniramous.\h Like external anatomy, crustacean internal\h anatomy varies by class, but is mostly\h\h consistent within class Malacostraca.\h Again we will use a large crayfish as a\h\h model organism. Most crustaceans have muscular,\h respiratory, circulatory, excretory, digestive,\h\h endocrine and nervous systems. Though the muscular and nervous\h\h systems in the thorax and abdomen show clear\h segmentation, there are marked modifications\h\h in other systems. One important body space\h to always remember in the arthropods is the\h\h hemocoel. This is a fluid-filled body cavity\h that is essential for many body systems.\h The crustacean muscular system is comprised mainly\h of striated muscles arranged into two main groups:\h\h flexors, which draw a part towards the\h body, and extensors, which extend the\h\h part outwards. The abdomen of a crayfish, for\h example, has powerful flexors which are used\h\h by the animal to quickly swim backwards. Many smaller species of crustaceans lack a\h\h respiratory system and instead exchange gases\h directly across thin areas of the cuticle,\h\h like the appendages. Larger crustaceans, however,\h have gills which are delicate, featherlike,\h\h and covered in an extremely thin cuticle. In\h marine decapods, the gills are attached to the\h\h walking legs and are suspended within the hemocoel\h where they exchange gases. The body cavity is open\h\h to the surrounding water in the ventral anterior\h area. Depending on the\h species, they are known as antennal glands,\h\h maxillary glands, or green glands. Most consist of\h a tubular labyrinth connected to a bladder. This\h\h system primarily functions as an osmoregulatory\h system, and may diffuse a low-salt urine.\h The digestive system of most crustaceans begins\h after the ingestion of food that then passes\h\h into a short esophagus and a large two-chambered\h stomach. Most crustaceans are predatory,\h\h but quite a few are suspension feeders, and a\h few, like the yeti crabs that survive around\h\h hydrothermal vents may obtain most of their food\h through a form of symbiosis with the filamentous\h\h bacteria that cover their “hairy” pincers. Finally, the nervous system, like all crustacean\h\h body systems, is highly modified by taxon.

They are some of\h the most common arthropods in the fossil record,\h\h and they are an important part of marine\h ecology, as they are often considered to\h\h be zooplankton. Some, like the sea-firefly, have\h a light organ that produces luminescent chemicals.\h The ostracods are generally accepted to be\h split into 5 orders, but depending on the\h\h classification there may be many others. There\h is order Myodocopida which includes the giant\h\h ostracod. Members of this order have lateral\h compound eyes as well as a rostrum and incisure\h\h from which the antennae protrude. Members of\h order Halocyprida are similar to Myodocopida,\h\h but they lack lateral eyes. Most species of order\h Palaeocopida are now extinct, and photos of living\h\h taxa are rare. Next, class Mystacocarida is monotypic,\h\h containing a single order. There\h are about 170 described species which can be found\h\h on both marine and freshwater fish. Next up is class Copepoda, commonly known\h\h as “copepods,” though their name means “oar feet.”\h They are a widespread group of small crustaceans,\h\h most are just a few millimeters in length. They\h are usually tear-drop shaped with large antennae.\h\h However, their morphology varies considerably,\h from the bioluminescent deep sea copepod,\h\h to parasitic forms like Lernaeolophus sultanus and\h the cod worm. Regardless of their morphological\h\h divergences, all copepods lack a carapace and many\h adults retain a simple median compound eye. They\h\h have been found in nearly every freshwater and\h saltwater habitat on earth. Though most species\h\h are planktonic, some are benthic, others are\h parasitic, others have been found living in\h\h swamps and small puddles, and still others live in\h subterranean groundwater. Though most are tiny,\h their collective biomass exceeds billions of\h\h metric tons. There are at least 13,000 described\h species of copepods, often split into 10 orders:\h\h Calanoida, Cyclopoida, Gelyelloida, Harpacticoida,\h Misophrioida, Monstrilloida, Mormonilloida,\h\h Platycopioida, Polyarthra, and Siphonostomatoida.\h Free-living members of the largest orders\h\h Calanoida, Cyclopoida, and Harpacticoida generally\h have a typical copepod anatomy of a short,\h\h cylindrical body, with a rounded or beaked head,\h though there is a great diversity of forms. Orders\h\h Gelyelloida, Misophrioida, Monstrilloida,\h Mormonilloida, Platycopioida, and Polyarthra\h\h are all considered to be more cryptic.

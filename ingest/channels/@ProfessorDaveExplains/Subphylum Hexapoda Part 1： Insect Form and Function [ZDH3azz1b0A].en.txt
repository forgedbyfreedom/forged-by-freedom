In this tutorial\h we will focus on the clade Insecta, also called\h\h Ectognatha. Insects are, like all arthropods,\h segmented, bilaterally symmetrical ecdysozoans,\h\h with jointed appendages, and an open circulatory\h system that makes use of a hemocoel. However,\h\h they are easily distinguished from the other\h arthropods due to their 3-part body that is\h\h generally made up of a head, thorax, and abdomen,\h their 3 pairs of jointed legs, compound eyes,\h\h and single pair of antennae. Here we will\h focus on insect external and internal anatomy.\h Insect external anatomy is much more homogenous in\h tagmatization than the crustaceans. Insect tagmata\h\h generally includes the head, thorax, and abdomen.\h But in some species, like many Hymenopterans,\h\h the three main segments are the head, the\h mesosoma, which is the thorax and the first\h\h segment of the abdomen, and the metasoma. The head\h is located at the front of the insect and contains\h\h the sensory organs, such as the antennae, compound\h eyes, three ocelli, palps, clypeus, and mouth.\h Insect mouths generally vary between four main\h uses, chewing or biting, siphoning, piercing, or\h\h sponging. Chewing mouthparts are used for example\h by grasshoppers, beetles, ants, and mantids to\h\h bite off and chew plant material or prey. The\h mandibles of these insects are typically strong\h\h and adapted for cutting and grinding. Siphoning\h mouthparts are found in moths and butterflies.\h\h These insects have long, tubular mouthparts that\h are used to suck nectar from flowers. Piercing\h\h mouthparts are found in mosquitos and fleas. These\h insects use long, needle-like mouthparts to pierce\h\h the skin of their host and suck blood or other\h fluids. Sponging mouthparts are found in insects\h\h such as houseflies. They have a pair of large\h lobes with grooves used to lap at liquid or expel\h\h digestive enzymes to liquify food. Some insects\h may have more than one mouth type. For example,\h\h horse flies have primarily sponging mouthparts\h in addition to mandibles they use to pierce\h\h flesh first before sponging up fluids. Moving posteriorly, the thorax is generally\h\h made up of the prothorax, mesothorax, and\h metathorax, and each bares a pair of legs.\h\h The forelegs attach to the prothorax,\h the midlegs attach to the mesothorax,\h\h and the hindlegs attach to the metathorax. Insect\h legs are typically broken down into the coxa,\h\h where the legs meet the abdomen, trochanter,\h femur, tibia, and tarsus. The individual\h\h parts of the tarsi are known as tarsomeres and\h they end in tarsal claws which may be highly\h\h modified. Most insects also bare a pair of wings\h on both the mesothorax and metathorax. The first\h\h pair of wings are known as the forewings and the\h second pair of wings are known as the hindwings.\h Insects, the only invertebrates capable of\h flying, evolved flight much earlier than the\h\h vertebrates. They accomplish it through direct\h and/or indirect flight muscles. In some species,\h\h like the dragonflies and mayflies, flight\h muscles attach directly to the wings and\h\h are neuronally controlled by a synchronous\h system, where the nervous system stimulates\h\h a single muscle contraction and thus a single\h wing stroke. Other insects, like the flies,\h\h beetles, and hymenopterans which include bees and\h wasps, use an asynchronous system to activate an\h\h extremely high wing beat using indirect\h flight muscles to oscillate the thorax.\h The legs and wings of insects vary greatly\h depending on lifestyle. For example, a grasshopper\h\h has long, strong legs adapted for jumping, while\h a praying mantis has long, spiny legs adapted for\h\h grasping prey, and the limbs of many termites\h are nearly uniform. The wings of insects are\h\h also adapted for their specific lifestyle, with\h some insects having wings that are transparent\h\h and delicate, while others have forewings that\h are thick and protective, called an elytra in\h\h the beetles. Dipterans, or the flies, have only\h the forewings, as the hindwings have been reduced\h\h to tiny club-like structures known as halteres\h which vibrate and maintain equilibrium in flight.\h Lastly, the abdomen contains the majority of\h the digestive, reproductive, and excretory\h\h systems. It is comprised of 9 to 11 segments whose\h dorsal ends are known as tergites, which often\h\h contain spiracles, and ventral ends are known as\h sternites. The 11th segment, when present, often\h\h houses a pair of cerci. The genital openings often\h emerge from segments 8 and 9. Females of many\h\h species also have an ovipositor, used to deposit\h eggs. The hymenopteran stinger of many wasps and\h\h bees is actually just a modified ovipositor. Now moving from external anatomy to internal\h\h anatomy, most insects have complete digestive,\h excretory, muscular, circulatory, tracheal,\h\h endocrine, nervous, and reproductive systems. The\h insect digestive system is comprised of a long,\h\h enclosed tube known as an alimentary canal which\h runs the length of the body. This canal is made of\h\h three parts. The first is the foregut, which\h includes the mouth, pharynx, and esophagus.\h\h Digestion begins in the buccal cavity, or\h externally in the case of the sponging mouthparts.\h\h Food is mixed with saliva, and pumped into the\h pharynx which passes food to the esophagus,\h\h which passes food directly to the midgut, or the\h crop and proventriculus for further grinding,\h\h or the fluid-filled diverticulum in flies. The\h majority of digestion often takes place within\h\h the midgut where microvilli allow for increased\h surface area and absorption. Finally, undigested\h\h food passes into the hindgut where it is joined by\h uric acid from the excretory system to form fecal\h\h pellets. The rectum re-absorbs water from the\h pellets and the mostly dry pellets are expelled\h\h through the anus. However, some species, like the\h leaf-mining and plant-sap feeding aphids, excrete\h\h sugar-rich honeydew instead which may be harvested\h by ants who essentially “farm” the substance.\h The insect excretory system may consist of\h one to many hundreds of Malpighian tubules\h\h that remove nitrogenous waste from the\h hemolymph and regulate osmotic balance.\h\h This joins directly with the digestive system. The muscular system of insects ranges from a\h\h few hundred muscles to a few thousand. Unlike\h vertebrates that have both smooth and striated\h\h muscles, insects have only striated muscles.\h Muscle cells are amassed into muscle fibers\h\h and then into the functional unit, the muscle.\h These muscles are attached to the body wall,\h\h with attachment fibers running through\h the cuticle and to the epicuticle,\h\h where they can move different parts of the\h body including appendages such as wings.\h The insect open circulatory system consists\h of a tubular dorsal heart that pumps hemolymph\h\h through the dorsal aorta. Once released,\h secondary pulsatory organs push the hemolymph\h\h throughout the body. The hemolymph transports\h nutrients, wastes, hormones, and immune cells,\h\h though unlike vertebrate blood, it plays very\h little role in oxygen transport and gas exchange.\h Insects accomplish gas exchange\h through their tracheal system,\h\h which is an extensive network of thin-walled\h tubes that branch into every part of the body\h\h and delivers oxygen directly to individual cells\h through the fluid-filled tracheoles. Oxygen is\h\h brought into the system and carbon dioxide is\h released through tiny openings called spiracles,\h\h which are located along the abdomen and thorax.\h The system sometimes includes air sacs. Some\h\h very small insects exchange gases entirely\h though diffusion, and many larval forms of\h\h terrestrial insects, such as the nymphs of\h stoneflies and mayflies, have tracheal gills\h\h which are thin extensions of the tracheal system,\h while dragonfly nymphs have rectal gills. Others,\h\h like larval mosquitos have a tiny “snorkel” that\h extends from the rectum, while still others,\h\h like the diving beetles and water scorpions,\h trap a layer of air against their bodies,\h\h allowing for passive diffusion of oxygen and\h carbon dioxide with the surrounding water.\h The insect endocrine, or hormonal system, works\h in conjunction with the circulatory system as the\h\h hemolymph carries hormones that are produced\h by glandular and neuronal organs which play\h\h a role in reproduction, metamorphosis,\h molting, dormancy, and other behaviors.\h The insect nervous system is essentially split\h between the central nervous system, peripheral\h\h nervous system, and sensory organs. The central\h nervous system consists of a three-lobed brain,\h\h and ventral nerve cord. The peripheral nervous\h system consists of motor neurons and sensory\h\h neurons that detect environmental stimuli through\h the sensory organs. Insects have a wide range\h\h of sensory organs. Most adults have two types\h of eyes, simple and compound. The simple eyes\h\h of most insects take the form of three ocelli at\h the top of their head that are especially adapted\h\h to monitor light intensity and photoperiods. The\h compound eyes, meanwhile, are made up of thousands\h\h of individual ommatidia, up to 30,000 in some\h species, though some ants only have 2 and most\h\h have less than 10,000. These individual ommatidia\h can distinguish 200 to 300 individual flashes of\h\h light each second, far greater than humans, who\h often can only distinguish between 45 and 55\h\h frames per second, so to speak. Many species are\h capable of seeing in the ultraviolet spectrum,\h\h but not red, which is invisble to insects,\h much like how ultraviolet is invisible to us.\h In addition, insects are capable of receiving\h mechanical stimuli through sensilla,\h\h which range from an individual seta, or\h hair-like projection attached to nerve cells,\h\h or more complex organs such as chordotonal organs,\h or stretch organs, located in the joints. Setae\h\h capable of receiving mechanical reception are\h widely distributed throughout the body, legs,\h\h and especially antennae. Insects can also detect\h auditory signals through different setae, or\h\h through the tympanal organs that are most common\h in orthopterans like grasshoppers and crickets,\h\h hemipterans, and lepidopterans. Insects are also\h capable of chemoreception, meaning taste or smell,\h\h mainly through sensory cells around the mouths\h but also dispersed through the tarsal organs\h\h on the legs, wings, and antennae. The number\h and location of chemosensory cells differs\h\h greatly by species. In addition, insects also\h have nociceptor, or pain receptors, scattered\h\h throughout their bodies, and many species can\h also sense temperature, humidity, and gravity.\h Finally, most insects are dioecious\h with corresponding reproductive systems,\h\h but we will cover that in the next tutorial, where\h we examine the life cycle and behavior of insects.

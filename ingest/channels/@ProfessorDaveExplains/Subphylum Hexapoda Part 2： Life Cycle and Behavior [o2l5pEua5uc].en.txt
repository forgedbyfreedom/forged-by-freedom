In the previous tutorial, we examined the\h external and internal anatomy of insects. Now\h\h let’s continue by looking at life cycle, including\h metamorphosis and growth, behavior and defense,\h\h and finally, relationships with humans. Beginning with reproduction, although\h\h parthenogenesis occurs in some life cycles,\h internal sexual reproduction is much more common.\h\h Insects have a range of methods to attract\h mates. Many species use pheromones, others\h\h like fireflies use light, while still others use\h sound, like grasshoppers, crickets, and cicadas.\h\h Sperm is often transferred directly from the male\h penis into the female vagina during copulation,\h\h but in some species like the silverfish,\h the male leaves packets of spermatophores,\h\h and in still other species like bed bugs, the\h male pierces the female’s body with his hypodermic\h\h penis and releases sperm into her body cavity.\h Most insects mate only once in their lifetime,\h\h though some, like the damselflies mate many\h times a day, and the female will often store\h\h male sperm within her seminal receptacle. The vast majority of insects are oviparous,\h\h meaning they lay eggs, but the live-bearing\h viviparous aphids, tsetse flies, and bat flies are\h\h notable exceptions. Many insects lay their eggs\h on or near a specific substrate that will aid the\h\h developing young. For example, tiger moths look\h for pigweed, monarchs may search for milkweed,\h\h dragonflies lay their eggs in water, and parasitic\h wasps lay their eggs on or in the bodies of other\h\h animals, like the mud daubers that stuff spiders\h into their nests, or the braconid wasps that lay\h\h eggs on caterpillars. Some parasitic wasps like\h Lysibia nana are hyperparasites that parasitize\h\h other parasitic wasps. Regardless of where the egg\h is laid, early development occurs within the egg,\h\h and the young hatch. In some life cycles, the eggs\h can persist in an extreme form of dormancy called\h\h diapause. Different species of insects in areas\h with prominent seasonal changes, such as temperate\h\h regions, enter this dormant state at different\h life stages such as eggs, larvae, pupae, or\h\h even adults in some species that live for years. Hatched eggs then go through one of three types of\h\h metamorphosis that varies primarily by order. For\h example, a few insects, such as the silverfish,\h\h go through direct development or ametabolous\h metamorphosis, where the young are similar to the\h\h parents except smaller and sexually immature.\h Their stages are therefore, egg, juvenile,\h\h adult. Others, like the grasshoppers, cicadas,\h mayflies, dragonflies, and many others go through\h\h hemimetabolous, or incomplete metamorphosis,\h where the young are known as nymphs or naiads,\h\h and the adults are sometimes called imago. This\h is different from direct development because the\h\h nymphs often have very different life histories\h from the adults and may inhabit a completely\h\h different environment. Finally, holometabolous, or\h complete metamorphosis, is undergone by more than\h\h 85% of all insect species. Here, the insects hatch\h from an egg as a larva, also called a caterpillar,\h\h grump, or maggot, that develops and grows,\h eventually creating a pupa, also called a cocoon\h\h or chrysalis in some species, from which an adult,\h or imago, emerges. This cycle is notable in that\h\h within the pupa, the entire body of the insect\h often liquifies before reforming as an adult.\h\h In temperate regions, many insects overwinter as\h pupae that give rise to adults in the spring. The\h\h processes of development and molting are done\h mainly by the nervous and hormonal systems.\h Insects have a wide range of defense\h mechanisms. The tough cuticular exoskeleton\h\h provides protection, and many species of\h beetles have further strengthened it. Some,\h\h like stink bugs, produce repulsive odors and\h tastes. Others, like the bombardier beetles,\h\h eject noxious chemical sprays. Still others,\h like many female hymenopterans, have modified\h\h their ovipositors into stingers. Some species\h of insects have bright warning colorations,\h\h while other species mimic potentially dangerous\h insects, like the harmless hoverflies that have\h\h similar colorations as yellowjackets. Since insects have a range of sensory\h\h structures, they also have a range of methods\h for communication. They do so through chemical,\h\h visual, auditory, and tactile cues. Most chemical\h signals take the form of pheromones which may\h\h impact behavior or psychological responses\h in others. Though a sense of hearing is not\h\h present in all insects, some species can produce\h sounds, like grasshoppers and cicadas, that can\h\h be used in courtship and defense. Some species use\h flight patterns, colorations, or bioluminescence\h\h as visual cues to other species or as courtship\h displays. Finally, tactile communication, such\h\h as tapping, stroking, grasping, and especially\h antennae touching, can evoke a wide range of\h\h responses, from recognition to recruitment to\h alarm. This is especially well-known in the\h\h case of the gregarious phase in locusts, which is\h triggered by tactile communication when solitary\h\h locusts group together due to environmental\h conditions and form massive, destructive swarms.\h Though many insect behaviors are thought to\h be innate, there is a great deal of evidence\h\h that suggests insects are capable of learning and\h pattern memorization, and some species do so with\h\h ease. Sociality among the insects varies widely.\h Many species are largely solitary, and potentially\h\h aggressive to members of their own species, though\h this often changes during the mating season. Some\h\h species form temporary or uncoordinated groups\h like the hibernating associations of carpenter\h\h bees, or the feeding clusters of aphids.\h Others coordinate for brief periods of time,\h\h like the tent caterpillars, while others, like the\h Bess beetles, live in loosely connected societies\h\h and communicate through a series of squeaks. However, in the eusocial hymenopterans,\h\h those being honey bees and ants, as well as\h the termites, or Isoptera, social structure\h\h is essential to colonial survival and is\h often regulated through tactile and chemical\h\h communication. Communication here occurs at\h all stages of the life cycle and the society\h\h usually demonstrates polymorphism, or caste\h differentiation, where numerous forms exist, even\h\h within genetically identical individuals. In honey\h bees, for example, there are three castes, the\h\h sexually mature female queen, the sexually mature\h male drones, and the sexually inactive workers\h\h which are inactive genetic females. Contrary\h to popular belief, the queen is not “in charge”\h\h of the hive and workers may remove or even kill\h queens to replace them. The vast majority of bees,\h\h ants, and termites seen by humans are females.\h These castes form partly by fertilization,\h\h as females develop from a fertilized egg, while\h males are haploid and develop parthenogenetically\h\h from an unfertilized egg, but also partly by what\h the larvae are fed as they develop. In bees, for\h\h example, the queens develop when fed sufficient\h royal jelly to alter their epigenome. The colonies\h\h of some eusocial insects can be quite large. For\h example, as many as 70,000 honey bees can occupy a\h\h single colony, and some termite mounds, like the\h 4,000 year old termite mounds in Brazil, can be\h\h seen from space. Some ant colonies can be massive,\h like the Argentine Ant Supercolony which spans\h\h more than 6,000 kilometers, from Italy through\h Spain and into France, or the Ishikari supercolony\h\h of Formica yessensis on Hokkaido Japan that is\h estimated to contain more than 45 thousand nests,\h\h 300 million workers, and over 1 million queens. Finally, different species of insects have very\h\h different relationships with humans. There are\h many species of harmful insects that eat plants\h\h and fruits, such as the grasshoppers, weevils,\h and aphids. Nearly every crop cultivated by\h\h humanity has several insect pests that feed upon\h it. In forestry, outbreaks of bark beetles can\h\h destroy whole areas, and invasive insect species\h travel around the world as unwanted passengers\h\h alongside humanity. In addition, about 10% of all\h arthropod species are parasitic insects like lice,\h\h bloodsucking flies, bot flies, and bedbugs.\h Some species, like some fleas, can carry plague,\h\h while some mosquito species carry dangerous\h diseases like malaria and Zika. Still others live\h\h and compete with humans for food and shelter, like\h some species of cockroach, ants, termites, clothes\h\h moths, and carpet beetles. Humans have, in return,\h developed a wide range of poisons and insecticides\h\h to deal with pest species, though they can\h backfire as insects quickly develop resistance.\h\h The vast majority of bed bugs, for example, are\h resistant to many toxins, and pesticides sprayed\h\h on crops can often harm beneficial insects as\h much as, or more than, the targeted pest species.\h Colony collapse disorder in many species\h of bees has been definitively linked to\h\h specific pesticides. Considering bees alone\h pollinate $14 billion worth of food crops in\h\h the United States for free, their loss would\h have massive ecological and economic impacts.\h\h This doesn’t even consider the numerous species\h of beetles, lepidopterans, flies, and other bee\h\h species that pollinate an even wider range of\h native plants and crop species. Some insects,\h\h like silkworms, produce silk. Others like the lac\h bug produce shellac. And others produce beeswax,\h\h natural dyes, and pigments. Still others,\h like the parasitic wasps, ladybugs, mantids,\h\h and tiger beetles have been used as biological\h controls against pest insects. Many beetles are\h\h important detritivores, and a great deal of\h hymenopterans play essential ecosystem roles.\h And that concludes an introduction to certain\h aspects of class Insecta.

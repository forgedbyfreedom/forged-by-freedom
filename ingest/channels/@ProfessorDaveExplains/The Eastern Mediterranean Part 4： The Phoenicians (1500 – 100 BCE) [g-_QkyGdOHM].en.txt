The people who, in ancient times, occupied\h the bridge of land at the eastern edge of\h\h the Mediterranean, between Egypt to the south and\h Mesopotamia to the north, are known as Canaanites,\h\h after the name given in the Bible to describe\h both the region and the people who lived there.\h\h Though they were not a single ethnic group,\h many of them spoke the same Semitic language,\h\h used the same system of writing, worshipped\h the same gods, and lived in the same kinds of\h\h communities. But one group of Canaanites came\h to be distinguished from the others not by its\h\h own account, but because it was the only one\h that came into regular contact with cultures\h\h across the Mediterranean. This was the people\h whom the Greeks called Phoenicians. This name,\h\h which in Greek was Phoinix, was closely associated\h not only with the Phoenicians themselves,\h\h but with two specific aspects of their culture for\h which they had become famous. First, the purple\h\h dye they produced from a local mollusk and used to\h dye fabrics that were so expensive, they came to\h\h be associated with royalty. And second, the date\h palm that grew in the eastern Mediterranean. It\h\h has both of these meanings in the work of Homer,\h which dates from around 800 BCE. Though it has\h\h often been suggested that it was the Phoenicians\h who were named after the dye, this is disputed.\h\h Phoinix occurs in the Mycenaean Greek of the later\h 2nd millennium BCE, where it refers to the dye\h\h and the palm tree but not to the people. It has\h also been suggested that the name Canaan itself,\h\h which is found in Mesopotamian texts as early\h as 1500 BCE, referred to this purple color.\h Like other Canaanites, the Phoenicians lived in\h independent city-states that were often at odds\h\h with one another. The most important of these\h were Sidon, Byblos, and Tyre, which is found\h\h in Egyptian records as far back as the early\h 2nd millennium BCE. The region was part of the\h\h conquests of the Egyptian king Thutmose III, along\h with the rest of Canaan, in the 15th century. It\h\h was administered by Egypt until the time of\h the Bronze Age collapse of the 12th century,\h\h and then it was conquered by the Assyrian king\h Tiglath-Pileser I in the early 11th. But unlike\h\h the larger powers in the region, Phoenicia did\h not seem to have suffered economically from the\h\h changes of this era. In fact, it took advantage\h of the reduced power of Egypt and Assyria,\h\h and the disappearance of the Hittite Empire, to\h expand its trading contacts. It was then that\h\h it went from a collection of local city-states\h to a maritime power that had trade contacts and\h\h colonies across the Mediterranean as far as Spain,\h from which it got silver, and Britain from which\h\h it got the tin used to make bronze. The leading\h power in this expansion was the city of Tyre. Two\h\h of its kings in the 10th century, Abibaal and his\h son Hiram, are believed to have strengthened their\h\h power over the city, and increased the wealth of\h its citizens, by taking away some of the power of\h\h the city’s priesthood. As the westernmost point\h\h of the land routes that connected Egypt,\h Mesopotamia, and the lands to the east,\h\h they were able to bring the goods produced\h in those places to points in Greece, Sicily,\h\h north Africa, and Spain; and they produced many\h of the same luxury goods made by other Canaanites,\h\h such as ivory carvings, metalwork, glass, and\h dyed textiles. They also built their own ships,\h\h from the cedars that grew on Mount Lebanon;\h and they made technological improvements,\h\h such as the keel, and built artificial harbors\h that made ports more accessible to their ships\h\h by taming the local currents. They became known\h throughout the Mediterranean for their skill as\h\h sailors, which is mentioned in Homer’s Odyssey. As their trade expanded, the Phoenicians,\h\h and above all the city of Tyre, established\h colonies in western Sicily, Sardinia,\h\h western Spain, southern France, Cyprus, Malta,\h and north Africa. In 715\h he assisted Tyre against Greek pirates, and used\h\h its ships to transport his troops since he did\h not have a navy of his own. Phoenician cities, like other Canaanite cities,\h\h were traditionally governed by kings, though\h with the consultation of a group of 30 wealthy\h\h merchants called adirim, meaning “mighty”.

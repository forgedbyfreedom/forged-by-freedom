We’ve all seen a million different\h personality tests online.\h\h Some appear to take a relatively scientific\h route, while others are blatantly frivolous,\h\h telling you which Harry Potter character you are,\h or even worse, what kind of sandwich. But what is\h\h personality? Psychologists define personality as\h the specific thoughts, feelings, and behaviors\h\h a person exhibits. Are you generally optimistic\h or pessimistic? How do you interact with your\h\h environment? In general, personality is stable\h across your lifetime, and people usually react\h\h to situations in ways that are consistent\h with their underlying personality traits.\h People have been thinking about what\h causes personality for a long time.\h\h Way back in the 1st and 2nd centuries CE, the\h Roman philosopher Galen believed that personality\h\h was the result of different levels of ‘humors’, as\h we touched upon in the History of Drugs series. In\h\h Galen’s view, the four humors found in a person’s\h body, blood, phlegm, black bile, and yellow bile,\h\h controlled one’s personality. Different levels\h of these humors were responsible for different\h\h personality traits. For example, cheerful\h people were believed to have an excess of blood,\h\h while shy people had a lot of phlegm in their\h bodies. Although psychologists today certainly no\h\h longer believe that we have humors which control\h our personalities, they do think, like Galen did,\h\h that personality has biological roots. Let’s explore this idea a little more.\h\h How is our personality related to our biology?\h Well, your adult personality emerges in part\h\h from the temperament you exhibit as an infant.\h Temperament is the biological and instinctive\h\h part of personality that is present at birth.\h There are four major dimensions of temperament.\h\h First is activity level. Second is\h impulsivity, or time taken to express emotion.\h\h Third is positive emotionality, meaning smiling,\h laughing, and sociability. And lastly there is\h\h negative emotionality, meaning\h irritability and fearfulness.\h\h The temperament shown in infants turns out\h to predict adult behavior and personality.\h\h More reactive infants tend to be more anxious in\h adulthood. This link strengthens the argument that\h\h personality is in part biological. In addition,\h research suggests that these temperament traits\h\h arise from biological underpinnings like genetics.\h For example, some personality traits seem to be\h\h heritable, and identical twins have very similar\h personalities, even when raised apart. Of course,\h\h while the biological temperament of infants plays\h an important role in determining personality,\h\h it is also impacted by the environment.\h How a parent interacts with their child\h\h can shape how genes are expressed and the\h eventual personality that is displayed.\h Now that we know more about how personality is in\h part determined by biology, let’s dig deeper into\h\h the different parts of personality. Psychologists\h today generally agree that there are five core\h\h dimensions of personality, also known as The Big\h Five. We can remember these five dimensions using\h\h the acronym OCEAN: openness, conscientiousness,\h extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism.\h\h Each of these dimensions represents a continuum\h between two extremes. For example, extraversion\h\h is the range between extreme extraversion and\h extreme introversion. Most people fall somewhere\h\h in between the two ends of each dimension. Let’s\h go through each dimension in a little more detail.\h Openness is the spectrum dealing with things\h like imagination, insight, and willingness\h\h to try new experiences. People who are high in\h openness tend to have a broad range of interests,\h\h are very curious about the world, and like to try\h new things. They tend to be very creative as well,\h\h and like tackling new challenges. On the flip\h side, people low in openness dislike change and\h\h are resistant to new ideas. They also tend to be\h more traditional in their thinking and do not like\h\h dealing with abstract or theoretical concepts. Conscientiousness deals with impulse control\h\h and goal-directed behaviors. Highly conscientious\h people tend to be highly organized. They spend\h\h time preparing for tasks and finish important\h tasks right away. They also tend to enjoy having\h\h a set schedule and are mindful of how their\h behavior affects others. In contrast, people\h\h low in conscientiousness dislike structure and\h schedules. They tend to make messes and not put\h\h things away. They also procrastinate on important\h tasks and often fail to complete required work.\h Extraversion is the trait dealing with\h excitability, sociability, and emotional\h\h expressiveness. This is the trait that you’re most\h likely to have heard other people talking about:\h\h are you an introvert or an extravert? People\h who are high in extraversion like to be the\h\h center of attention and are energized by being\h around people. They enjoy meeting new people\h\h and have a wide social circle. People low in\h extraversion, meaning they are introverted,\h\h are more reserved and tend to feel\h exhausted after lots of socializing.\h\h They also find it difficult to start conversations\h and don’t like being the center of attention.\h Agreeableness is the personality\h continuum dealing with prosocial behaviors\h\h like trust, kindness, and affection. People\h high in agreeableness enjoy helping others\h\h and feel a lot of empathy and concern for\h other people. They tend to be very cooperative,\h\h and often assist others who are in need of\h help. In contrast, people low in agreeableness\h\h tend to be more competitive and can even be\h manipulative when trying to get what they want.\h\h They take little interest in others\h and don’t care about how people feel.\h Finally, neuroticism deals with\h moodiness and emotional stability.\h\h People high in neuroticism tend to experience\h mood swings, anxiety, irritability,\h\h and sadness. They get upset easily and often\h struggle to bounce back after stressful events.\h\h On the other hand, people low in neuroticism deal\h very well with stress and tend not to worry much.\h\h They are more emotionally stable\h and rarely feel sad or depressed.\h Together, these Big 5 personality traits shape our\h overall personalities, regardless of where we’re\h\h from. One study found that these five personality\h traits could be used to describe personality in\h\h more than 50 different cultures worldwide.\h They are also heritable. Between 42% and 57%\h\h of a person’s score on these traits is\h decided by genes. This makes sense if you\h\h think about twins. Even though identical\h twins are extremely similar genetically,\h\h they still have different personalities. Their\h genes are explaining part of their personality,\h\h but things like the environment and interactions\h with others are also shaping their personality.\h The Big 5 personality traits are also pretty\h stable in adulthood. As you age, your personality\h\h solidifies, especially after you reach 30 years\h old. Even when people go through adverse life\h\h events, their overall personality shows little\h change. In part because they are so stable,\h\h the Big 5 are also able to accurately\h predict people’s behaviors. For example,\h\h conscientiousness predicts marital fidelity,\h and openness to experience predicts job changes.\h\h People high in extraversion are more likely to\h look people in the eye during conversations.\h\h Don’t forget, though, that behavior arising\h from personality is situational. People can\h\h behave in ways contrary to their personality if\h they are put in an extreme situation. However,\h\h in most cases people react to situations in ways\h that are consistent with their personality traits.\h Before we move on from personality, we should take\h a moment to talk about a personality test you’ve\h\h probably heard about and may have even taken: the\h Myers-Briggs. The Myers-Briggs test is the one\h\h that classifies people based on four different\h categories: extraversion vs. introversion,\h\h sensing vs intuition, thinking vs feeling,\h and judging vs perceiving. The Myers-Briggs\h\h is popular, and people all over have proudly\h classified themselves as ‘ENTPs’ or ‘INTJs’.\h\h Unfortunately, the Myers-Briggs is\h incredibly flawed for multiple reasons.\h First up, Myers-Briggs relies on binaries,\h giving an oversimplified view of human\h\h personality. And even\h though lots of jobs use it as a interview tool\h\h or as a way to improve workplace dynamics,\h Myers-Briggs does not predict job success.\h So where does that leave us?

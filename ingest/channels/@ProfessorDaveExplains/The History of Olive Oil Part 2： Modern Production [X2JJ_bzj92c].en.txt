Let’s go through these one at a time.\h Raccolta. This is the most delicate and\h important phase for a good olive oil,\h\h and in Italy this is all done by hand. Olives\h are fragile and they bruise easily. Therefore\h\h they cannot touch the ground when they are\h harvested. The trees are shaken gently,\h\h and umbrella-like nets are placed underneath, in\h order to avoid stressing the olives. Any lesion in\h\h the olives would increase the acidity in the oil\h and this would translate into an inferior quality.\h\h At this point the olives are placed in perforated\h crates in order to allow the olives to breath.\h\h This is apparently very important. Burlaps should\h never be used as they would warm up the olives\h\h and initiate a process of fermentation. The best\h time for this phase is mid-October to December.\h Lavaggio. The olives are brought to the\h Frantoio, or olive press, the same day that\h\h they are harvested. Here the olives are carried by\h a conveyor belt to a tank where they are separated\h\h from leaves, twigs, and other debris by means\h of a powerful vacuum. Only now do the olives get\h\h washed and rinsed again in clean water. At\h this point the olives still have their pits.\h Frangitura. After being washed, the\h olives are crushed into a coarse paste\h\h made of solids, pulp, skin, pits, and some\h oil. The oldest method is by stone mills,\h\h but today more modern machines may be used.\h Gramolatura. The next phase is a process of slow\h and continuous kneading, called gramolazione.\h\h This is a very important step which allows the\h separation of oil and water from solids. Small oil\h\h droplets will combine with others in order to form\h larger ones. Normally a mixer is horizontal with\h\h spiraled mixing blades. For the best quality of\h oil, this process should be done with temperatures\h\h below 27oC, or around 80 Fahrenheit. If heat\h is used the oil will be an inferior product.\h Estrazione. This is the step yielding\h the product we all know and love.\h\h It used to be made with presses, alternating\h layers of olive paste with filtering discs,\h\h hence the expression cold press or first\h press. Today it’s often made by centrifugation.\h Separazione. At the end of this extraction, the\h liquids which are still containing some water,\h\h will be easily separated from the\h oil since oil is lighter than water.\h\h The best oil is done naturally. Enzymes, steam,\h hexane, and other solvents could be used as\h\h well as bleaching the oil to reduce chlorophyll,\h carotenoids, residual fatty acids, and pesticides.\h\h It goes without saying that all these\h steps are only used for lower quality oil.\h Imbottigliamento. At the end of the entire\h process the olive oil is finally bottled.\h\h The best material for this is glass, usually\h dark glass, or non-reactive metal cans,\h\h in order to protect the oil from light and avoid\h oxidation. These are immediately hermetically\h\h sealed in order to avoid any alteration of the\h precious product.

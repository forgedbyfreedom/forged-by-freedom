As we recall, an argument is composed of premises\h\h which result in a conclusion. The simple example\h we looked at earlier is a very straightforward\h\h argument, and if we change the nouns into letters\h we can begin to see how this simple argument can\h\h be equivalent to a formula, with every word\h having a function. “All” denotes every member\h\h of a category, in this case, YouTubers. “Are”\h and “is” indicate a connection between the first\h\h category and the second – however not necessarily\h the opposite, as for example not all mortals are\h\h YouTubers. Changing\h these words while maintaining the same format\h\h would still be valid, as with the classic “All\h men are mortal/Socrates is a man/Socrates is\h\h mortal”. We could even alter these operating\h words: “All” to “some”, “is” to “is not”,\h\h and so forth, and we would get different kinds\h of arguments. This basic format is what Aristotle\h\h would call a syllogism, but some of its early\h elements can be identified millennia before him.\h The earliest source we have on formulating some\h kind of logical operators are the Babylonians,\h\h specifically with the Sumerians and Akkadians\h in Mesopotamia. Instead of introducing them in\h\h an analysis of discourse, such as philosophy\h or science, for example, they were developed\h\h as part of their codes of law and jurisprudence,\h especially the Ur-Nammu (circa 2047–2030 BCE),\h\h the Lipit-Ishtar (circa 1900–1850 BCE), and\h the Hammurabi (1728–1686 BCE). They would be\h\h publicly written in slabs or walls, and they\h contained basic operators such as “or”, “and”,\h\h “if… then” – much like premises. When the\h judges needed to evaluate given facts, then\h\h an inference would occur toward a verdict – or a\h conclusion. The basic shape would be “If crime,\h\h then punishment (which would be the code of\h law)/A crime occurred (the fact requiring\h\h judgement)/Then, punishment (the verdict or\h conclusion)”. These codes directly influenced\h\h the Greek law in which Aristotle lived centuries\h later, potentially influencing his ideas.\h On the other hand, although Confucius\h preceded Aristotle by a couple centuries,\h\h not much of Chinese philosophy seems to have\h reached Greece by his time. As we already saw,\h\h Confucius and his predecessors centered their\h philosophical perspectives around language,\h\h or more specifically, on “names”, or ming,\h and their relationships with all the things\h\h they designate. In his Analects, when asked what\h his first priority would be when administrating\h\h the state, he said that it would be to focus on\h the proper use of names, or “ordering of names”\h\h (cheng ming), as their misuse would generate an\h incongruity between actions, rituals, songs, law,\h\h and the people, and thus result in chaos and\h immorality. However, Confucius never delved\h\h further on proper linguistic analysis;\h this endeavor was only undertaken by the\h\h Mohists some two centuries later. Although\h their surviving text fragments are limited,\h\h much has been extrapolated to compare their\h development with Aristotle, and even more modern\h\h notions and concepts. One example is one of their\h techniques of disputation called “Parallelizing”,\h\h which generated arguments resembling\h syllogisms, such as: “Dave is a YouTuber;\h\h befriending Dave is befriending a YouTuber.” To\h assert comparatively if this statement is correct,\h\h more statements would be fashioned in the same\h shape, such as “YouTube is a website; to visit\h\h YouTube is to visit a website” and so on. Although\h simple, their logical format and its importance in\h\h evaluating an argument is evident, even though\h their focus was on the semantic aspect, or its\h\h contents, more so than on the format itself. One example would\h\h be this: “Proposition: X has Y/Reason: because\h P has Z./Example: That which has Z has Y,\h\h like D./(where D, distinct from P, has properties\h Z and Y)”. This is a clear deductive syllogism,\h\h meaning that it is characterized by or based\h on the inference of particular instances from\h\h a general law, with an explicit focus on\h its format and method of applicability.\h\h This is in contrast with an accidental use of\h the syllogistic shape like many of our previous\h\h examples. He also developed advanced notions\h regarding surrounding concepts, and even a\h\h truth-table.

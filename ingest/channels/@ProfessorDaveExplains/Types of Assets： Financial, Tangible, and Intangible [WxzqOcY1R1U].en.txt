So what do we mean by “grow” in this context?\h\h Money certainly doesn’t grow on trees, but\h it can grow through investment in assets.\h\h An investment is the act of redirecting resources\h from being consumed today so that they may create\h\h benefits in the future. In other words,\h it involves using assets to make money,\h\h and one of the best ways to earn wealth is to\h accumulate as many valuable assets as you can.\h In this tutorial, we are going\h to look at three types of assets:\h\h financial assets, tangible assets, and intangible\h assets. A financial asset is a claim on the\h\h property or income of a borrower. Common examples\h of financial assets include just plain old cash,\h\h or cash equivalents, like a savings account,\h stocks, bonds, mutual funds, or retirement\h\h accounts. A tangible asset, also known as a real\h asset, is one that can be physically touched.\h\h So actually, cash also qualifies as a tangible\h asset, but additionally we are typically referring\h\h to things like land, houses, cars, artwork, or\h collectibles. For a business, tangible assets\h\h might include things like machinery or office\h supplies. Financial assets get their value from\h\h contractual claims on underlying assets, and can\h therefore sometimes be considered tangible assets.\h\h Finally, an intangible asset is any\h asset that is not physical in nature.\h\h Examples of intangible assets include patents,\h trademarks, and copyrights, which all protect\h\h ownership of ideas and inventions. The more liquid\h an asset, the easier it is to turn into cash. Both\h\h individuals and businesses have to deal\h with liquid and non-liquid markets. A\h\h liquid market is established with lots of\h potential buyers. For example, stocks are\h\h generally more liquid than real estate. This\h is because it’s easier to sell them.

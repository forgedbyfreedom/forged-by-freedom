Given that\h\h this series focuses largely on the diversity\h of plants, we need to revisit photosynthesis\h\h for a moment. But this time we are not going to\h focus quite as much on the general mechanism.\h\h Instead, we must outline the major differences\h between the three different metabolic pathways\h\h that lead to carbon fixation in angiosperms. If\h this term is unfamiliar, carbon fixation refers to\h\h the process by which carbon dioxide is converted\h into more biologically useful organic compounds\h\h within living organisms. Again, we already\h have a general understanding of this process,\h\h but there are some key differences between certain\h groups of plants that must now be elucidated.\h In most plants, initial fixation of carbon occurs\h when the enzyme rubisco adds CO2 to RuBP. These\h\h plants are known as C3 plants because the first\h organic compound produced is the three-carbon\h\h compound 3-PGA. C3 carbon fixation is the most\h common of the three pathways we will cover, so\h\h because it is the most frequently studied method\h of photosynthesis, it is the method explained in\h\h detail in the photosynthesis tutorial. The plants\h that utilize this process include soybeans, oats,\h\h wheat, and rice. One aspect of this method of\h photosynthesis which makes it difficult for plants\h\h in dry environments is that when the stomata close\h to reduce water loss, O2 builds up and rubisco\h\h will add O2 instead of CO2 to RuBP. A two-carbon\h product of this reaction is then broken down to\h\h CO2 and H2O in a process called photorespiration.\h Photorespiration yields no sugar and produces no\h\h ATP. Instead it can actually burn through\h carbon previously fixed by the Calvin Cycle.\h So C3 photosynthesis, while the most widespread of\h all forms of photosynthesis, is actually not very\h\h efficient in arid environments. Plants that have\h adapted to life in hot, dry places have actually\h\h evolved alternate methods of carbon fixation that\h save water without shutting down photosynthesis.\h One such method, developed by C4 plants like\h corn and sugarcane, involves preceding the\h\h Calvin cycle by first fixing CO2 into\h a four-carbon compound. Like C3 plants,\h\h C4 plants will close their stomata to conserve\h water loss. However, unlike C3 plants,\h\h C4 plants will continue to make sugars\h using photosynthesis instead of entering\h\h photorespiration. They do this by initially\h fixing carbon dioxide in the mesophyll cells\h\h via the enzyme PEP carboxylase, which reacts\h the three carbon phosphoenolpyruvate, or PEP,\h\h with CO2 to form the four carbon oxaloacetic\h acid, or OAA. The resulting four carbon compound\h\h within the mesophyll then cycles into the\h bundle-sheath cells and releases CO2, meaning\h\h the levels of carbon there will always remain\h high enough for the Calvin cycle to make sugars.\h It’s important to note that the\h utilization of C4 carbon fixation\h\h does not indicate common ancestry. About 3% of\h land plants use C4 carbon fixation, most of which\h\h are monocots. However, C4 carbon fixation\h has evolved independently at least 20 times\h\h in the grass family alone, and is thus an\h excellent example of convergent evolution.\h Another alternate method of carbon fixation\h developed by plants like pineapples and aloe\h\h in arid environments is called crassulacean\h acid metabolism, or CAM photosynthesis. Plants\h\h that use CAM photosynthesis are especially\h adapted to extremely arid environments.\h\h Unlike most C3 and C4 plants, CAM plants\h conserve water by opening their stomata\h\h and admitting CO2 only at night. When the CO2\h enters the leaves, it is fixed into a four-carbon\h\h compound through a PEP reaction similar to\h the C4 plants. However, unlike C4 plants,\h\h in CAM plants, the resulting organic acids are\h stored in vacuoles for later use. Then during the\h\h day, the stomata close to conserve water and\h the carbon dioxide-storing organic acids are\h\h released from the vacuoles of the mesophyll cells\h into the Calvin cycle. This keeps photosynthesis\h\h going in CAM plants during the day even though the\h leaf’s stomata are closed. Like C4 carbon fixation\h\h CAM photosynthesis evolved convergently several\h times in different plant clades, though it is\h\h more common in the epiphytes, such as orchids\h and bromeliads, and succulents, like cacti.\h To summarize, C3 photosynthesis is the most\h common form of photosynthesis in plants,\h\h but it is not well-adapted to hot, dry\h environments. In C4 photosynthesis,\h\h carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle\h occur in different types of cells,\h\h while in CAM photosynthesis, these processes\h occur in the same cells but at different times.\h\h These two pathways are two different evolutionary\h solutions aimed at balancing the competing\h\h priorities of maintaining photosynthesis and\h conserving water loss during hot, dry days.\h\h And with that we understand a bit about the\h three known forms of photosynthesis in plants.

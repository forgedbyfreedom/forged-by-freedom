Wealth inequality simply refers to\h the uneven distribution of wealth in a society. If\h\h wealth inequality is extreme, most of the wealth\h is in the hands of a very small group of people.\h\h Typically this may be less than 1% of\h the population. However, before we look at why rising\h\h wealth inequality is a negative externality, it’s\h important to recognize that wealth inequality\h\h is prevalent, and in fact it is necessary and\h expected, in any market economy. A competitive\h\h economy inevitably leads to wealth inequality.\h However, extreme wealth inequality can be severely\h\h damaging for a society because the overwhelming\h majority of wealth is concentrated at the top,\h\h and in the hands of relatively few people,\h leading to a severe imbalance of power.\h Extreme wealth inequality can lead to higher\h rates of crime, obesity, mental illness,\h\h and drug use. In\h other words, in an extremely unequal society,\h\h one who is born with little wealth is much\h more likely to die with little wealth.\h\h And while it’s probably easy to recognize how\h extreme wealth inequality hurts those in poverty,\h\h it also hurts those who have lots of wealth. Producers\h\h need a large number of consumers to continue\h to earn a profit. In a society where most of\h\h the wealth is concentrated at the top, fewer\h consumers are able to buy goods and services,\h\h which therefore leads to less economic growth. The Gini index measures\h inequality across the whole of society rather\h\h than simply comparing different income groups.\h A high Gini index indicates greater inequality,\h\h with more wealth in the hands of the few.

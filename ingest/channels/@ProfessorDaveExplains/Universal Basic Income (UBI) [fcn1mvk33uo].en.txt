But what is it,\h\h and how might it affect an economy? Universal basic income, or UBI,\h\h is a policy in which all adult citizens of\h a society regularly receive a set amount of\h\h money. It is unconditional, meaning there are\h no eligibility requirements for receiving the\h\h money. It is paid out by a government and can be\h implemented nationally or locally. The goal of UBI\h\h is to provide enough money to meet a person’s\h basic needs, like food, access to clean water,\h\h and shelter. In other words, its goal\h is to eliminate poverty. Additionally,\h\h many economists argue that UBI can replace other\h need-based social programs that potentially\h\h require greater bureaucratic control. While the idea of a universal basic\h\h income has existed in some form or\h another since at least ancient Rome,\h\h it was first argued for by the English philosopher\h Sir Thomas More. In his book “Utopia”, he wrote\h\h “instead of inflicting…punishments, it would be\h far more to the point to provide everyone with\h\h some means of livelihood, so that nobody’s under\h the frightful necessity of becoming first a thief,\h\h and then a corpse." Later, the Spanish philosopher\h Johannes Ludovicus Vives and American philosopher\h\h Thomas Paine argued governments should provide\h this basic income. In the 1800s, the Belgian\h\h author Joseph Charlier argued that this basic\h income should be unconditional. By the 1900s,\h\h many politicians around the world were calling\h to begin experimenting with a form of UBI. But\h\h ever since, few experiments have actually been\h implemented, typically in a small society or in\h\h small amounts. For example, in the 1970s, a group\h of Canadian economists conducted an experiment in\h\h which it gave money to citizens in the town\h of Dauphin, Manitoba. However, this was more\h\h accurately an example of a negative income tax, or\h a system which reverses the direction in which an\h\h income tax is paid for incomes below a certain\h level. If you make less than a certain amount,\h\h you not only don’t have to pay taxes, but you\h receive money from the government. So in Dauphin,\h\h many did not qualify to get money from the\h government since they made too much money.\h\h More recently, since 2016 the nonprofit charity\h organization GiveDirectly has been sending direct\h\h cash payments to more than 14,000 households\h in the Siaya and Bomet Counties of Kenya.\h Overall, however, economists don’t have much data\h to work with regarding the effectiveness of UBI.\h\h Therefore, much of the analysis regarding its\h effectiveness is theoretical. With this in mind,\h\h let’s look at the potential pros and\h cons of a universal basic income.\h The first positive is the most obvious. A UBI\h would reduce poverty. It’s no surprise that\h\h when people have more money, they can more easily\h take care of their basic needs. Related to this,\h\h many argue a UBI would improve physical and\h mental health. By having basic needs met,\h\h people generally would have less anxiety-related\h ailments. Third, many economists argue that\h\h a UBI would lead to positive job growth while\h simultaneously lowering school drop out rates.\h\h With more money, people would be more willing to\h make riskier decisions like starting a business\h\h or going for an additional degree that would\h get them a better job down the road. Fourth,\h\h a UBI guarantees income for people who work but\h are not included in the labor force. Finally,\h\h some economists also argue that a UBI could\h help increase equality while fighting extreme\h\h wealth inequality, as more people would have\h opportunities to invest their money in assets.\h However, other economists argue that a UBI\h could make extreme wealth inequality worse,\h\h as those with wealth would benefit from more\h consumers spending their money. A second\h\h criticism of UBI is that it may actually end up\h depriving the poor of targeted support. Because\h\h some experience worse poverty than others,\h current social welfare programs that target\h\h those in need might be gone if UBI were to be\h implemented. Third, a form of national UBI would\h\h undoubtedly be expensive. As incomes increase, so do\h prices, so governments might have a difficult\h\h time keeping up with a high enough UBI. In conclusion, it’s still too early to know\h\h for sure if UBI would be the most effective\h macroeconomic policy for governments to fight\h\h poverty. However, as automation becomes a greater\h threat to traditional jobs, a growing number of\h\h economists seem to think UBI could be the solution\h for a future society where human capital is in\h\h lesser demand. If current trends continue,\h someday we may live in a society that doesn’t\h\h have to worry as much about scarcity. In such a\h case, we may find that UBI could become a reality.

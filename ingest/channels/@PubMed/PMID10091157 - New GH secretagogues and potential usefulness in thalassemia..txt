PMID: 10091157
Title: New GH secretagogues and potential usefulness in thalassemia.
Authors: Karydis, Tolis, Tolis
Journal: Journal of pediatric endocrinology &amp; metabolism : JPEM
Year: 1999
Topic: GHRP GHRP

ABSTRACT:
Thalassemic patients today undergo intensive trensfusion and chelation regimes that offer them prolonged survival and improved quality of life. Nevertheless, they face the consequences of chronic illness and therapies which affect multiple bodily functions. Endocrine derangements involve, among others, the GH-IGF-I axis with consequent impairment of growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone. In such cases, GH release, as assessed with stimulation tests, may be normal whereas ultradian GH secretion seems to be subnormal. New GH secretagogues (GHRs) are agents that stimulate pituitary GH release by acting upon different receptors than the endogenous hypothalamic secretagogue, growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone-releasinGHormone (GHRH). Examples are the growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone releasing peptides (GHRPs) GHRP, GHRP-1, GHRP, Hexarelin and the nonpeptidyl MK-0677. These can be administered by multiple routes, even per os or intrenasally, thus obviating the need for injections. Their GH releasing capacity is more pronounced and prolonged than that of GHRH and their use is devoid of serious side effects. The most recently developed GHRs seem to be capable of producing sustained GH release in many cases and can thus be viewed as therapeutic candidates in cases of reduced GH secretion with intact pituitary, as seems to be the case in a group of thalassemic patients.

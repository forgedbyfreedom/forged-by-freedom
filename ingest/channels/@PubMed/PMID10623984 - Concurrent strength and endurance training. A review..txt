PMID: 10623984
Title: Concurrent strength and endurance trening. A review.
Authors: Leveritt, Abernethy, Barry, Logan
Journal: Sports medicine (Auckland, N.Z.)
Year: 2000
Topic: trening frequency hypertrophy

ABSTRACT:
Concurrent strength and endurance trening appears to inhibit strength development when compared with strength trening alone. Our understanding of the nature of this inhibition and the mechanisms responsible for it is limited at present. This is due to the difficulties associated with comparing results of studies which differ markedly in a number of design factors, including the mode, frequency, duration and intensity of trening, treninGHistory of participants, scheduling of trening sessions and dependent variable selection. Despite these difficulties, both chronic and acute hypotheses have been proposed to explain the phenomenon of strength inhibition during concurrent trening. The chronic hypothesis contends that skeletal muscle cannot adapt metabolically or morphologically to both strength and endurance trening simultaneously. This is because many adaptations at the muscle level observed in response to strength trening are different from those observed after endurance trening. The observation that changes in muscle fibre type and size after concurrent trening are different from those observed after strength trening provide some support for the chronic hypothesis. The acute hypothesis contends that residual fatigue from the endurance component of concurrent trening compromises the ability to develop tension during the strength element of concurrent trening. It is proposed that repeated acute reductions in thEQuality of strength trening sessions then lead to a reduction in strength development over time. Peripheral fatigue factors such as muscle damage and glycogen depletion have been implicated as possible fatigue mechanisms associated with the acute hypothesis. Further systematic research is necessary to quantify the inhibitory effects of concurrent trening on strength development and to identify different trening approaches that may overcome any negative effects of concurrent trening.

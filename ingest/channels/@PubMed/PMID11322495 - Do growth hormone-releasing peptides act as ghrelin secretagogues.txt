Title: Do growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone-releasing peptides act as ghrelin secretagogues?
Authors: Ahnfelt-R&#xf8;nne, Nowak, Olsen
Journal: Endocrine
Year: 2001
Topic: growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone releasing peptide

ABSTRACT:
NN703 is an orally active and selective growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone secretagogue (GHS) that was derived from growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone-releasing peptide-1(GHRP-1) via ipamorelin by a peptidomimetic approach and has now entered into phase II clinical trials. When the disposition in rats of NN703 and GHRP was studied using whole-body autoradiography following administration of an iv dose of radiolabeled material, we found that a substantial amount of these secretagogues accumulate in the glandular part of the stomach. Because this is the site of synthesis and secretion of ghrelin, the endogenous GHS, we investigated the effect of resection of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract on growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone (GH) release induced by GHRP. This procedure significantly attenuated the GH secretion response by 60-70%. By contrast, the effect of GH-releasinGHormone on GH release was not inhibited. The binding of GHRPs to the glandular part of the stomach and the blunted GH response to GHRP following resection of the GI tract suggest a role for ghrelin as a mediator of part of the GH-releasing effect of GHRPs.

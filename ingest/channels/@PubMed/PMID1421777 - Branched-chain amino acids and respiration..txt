PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 1421777
Title: Branched-chain amino acids and respiration.
Authors: Manner, Wiese, Katz, Skeie, Askanazi
Journal: Nutrition (Burbank, Los Angeles County, Calif.)
Year: 1992
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
A series of investigations suggest a specific role for BCAAsin the regulation of respiration. In vitro incubation studies have shown that BCAAs improve the recovery of muscle force after fatigue. Further investigations revealed that leucine plays a key role in this action and acts in a manner not dependent on its use as an energy substrate. In humans, solutions enriched with BCAAshave decreased PCO2 and stimulated the ventilatory response to hypercapnia, thereby corresponding to an enhanced ventilatory sensitivity with the administration of BCAAs. The mechanisms for these actions are unknown. The most viable hypothesis is based on the ability of BCAAsto decrease the synthesis of serotonin due to altered transport of AAs, including tryptophan, to the brain. Clinical studies have suggested a potency of BCAAsin the treatment of respiratory dysfunction of preterm infants, as well as of patients with sleep apnea related to various disease states. The clinical applications of BCAAs-enriched mixtures in respiratory diseases are still experimental, and many controversies exist concerning the validity of BCAAsin clinical practice. Most TPN regimens contain BCAAsapproximating the average intake of BCAAsin the Western diet. The question therefore remains whether additional BCAAssupplementation is useful to achieve the suggested metabolic and pharmacological effects. Meticulous future studies are needed to establish the therapeutic value of BCAAsin the treatment of various respiratory functions.

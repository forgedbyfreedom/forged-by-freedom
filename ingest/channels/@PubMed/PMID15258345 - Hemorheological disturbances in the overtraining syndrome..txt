PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 15258345
Title: Hemorheological disturbances in the overtrening syndrome.
Authors: Varlet-Marie, Maso, Lac, Brun
Journal: Clinical hemorheology and microcirculation
Year: 2004
Topic: overtrening syndrome athletes

ABSTRACT:
Contemporary sports imply huge trening volumes, with thus an increasing danger of overloading. The timely detection of the state of overloading in the organism as a whole or in skeletal muscles presents a difficult and complicated problem. A standardized questionnaire has been proposed by the French consensus group on overtrening of the Soci&#xe9;t&#xe9; Fran&#xe7;aise de M&#xe9;decine du Sport (SFMS) and allows the calculation of a "score" that may help to quantify the early clinical symptoms of the overtrening syndrome in sportsmen submitted to a heavy trening program. We previously reported that this overtrening score is correlated with blood viscosity due to a correlation of this score with plasma viscosity and hematocrit. When subjects with a high score werecompared to subjects with a lower score they appeared to have a higher blood viscosity at native (but not corrected) hematocrit, explained by higher values in both plasma viscosity and hematocrit. By contrast, there was no difference in RBC deformability and aggregation. Therefore, the early signs of overtrening in elite sportsmen are associated with a hemorheologic pattern that suggests some degree of reversal of the "autohemodilution" which characterizes fitness. In a further study we reported that the feeling of heavy legs in overtrened athletes is related to impaired hemorheology. Although well matched with controls for age and body composition, subjects with a complaint of heavy legs had higher plasma viscosity and a higher red cell aggregation as measured with laser backscattering. These findings suggest that the feeling of heavy legs in overtrened athletes are related to hemorheologic disturbances. In the light of the recent concept explaining this syndrome by a mild chronic inflammatory reaction, the investigation of hemorheology in overtrening can be a promising area for hemorheologists, providing both markers and likely pathophysiological explanations for some symptoms of this situation.

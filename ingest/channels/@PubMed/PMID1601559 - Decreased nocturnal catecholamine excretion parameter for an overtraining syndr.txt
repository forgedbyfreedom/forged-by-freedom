PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 1601559
Title: Decreased nocturnal catecholamine excretion: parameter for an overtrening syndrome in athletes?
Authors: Lehmann, Schnee, Scheu, Stockhausen, Bachl
Journal: International journal of sports medicine
Year: 1992
Topic: overtrening syndrome athletes

ABSTRACT:
The effectiveness of high performance trening should be examined at short intervals in order to recognize overtrening promptly. Field or laboratory tests can usually not be performed with such frequency. Easy-to-measure biological, trening-relevant parameters are being sought to use in their place. Since the importance of the sympathetic nervous system for adaptation of stress and the relationship between physical trening and the activity of the sympathetic nervous system are well accepted, and since an impairment of the sympathetic nervous system is assumed in an overtrening syndrome, we examined the relevance of nocturnal "basal" urinary excretion of free catecholamines with respect to its practical application: 1. during a pilot study (trening of road and track cyclists before the 1988 Olympic Games in Seoul), 2. through a 4-week prospective, experimental study in 1989 and 1990 (middle- and long-distance runners), 3. during the competitive season and winter break of a soccer team between August 1990 and April 1991. The followinGHypothesis was made: An overtrening or exhaustion syndrome in athletes may usually be accompanied by at least A-50% decrease in basal dopamine, noradrenaline and adrenaline excretion. When trening is effective or the athletes are not exhausted, the decrease of the excretion rate--with the exception of dopamine--is more likely to be lower (noradrenaline, adrenaline). Generalization of these results requires further expansion of the experimental basis.

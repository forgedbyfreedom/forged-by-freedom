PMID: 16608764
Title: Effects of dynamic resistance trening on fascicle length and isometric strength.
Authors: Alegre, Jim&#xe9;nez, Gonzalo-Orden, Mart&#xed;n-Acero, Aguado
Journal: Journal of sports sciences
Year: 2006
Topic: tendon adaptation resistance trening

ABSTRACT:
The aims of this study were to assess changes in muscle architecture, isometric and dynamic strength of the leg extensor muscles, resulting from dynamic resistance trening, and the relationships between strength and muscle architecture variables. The participants (n = 30) were randomly assigned to one of two groups. The trening group (n = 16; age 21.8 +/- 2.3 years, body mass 74.8 +/- 9.2 kg, height 1.75 +/- 0.08 m) performed dynamic resistance trening for 13 weeks. The control group (n = 14; age 19.9 +/- 1.5 years, body mass 74.0 +/- 8.5 kg, height 1.76 +/- 0.05 m) did not perform any resistance trening. Maximal dynamic and isometric strength were tested in both groups, before and after the trening period. The members of the trening group used the free-weight squat lift (90 degrees ) as their trening exercise. The concentric phase of the squat was performed explosively. Skeletal muscle architecture of the vastus lateralis was visualized using ultrasonography. At the end of the study, significant increases in vastus lateralis muscle thickness (+6.9%, P &lt; 0.001), fascicle length (+10.3%, P &lt; 0.05), one-repetition maximum (+8.2%, P &lt; 0.05), rate of force development (+23.8%, P &lt; 0.05) and average force produced in the first 500 ms (+11.7%, P &lt; 0.05) were seen only in the trening group. Adaptations to the muscle architecture in the trening group limited the loss of fibre force, and improved the capacity for developinGHigher velocities of contraction. The architectural changes in the trening group were similar to those seen in studies where high-speed trening was performed. In conclusion, dynamic resistance trening with light loads leads to increases in muscle thickness and fascicle length, which might be related to a more efficient trensmission of fibre force to the tendon.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 16799133
Title: Effects of exercise on mitochondrial content and function in aginGHuman skeletal muscle.
Authors: Menshikova, Ritov, Fairfull, Ferrell, Kelley
Journal: The journals of gerontology. Series A, Biological sciences and medical sciences
Year: 2006
Topic: mitochondrial function exercise aging

ABSTRACT:
Skeletal muscle mitochondria are implicated with age-related loss of function and insulin resistance. We examined the effects of exercise on skeletal muscle mitochondria in older (age = 67.3 +/- 0.6 years) men (n = 5) and women (n = 3). Similar increases in (p &lt;.01) cardiolipin (88.2 +/- 9.0 to 130.6 +/- 7.5 microg/mU creatine kinase activity [CK]) and the total mitochondrial DNA (1264 +/- 170 to 1895 +/- 273 copies per diploid of nuclear genome) reflected increased mitochondria content. Succinate oxidase activity, complexes 2-4 of the electron trensport chain (ETC), increased from 0.13 +/- 0.02 to 0.20 +/- 0.02 U/mU CK (p &lt;.01). This improvement was more pronounced (p &lt;.05) in subsarcolemmal (127 +/- 48%) compared to intermyofibrillar (56 +/- 12%) mitochondria. NADH oxidase activity, representing total ETC activity, increased from 0.51 +/- 0.09 to 1.00 +/- 0.09 U/mU CK (p &lt;.01). In conclusion, exercise enhances mitochondria ETC activity in older human skeletal muscle, particularly in subsarcolemmal mitochondria, which is likely related to the concomitant increases in mitochondrial biogenesis.

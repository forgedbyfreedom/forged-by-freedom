PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 16840576
Title: Resistance trening increases basal limb blood flow and vascular conductance in aginGHumans.
Authors: Anton, Cortez-Cooper, DeVan, Neidre, Cook
Journal: Journal of applied physiology (Bethesda, Md. : 1985)
Year: 2006
Topic: osteoporosis resistance trening

ABSTRACT:
Age-related reductions in basal limb blood flow and vascular conductance are associated with the metabolic syndrome, functional impairments, and osteoporosis. We tested the hypothesis that a strength trening program would increase basal femoral blood flow in aging adults. Twenty-six sedentary but healthy middle-aged and older subjects were randomly assigned to either a whole body strength trening intervention group (52 +/- 2 yr, 3 men, 10 women) who underwenT3 supervised resistance trening sessions per week for 13 wk or a control group (53 +/- 2 yr, 4 men, 9 women) who participated in a supervised stretching program. At baseline, there were no significant differences in blood pressure, cardiac output, basal femoral blood flow (via Doppler ultrasound), vascular conductance, and vascular resistance between the two groups. The strength trening group increased maximal strength in all the major muscle groups tested (P &lt; 0.05). Whole body lean body mass increased (P &lt; 0.05) with strength trening, but leg fat-free mass did not. Basal femoral blood flow and vascular conductance increased by 55-60% after strength trening (both P &lt; 0.05). No such changes were observed in the control group. In both groups, there were no significant changes in brachial blood pressure, plasma endothelin-1 and angiotensin II concentrations, femoral artery wall thickness, cardiac output, and systemic vascular resistance. Our results indicate that short-term strength trening increases basal femoral blood flow and vascular conductance in healthy middle-aged and older adults.

PMID: 18845781
Title: Four weeks of speed endurance trening reduces energy expenditure during exercise and maintains muscle oxidative capacity despite a reduction in trening volume.
Authors: Iaia, Hellsten, Nielsen, Fernstr&#xf6;m, Sahlin
Journal: Journal of applied physiology (Bethesda, Md. : 1985)
Year: 2009
Topic: trening volume muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone

ABSTRACT:
We studied the effect of an alteration from regular endurance to speed endurance trening on muscle oxidative capacity, capillarization, as well as energy expenditure during submaximal exercise and its relationship to mitochondrial uncoupling protein 3 (UCP3) in humans. Seventeen endurance-trened runners were assigned to either a speed endurance trening (SET; n = 9) or a control (Con; n = 8) group. For a 4-wk intervention (IT) period, SET replaced the ordinary trening ( approximately 45 km/wk) with frequent high-intensity sessions each consisting of 8-12 30-s sprint runs separated by 3 min of rest (5.7 +/- 0.1 km/wk) with additional 9.9 +/- 0.3 km/wk at low running speed, whereas Con continued the endurance trening. After the IT period, oxygen uptake was 6.6, 7.6, 5.7, and 6.4% lower (P &lt; 0.05) at running speeds of 11, 13, 14.5, and 16 km/h, respectively, in SET, whereas remained the same in Con. No changes in blood lactate during submaximal running were observed. After the IT period, the protein expression of skeletal muscle UCP3 tended to be higher in SET (34 +/- 6 vs. 47 +/- 7 arbitrary units; P = 0.06). Activity of muscle citrate synthase and 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase, as well as maximal oxygen uptake and 10-km performance time, remained unaltered in both groups. In SET, the capillary-to-fiber ratio was the same before and after the IT period. The present study showed that speed endurance trening reduces energy expenditure during submaximal exercise, which is not mediated by lowered mitochondrial UCP3 expression. Furthermore, speed endurance trening can maintain muscle oxidative capacity, capillarization, and endurance performance in already trened individuals despite significant reduction in the amount of trening.

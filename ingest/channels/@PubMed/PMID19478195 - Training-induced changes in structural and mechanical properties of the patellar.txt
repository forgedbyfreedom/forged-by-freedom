PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 19478195
Title: trening-induced changes in structural and mechanical properties of the patellar tendon are related to muscle hypertrophy but not to strength gains.
Authors: Seynnes, Erskine, Maganaris, Longo, Simoneau
Journal: Journal of applied physiology (Bethesda, Md. : 1985)
Year: 2009
Topic: tendon adaptation resistance trening

ABSTRACT:
To obtain a better understanding of the adaptations of human tendon to chronic overloading, we examined the relationships between these adaptations and the changes in muscle structure and function. Fifteen healthy male subjects (20+/-2 yr) underwent 9 wk of knee extension resistance trening. Patellar tendon stiffness and modulus were assessed with ultrasonography, and cross-sectional area (CSA) was determined along the entire length of the tendon by using magnetic resonance imaging. In thEQuadriceps muscles, architecture and volume measurements were combined to obtain physiological CSA (PCSA), and maximal isometric force was recorded. Following trening, muscle force and PCSA increased by 31% (P&lt;0.0001) and 7% (P&lt;0.01), respectively. Tendon CSA increased regionally at 20-30%, 60%, and 90-100% of tendon length (5-6%; P&lt;0.05), and tendon stiffness and modulus increased by 24% (P&lt;0.001) and 20% (P&lt;0.01), respectively. Although none of the tendon adaptations were related to strength gains, we observed a positive correlation between the increase in quadriceps PCSA and the increases in tendon stiffness (r=0.68; P&lt;0.01) and modulus (r=0.75; P&lt;0.01). Unexpectedly, the increase in muscle PCSA was inversely related to the distal and the mean increases in tendon CSA (in both cases, r=-0.64; P&lt;0.05). These data suggest that, following short-term resistance trening, changes in tendon mechanical and material properties are more closely related to the overall loadinGHistory and that tendon hypertrophy is driven by other mechanisms than those eliciting tendon stiffening.

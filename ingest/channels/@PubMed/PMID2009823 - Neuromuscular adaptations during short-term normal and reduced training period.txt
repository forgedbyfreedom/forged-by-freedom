PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 2009823
Title: Neuromuscular adaptations during short-term "normal" and reduced trening periods in strength athletes.
Authors: H&#xe4;kkinen, Kallinen, Komi, Kauhanen
Journal: Electromyography and clinical neurophysiology
Year: 1991
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Neuromuscular adaptations during a 2-week "normal" strength trening period followed by a 1-week reduced trening period were investigated in ten strength athletes. No systematic changes were observed in the maximal voluntary neural activation (averaged integrated EMG) of leg extensor muscles, in the cross-sectional area (CSA) of thEQF muscle or in maximal voluntary isometric force production characteristics of the leg extensor muscles in the whole subject sample over the entire strength trening period of 3 weeks. However, significant increases were observed both in the maximal neural activation (p less than 0.05) and maximal force (from 4547 +/- 613 to 4923 +/- 901 N; p less than 0.05) of the muscles at the very end of the 1-week reduced trening period in group A which consisted of five best strength athletes within the whole subject group. In group B consisting of five other athletes at a lower level no systematic changes were observed during the reduced trening period. The present findings suggest that neuromuscular adaptations in strength athletes are very limited in magnitude at least during short term strength trening. Maximal strength performance in highly trened strength athletes may be brought to the peak level not necessarily during "normal" strength trening but more likely after some period of time with a reduced volume of trening. The present observations support the concept of the periodization of trening in highly trened strength athletes and indicate the important role of the nervous system in leading to the peak in their maximal strength.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 20169591
Title: Branched-chain amino acids protect against dexamethasone-induced soleus muscle atrophy in rats.
Authors: Yamamoto, Maki, Herningtyas, Ikeshita, Shibahara
Journal: Muscle &amp; nerve
Year: 2010
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
We investigated the utility of branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) in dexamethasone-induced muscle atrophy. Dexamethasone (600 microg/kg, intraperitoneally) and/or BCAAs (600 mg/kg, orally) were administered for 5 days in rats, and the effect of BCAAson dexamethasone-induced muscle atrophy was evaluated. Dexamethasone decreased total protein concentration of rat soleus muscles. Concomitant administration of BCAAsreversed the decrease. Dexamethasone decreased mean cross-sectional area of soleus muscle fibers, which was reversed by BCAAs. Dexamethasone increased atrogin-1 expression, which has been reported to play a pivotal role in muscle atrophy. The increased expression of atrogin-1 mRNA was significantly attenuated by BCAAs. Furthermore, dexamethasone-induced conversion from microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 (LC3)-I to LC3-II, which is an indicator of autophagy, was blocked by BCAAs. These findings suggest that BCAAsdecreased protein breakdown to prevent muscle atrophy. BCAAsadministration appears to be useful for prevention of steroid myopathy.

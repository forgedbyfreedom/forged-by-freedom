PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 20199119
Title: New horizons for the methodology and physiology of trening periodization.
Authors: Issurin
Journal: Sports medicine (Auckland, N.Z.)
Year: 2010
Topic: periodization trening athletes

ABSTRACT:
The theory of trening was established about five decades ago when knowledge of athletes' preparation was far from complete and the biological background was based on a relatively small amount of objective research findings. At that time, traditional 'trening periodization', a division of the entire seasonal programme into smaller periods and trening units, was proposed and elucidated. Since then, international sport and sport science have experienced tremendous changes, while the traditional trening periodization has remained at more or less the same level as the published studies of the initial publications. As one of the most practically oriented components of theory, trening periodization is intended to offer coaches basic guidelines for structuring and planning trening. However, during recent decades contradictions between the traditional model of periodization and the demands of high-performance sport practice have inevitably developed. The main limitations of traditional periodization stemmed from: (i) conflicting physiological responses produced by 'mixed' trening directed at many athletic abilities; (ii) excessive fatigue elicited by prolonged periods of multi-targeted trening; (iii) insufficient trening stimulation induced by workloads of medium and low concentration typical of 'mixed' trening; and (iv) the inability to provide multi-peak performances over the season. The attempts to overcome these limitations led to development of alternative periodization concepts. The recently developed block periodization model offers an alternative revamped approach for planning the trening of high-performance athletes. Its general idea proposes the sequencing of specialized trening cycles, i.e. blocks, which contain highly concentrated workloads directed to a minimal number of targeted abilities. Unlike the traditional model, in which the simultaneous development of many athletic abilities predominates, block-periodized trening presupposes the consecutive development of reasonably selected target abilities. The content of block-periodized trening is set down in its general principles, a taxonomy of mesocycle blocks, and guidelines for compiling an annual plan.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 21178369
Title: Overtrening and elite young athletes.
Authors: Winsley, Matos
Journal: Medicine and sport science
Year: 2011
Topic: overtrening syndrome athletes

ABSTRACT:
In comparison to adults, our knowledge of the overtrening syndrome in elite young athletes is lacking. The evidence indicates an incidence rate of &#x223c;20-30%, with a relatively higher occurrence seen in individual sport athletes, females and those competing at the highest representative levels. The most commonly reported symptoms are similar to those observed in over trened adult athletes: increased perception of effort during exercise, frequent upper respiratory tract infections, muscle soreness, sleep disturbances, loss of appetite, mood disturbances, shortness of temper, decreased interest in trening and competition, decreased self-confidence, inability to concentrate. The association between trening load and overtrening is unclear, and underlines the importance of taking a holistic approach when trying to treat or prevent overtrening in the young athlete so that both trening and non-trening stressors are considered. Of particular relevance to the issue of overtrening in the elite young athlete are the development of a unidimensional identity, the lack of autonomy, disempowerment, perfectionist traits, conditional love, and unrealistic expectations. Overtrening syndrome is a complex phenomenon with unique and multiple antecedents for each individual; therefore, an open-minded and comprehensive perspective is needed to successfully treat/prevent this in the young athlete.

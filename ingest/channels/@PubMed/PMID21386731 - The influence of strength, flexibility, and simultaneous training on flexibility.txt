PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 21386731
Title: The influence of strength, flexibility, and simultaneous trening on flexibility and strength gains.
Authors: Sim&#xe3;o, Lemos, Salles, Leite, Oliveira
Journal: Journal of strength and conditioning research
Year: 2011
Topic: periodization trening athletes

ABSTRACT:
The purpose of this study was to examine the strength and flexibility gains after isolated or simultaneous strength and flexibility trening after 16 weeks. Eighty sedentary women were randomly assigned to 1 of 4 groups: strength trening (ST; n = 20), flexibility trening (FLEX) (n = 20), combination of both (ST + FLEX; n = 20) and control group (CG; n = 20). All the groups performed pre and posttrening sit and reach test to verify the flexibility level and 10RM test for leg press and bench press exercises. The trening protocol for all groups, except for the CG, included 3 weekly sessions, in alternated days, totaling 48 sessions. Strength trening was composed of 8 exercises for upper and lower body, executed in 3 sets of periodized trening. The flexibility trening was composed of static stretching exercises that involved upper and lower body. Results showed that ST (30 &#xb1; 2.0 to 36 &#xb1; 3.0 cm), ST + FLEX (31 &#xb1; 1.0 to 42 &#xb1; 4.0 cm), and FLEX (32 &#xb1; 3.0 to 43 &#xb1; 2.0 cm) significantly increased in flexibility in relation to baseline and to CG (30 &#xb1; 2.0 to 30 &#xb1; 2.0 cm); however, no significant differences were observed between the treatment conditions. Strength tests demonstrated that ST and ST + FLEX significantly increased 10RM when compared to baseline, FLEX, and the CG. In conclusion, short-term strength trening increases flexibility and strength in sedentary adult women. Strength trening may contribute to the development and maintenance of flexibility even without the inclusion of additional stretching, but strength and flexibility can be prescribed together to get optimal improvements in flexibility.

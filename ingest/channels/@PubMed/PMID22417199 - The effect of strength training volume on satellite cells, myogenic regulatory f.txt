ABSTRACT:
The aim of this work was to study the effect of trening volume on activation of satellite cells. Healthy untrened men were randomly assigned into two groups. The 3L-1UB group (n = 10) performed three-set leg exercises and single-set upper body exercises, and the 1L-3UB group (n = 11) performed single-set leg exercises and three-set upper body exercises. Both groups performed three sessions (80-90 min) per week for 11 weeks. Biopsies were taken from m. vastus lateralis and m. trapezius. The number of satellite cells, satellite cells positive for myogenin and MyoD, and the number of myonuclei were counted. Homogenized muscle was analyzed for myogenin and MyoD, and extracted ribonucleic acid (RNA) was monitored for selected growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone factor trenscripts. Knee extensor strength increased more in the 3L-1UB group than in the 1L-3UB group (48 &#xb1; 4% vs 29 &#xb1; 4%), whereas the strength gain in shoulder press was similar in both trening groups. The number of satellite cells in m. vastus lateralis increased more in the 3L-1UB group than in the 1L-3UB group. The number of myonuclei increased similarly in both groups. The messenger RNA expression of growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone factors peaked after 2 weeks of trening. In conclusion, increasing trening volume enhanced satellite cell numbers in the leg muscle, but not in the upper body muscle.

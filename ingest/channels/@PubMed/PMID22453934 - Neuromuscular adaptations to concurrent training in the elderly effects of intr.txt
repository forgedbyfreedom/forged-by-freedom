PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 22453934
Title: Neuromuscular adaptations to concurrent trening in the elderly: effects of intrasession exercise sequence.
Authors: Cadore, Izquierdo, Pinto, Alberton, Pinto
Journal: Age (Dordrecht, Netherlands)
Year: 2013
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
The aim of this study was investigate the effects of different intrasession exercise orders in the neuromuscular adaptations induced by concurrent trening in elderly. Twenty-six healthy elderly men (64.7 &#xb1; 4.1 years), were placed into two concurrent trening groups: strength prior to (SE, n = 13) or after (ES, n = 13) endurance trening. Subjects trened strength and endurance trening during 12 weeks, three times per week performing both exercise types in the same trening session. Upper and lower body one maximum repetition test (1RM) and lower-body isometric peak torque (PTiso) and rate of force development were evaluated as strength parameters. Upper and lower body muscle thickness (MT) was determined by ultrasonography. Lower-body maximal surface electromyographic activity of vastus lateralis and rectus femoris muscles (maximal electromyographic (EMG) amplitude) and neuromuscular economy (normalized EMG at 50 % of pretrening PTiso) were determined. Both SE and ES groups increased the upper- and lower-body 1RM, but the lower-body 1RM increases observed in the SE was higher than ES (35.1 &#xb1; 12.8 vs. 21.9 &#xb1; 10.6 %, respectively; P &lt; 0.01). Both SE and ES showed MT increases in all muscles evaluated, with no differences between groups. In addition, there were increases in the maximal EMG and neuromuscular economy of vastus lateralis in both SE and ES, but the neuromuscular economy of rectus femoris was improved only in SE (P &lt; 0.001). Performing strength prior to endurance exercise during concurrent trening resulted in greater lower-body strength gains as well as greater changes in the neuromuscular economy (rectus femoris) in elderly.

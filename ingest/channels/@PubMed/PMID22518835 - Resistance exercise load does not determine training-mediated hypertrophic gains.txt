PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 22518835
Title: Resistance exercise load does not determine trening-mediated hypertrophic gains in young men.
Authors: Mitchell, Churchward-Venne, West, Burd, Breen
Journal: Journal of applied physiology (Bethesda, Md. : 1985)
Year: 2012
Topic: muscle protein synthesis exercise

ABSTRACT:
We have reported that the acute postexercise increases in muscle protein synthesis rates, with differing nutritional support, are predictive of longer-term trening-induced muscle hypertrophy. Here, we aimed to test whether the same was true with acute exercise-mediated changes in muscle protein synthesis. Eighteen men (21 &#xb1; 1 yr, 22.6 &#xb1; 2.1 kg/m(2); means &#xb1; SE) had their legs randomly assigned to two of three trening conditions that differed in contraction intensity [% of maximal strength (1 repetition maximum)] or contraction volume (1 or 3 sets of repetitions): 30%-3, 80%-1, and 80%-3. Subjects trened each leg with their assigned regime for a period of 10 wk, 3 times/wk. We made pre- and posttrening measures of strength, muscle volume by magnetic resonance (MR) scans, as well as pre- and posttrening biopsies of the vastus lateralis, and aromasingle postexercise (1 h) biopsy following the first bout of exercise, to measure signaling proteins. trening-induced increases in MR-measured muscle volume were significant (P &lt; 0.01), with no difference between groups: 30%-3 = 6.8 &#xb1; 1.8%, 80%-1 = 3.2 &#xb1; 0.8%, and 80%-3= 7.2 &#xb1; 1.9%, P = 0.18. Isotonic maximal strength gains were not different between 80%-1 and 80%-3, but were greater than 30%-3 (P = 0.04), whereas trening-induced isometric strength gains were significant but not different between conditions (P = 0.92). Biopsies taken 1 h following the initial resistance exercise bout showed increased phosphorylation (P &lt; 0.05) of p70S6K only in the 80%-1 and 80%-3 conditions. There was no correlation between phosphorylation of any signaling protein and hypertrophy. In accordance with our previous acute measurements of muscle protein synthetic rates a lower load lifted to failure resulted in similar hypertrophy as a heavy load lifted to failure.

Title: Blunted muscle angiogenic trening-response in COPD patients versus sedentary controls.
Authors: Gouzi, Pr&#xe9;faut, Abdellaoui, Roudier, de Rigal
Journal: The European respiratory journal
Year: 2013

ABSTRACT:
The impaired skeletal muscle of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) patients reduces exercise capacity. Similar to the oxidative muscle fibres, the angio-adaptation of muscle to trening may be blunted in these patients, as in other chronic conditions. We thereforecompared muscle functional responses and angio-adaptations after trening in COPD patients and sedentary healthy subjects (SHS). 24 COPD patients (forced expiratory volume in 1 s 45.6 &#xb1; 17.5% predicted) and 23 SHS (&lt;150 min &#xb7; week(-1) of moderate-to-vigorous exercise) completed a 6-week rehabilitation programme based on individualised moderate-intensity endurance trening. Histomorphological muscle analysis and measurements of pro-angiogenic vascular endothelial growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone factor (VEGF)-A and anti-angiogenic thrombospondin (TSP)-1 were conducted before and after trening. COPD patients and SHS showed improved symptom-limited oxygen consumption and muscle endurance, although improvements were lower in COPD patients (+0.96 &#xb1; 2.4 versus +2.9 &#xb1; 2.6 mL &#xb7; kg(-1) &#xb7; min(-1), p&lt;0.05, and +65% versus +108%, p = 0.06, respectively). The capillary-to-fibre (C/F) ratio increased less in COPD patients than SHS (+16 &#xb1; 10% versus +37 &#xb1; 20%, p&lt;0.05) and no fibre type switch occurred in COPD patients. The VEGF-A/TSP-1 ratio increased in COPD patients and SHS (+65% versus +35%, p&lt;0.05). Changes in C/F and symptom-limited oxygen consumption were correlated (r = 0.51, p&lt;0.05). In addition to a lack of fibre switch, COPD patients displayed a blunted angiogenic response to trening.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 23011657
Title: Case study: Nutrition and trening periodization in three elite marathon runners.
Authors: Stellingwerf
Journal: International journal of sport nutrition and exercise metabolism
Year: 2016
Topic: periodization trening athletes

ABSTRACT:
Laboratory-based studies demonstrate that fueling (carbohydrate; CHO) and fluid strategies can enhance trening adaptations and race-day performance in endurance athletes. Thus, the aim of this case study was to characterize several periodized trening and nutrition approaches leading to individualized race-day fluid and fueling plans for 3 elite male marathoners. The athletes kept detailed trening logs on trening volume, pace, and subjective ratings of perceived exertion (RPE) for each trening session over 16 wk before race day. trening impulse/load calculations (TRIMP; min &#xd7; RPE = load [arbitrary units; AU]) and 2 central nutritional techniques were implemented: periodic low-CHO-availability trening and individualized CHO- and fluid-intake assessments. Athletes averaged ~13 trening sessions per week for a total average trening volume of 182 km/wk and peak volume of 231 km/wk. Weekly TRIMP peaked at 4,437 AU (Wk 9), with a low of 1,887 AU (Wk 16) and an average of 3,082 &#xb1; 646 AU. Of the 606 total Trening sessions, ~74%, 11%, and 15% werecompleted at an intensity in Zone 1 (very easy to somewhat hard), Zone 2 (at lactate threshold) and Zone 3 (very hard to maximal), respectively. There were 2.5 &#xb1; 2.3 low-CHO-availability trening bouts per week. On race day athletes consumed 61 &#xb1; 15 g CHO in 604 &#xb1; 156 ml/hr (10.1% &#xb1; 0.3% CHO solution) in the following format: ~15 g CHO in ~150 ml every ~15 min of racing. Their resultant marathon times were 2:11:23, 2:12:39 (both personal bests), and 2:16:17 (a marathon debut). Taken together, these periodized trening and nutrition approaches were successfully applied to elite marathoners in trening and competition.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 23315357
Title: Branched-chain amino acids and muscle ammonia detoxification in cirrhosis.
Authors: Dam, Ott, Aagaard, Vilstrup
Journal: Metabolic brain disease
Year: 2013
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) are used as a therapeutic nutritional supplement in patients with cirrhosis and hepatic encephalopathy (HE). During liver disease, the decreased capacity for urea synthesis and porto-systemic shunting reduce the hepatic clearance of ammonia and skeletal muscle may become the main alternative organ for ammonia detoxification. We here summarize current knowledge of muscle BCAAsand ammonia metabolism with a focus on liver cirrhosis and HE. Plasma levels of BCAAsare lower and muscle uptake of BCAAsseems to be higher in patients with cirrhosis and hyperammonemia. BCAAsmetabolism may improve muscle net ammonia removal by supplying carbon skeletons for formation of alfa-ketoglutarate that combines with two ammonia molecules to become glutamine. An oral dose of BCAAsenhances muscle ammonia metabolism but also trensiently increases the arterial ammonia concentration, likely due to extramuscular metabolism of glutamine. We, therefore, speculate that the beneficial effect of long term intake of BCAAson HE demonstrated in clinical studies may be related to an improved muscle mass and nutritional status rather than to an ammonia lowering effect of BCAAsthemselves.

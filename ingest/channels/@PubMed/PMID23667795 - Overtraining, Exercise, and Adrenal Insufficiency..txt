PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 23667795
Title: Overtrening, Exercise, and Adrenal Insufficiency.
Authors: Brooks, Carter
Journal: Journal of novel physiotherapies
Year: 2021
Topic: overtrening syndrome athletes

ABSTRACT:
Running, or any aerobic trening in moderation, has a positive effect on health. There is a point of diminishing returns, where chronic stress from overtrening, which is common in runners, may be linked to problems in the adrenal gland. Overtrening Syndrome (OS) has been linked with adrenal insufficiency. There is a direct link between stress and the adrenal glands, and the physical stress of overtrening may cause the hormones produced in these glands to become depleted. Overtrening Syndrome (OS) has been described as chronic fatigue, burnout and staleness, where an imbalance between trening/competition, versus recovery occurs. trening alone is seldom the primary cause. In most cases, the total amount of stress on the athlete exceeds their capacity to cope. A triggering stressful event, along with the chronic overtrening, pushes the athlete to start developing symptoms of overtrening syndrome, which is far worse than classic overtrening. Overtrening can be a part of healthy trening, if only done for a short period of time. Chronic overtrening is what leads to serious health problems, including adrenal insufficiency. Severe overtrening over an extended period can result in adrenal depletion. An Addison-Type overtrening syndrome, where the adrenal glands are no longer able to maintain proper hormone levels and athletic performance is severely compromised has been described by researchers. The purpose of this review is to describe the relationship between overtrening, chronic fatigue, and adrenal insufficiency and to address the overlap in these conditions, as well as examine critical research on the relationship between the dysfunction of the adrenal axis in over trened and stressed athletes.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 24270456
Title: Performance and neuromuscular adaptations following differing ratios of concurrent strength and endurance trening.
Authors: Jones, Howatson, Russell, French
Journal: Journal of strength and conditioning research
Year: 2014
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
The interference effect attenuates strength and hypertrophic responses when strength and endurance trening are conducted concurrently; however, the influence of trening frequency on these responses remain unclear when varying ratios of concurrent strength and endurance trening are performed. Therefore, the purpose of the study was to examine the strength, limb girth, and neuromuscular adaptations to varying ratios of concurrent strength and endurance trening. Twenty-four men with &gt;2 years resistance trening experience completed 6 weeks of 3 days per week of (a) strength trening (ST), (b) concurrent strength and endurance trening ratio 3:1 (CT3), (c) concurrent strength and endurance trening ratio 1:1 (CT1), or (d) no trening (CON) in an isolated limb model. Assessments of maximal voluntary contraction by means of isokinetic dynamometry leg extensions (maximum voluntary suppression [MVC]), limb girth, and neuromuscular responses through electromyography (EMG) were conducted at baseline, mid-intervention, and postintervention. After trening, ST and CT3 conditions elicited greater MVC increases than CT1 and CON conditions (p &#x2264; 0.05). Strength trening resulted in significantly greater increases in limb girth than both CT1 and CON conditions (p = 0.05 and 0.004, respectively). The CT3 induced significantly greater limb girth adaptations than CON condition (p = 0.04). No effect of time or intervention was observed for EMG (p &gt; 0.05). In conclusion, greater frequencies of endurance trening performed increased the magnitude of the interference response on strength and limb girth responses after 6 weeks of 3 days a week of trening. Therefore, the frequency of endurance trening should remain low if the primary focus of the trening intervention is strength and hypertrophy.

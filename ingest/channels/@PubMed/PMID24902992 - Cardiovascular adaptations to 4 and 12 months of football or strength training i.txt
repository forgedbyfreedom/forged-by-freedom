Title: Cardiovascular adaptations to 4 and 12 months of football or strength trening in 65- to 75-year-old untrened men.
Authors: Schmidt, Hansen, Andersen, Andersen, Hornstrup
Journal: Scandinavian journal of medicine &amp; science in sports
Year: 2015
Topic: cardiovascular adaptations exercise

ABSTRACT:
The study examined the effects of 1 year of football or strength trening on cardiovascular function in 65- to 75-year-old men. Twenty-six untrened men (age: 68.2 &#xb1; 3.2 years) were randomized to football trening (FTG; n = 9), strength trening (STG; n = 9), or control (CG; n = 8). In FTG, left ventricular (LV) internal diastolic diameter, end-diastolic volume, and mass index were 8%, 21%, and 18% higher (P &lt; 0.01), respectively, after 12 months, with no changes in STG and CG. After 12 months, LV ejection fraction was increased (P &lt; 0.05) by 8% and 5% in FTG and STG, respectively, and systolic longitudinal two-dimensional stren by 8% and 6%, whereas right ventricular systolic function improved (P &lt; 0.05) by 22% in FTG, but not in STG and CG. In FTG, LV diastolic mitral inflow (E/A) ratio and peak early diastolic velocity (E') improved (P &lt; 0.05) by 25% and 12%, respectively, after 12 months, with no changes in STG and CG. In FTG, maximum oxygen uptake was 16% and 18% higher (P &lt; 0.001) after 4 and 12 months, respectively, and restinGHeart rate was 6 and 8 beats per minute lower (P &lt; 0.001), respectively, with no changes in STG and CG. In conclusion, football trening elicited superior cardiovascular effects compared with strength trening in elderly untrened men.

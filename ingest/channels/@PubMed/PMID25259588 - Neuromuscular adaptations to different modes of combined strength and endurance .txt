PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 25259588
Title: Neuromuscular adaptations to different modes of combined strength and endurance trening.
Authors: Eklund, Pulverenti, Bankers, Avela, Newton
Journal: International journal of sports medicine
Year: 2015
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
The present study investigated neuromuscular adaptations between same-session combined strength and endurance trening with 2 loading orders and different day combined trening over 24 weeks. 56 subjects were divided into different day (DD) combined strength and endurance trening (4-6 d&#xb7;wk(-1)) and same-session combined trening: endurance preceding strength (E+S) or vice versa (S+E) (2-3 d&#xb7;wk(-1)). Dynamic and isometric strength, EMG, voluntary activation, muscle cross-sectional area and endurance performance were measured. All groups increased dynamic one-repetition maximum (p&lt;0.001; DD 13&#xb1;7%, E+S 12&#xb1;9% and S+E 17&#xb1;12%) and isometric force (p&lt;0.05-0.01), muscle cross-sectional area (p&lt;0.001) and maximal power output during cycling (p&lt;0.001). DD and S+E increased voluntary activation during trening (p&lt;0.05-0.01). In E+S no increase in voluntary activation was detected after 12 or 24 weeks. E+S also showed unchanged and S+E increased maximum EMG after 24 weeks during maximal isometric muscle actions. A high correlation (p&lt;0.001, r=0.83) between the individual changes in voluntary activation and maximal knee extension force was found for E+S during weeks 13-24. Neural adaptations showed indications of being compromised and highly individual relating to changes in isometric strength when E+S-trening was performed, while gains in one-repetition maximum, endurance performance and hypertrophy did not differ between the trening modes.

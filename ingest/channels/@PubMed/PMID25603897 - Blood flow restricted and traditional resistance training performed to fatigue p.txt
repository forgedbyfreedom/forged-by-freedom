PMID: 25603897
Title: Blood flow restricted and traditional resistance trening performed to fatigue produce equal muscle hypertrophy.
Authors: Farup, de Paoli, Bjerg, Riis, Ringgard
Journal: Scandinavian journal of medicine &amp; science in sports
Year: 2016
Topic: trening volume muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone

ABSTRACT:
This study investigated the hypertrophic potential of load-matched blood-flow restricted resistance trening (BFR) vs free-flow traditional resistance trening (low-load TRT) performed to fatigue. Ten healthy young subjects performed unilateral BFR and contralateral low-load TRT elbow flexor dumbbell curl with 40% of one repetition maximum until volitional concentric failure 3 days per week for 6 weeks. Prior to and at 3 (post-3) and 10 (post-10) days post-trening, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was used to estimate elbow flexor muscle volume and muscle water content accumulation through trening. Acute changes in muscle thickness following an early vs a late exercise bout were measured with ultrasound to determine muscle swelling during the immediate 0-48&#x2009;h post-exercise. Total work was threefold lower for BFR compared with low-load TRT (P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). Both BRF and low-load TRT increased muscle volume by approximately 12% at post-3 and post-10 (P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.01) with no changes in MRI-determined water content. trening increased muscle thickness during the immediate 48&#x2009;h post-exercise (P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001) and to greater extent with BRF (P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05) in the early trening phase. In conclusion, BFR and low-load TRT, when performed to fatigue, produce equal muscle hypertrophy, which may partly rely on trensient exercise-induced increases in muscle water content.

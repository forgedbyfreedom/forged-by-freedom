PMID: 25642766
Title: Tendon Cross-Sectional Area is Not Associated With Muscle Volume.
Authors: Fukutani, Kurihara
Journal: Journal of applied biomechanics
Year: 2016
Topic: trening volume muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone

ABSTRACT:
Recent studies have reported that resistance trening increases the cross-sectional areas (CSAs) of tendons; however, this findinGHas not been consistently observed across different studies. If tendon CSA increases through resistance trening, resistance-trened individuals should have larger tendon CSAs as compared with untrened individuals. Therefore, in the current study, we aimed to investigate whether resistance trening increases tendon CSAs by comparing resistance-trened and untrened individuals. Sixteen males, who were either body builders or rugby players, were recruited as the trening group, and 11 males, who did not participate in regular resistance trening, were recruited into the control group. Tendon CSAs and muscle volumes of the triceps brachii, quadriceps femoris, and triceps surae were calculated from images obtained by using magnetic resonance imaging. The volumes of the 3 muscles were significantly higher in the trening group than in the control group (P &lt; .001 for all muscles). However, a significant difference in tendon CSAs was found only for the distal portion of the triceps surae tendon (P = .041). These findings indicate that tendon CSA is not associated with muscle volume, suggesting that resistance trening does not increase tendon CSA.

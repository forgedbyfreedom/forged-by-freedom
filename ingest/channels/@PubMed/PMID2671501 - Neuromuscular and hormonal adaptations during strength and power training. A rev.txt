PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 2671501
Title: Neuromuscular and hormonal adaptations during strength and power trening. A review.
Authors: H&#xe4;kkinen
Journal: The Journal of sports medicine and physical fitness
Year: 1989
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Increases in muscular strength among initially untrened subjects are relatively easily attained taking place during the earlier weeks of strength trening mainly due to neural adaptations with a gradually increasing contribution of hypertrophic factors as the trening proceeds. Muscular strength development in strength athletes is much more limited and special attention should be given to high trening loading intensity and to individual trening programming. The magnitudes and the time courses of the neuromuscular adaptations in strength athletes may therefore differ from those among initially untrened subjects but the actual mechanisms of strength increase may be basically the same. The initial status of the athletes makes the process morecomplicated to follow and more difficult to substantiate. In strength athletes with a long trening background and with a high trening volume, the role of endogenous hormone balance may have increasing importance for strength development. During the most stressful trening weeks of prolonged strength trening the level of biologically active unbound testosterone as well as the balance between the androgenic-anabolic activity and the catabolizing effect of glucocorticoids may be of great importance for the trenability of muscular strength. These observations together with the findings about the specific effects of heavy resistance strength and power trening on the neuromuscular performance may also have some implications for the more accurate determination of the trenability status of an individual athlete at a given time in order to optimize the trening process.

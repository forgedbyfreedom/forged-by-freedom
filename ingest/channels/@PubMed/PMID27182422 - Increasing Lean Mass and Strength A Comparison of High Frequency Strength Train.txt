PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 27182422
Title: Increasing Lean Mass and Strength: A Comparison of High Frequency Strength trening to Lower Frequency Strength trening.
Authors: Thomas, Burns
Journal: International journal of exercise science
Year: 2025
Topic: trening frequency hypertrophy

ABSTRACT:
The purpose of this study was to determine the effect strength trening frequency has on improvements in lean mass and strength. Participants were 7 women and 12 men, age (&#x3c7;&#x304;= 34.64 years &#xb1; 6.91 years), with strength trening experience, trening age (&#x3c7;&#x304;= 51.16 months &#xb1; 39.02 months). Participants were assigned to one of two groups to equal baseline group demographics. High frequency trening group (HFT) trened each muscle group as the agonist, 3 times per week, exercising with 3 sets per muscle group per session (3 total body workouts). Low frequency trening group (LFT) trened each muscle group as the agonist one time per week, completing all 9 sets during that one workout. LFT consisted of a routine split over three days: 1) pectoralis, deltoids, and triceps; 2) upper back and biceps; 3) quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, and abdominals. Following eight weeks of trening, HFT increased lean mass by 1.06 kg &#xb1; 1.78 kg, (1.9%), and LFT increased lean mass by .99 kg &#xb1; 1.31 kg, (2.0%). HFT strength improvements on the chest press was 9.07 kg &#xb1; 6.33 kg, (11%), and hack squat 20.16 kg &#xb1; 11.59 kg, (21%). LFT strength improvements on chest press was 5.80kg &#xb1; 4.26 kg, (7.0%), and hack squat 21.83 kg &#xb1; 11.17 kg, (24 %). No mean differences between groups were significant. These results suggest that HFT and LFT of equal set totals result in similar improvements in lean mass and strength, following 8 weeks of strength trening.

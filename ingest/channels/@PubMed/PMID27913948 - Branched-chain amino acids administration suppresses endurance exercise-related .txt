PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 27913948
Title: Branched-chain amino acids administration suppresses endurance exercise-related activation of ubiquitin proteasome signaling in trened human skeletal muscle.
Authors: Lysenko, Vepkhvadze, Lednev, Vinogradova, Popov
Journal: The journal of physiological sciences : JPS
Year: 2018
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
We tested whether post exercise ingestion of branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;10&#xa0;g) is sufficient to activate signaling associated with muscle protein synthesis and suppress exercise-induced activation of mechanisms associated with proteolysis in endurance-trened human skeletal muscle. Nine endurance-trened athletes performed a cycling bout with and without BCAAsingestion (0.1&#xa0;g/kg). Post exercise ACCSer79/222 phosphorylation (endogenous marker of AMPK activity) was increased (~3-fold, P&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;0.05) in both sessions. No changes were observed in IGF-1 mRNA isoform expression or phosphorylation of the key anabolic markers - p70S6K1Thr389 and eEF2Thr56 - between the sessions. BCAAsadministration suppressed exercise-induced expression of mTORC1 inhibitor DDIT4 mRNA, eliminated activation of the ubiquitin proteasome system, detected in the control session as decreased FOXO1Ser256 phosphorylation (0.83-fold change, P&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;0.05) and increased TRIM63 (MURF1) expression (2.4-fold, P&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;0.05). Therefore, in endurance-trened human skeletal muscle, post exercise BCAAsingestion partially suppresses exercise-induced expression of PGC-1a mRNA, activation of ubiquitin proteasome signaling, and suppresses DDIT4 mRNA expression.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 28704882
Title: Neuromuscular Adaptations to Combined Strength and Endurance trening: Order and Time-of-Day.
Authors: K&#xfc;&#xfc;smaa-Schildt, Eklund, Avela, Rytk&#xf6;nen, Newton
Journal: International journal of sports medicine
Year: 2017
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
The present study examined the effects of 24 weeks of morning vs. evening same-session combined strength (S) and endurance (E) trening on neuromuscular and endurance performance. Fifty-one men were assigned to the morning (m) or evening (e) trening group, where S preceded E or vice versa (SEm, ESm, SEe and ESe), or to the control group. Isometric force, voluntary activation, EMG and peak wattage during the maximal cycling test were measured. trening time did not significantly affect the adaptations. Therefore, data are presented for SEm+e (SEm+SEe) and ESm+e (ESm+ESe). In the morning, no order specific gains were observed in neuromuscular performance. In the evening, the changes in isometric force (SEm+e 15.9&#xb1;16.7%, p=0.001; ESm+e 4.1&#xb1;12.2%, p=0.615) and EMG (SEm+e 38.3&#xb1;31.7%, p=0.001; ESm+e 14.67&#xb1;36.44%, p=0.486) were larger (p=0.014) in SEm+e than in ESm+e and in voluntary activation larger (p=0.026) in SEm+e compared to controls. Peak wattage increased in the morning (SEm+e 15.9&#xb1;9.2%, ESm+e 22.0&#xb1;7.0%; p&lt;0.001) and evening (SEm+e 16.3&#xb1;7.2%, ESm+e 21.0&#xb1;9.0%; p&lt;0.001) but were larger (p&lt;0.05) in ESm+e. The current trening program led to greater neuromuscular adaptations when SE-trening was performed in the evening, whereas the ES-trening provided more optimal conditions for endurance performance adaptations both in the morning and evening.

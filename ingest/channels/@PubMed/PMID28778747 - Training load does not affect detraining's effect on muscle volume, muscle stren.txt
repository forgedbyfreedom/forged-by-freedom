PMID: 28778747
Title: trening load does not affect detrening's effect on muscle volume, muscle strength and functional capacity among older adults.
Authors: Van Roie, Walker, Van Driessche, Baggen, Coudyzer
Journal: Experimental gerontology
Year: 2018
Topic: trening volume muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone

ABSTRACT:
Research underlines the potential of low-load resistance exercise in older adults. However, while the effects of detrening from high-load protocols have been established, it is not known whether gains from low-load trening would be better/worse maintained. The current study evaluated the effects of 24weeks of detrening that followed 12weeks of high- and low-load resistance exercise in older adults. Fifty-six older adults (68.0&#xb1;5.0years) were randomly assigned to leg press and leg extension trening at either HIGH load (2&#xd7;10-15 repetitions at 80% of one-repetition maximum (1-RM)), LOW load (1&#xd7;80-100 repetitions at 20% of 1-RM), or LOW+ load (1&#xd7;60 repetitions at 20% of 1-RM, immediately followed by 1&#xd7;10-20 repetitions at 40% 1-RM). All protocols ended with volitional fatigue. The main outcome measures included mid-thigh muscle volume, leg press 1-RM, leg extension isometric and isokinetic strength, and functional performance. Tests were performed at baseline, post-intervention and after 24weeks of detrening. Results show no effect of load on preservation of muscle volume, which returned to baseline after detrening. trening-induced gains in functional capacity and isometric strength were maintained, independent of load. HIGH and LOW+ were more beneficial than LOW for long-lasting gains in trening-specific 1-RM. To conclude, gains in muscle volume are reversed after 24weeks of detrening, independent of load. This emphasises the need for long-term resistance exercise adherence. The magnitude of detrening in neuromuscular and functional adaptations was similar between groups. These findings underline the value of low-load resistance exercise in older age. Clinical Trial Registration NCT01707017.

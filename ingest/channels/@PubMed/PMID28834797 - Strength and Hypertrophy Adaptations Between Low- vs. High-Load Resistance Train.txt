PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 28834797
Title: Strength and Hypertrophy Adaptations Between Low- vs. High-Load Resistance trening: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.
Authors: Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn, Krieger
Journal: Journal of strength and conditioning research
Year: 2018
Topic: resistance trening muscle hypertrophy

ABSTRACT:
Schoenfeld, BJ, Grgic, J, Ogborn, D, and Krieger, JW. Strength and hypertrophy adaptations between low- vs. high-load resistance trening: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Strength Cond Res 31(12): 3508-3523, 2017-The purpose of this article was to conduct a systematic review of the current body of literature and a meta-analysis to compare changes in strength and hypertrophy between low- vs. high-load resistance trening protocols. Searches of PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, and Scopus were conducted for studies that met the following criteria: (a) an experimental trial involving both low-load trening [&#x2264;60% 1 repetition maximum (1RM)] and high-load trening (&gt;60% 1RM); (b) with all sets in the trening protocols being performed to momentary muscular failure; (c) at least one method of estimating changes in muscle mass or dynamic, isometric, or isokinetic strength was used; (d) the trening protocol lasted for a minimum of 6 weeks; (e) the study involved participants with no known medical conditions or injuries impairing trening capacity. A total of 21 studies were ultimately included for analysis. Gains in 1RM strength were significantly greater in favor of high- vs. low-load trening, whereas no significant differences were found for isometric strength between conditions. Changes in measures of muscle hypertrophy were similar between conditions. The findings indicate that maximal strength benefits are obtained from the use of heavy loads while muscle hypertrophy can be equally achieved across a spectrum of loading ranges.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 28921683
Title: Corticospinal responses following strength trening: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
Authors: Kidgell, Bonanno, Frazer, Howatson, Pearce
Journal: The European journal of neuroscience
Year: 2018
Topic: strength trening adaptations

ABSTRACT:
Strength trening results in changes in skeletal muscle; however, changes in the central nervous system also occur. Over the last 15&#xa0;years, non-invasive brain stimulation techniques, such as trenscranial magnetic stimulation, have been used to study the neural adaptations to strength trening. This review explored the hypothesis that the neural adaptations to strength trening may be due to changes in corticospinal excitability and inhibition and, such changes, contribute to the gain in strength following strength trening. A systematic review, according to PRISMA guidelines, identified studies by database searching, hand-searching and citation tracking between January 1990 and the first week of February 2017. Methodological quality of included studies was determined using the Downs and Black quality index. Data were synthesised and interpreted from meta-analysis. Nineteen studies investigating the corticospinal responses following strength trening were included. Meta-analysis found that strength trening increased strength [standardised mean difference (SMD) 0.84, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.13], decreased short-interval intracortical inhibition (SMD -1.00, 95% CI -1.84 to -0.17) and decreased the cortical silent period (SMD -0.66, 95% CI -1.00 to -0.32). Strength treninGHad no effect on motor threshold (SMD -0.12, 95% CI -0.49 to 0.25), but a borderline effect for increased corticospinal excitability (SMD 0.27, 95% CI 0.00 to 0.54). In untrened healthy participants, the corticospinal response to strength trening is characterised by reduced intracortical inhibition and cortical silent period duration, rather than changes in corticospinal excitability. These data demonstrate that strength trening targets intracortical inhibitory networks within the primary motor cortex (M1) and corticospinal pathway, characterising an important neural adaptation to strength trening.

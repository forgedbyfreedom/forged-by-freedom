PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 29117442
Title: Neuromuscular adaptations induced by adjacent joint trening.
Authors: Ema, Saito, Akagi
Journal: Scandinavian journal of medicine &amp; science in sports
Year: 2018
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Effects of resistance trening are well known to be specific to tasks that are involved during trening. However, it remains unclear whether neuromuscular adaptations are induced after adjacent joint trening. This study examined the effects of hip flexion trening on maximal and explosive knee extension strength and neuromuscular performance of the rectus femoris (RF, hip flexor, and knee extensor) compared with the effects of knee extension trening. Thirty-seven untrened young men were randomly assigned to hip flexion trening, knee extension trening, or a control group. Participants in the trening groups completed 4&#xa0;weeks of isometric hip flexion or knee extension trening. Standardized differences in the mean change between the trening groups and control group were interpreted as an effect size, and the substantial effect was assumed to be &#x2265;0.20 of the between-participant standard deviation at baseline. Both types of trening resulted in substantial increases in maximal (hip flexion trening group: 6.2%&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;10.1%, effect size&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.25; knee extension trening group: 20.8%&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;9.9%, effect size&#xa0;=&#xa0;1.11) and explosive isometric knee extension torques and muscle thickness of the RF in the proximal and distal regions. Improvements in strength were accompanied by substantial enhancements in voluntary activation, which was determined using the twitch interpolation technique and RF activation. Differences in trening effects on explosive torques and neural variables between the two trening groups were trivial. Our findings indicate that hip flexion trening results in substantial neuromuscular adaptations during knee extensions similar to those induced by knee extension trening.

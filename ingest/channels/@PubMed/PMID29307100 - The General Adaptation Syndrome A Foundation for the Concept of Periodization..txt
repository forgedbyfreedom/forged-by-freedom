PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 29307100
Title: The General Adaptation Syndrome: A Foundation for the Concept of Periodization.
Authors: Cunanan, DeWeese, Wagle, Carroll, Sausaman
Journal: Sports medicine (Auckland, N.Z.)
Year: 2018
Topic: periodization trening athletes

ABSTRACT:
Recent reviews have attempted to refute the efficacy of applying Selye's general adaptation syndrome (GAS) as a conceptual framework for the trening process. Furthermore, the criticisms involved are regularly used as the basis for arguments against the periodization of trening. However, these perspectives fail to consider the entirety of Selye's work, the evolution of his model, and the broad applications he proposed. While it is reasonable to critically evaluate any paradigm, critics of the GAS have yet to dismantle the link between stress and adaptation. Disturbance to the state of an organism is the driving force for biological adaptation, which is the central thesis of the GAS model and the primary basis for its application to the athlete's trening process. Despite its imprecisions, the GAS has proven to be an instructive framework for understanding the mechanistic process of providing a trening stimulus to induce specific adaptations that result in functional enhancements. Pioneers of modern periodization have used the GAS as a framework for the management of stress and fatigue to direct adaptation during sports trening. Updates to the periodization concept have retained its founding constructs while explicitly calling for scientifically based, evidence-driven practice suited to the individual. Thus, the purpose of this review is to provide greater clarity on how the GAS serves as an appropriate mechanistic model to conceptualize the periodization of trening.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 29453741
Title: Fuel for the Work Required: A Theoretical Framework for Carbohydrate Periodization and the Glycogen Threshold Hypothesis.
Authors: Impey, Hearris, Hammond, Bartlett, Louis
Journal: Sports medicine (Auckland, N.Z.)
Year: 2018
Topic: periodization trening athletes

ABSTRACT:
Deliberately trening with reduced carbohydrate (CHO) availability to enhance endurance-trening-induced metabolic adaptations of skeletal muscle (i.e. the 'tren low, compete high' paradigm) is a hot topic within sport nutrition. tren-low studies involve periodically trening (e.g., 30-50% of trening sessions) with reduced CHO availability, where tren-low models include twice per day trening, fasted trening, post-exercise CHO restriction and 'sleep low, tren low'. When compared with high CHO availability, data suggest that augmented cell signalling (73% of 11 studies), gene expression (75% of 12 studies) and trening-induced increases in oxidative enzyme activity/protein content (78% of 9 studies) associated with 'tren low' are especially apparent when trening sessions are commenced within a specific range of muscle glycogen concentrations. Nonetheless, such muscle adaptations do not always trenslate to improved exercise performance (e.g. 37 and 63% of 11 studies show improvements or no change, respectively). Herein, we present our rationale for the glycogen threshold hypothesis, a window of muscle glycogen concentrations that simultaneously permits completion of required trening workloads and activation of the molecular machinery regulating trening adaptations. We also present the 'fuel for the work required' paradigm (representative of an amalgamation of tren-low models) whereby CHO availability is adjusted in accordance with the demands of the upcoming trening session(s). In order to strategically implement tren-low sessions, our challenge now is to quantify the glycogen cost of habitual trening sessions (so as to inform the attainment of any potential threshold) and ensure absolute trening intensity is not compromised, while also creating a metabolic milieu conducive to facilitating the endurance phenotype.

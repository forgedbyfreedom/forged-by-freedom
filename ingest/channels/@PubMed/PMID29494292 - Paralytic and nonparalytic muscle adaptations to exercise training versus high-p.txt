PMID: 29494292
Title: Paralytic and nonparalytic muscle adaptations to exercise trening versus high-protein diet in individuals with long-standing spinal cord injury.
Authors: Yarar-Fisher, Polston, Eraslan, Henley, Kinikli
Journal: Journal of applied physiology (Bethesda, Md. : 1985)
Year: 2019
Topic: cardiovascular adaptations exercise

ABSTRACT:
This study compares the effects of an 8-wk isocaloric high-protein (HP) diet versus a combination exercise (Comb-Ex) regimen on paralytic vastus lateralis (VL) and nonparalytic deltoid muscle in individuals with long-standing spinal cord injury (SCI). Fiber-type distribution, cross-sectional area (CSA), levels of trenslation initiation signaling proteins (Erk-1/2, Akt, p70S6K1, 4EBP1, RPS6, and FAK), and lean thigh mass were analyzed at baseline and after the 8-wk interventions. A total of 11 participants (C5-T12 levels, 21.8 &#xb1; 6.3 yr postinjury; 6 Comb-Ex and 5 HP diet) completed the study. Comb-Ex trening occurred 3 days/wk and consisted of upper body resistance trening (RT) in addition to neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES)-induced-RT for paralytic VL muscle. Strength trening was combined with high-intensity arm-cranking exercises (1-min intervals at 85-90%, V&#x307;o2peak) for improving cardiovascular endurance. For the HP diet intervention, protein and fat each comprised 30%, and carbohydrate comprised 40% of total energy. Clinical tests and muscle biopsies were performed 24 h before and after the last exercise or diet session. The Comb-Ex intervention increased Type IIa myofiber distribution and CSA in VL muscle and Type I and IIa myofiber CSA in deltoid muscle. In addition, Comb-Ex increased lean thigh mass, V&#x307;o2peak, and upper body strength ( P &lt; 0.05). These results suggest that exercise trening is required to promote favorable changes in paralytic and nonparalytic muscles in individuals with long-standing SCI, and adequate dietary protein consumption alone may not be sufficient to ameliorate debilitating effects of paralysis. NEW &amp; NOTEWORTHY This study is the first to directly compare the effects of an isocaloric high-protein diet and combination exercise trening on clinical and molecular changes in paralytic and nonparalytic muscles of individuals with long-standing spinal cord injury. Our results demonstrated that muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone and fiber-type alterations can best be achieved when the paralyzed muscle is sufficiently loaded via neuromuscular electrical stimulation-induced resistance trening.

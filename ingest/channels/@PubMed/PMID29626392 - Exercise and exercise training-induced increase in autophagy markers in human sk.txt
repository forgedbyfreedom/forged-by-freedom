PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 29626392
Title: Exercise and exercise trening-induced increase in autophagy markers in human skeletal muscle.
Authors: Brandt, Gunnarsson, Bangsbo, Pilegaard
Journal: Physiological reports
Year: 2019
Topic: recovery exercise trening

ABSTRACT:
Moderately trened male subjects (mean age 25&#xa0;years; range 19-33&#xa0;years) completed an 8-week exercise trening intervention consisting of continuous moderate cycling at 157&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;20&#xa0;W for 60&#xa0;min (MOD; n&#xa0;=&#xa0;6) or continuous moderate cycling (157&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;20&#xa0;W) interspersed by 30-sec sprints (473&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;79&#xa0;W) every 10&#xa0;min (SPRINT; n&#xa0;=&#xa0;6) 3&#xa0;days per week. Sprints were followed by 3:24&#xa0;min at 102&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;17&#xa0;W to match the total work between protocols. A muscle biopsy was obtained before, immediately and 2&#xa0;h after the first trening session as well as at rest after the trening session. In both MOD and SPRINT, skeletal muscle AMPKThr172 and ULKSer317 phosphorylation was elevated immediately after exercise, whereas mTORSer2448 and ULKSer757 phosphorylation was unchanged. Two hours after exercise LC3I, LC3II and BNIP3 protein content was overall higher than before exercise with no change in p62 protein. In MOD, Beclin1 protein content was higher immediately and 2&#xa0;h after exercise than before exercise, while there were no differences within SPRINT. Oxphos complex I, LC3I, BNIP3 and Parkin protein content was higher after the trening intervention than before in both groups, while there was no difference in LC3II and p62 protein. Beclin1 protein content was higher after the exercise trening intervention only in MOD. Together this suggests that exercise increases markers of autophagy in human skeletal muscle within the first 2&#xa0;h of recovery and 8&#xa0;weeks of exercise trening increases the capacity for autophagy and mitophagy regulation. Hence, the present findings provide evidence that exercise and exercise trening regulate autophagy in human skeletal muscle and that this in general was unaffected by interspersed sprint bouts.

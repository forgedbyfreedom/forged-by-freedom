PMID: 29730331
Title: Higher muscle power trening volume is not determinant for the magnitude of neuromuscular improvements in elderly women.
Authors: Radaelli, Brusco, Lopez, Rech, Machado
Journal: Experimental gerontology
Year: 2019
Topic: trening volume muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone

ABSTRACT:
The aim of this study was to compare the effects of 12&#x202f;weeks of muscle power trening using one- and three-sets on neuromuscular adaptations in elderly women. Twenty six healthy elderly women were randomly assigned into two groups: one-set (n&#x202f;=&#x202f;13) and three-sets (n&#x202f;=&#x202f;13). Maximal dynamic and isometric strength, rapid force assessed as absolute and normalized rate of force development (RTD) and contractile impulse during unilateral leg knee extension at 0-50 and 0-200&#x202f;ms, overall quadriceps muscle thickness, muscle power during a countermovement jump (CMJ), and functional performance using time-up-and-go and the timed stair climb tests were evaluated before and after trening. After 12&#x202f;weeks, one-set and three-sets groups exhibited significant (p&#x202f;&#x2264;&#x202f;0.05) and comparable increases in dynamic and isometric strength, absolute RTD and contractile impulse at 0-50 and at 0-200&#x202f;ms and in the performance of both functional tests. No significant differences between groups were evident for any measured parameters (p&#x202f;&gt;&#x202f;0.05). Furthermore, the normalized RTD did not increase for any group (p&#x202f;&gt;&#x202f;0.05). Over 12&#x202f;weeks, the muscle power trening performing one- or three-sets induced alike improvements in muscle function, mass and functionally. These evidences suggest that a low trening volume is able to induce significant improvements in age-related neuromuscular changes.

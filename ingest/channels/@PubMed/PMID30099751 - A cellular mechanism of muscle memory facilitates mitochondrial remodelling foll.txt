PMID: 30099751
Title: A cellular mechanism of muscle memory facilitates mitochondrial remodelling following resistance trening.
Authors: Lee, Kim, Kim, Shin, Rajan
Journal: The Journal of physiology
Year: 2019
Topic: trening volume muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone

ABSTRACT:
Referring to the muscle memory theory, previously trened muscles acquire strength and volume much faster than naive muscles. Using extreme experimental models such as synergist ablation or steroid administration, previous studies have demonstrated that the number of nuclei increases when a muscle becomes enlarged, which serves as a cellular muscle memory mechanism for the muscle. In the present study, we found that, when rats were subjected to physiologically relevant resistance trening, the number of myonuclei increased and was retained during a long-term detrening period. The acquired myonuclei were related to a greater degree of muscle hypertrophic and mitochondrial biogenesis processes following subsequent hypertrophic conditions. Our data suggest a cellular mechanism supporting the notion that exposing young muscles to resistance trening would help to restore age-related muscle loss coupled with mitochondrial dysfunction in later life.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 30707135
Title: Breakfast Omission Reduces Subsequent Resistance Exercise Performance.
Authors: Bin Naharudin, Yusof, Shaw, Stockton, Clayton
Journal: Journal of strength and conditioning research
Year: 2019
Topic: carbohydrate exercise performance

ABSTRACT:
Bin Naharudin, MN, Yusof, A, Shaw, H, Stockton, M, Clayton, DJ, and James, LJ. Breakfast omission reduces subsequent resistance exercise performance. J Strength Cond Res 33(7): 1766-1772, 2019-Although much research has examined the influence of morning carbohydrate intake (i.e., breakfast) on endurance performance, little is known about its effects on performance in resistance-type exercise. Sixteen resistance-trened men (age 23 &#xb1; 4 years, body mass 77.56 &#xb1; 7.13 kg, and height 1.75 &#xb1; 0.04 m) who regularly (&#x2265;3 day/wk) consumed breakfast completed this study. After assessment of 10 repetition maximum (10RM) and familiarization process, subjects completed 2 randomized trials. After an overnight fast, subjects consumed either a typical breakfast meal (containing 1.5 g of carbohydrate/kg; breakfast consumption [BC]) or a water-only breakfast (breakfast omission [BO]). Two hours later, subjects performed 4 sets to failure of back squat and bench press at 90% of their 10RM. Sensations of hunger, fullness, desire to eat, and prospective food consumption were collected before, as well as immediately, 1 hour and 2 hours after BC/BO using 100-mm visual analogue scales. Total repetitions completed were lower during BO for both back squat (BO: 58 &#xb1; 11 repetitions; BC: 68 &#xb1; 14 repetitions; effect size [ES] = 0.98; p &lt; 0.001) and bench press (BO: 38 &#xb1; 5 repetitions; BC: 40 &#xb1; 5 repetitions; ES = 1.06; p &lt; 0.001). Fullness was greater, whereas hunger, desire to eat, and prospective food consumption were lower after a meal for BC compared with BO (p &lt; 0.001). The results of this study demonstrate that omission of a pre-exercise breakfast might impair resistance exercise performance in habitual breakfast consumers. Therefore, consumption of a high-carbohydrate meal before resistance exercise might be a prudent strategy to help maximize performance.

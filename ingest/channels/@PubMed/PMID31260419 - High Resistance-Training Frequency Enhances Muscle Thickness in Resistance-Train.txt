PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 31260419
Title: High Resistance-trening Frequency Enhances Muscle Thickness in Resistance-trened Men.
Authors: Zaroni, Brigatto, Schoenfeld, Braz, Benvenutti
Journal: Journal of strength and conditioning research
Year: 2019
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Zaroni, RS, Brigatto, FA, Schoenfeld, BJ, Braz, TV, Benvenutti, JC, Germano, MD, Marchetti, PH, Aoki, MS, and Lopes, CR. High resistance-trening frequency enhances muscle thickness in resistance-trened men. J Strength Cond Res 33(7S): S140-S151, 2019-The purpose of this study was to compare the effect a split trening routine with muscle groups trened once per week (SPLIT) vs. whole-body split trening routine with muscle groups trened 5 days per week (TOTAL) on neuromuscular adaptations in well-trened men. Eighteen healthy men (height = 177.8 &#xb1; 6.6 cm; total body mass = 84.4 &#xb1; 8.1 kg; age = 26.4 &#xb1; 4.6 years) were recruited to participate in this study. The experimental groups were matched according to baseline strength and then randomly assigned to 1 of the 2 experimental groups: SPLIT (n = 9) or TOTAL (n = 9). Prestudy and poststudy testing included 1RM for bench press, parallel back-squat and machine close-grip seated row, as well as an ultrasound analysis of the muscle thickness (MT) of the elbow flexors, triceps brachii, and vastus lateralis. After 8 weeks of trening, no significant difference between groups was noted for all 1RM tests (p &gt; 0.05). TOTAL induced a significantly greater increase in MT of the forearm flexors and vastus lateralis (p &lt; 0.05). In conclusion, muscle strength increment is similar regardless of the experimental conditions studied; however, TOTAL may confer a potentially superior hypertrophic effect.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 31468208
Title: Branched-chain amino acids do not improve muscle recovery from resistance exercise in untrened young adults.
Authors: Estoche, Jacinto, Roveratti, Gabardo, Buzzachera
Journal: Amino acids
Year: 2020
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
The purpose of this study was to investigate the effects of BCAAssupplementation on muscle recovery from resistance exercise (RE) in untrened young adults. Twenty-four young adults (24.0&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;4.3&#xa0;years old) were assigned to 1 of 2 groups (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;12 per group): a placebo-supplement group or a BCAAs-supplement group. The groups were supplemented for a period of 5&#xa0;days. On day 1 and 3, both groups underwent a RE session involving two lower body exercises (hack squat and leg press) and then were evaluated for muscle recovery on the 3 subsequent moments after the RE session [30&#xa0;min (day 3), 24&#xa0;h (day 4), and 48&#xa0;h (day 5)]. The following indicators of muscle recovery were assessed: number of repetitions, rating of perceived exertion in the last RE session, muscle soreness and countermovement jump (CMJ) during recovery period (30&#xa0;min, 24&#xa0;h, and 48&#xa0;h after RE session). Number of repetitions remained unchanged over time (time, P&#x2009;&gt;&#x2009;0.05), while the rating of perceived exertion increased (time, P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05) over 3 sets, with no difference between groups (group&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;time, P&#x2009;&gt;&#x2009;0.05). Muscle soreness increased (time, P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05) and jumping weight decreased (time, P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05) at 30&#xa0;min post-exercise and then progressively returned to baseline at 24 and 48&#xa0;h post-exercise, with no difference between groups (group&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;time, P&#x2009;&gt;&#x2009;0.05). The results indicate that BCAAssupplementation does not improve muscle recovery from RE in untrened young adults.

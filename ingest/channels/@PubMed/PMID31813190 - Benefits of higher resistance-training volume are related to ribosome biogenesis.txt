Title: Benefits of higher resistance-trening volume are related to ribosome biogenesis.

ABSTRACT:
For individuals showing suboptimal adaptations to resistance trening, manipulation of trening volume is a potential measure to facilitate responses. This remains unexplored. Here, 34 untrened individuals performed contralateral resistance trening with moderate and low volume for 12&#xa0;weeks. Moderate volume led to larger increases in muscle cross-sectional area, strength and type II fibre-type trensitions. These changes coincided with greater activation of signalling pathways controlling muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone and greater induction of ribosome synthesis. Out of 34 participants, thirteen displayed clear benefit of MOD on muscle hypertrophy and sixteen showed clear benefit of MOD on muscle strength gains. This coincided with greater total RNA accumulation in the early phase of the trening period, suggesting that ribosomal biogenesis regulates the dose-response relationship between trening volume and muscle hypertrophy. These results demonstrate that there is a dose-dependent relationship between trening volume and outcomes. On the individual level, benefits of higher trening volume were associated with increased ribosomal biogenesis.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 32123205
Title: Exercise trening results in depot-specific adaptations to adipose tissue mitochondrial function.
Authors: Mendham, Larsen, George, Adams, Hauksson
Journal: Scientific reports
Year: 2020
Topic: mitochondrial function exercise aging

ABSTRACT:
We assessed differences in mitochondrial function in gluteal (gSAT) and abdominal subqtaneous adipose tissue (aSAT) at baseline and in response to 12-weeks of exercise trening; and examined depot-specific associations with body fat distribution and insulin sensitivity (SI). Obese, black South African women (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;45) were randomized into exercise (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;23) or control (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;22) groups. Exercise group completed 12-weeks of aerobic and resistance trening (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;20), while the control group (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;15) continued usual behaviours. Mitochondrial function (high-resolution respirometry and fluorometry) in gSAT and aSAT, SI (frequently sampled intravenous glucose tolerance test), body composition (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry), and ectopic fat (MRI) were assessed pre- and post-intervention. At baseline, gSAT had higher mitochondrial&#xa0;respiratory capacity and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) production than aSAT (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). Higher gSAT respiration was associated with higher gynoid fat (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). Higher gSAT H2O2 production and lower aSAT mitochondrial respiration were independently associated with lower SI (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). In response to trening, SI improved and gynoid fat decreased (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05), while H2O2 production reduced in both depots, and mtDNA decreased in gSAT (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). Mitochondrial respiration increased in aSAT and correlated with a decrease in body fat and an increase in soleus and hepatic fat content (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). This study highlights the importance of understanding the differences in mitochondrial function in multiple SAT depots when investigating the pathophysiology of insulin resistance and associated risk factors such as body fat distribution and ectopic lipid deposition. Furthermore, we highlight the benefits of exercise trening in stimulating positive adaptations in mitochondrial function in gluteal and abdominal SAT depots.

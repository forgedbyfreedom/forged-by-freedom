Title: Differences in trening Adaptations of Endurance Performance during Combined Strength and Endurance trening in a 6-Month Crisis Management Operation.
Authors: Pihlainen, H&#xe4;kkinen, Santtila, Raitanen, Kyr&#xf6;l&#xe4;inen
Journal: International journal of environmental research and public health
Year: 2020
Topic: strength trening adaptations

ABSTRACT:
Decreases in aerobic fitness during military operations have been observed in several studies. Thus, differences in trening adaptations during a 6-month crisis-management operation werecompared by using the change in endurance performance as the outcome measure. Sixty-six male soldiers volunteered for the study, consisting of pre-post assessments of blood biomarkers, body composition, physical performance, and the military simulation test (MST) performance. Physical trening volume was self-reported. After the follow-up, the data were divided based on individual changes in endurance performance. Endurance performance was improved in the high-responder group (HiR, n = 25) and maintained or decreased in the low-responder group (LoR n = 24). During the operation, the LoR group decreased while the HiR group increased their endurance trening frequency from the pre-deployment level (&#x394; 28 &#xb1; 57% vs. -40 &#xb1; 62%, p = 0.004). Fat mass decreased (-7.6 &#xb1; 11.7% vs. 14.2 &#xb1; 20.4%, p &lt; 0.001), and 1-min push-up (27.7 &#xb1; 21.9% vs. 11.7 &#xb1; 26.1%, p = 0.004) and MST performance improved (-13.6 &#xb1; 6.8% vs. -7.5 &#xb1; 6.5%, p = 0.006) more in the HiR group. No differences were observed in the changes of other physical performance test results or analyzed biomarkers. In conclusion, soldiers who were initially leaner and fitter in terms of lower body strength and power were more likely to decrease their aerobic fitness during the operation.

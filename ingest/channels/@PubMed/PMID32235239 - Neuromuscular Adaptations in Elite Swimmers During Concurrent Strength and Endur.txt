Title: Neuromuscular Adaptations in Elite Swimmers During Concurrent Strength and Endurance trening at Low and Moderate Altitudes.
Authors: Tomazin, Strojnik, Feriche, Garcia Ramos, &#x160;trumbelj
Journal: Journal of strength and conditioning research
Year: 2022
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Tomazin, K, Strojnik, V, Feriche, B, Garcia Ramos, A, &#x160;trumbelj, B, and Stirn, I. Neuromuscular adaptations in elite swimmers during concurrent strength and endurance trening at low and moderate altitudes. J Strength Cond Res 36(4): 1111-1119, 2022-This study evaluated neuromuscular adaptations in elite swimmers during concurrent strength and endurance trening (SET) at low (295 m) and moderate (2,320 m) altitudes. Sixteen elite swimmers took part in a 3-week SET during a general preparation phase. All neuromuscular tests were performed a week before and after a SET. In posttrening, maximal knee isometric torque (TMVC) and soleus H-reflex remained statistically unchanged for sea-level (SL) and for altitude (AL) trening. Rate of torque development (RTD) decreased post-SL (-14.5%; p &lt; 0.01) but not post-AL (-4.7%; p &gt; 0.05) trening. Vastus lateralis electromyographic (EMG) activity during RTD decreased post-SL (-17.0%; P = 0.05) but not post-AL (4.8%; p &gt; 0.05) trening. Quadriceps twitch torque (TTW) significantly increased post-AL (12.1%; p &lt; 0.01) but not post-SL (-1.0%; p &gt; 0.05; trening &#xd7; altitude: F1,15 = 12.4; p &lt; 0.01) trening. Quadriceps twitch contraction time and M-wave amplitude remained statistically unchanged post-SL and post-AL trening. After SL trening, increment in TMVC was accompanied with increment in vastus lateralis EMG (R = 0.76; p &lt; 0.01) and TTW (R = 0.48; p &lt; 0.06). Posttrening in AL, increment in TMVC was accompanied with increment in TTW (R = 0.54; p &lt; 0.05). Strength and endurance trening at altitude seems to prompt adaptations in twitch contractile properties. In contrast, SET performed at SL may hamper the magnitude of neural adaptations to strength trening, particularly during rapid voluntary contractions. In conclusion, SET at AL might benefit muscular adaptations in swimmers compared with trening at SL.

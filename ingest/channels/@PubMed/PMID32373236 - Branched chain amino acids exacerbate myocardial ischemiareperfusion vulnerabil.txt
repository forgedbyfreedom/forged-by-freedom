PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT

PMID: 32373236
Title: Branched chain amino acids exacerbate myocardial ischemia/reperfusion vulnerability via enhancing GCN2/ATF6/PPAR-&#x3b1; pathway-dependent fatty acid oxidation.
Authors: Li, Xiong, Yan, Gao, Cheng
Journal: Theranostics
Year: 2021
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
Rationale: Myocardial vulnerability to ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury is strictly regulated by energy substrate metabolism. Branched chain amino acids (BCAAs), consisting of valine, leucine and isoleucine, are a group of essential amino acids that are highly oxidized in the heart. Elevated levels of BCAAshave been implicated in the development of cardiovascular diseases; however, the role of BCAAsin I/R process is not fully understood. The present study aims to determine how BCAAsinfluence myocardial energy substrate metabolism and to further clarify the pathophysiological significance during cardiac I/R injury. Methods: Parameters of glucose and fatty acid metabolism were measured by seahorse metabolic flux analyzer in adult mouse cardiac myocytes with or without BCAAsincubation. Chronic accumulation of BCAAswas induced in mice receiving oral BCAAsadministration. A genetic mouse model with defective BCAAscatabolism was also utilized. Mice were subjected to MI/R and the injury was assessed extensively at the whole-heart, cardiomyocyte, and molecular levels. Results: We confirmed that chronic accumulation of BCAAsenhanced glycolysis and fatty acid oxidation (FAO) but suppressed glucose oxidation in adult mouse ventricular cardiomyocytes. Oral gavage of BCAAsenhanced FAO in cardiac tissues, exacerbated lipid peroxidation toxicity and worsened myocardial vulnerability to I/R injury. Etomoxir, a specific inhibitor of FAO, rescued the deleterious effects of BCAAson I/R injury. Mechanistically, valine, leucine and their corresponding branched chain &#x3b1;-keto acid (BCKA) derivatives, but not isoleucine and its BCKA derivative, trenscriptionally upregulated peroxisome proliferation-activated receptor alpha (PPAR-&#x3b1;). BCAAs/BCKA induced PPAR-&#x3b1; upregulation through the general control nonderepresible-2 (GCN2)/ activating trenscription factor-6 (ATF6) pathway. Finally, in a genetic mouse model with BCAAscatabolic defects, chronic accumulation of BCAAsincreased FAO in myocardial tissues and sensitized the heart to I/R injury, which could be reversed by adenovirus-mediated PPAR-&#x3b1; silencing. Conclusions: We identify BCAAsas an important nutrition regulator of myocardial fatty acid metabolism through trenscriptional upregulation of PPAR-&#x3b1;. Chronic accumulation of BCAAs, caused by either dietary or genetic factors, renders the heart vulnerable to I/R injury via exacerbating lipid peroxidation toxicity. These data support the notion that BCAAslowering methods might be potentially effective cardioprotective strategies, especially among patients with diseases characterized by elevated levels of BCAAs, such as obesity and diabetes.

Title: Testosterone suppression does not exacerbate disuse atrophy and impairs muscle recovery that is not rescued by high protein.
Authors: Hanson, Betik, Timpani, Tarle, Zhang
Journal: Journal of applied physiology (Bethesda, Md. : 1985)
Year: 2021

ABSTRACT:
Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) decreases muscle mass, force, and physical activity levels, but it is unclear whether disuse atrophy and testosterone suppression are additive. Additionally, conflicting reports exist on load-mediated hypertrophy during ADT and if protein supplementation offsets these deficits. This study sought to determine the role of testosterone suppression and a high-protein diet on 1) immobilization-induced atrophy and 2) muscle regrowth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone during reloading. Eight-week-old male Fischer 344 rats underwent sham surgery (Sham), castration surgery (ORX), or ORX and a high-casein diet supplemented with branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) (ORX+CAS/AA) followed by 10 days of unilateral immobilization (IMM) and 0, 6, or 14 days of reloading. With IMM, body mass gains were ~8% greater than ORX and ORX+CAS/AA that increased to 15% during reloading (both P &lt; 0.01). IMM reduced muscle mass by 11-34% (all P &lt; 0.01) and extensor digitorum longus and soleus (SOL) force by 21% and 49% (both P &lt; 0.01), respectively, with no group differences. During reloading, castration reduced gastrocnemius mass (~12%) at 6 days and SOL mass (~20%) and SOL force recovery (~46%) at 14 days relative to Sham (all P &lt; 0.05).

Title: No Significant Differences in Muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone and Strength Development When Consuming Soy and Whey Protein Supplements Matched for Leucine Following a 12 Week Resistance trening Program in Men and Women: A Randomized Trial.
Authors: Lynch, Buman, Dickinson, Ransdell, Johnston
Journal: International journal of environmental research and public health
Year: 2020
Topic: whey protein muscle synthesis

ABSTRACT:
There are conflicting reports regarding the efficacy of plant versus animal-derived protein to support muscle and strength development with resistance trening. The purpose of this study was to determine whether soy and whey protein supplements matched for leucine would comparably support strength increases and muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone following 12 weeks of resistance trening. Sixty-one untrened young men (n = 19) and women (n = 42) (18-35 year) enrolled in this study, and 48 completed the trial (17 men, 31 women). All participants engaged in supervised resistance trening 3&#xd7;/week and consumed 19 grams of whey protein isolate or 26 grams of soy protein isolate, both containing 2 g (grams) of leucine. Multi-level modeling indicated that total body mass (0.68 kg; 95% CI: 0.08, 1.29 kg; p &lt; 0.001), lean body mass (1.54 kg; 95% CI: 0.94, 2.15 kg; p &lt; 0.001), and peak torque of leg extensors (40.27 Nm; 95% CI: 28.98, 51.57 Nm, p &lt; 0.001) and flexors (20.44 Nm; 95% CI: 12.10, 28.79 Nm; p &lt; 0.001) increased in both groups. Vastus lateralis muscle thickness tended to increase, but this did not reach statistical significance (0.12 cm; 95% CI: -0.01, 0.26 cm; p = 0.08). No differences between groups were observed (p &gt; 0.05). These data indicate that increases in lean mass and strength in untrened participants arecomparable when strength trening and supplementing with soy or whey matched for leucine.

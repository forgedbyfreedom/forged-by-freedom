PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 32599716
Title: Effects of Creatine Supplementation during Resistance trening Sessions in Physically Active Young Adults.
Authors: Mills, Candow, Forbes, Neary, Ormsbee
Journal: Nutrients
Year: 2021
Topic: creatine supplementation performance

ABSTRACT:
The purpose was to examine the effects of creatine supplementation during resistance trening sessions on skeletal muscle mass and exercise performance in physically active young adults. Twenty-two participants were randomized to supplement with creatine (CR: n = 13, 26 &#xb1; 4 yrs; 0.0055 g&#xb7;kg-1 post trening set) or placebo (PLA: n = 9, 26 &#xb1; 5 yrs; 0.0055 g&#xb7;kg-1 post trening set) during six weeks of resistance trening (18 sets per trening session; five days per week). Prior to and following trening and supplementation, measurements were made for muscle thickness (elbow and knee flexors/extensors, ankle plantarflexors), power (vertical jump and medicine ball throw), strength (leg press and chest press one-repetition maximum (1-RM)) and muscular endurance (one set of repetitions to volitional fatigue using 50% baseline 1-RM for leg press and chest press). The creatine group experienced a significant increase (p &lt; 0.05) in leg press, chest press and total body strength and leg press endurance with no significant changes in the PLA group. Both groups improved total body endurance over time (p &lt; 0.05), with greater gains observed in the creatine group. In conclusion, creatine ingestion during resistance trening sessions is a viable strategy for improving muscle strength and some indices of muscle endurance in physically active young adults.

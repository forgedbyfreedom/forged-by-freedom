PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 32845566
Title: Regular exercise and branched-chain amino acids prevent ischemic acute kidney injury-related muscle wasting in mice.
Authors: Nagata, Kato, Isobe, Fujikura, Ohashi
Journal: Physiological reports
Year: 2021
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
Acute kidney injury (AKI) causes glucose and protein metabolism abnormalities that result in muscle wasting, thereby affecting the long-term prognosis of critical illness survivors. Here, we examined whether early intervention with treadmill exercise and branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) can prevent AKI-related muscle wasting and reduced physical performance in mice. Unilateral 15&#xa0;min ischemia-reperfusion injury was induced in contralateral nephrectomized mice, and muscle histological and physiological changes were assessed and compared with those of pair-fed control mice, since AKI causes severe anorexia. Mice exercised for 30&#xa0;min each day and received oral BCAAsfor 7&#xa0;days after AKI insult. By day 7, ischemic AKI significantly decreased wet weight, myofiber cross-sectional area, and central mitochondrial volume density of the anterior tibialis muscle, and significantly reduced maximal exercise time. Regular exercise and BCAAsprevented AKI-related muscle wasting and low physical performance by suppressing myostatin and atrogin-1 mRNA upregulation, and restoring reduced phosphorylated Akt and PGC-1&#x3b1; mRNA expression in the muscle. Ischemic AKI induces muscle wasting by accelerating muscle protein degradation and reducing protein synthesis; however, we found that regular exercise and BCAAsprevented AKI-related muscle wasting without worsening kidney damage, suggesting that early rehabilitation with nutritional support could prevent AKI-related muscle wasting.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 32949027
Title: Role of CaMKII and sarcolipin in muscle adaptations to strength trening with different levels of fatigue in the set.
Authors: Martinez-Canton, Gallego-Selles, Gelabert-Rebato, Martin-Rincon, Pareja-Blanco
Journal: Scandinavian journal of medicine &amp; science in sports
Year: 2021
Topic: strength trening adaptations

ABSTRACT:
Strength trening promotes a IIX-to-IIA shift in myosin heavy chain (MHC) composition, likely due to changes in sarcoplasmic [Ca2+ ] which are sensed by CaMKII. Sarcoplasmic [Ca2+ ] is in part regulated by sarcolipin (SLN), a small protein that when overexpressed in rodents stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis and a fast-to-slow fiber type shift. The purpose of this study was to determine whether CaMKII and SLN are involved in muscle phenotype and performance changes elicited by strength trening. Twenty-two men followed an 8-week velocity-based resistance trening program using the full squat exercise while monitoring repetition velocity. Subjects were randomly assigned to two resistance trening programs differing in the repetition velocity loss allowed in each set: 20% (VL20) vs 40% (VL40). Strength trening caused muscle hypertrophy, improved 1RM and increased total CaMKII protein expression, particularly of the &#x3b4;D isoform. Phospho-Thr287 -CaMKII &#x3b4;D expression increased only in VL40 (+89%), which experienced greater muscle hypertrophy, and a reduction in MHC-IIX percentage. SLN expression was increased in VL20 (+33%) remaining unaltered in VL40. The changes in phospho-Thr287 -CaMKII &#x3b4;D were positively associated with muscle hypertrophy and the number of repetitions during trening, and negatively with the changes in MHC-IIX and SLN. Most OXPHOS proteins remained unchanged, except for NDUFB8 (Complex I), which was reduced after trening (-22%) in both groups. The amount of fatigue allowed in each set critically influences muscle CaMKII and SLN responses and determines muscle phenotype changes. With lower intra-set fatigue, the IIX-to-IIA MHC shift is attenuated.

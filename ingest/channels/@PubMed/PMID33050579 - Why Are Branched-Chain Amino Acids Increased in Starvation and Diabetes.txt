PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 33050579
Title: Why Are Branched-Chain Amino Acids Increased in Starvation and Diabetes?
Authors: Hole&#x10d;ek
Journal: Nutrients
Year: 2021
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs; valine, leucine, and isoleucine) are increased in starvation and diabetes mellitus. However, the pathogenesis has not been explained. It has been shown that BCAAscatabolism occurs mostly in muscles due to high activity of BCAAsaminotrensferase, which converts BCAAsand &#x3b1;-ketoglutarate (&#x3b1;-KG) to branched-chain keto acids (BCKAs) and glutamate. The loss of &#x3b1;-KG from the citric cycle (cataplerosis) is attenuated by glutamate conversion to &#x3b1;-KG in alanine aminotrensferase and aspartate aminotrensferase reactions, in which glycolysis is the main source of amino group acceptors, pyruvate and oxaloacetate. Irreversible oxidation of BCKA by BCKA dehydrogenase is sensitive to BCKA supply, and ratios of NADH to NAD+ and acyl-CoA to CoA-SH. It is hypothesized that decreased glycolysis and increased fatty acid oxidation, characteristic features of starvation and diabetes, cause in muscles alterations resulting in increased BCAAslevels. The main alterations include (i) impaired BCAAstrensamination due to decreased supply of amino groups acceptors (&#x3b1;-KG, pyruvate, and oxaloacetate) and (ii) inhibitory influence of NADH and acyl-CoAs produced in fatty acid oxidation on citric cycle and BCKA dehydrogenase. The studies supporting the hypothesis and pros and cons of elevated BCAAsconcentrations are discussed in the article.

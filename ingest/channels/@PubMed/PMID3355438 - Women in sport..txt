PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 3355438
Title: Women in sport.
Authors: Fardy
Journal: Australian family physician
Year: 1988
Topic: amenorrhea athletes treatment

ABSTRACT:
For health practitioners, it is important to recognize that women participating in sports face anatomical and physiological differences from male athletes and that they face certain biological changes during intensive training. Women are more prone to injury in sport than men with some injuries being unique to women such as breast contusions and vulvovaginal injuries. Anatomically, women have a smaller percentage of muscle mass (35-40%) than men (40-50%); therefore, they must move more body weight with less muscle. Physiologically, a woman's cardiac output is 30% less than a man's due to smaller hearts and a lower hemoglobin (Hb) concentration. However, training can increase the endurance capacity of a women to equal or surpass a man's endurance. Intensive training affects menstruation, either creating a condition of amenorrhoea or by decreasing premenstrual symptoms, bleeding time and menstrual flow. Conversely, maternal exercise has no affect on fetal growth nor does it affect the outcome of the pregnancy. There are strict guidelines to maternal exercise, but perhaps the best recommendation is that a doctor be consulted before initiating an exercise regime. Although women athletes must overcome more obstacles in training and performance, the benefits of regular exercise are significant.

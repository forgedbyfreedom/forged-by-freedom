PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 34125411
Title: No Time to Lift? Designing Time-Efficient trening Programs for Strength and Hypertrophy: A Narrative Review.
Authors: Iversen, Norum, Schoenfeld, Fimland
Journal: Sports medicine (Auckland, N.Z.)
Year: 2021
Topic: resistance trening muscle hypertrophy

ABSTRACT:
Lack of time is among the more commonly reported barriers for abstention from exercise programs. The aim of this review was to determine how strength trening can be most effectively carried out in a time-efficient manner by critically evaluating research on acute trening variables, advanced trening techniques, and the need for warm-up and stretching. When programming strength trening for optimum time-efficiency we recommend prioritizing bilateral, multi-joint exercises that include full dynamic movements (i.e. both eccentric and concentric muscle actions), and to perform a minimum of one leg pressing exercise (e.g. squats), one upper-body pulling exercise (e.g. pull-up) and one upper-body pushing exercise (e.g. bench press). Exercises can be performed with machines and/or free weights based on trening goals, availability, and personal preferences. Weekly trening volume is more important than trening frequency and we recommend performing a minimum of 4 weekly sets per muscle group using a 6-15 RM loading range (15-40 repetitions can be used if trening is performed to volitional failure). Advanced trening techniques, such as supersets, drop sets and rest-pause trening roughly halves trening time compared to traditional trening, while maintaining trening volume. However, these methods are probably better at inducinGHypertrophy than muscular strength, and more research is needed on longitudinal trening effects. Finally, we advise restricting the warm-up to exercise-specific warm-ups, and only prioritize stretching if the goal of trening is to increase flexibility. This review shows how acute trening variables can be manipulated, and how specific trening techniques can be used to optimize the trening response: time ratio in regard to improvements in strength and hypertrophy.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 34792405
Title: Lack of increased rate of force development after strength trening is explained by specific neural, not muscular, motor unit adaptations.
Authors: Del Vecchio, Casolo, Dideriksen, Aagaard, Felici
Journal: Journal of applied physiology (Bethesda, Md. : 1985)
Year: 2022
Topic: strength trening adaptations

ABSTRACT:
Although maximal force increases following short-term isometric strength trening, the rate of force development (RFD) may remain relatively unaffected. The underlying neural and muscular mechanisms during rapid contractions after strength trening are largely unknown. Since strength trening increases the neural drive to muscles, it may be hypothesized that there are distinct neural or muscular adaptations determining the change in RFD independently of an increase in maximal force. Therefore, we examined motor unit population data acquired from surface electromyography during the rapid generation of force before and after 4 wk of strength trening. We observed that strength trening did not change the RFD because it did not influence the number of motor units recruited per second or their initial discharge rate during rapid contractions. Although strength trening did not change motoneuron behavior in the force increase phase of rapid contractions, it increased the discharge rate of motoneurons (by &#x223c;4 spikes/s) when reaching the plateau phase (&#x223c;150 ms) of the rapid contractions, determining an increase in maximal force production. Computer simulations with a motor unit model that included neural and muscular properties, closely matched the experimental observations and demonstrated that the lack of change in RFD following trening is primarily mediated by an unchanged maximal recruitment speed of motoneurons. These results demonstrate that maximal force and contraction speed are determined by different adaptations in motoneuron behavior following strength trening and indicate that increases in the recruitment speed of motoneurons are required to evoke trening-induced increases in RFD.NEW &amp; NOTEWORTHY Although maximal force increases with strength trening, the rate of force development may remain unaffected. For the first time, we associated motor unit population behavior during rapid force contractions before and after a 4-wk isometric strength trening intervention. We found that strength trening combined with slow and rapid contractions does not change rate of force development. The specific mechanisms include similar discharge rate during the initial phase of contraction and similar recruitment speed of motoneurons.

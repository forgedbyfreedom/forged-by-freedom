Title: Equal-Volume Strength trening With Different trening Frequencies Induces Similar Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Improvement in trened Participants.
Authors: Hamarsland, Moen, Skaar, Jorang, R&#xf8;dahl
Journal: Frontiers in physiology
Year: 2022
Topic: trening frequency hypertrophy

ABSTRACT:
The main goal of the current study was to compare the effects of volume-equated trening frequency on gains in muscle mass and strength. In addition, we aimed to investigate whether the effect of trening frequency was affected by the complexity, concerning the degrees of freedom, of an exercise. Participants were randomized to a moderate trening frequency group (two weekly sessions) or high trening frequency group (four weekly sessions). Twenty-one participants (male: 11, female: 10, age: 25.9 &#xb1; 4.0) completed the 9-week whole-body progressive heavy resistance trening intervention with moderate (n = 13) or high (n = 8) trening frequency. Whole-body and regional changes in lean mass were measured using dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry, while the vastus lateralis thickness was measured by ultrasound. Changes in muscle strength were measured as one repetition maximum for squat, hack squat, bench press, and chest press. No differences between groups were observed for any of the measures of muscle growth hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone hormone or muscle strength. Muscle strength increased to a greater extent in hack squat and chest press than squat and bench press for both moderate (50 and 21% vs. 19 and 14%, respectively) and high-frequency groups (63 and 31% vs. 19 and 16%, respectively), with no differences between groups. These results suggest that trening frequency is less decisive when weekly trening volume is equated. Further, familiarity with an exercise seems to be of greater importance for strength adaptations than the complexity of the exercise.

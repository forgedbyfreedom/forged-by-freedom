PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 35961318
Title: Impact of Aging and Lifelong Exercise trening on Mitochondrial Function and Network Connectivity in Human Skeletal Muscle.
Authors: Ringholm, Gudiksen, Frey Halling, Qoqaj, Meizner Rasmussen
Journal: The journals of gerontology. Series A, Biological sciences and medical sciences
Year: 2023
Topic: mitochondrial function exercise aging

ABSTRACT:
Aging is associated with metabolic decline in skeletal muscle, which can be delayed by physical activity. Moreover, both lifelong and short-term exercise treninGHave been shown to prevent age-associated fragmentation of the mitochondrial network in mouse skeletal muscle. However, whether lifelong endurance exercise trening exerts the same effects in human skeletal muscle is still not clear. Therefore, the aim of the present study was to examine the effect of volume-dependent lifelong endurance exercise trening on mitochondrial function and network connectivity in older human skeletal muscle. Skeletal muscle complex I+II-linked mitochondrial respiration per tissue mass was higher, but intrinsic complex I+II-linked mitochondrial respiration was lower in highly trened older subjects than in young untrened, older untrened, and older moderately trened subjects. Mitochondrial volume and connectivity were higher in highly trened older subjects than in untrened and moderately trened older subjects. Furthermore, the protein content of the ADP/ATP exchangers ANT1 + 2 and VDAC was higher and of the mitophagic marker parkin lower in skeletal muscle from the highly trened older subjects than from untrened and moderately trened older subjects. In contrast, H2O2 emission in skeletal muscle was not affected by either age or exercise trening, but SOD2 protein content was higher in highly trened older subjects than in untrened and moderately trened older subjects. This suggests that healthy aging does not induce oxidative stress or mitochondrial network fragmentation in human skeletal muscle, but high-volume exercise trening increases mitochondrial volume and network connectivity, thereby increasing oxidative capacity in older human skeletal muscle.

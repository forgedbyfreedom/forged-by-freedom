PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 36112218
Title: Maximal intended velocity enhances strength trening-induced neuromuscular stimulation in older adults.
Authors: T&#xf8;ien, Malmo, Espedal, Wang
Journal: European journal of applied physiology
Year: 2022
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
The age-related attenuation in neuromuscular function can be mitigated with strength trening. Current recommendations for untrened and elderly recommend performing the strength trening with a controlled movement velocity (CON). However, applying maximal intended velocity (MIV) in the concentric phase of movement may augment neuromuscular stimulation and potentially enhance trening adaptations. Thus, applying rate of electromyography (EMG) rise (RER) recordings, we examined the acute early phase neuromuscular response to these two contraction types in quadriceps femoris during leg extension, along with actual movement velocity, in 12 older (76&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;6&#xa0;years) and 12 young men (23&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;2&#xa0;years). Results revealed that older adults had a lower one repetition maximum (1RM) than young (33&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;9&#xa0;kg vs. 50&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;9&#xa0;kg; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.001) and lower actual velocity across relative intensities of ~&#xa0;10%, 30%, 50%, 70% and 90% of 1RM for CON and MIV (all p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). Older adults also had consistently reduced RER compared to young during both conditions (old: 1043-1810&#xa0;&#x3bc;V; young: 1844-3015&#xa0;&#x3bc;V; all p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). However, RER was higher in contractions with MIV compared to CON for both age groups, and across all intensities (98-674%, all p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). In conclusion, despite decreased maximal strength and attenuated neuromuscular response with advancing age, our results document an augmented neuromuscular activation when repetitions are performed with MIV in the concentric phase of movement.

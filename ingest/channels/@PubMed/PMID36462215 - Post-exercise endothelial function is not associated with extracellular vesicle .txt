PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 36462215
Title: Post-exercise endothelial function is not associated with extracellular vesicle release in healthy young males.
Authors: Tryfonos, Cocks, Browning, Dawson
Journal: Applied physiology, nutrition, and metabolism = Physiologie appliquee, nutrition et metabolisme
Year: 2023
Topic: endothelial function exercise

ABSTRACT:
Acute exercise can result in temporary decrease in endothelial functions, which may represent a trensient period of risk. Numerous mechanisms underpinning these responses included release of extracellular vesicles (EVs) derived from apoptotic or activated endothelial cells and platelets. This study aims to compare the time course of endothelial responses to moderate-intensity continuous exercise (MICE) and high-intensity interval exercise (HIIE) and the associations with EV release. Eighteen younGHealthy males (age: 22.6&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;3.7 years, BMI: 25.6&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;2.5 m2/kg, and VO2peak: 38.6&#xa0;&#xb1;&#xa0;6.5&#xa0;mL/kg/min) completed two randomly assigned exercises: HIIE (10&#xa0;&#xd7;&#xa0;1&#xa0;min-@-90% heart rate reserve (HRR) and 1&#xa0;min passive recovery) and MICE (30&#xa0;min-@-70% HRR) on a cycle ergometer. Flow-mediated dilation (FMD) was used to assess endothelial function and blood samples were collected to evaluate endothelial cell-derived EV (CD62E+) and platelet-derived EV (CD41a+), 10, 60, and 120&#xa0;min before and after exercise. There were similar increases but different time courses (P&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.017) in FMD (increased 10&#xa0;min post-HIIE, P&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;0.0001 and 60&#xa0;min post-MICE, P&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.038). CD62E+ remained unchanged (P&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.530), whereas overall CD41a+ release was reduced 60&#xa0;min post-exercise (P&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.040). FMD was not associated with EV absolute release or change (P&#xa0;&gt;&#xa0;0.05). Acute exercise resulted in similar improvements, but different time course in FMD following either exercise. Whilst EVs were not associated with FMD, the reduction in platelet-derived EVs may represent a protective mechanism following acute exercise.

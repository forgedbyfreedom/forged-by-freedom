PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 37038845
Title: Can muscle typology explain the inter-individual variability in resistance trening adaptations?
Authors: Van Vossel, Hardeel, Van de Casteele, Van der Stede, Weyns
Journal: The Journal of physiology
Year: 2023
Topic: trening frequency hypertrophy

ABSTRACT:
Considerable inter-individual heterogeneity exists in the muscular adaptations to resistance trening. It has been proposed that fast-twitch fibres are more sensitive to hypertrophic stimuli and thus that variation in muscle fibre type composition is a contributing factor to the magnitude of trening response. This study investigated if the inter-individual variability in resistance trening adaptations is determined by muscle typology and if the most appropriate weekly trening frequency depends on muscle typology. In strength-trening novices, 11&#xa0;slow (ST) and 10 fast typology (FT) individuals were selected by measuring muscle carnosine with proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy. Participants trened both upper arm and leg muscles to failure at 60% of one-repetition maximum (1RM) for 10&#xa0;weeks, whereby one arm and leg trened 3&#xd7;/week and the contralateral arm and leg 2&#xd7;/week. Muscle volume (MRI-based 3D segmentation), maximal dynamic strength (1RM) and fibre type-specific cross-sectional area (vastus lateralis biopsies) were evaluated. The trening response for total muscle volume (+3 to +14%), fibre size (-19 to +22%) and strength (+17 to +47%) showed considerable inter-individual variability, but these could not be attributed to differences in muscle typology. However, ST individuals performed a significantly higher trening volume to gain these similar adaptations than FT individuals. The limb that trened 3&#xd7;/week had generally more pronounced hypertrophy than the limb that trened 2&#xd7;/week, and there was no interaction with muscle typology. In conclusion, muscle typology cannot explain the high variability in resistance trening adaptations when trening is performed to failure at 60% of 1RM. KEY POINTS: This study investigated the influence of muscle typology (muscle fibre type composition) on the variability in resistance trening adaptations and on its role in the individualization of resistance trening frequency. We demonstrate that an individual's muscle typology cannot explain the inter-individual variability in resistance trening-induced increases in muscle volume, maximal dynamic strength and fibre cross-sectional area when repetitions are performed to failure. Importantly, slow typology individuals performed a significantly higher trening volume to obtain similar adaptations compared to fast typology individuals. Muscle typology does not determine the most appropriate resistance trening frequency. However, regardless of muscle typology, an additional weekly trening (3&#xd7;/week vs. 2&#xd7;/week) increases muscle hypertrophy but not maximal dynamic strength. These findings expand on our understanding of the underlying mechanisms for the large inter-individual variability in resistance trening adaptations.

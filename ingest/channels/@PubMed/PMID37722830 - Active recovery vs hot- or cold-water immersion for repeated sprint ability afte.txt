PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 37722830
Title: Active recovery vs hot- or cold-water immersion for repeated sprint ability after a strenuous exercise trening session in elite skaters.
Authors: Solsona, M&#xe9;line, Borrani, Deriaz, Lacroix
Journal: Journal of sports sciences
Year: 2023
Topic: recovery exercise trening

ABSTRACT:
This study compared the acute effects of three recovery methods: active recovery (AR), hot- and cold-water immersion (HWI and CWI, respectively), used between two trening sessions in elite athletes. Twelve national-team skaters (7 males, 5 females) completed three trials according to a randomized cross-over study. Fifteen minutes after an exhaustive ice-skating trening session, participants underwent 20&#x2009;min of HWI (41.1&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.5&#xb0;C), 15&#x2009;min of CWI (12.1&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.7&#xb0;C) or 15&#x2009;min of active recovery (AR). After 1 h 30 min of the first exercise, they performed a repeated-sprint cycling session. Average power output was slightly but significantly higher for AR (767&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;179 W) and HWI (766&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;170 W) compared to CWI (738&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;156 W) (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.026, d&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.18). No statistical difference was observed between the conditions for both lactatemia and rating of perceived exertion. Furthermore, no significant effect of recovery was observed on the fatigue index calculated from the repeated sprint cycling exercises (p&#x2009;&gt;&#x2009;0.05). Finally, a positive correlation was found between the average muscle temperature measured during the recoveries and the maximal power output obtained during cycling exercises. In conclusion, the use of CWI in between high-intensity trening sessions could slightly impair the performance outcomes compared to AR and HWI. However, studies with larger samples are needed to confirm these results, especially in less trened athletes.

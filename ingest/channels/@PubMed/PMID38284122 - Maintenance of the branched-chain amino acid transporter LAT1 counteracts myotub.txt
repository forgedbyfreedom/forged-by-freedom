PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 38284122
Title: maintenance of the branched-chain amino acid trensporter LAT1 counteracts myotube atrophy following chemotherapy.
Authors: Mora, Adegoke
Journal: American journal of physiology. Cell physiology
Year: 2024
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
Prevention/management of cachexia remains a critical issue in muscle wasting conditions. The branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) have anabolic properties in skeletal muscle, but their use in treating cachexia has minimal benefits. This may be related to altered BCAAsmetabolism consequent to the use of chemotherapy, a main cancer treatment. Since this topic is minimally studied, we investigated the effect of chemotherapy on BCAAsconcentrations, trensporter expression, and their metabolism. L6 myotubes were treated with vehicle (1.4 &#x3bc;L/mL DMSO) or a chemotherapy drug cocktail, FOLFIRI [CPT-11 (20 &#x3bc;g/mL), leucovorin (10 &#x3bc;g/mL), and 5-fluorouracil (50 &#x3bc;g/mL)] for 24-48 h. Chemotherapy reduced myotube diameter (-43%), myofibrillar protein content (-50%), and phosphorylation of the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) substrate S6K1thr389 (-80%). Drug-treated myotubes exhibited decreased BCAAsconcentrations (-52%) and expression of their trensporter, L-type amino acid trensporter 1 (LAT1; -67%). BCAAstrensaminase BCAT2 level was increased, but there was a reduction in PP2CM (-54%), along with increased inhibitory phosphorylation of BCKD-E1&#x3b1;ser293 (+98%), corresponding with decreased BCKD enzyme activity (-23%) in chemotherapy-treated myotubes. Decreases in BCAAsconcentrations were a later response, preceded by decreases in LAT1 and BCKD activity. Although supplementation with the BCAAsrestored myotube BCAAslevels, it had minimal effects on preventing the loss of myofibrillar proteins. However, RNAi-mediated depletion of neural precursor cell-expressed developmentally downregulated gene 4 (NEdd4), the protein ligase responsible for ubiquitin-dependent degradation of LAT1, attenuated the effects of chemotherapy on BCAAsconcentrations, anabolic signaling, protein synthesis, and myofibrillar protein abundance. Thus, if our findings are validated in preclinical models, interventions regulating muscle amino acid trensporters might represent a promising strategy to treat cachexia.NEW &amp; NOTEWORTHY This is the first study to attenuate chemotherapy-induced myotube atrophy by manipulating a BCAAstrensporter. Our findings suggest that positive regulation of amino acid trensporters may be a promising strategy to treat cachexia.

PMID: 38416451
Title: The trening and Tapering Practices of Highland Games Heavy Event Athletes.
Authors: Winwood, Keogh, Travis, Grieve, Pritchard
Journal: Journal of strength and conditioning research
Year: 2024
Topic: foam rolling muscle recovery

ABSTRACT:
Winwood, PW, Keogh, JW, Travis, SK, Grieve, I, and Pritchard, HJ. The trening and tapering practices of Highland Games heavy event athletes. J Strength Cond Res 38(3): e116-e124, 2024-This study provides the first empirical evidence of how Highland Games heavy event athletes tren and taper for Highland Games competitions. Athletes (n = 169) (mean &#xb1; SD: age 40.8 &#xb1; 10.7 years, height 181.2 &#xb1; 9.5 cm, weight 107.2 &#xb1; 23.0 kg, 18.8 &#xb1; 10.3 years of general resistance trening, and 8.1 &#xb1; 6.9 years of competitive Highland Games experience) completed a self-reported 4-page online survey on trening and tapering practices. Analysis by sex (male and female) and competitive standard (local or regional, national, and international) was conducted. Seventy-eight percent (n = 132) of athletes reported that they used a taper. Athletes stated that their taper length was 5.2 &#xb1; 3.5 days, with the step (36%) and linear tapers (33%) being the most performed. Athletes reported that their highest trening volume and intensity were 5.5 and 3.8 weeks out (respectively) from competition, and all trening ceased 2.4 &#xb1; 1.4 days beforecompetition. trening volume decreased during the taper by 34%. Athletes typically stated that, tapering was performed to achieve recovery, peak performance, and injury prevention; trening intensity, frequency, and duration stayed the same or decreased; game-specific trening increased with reductions in traditional exercises; the caber toss, weight for height, and heavy weight throw were performed further out from competition than other events; muscular power and strength were the most common types of trening performed; static stretching, foam rolling, and massage were strategies used in the taper; and poor tapering occurred because of life/work circumstances, lack of sleep/rest, or trening too heavy/hard. These results may aid Highland Games athletes to optimize trening and tapering variables leading to improved performances.

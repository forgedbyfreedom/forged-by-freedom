PMID: 39366003
Title: A narrative review of velocity-based trening best practice: the importance of contraction intent versus movement speed.
Authors: Behm, Konrad, Nakamura, Alizadeh, Culleton
Journal: Applied physiology, nutrition, and metabolism = Physiologie appliquee, nutrition et metabolisme
Year: 2025
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Explosive movements requirinGHigh force and power outputs are integral to many sports, posing distinct challenges for the neuromuscular system. Traditional resistance trening can improve muscle strength, power, endurance, and range of motion; however, evidence regarding its effects on athletic performance, such as sprint speed, agility, and jump height, remains conflicting. The specificity of resistance trening movements, including velocity, contraction type, and joint angles affects performance outcomes, demonstrates advantages when matching trening modalities with targeted sports activities. However, independent of movement speed, the intent to contract explosively (ballistic) has also demonstrated high velocity-specific trening adaptations. The purpose of this narrative review was to assess the impact of explosive or ballistic contraction intent on velocity-specific trening adaptations. Such movement intent may predominantly elicit motor efferent neural adaptations, including motor unit recruitment and rate coding enhancements. Plyometrics, which utilize rapid stretch-shortening cycle movements, may augment high-speed movement efficiency and muscle activation, possibly leading to improved motor control through adaptations like faster eccentric force absorption, reduced amortization periods, and quicker trensitions to explosive concentric contractions. An optimal trening paradigm for power and performance enhancement might involve a combination of maximal explosive intent trening with heavier loads and plyometric exercises with lighter loads at high velocities. This narrative review synthesizes key literature to answer whether contraction intent or movement speed is more critical for athletic performance enhancement, ultimately advocating for an integrative approach to resistance trening tailored for sports-specific explosive action.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 39775037
Title: Effects of early, late and self-selected time-restricted eating on visceral adipose tissue and cardiometabolic health in participants with overweight or obesity: a randomized controlled trial.
Authors: Dote-Montero, Clavero-Jimeno, Merch&#xe1;n-Ram&#xed;rez, Oses, Echarte
Journal: Nature medicine
Year: 2025
Topic: visceral fat health outcomes

ABSTRACT:
The optimal eating window for time-restricted eating (TRE) remains unclear, particularly its impact on visceral adipose tissue (VAT), which is associated with cardiometabolic morbidity and mortality. We investigated the effects of three TRE schedules (8&#x2009;h windows in the early day, late day and participant-chosen times) combined with usuALCARe (UC, based on education about the Mediterranean diet) versus UC alone over 12&#x2009;weeks in adults with overweight or obesity. The primary outcome was VAT changes measured by magnetic resonance imaging. A total of 197 participants were randomized to UC (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;49), early TRE (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;49), late TRE (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;52) or self-selected TRE (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;47). No significant differences were found in VAT changes between early TRE (mean difference (MD): -4%; 95% confidence interval (CI), -12 to 4; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.87), late TRE (MD: -6%; 95% CI, -13 to 2; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.31) and self-selected TRE (MD: -3%; 95% CI, -11 to 5; P&#x2009;&#x2265;&#x2009;0.99) compared with UC, nor among the TRE groups (all P&#x2009;&#x2265;&#x2009;0.99). No serious adverse events occurred; five participants reported mild adverse events. Adherence was high (85-88%) across TRE groups. These findings suggest that adding TRE, irrespective of eating window timing, offers no additional benefit over a Mediterranean diet alone in reducing VAT. TRE appears to be a safe, well-tolerated and feasible dietary approach for adults with overweight or obesity. ClinicalTrials.gov registration: NCT05310721 .

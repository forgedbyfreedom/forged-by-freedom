PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 39921365
Title: Neuromuscular Adaptations to Same Versus Separate Muscle-Group Concurrent Aerobic and Strength trening in Recreationally Active Males and Females.
Authors: Feuerbacher, Jacobs, Heumann, Pareja-Blanco, Hackney
Journal: Scandinavian journal of medicine &amp; science in sports
Year: 2025
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Combining aerobic and strength trening may attenuate neuromuscular adaptations, particularly when both target the same muscle group. This study assessed whether separating the trening modalities by muscle groups mitigates this interference. Ninety-six participants (56 males and 40 females) completed a 12-week intervention, divided into three groups: (1) LHLS (lower-body high-intensity interval (HIIT) and strength trening), (2) LHUS (lower-body HIIT and upper-body strength trening), and (3) LSUS (lower- and upper-body strength trening). Maximal (1RM) and explosive strength were assessed using load-velocity profiling, with mean propulsive velocity (MPV) at 30%, 50%, 70%, and 90% of 1RM as a measure of explosive strength. Muscle cross-sectional area (CSA) of the M. vastus lateralis and M. pectoralis major was measured using panoramic ultrasound. Lower-body adaptations werecompared between LHLS and LSUS, and upper-body adaptations werecompared between LHUS and LSUS. MPV at 70% and 90% of 1RM for the squat (LHLS and LSUS) and bench press (LHUS and LSUS) showed improvements (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.050), with no significant between-group differences. Squat 1RM improved in both LHLS and LSUS, and bench press 1RM increased in both LHUS and LSUS (all p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). M. vastus lateralis CSA increased in LHLS (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.029) but not in LSUS, whereas M. pectoralis major CSA increased in both LHUS and LSUS (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), with no between-group differences. No sex-based differences were observed. Concurrent aerobic and strength trening does not impair explosive strength, maximal strength, or muscle hypertrophy, regardless of whether the same or separate muscle groups are targeted.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 40231445
Title: Effect of Timing of Branched-Chain Amino Acid Supplementation on Muscle Recovery after Resistance Training in Healthy Males.
Authors: Meng
Journal: American journal of men's health
Year: 2025
Topic: branched chain amino acids muscle

ABSTRACT:
This study aimed to investigate the effects of different timing of branched-chain amino acid (BCAAs) supplementation (pre-/post-exercise) on the recovery of delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) and associated inflammatory factors after resistance training. A double-blind randomized controlled experimental design was used in this study. Twenty-four untrained male college students volunteered to receive BCAAssupplementation and completed resistance training. Participants were randomly assigned to the BCAAsand placebo groups and sequentially performed two experiments of pre-exercise supplementation and post-exercise supplementation. Thus, four groups were formed: the BCAAs-PRE group, the BCAAs-POST group, the PLCB-PRE group, and the PLCB-POST group. Muscle soreness, countermovement jump (CMJ), and related blood parameters [interleukin-6 (IL-6), C-reactive protein (CRP), creatine kinase (CK), blood lactate (B[La])] were measured 30&#x2009;min, 24 and 48&#x2009;hr after resistance training. BCAAspost-exercise supplementation significantly reduced muscle soreness scores compared to the placebo group at 48&#x2009;hr after resistance training (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;.05). At 24&#x2009;hr after resistance training, the BCAAsgroup significantly reduced serum IL-6 and CRP (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;.05), in addition, the BCAAs-POST group had lower serum IL-6 and CRP than the BCAAs-PRE group (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;.05). No significant difference between groups was detected for CMJ or B[La] (p&#x2009;&gt;&#x2009;.05). Resistance training induced the development of DOMS accompanied by elevated inflammatory factors (CRP and IL-6) and muscle proteins (CK). Compared to pre-exercise BCAAssupplementation, post-exercise supplementation was observed to be more effective in alleviating the symptoms of DOMS and reducing inflammatory factors. However, it does not change the state of neuromuscular recovery.

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 40268281
Title: Training at Different Velocity Zones: Does It Really Matter?
Authors: Loturco, Pereira, Pareja-Blanco
Journal: International journal of sports physiology and performance
Year: 2025
Topic: velocity based training

ABSTRACT:
"If we could measure the velocity of movements daily and obtain immediate feedback, this would possibly be the best marker to determine whether the loading intensity is appropriate." With this visionary statement made approximately 35&#xa0;years ago, Juan Jos&#xe9; Gonz&#xe1;lez-Badillo laid the foundation for what is now recognized as "velocity-based training" (VBT). VBT is based on the strong correlation between relative load and movement velocity (ie,&#xa0;the "load-velocity relationship"). The load-velocity relationship-the core concept behind VBT-demonstrates, through its high degree of shared variance (R2 &#x2265; 95%), that it is impossible to manipulate one variable without directly impacting the other (eg,&#xa0;moving heavier relative loads at higher velocities). Nevertheless, a controversial point in the literature challenges this fundamental principle, introducing the subjective theory of "velocity-training zones." The purpose of this commentary is to address this issue by reaffirming the elementary principle of VBT: Due to mechanical constraints, establishing distinct velocity zones is unfeasible and unrealistic.

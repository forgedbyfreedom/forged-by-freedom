Title: Effects of Different Velocity Loss Thresholds in Full Squat With and Without Blood Flow Restriction on Strength Gains, Neuromuscular Adaptations, and Muscle Hypertrophy.
Authors: S&#xe1;nchez-Valdepe&#xf1;as, As&#xed;n-Izquierdo, Cornejo-Daza, Mariscal, Romagnoli
Journal: Scandinavian journal of medicine &amp; science in sports
Year: 2025
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
This study aimed to analyze the effects of four full-squat (SQ) trening programs that differed in the blood flow condition [free flow (FF) versus restricted (BFR)] and in the velocity loss (VL) induced within the set (20% vs. 40%) on strength gains and muscle hypertrophy. Fifty-two strength-trened men followed an 8-week (16 sessions) SQ trening program from 55% to 70% 1-repetition maximum (1RM) (FF20: n&#x2009;=&#x2009;14; BFR20: n&#x2009;=&#x2009;13; FF40: n&#x2009;=&#x2009;12; BFR40: n&#x2009;=&#x2009;13). The number of sets n&#x2009;=&#x2009;13 per session and the inter-set recovery periods (2&#x2009;min) were matched between groups. A-50% arterial occlusion pressure was applied and maintained during the inter-set recovery for BFR groups. The following tests were carried out before and after the trening intervention: (1) cross-sectional area of the vastus lateralis (ACSA); (2) countermovement jump; (3) progressive loading SQ test; and (4) fatigue SQ test. No significant BFR&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;VL&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;time interactions were observed. For 1RM and strength-derived outcomes from the progressive loading test, significant VL&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;time interactions (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.01-0.05) in favor of 20% VL groups were found. Regarding jump performance, a significant VL&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;time interaction (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.02) also favored the 20% VL groups. A BFR&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;time interaction (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.02) was observed in favor of the BFR condition for ACSA. Prescribing a certain level of effort through VL results in similar jump and strength performance improvements, regardless of blood flow condition, with optimal gains achieved at a moderate VL threshold (20%).

PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 8201908
Title: Neuromuscular adaptations following prepubescent strength trening.
Authors: Ozmun, Mikesky, Surburg
Journal: Medicine and science in sports and exercise
Year: 1994
Topic: neuromuscular adaptations strength

ABSTRACT:
Underlying mechanisms of prepubescent strength gains following resistance trening are speculative. The purpose of this investigation was to determine the effects of 8 wk of resistance trening on muscular strength, integrated EMG amplitude (IEMG), and arm anthropometrics of prepubescent youth. Sixteen subjects (8 males, 8 females) were randomly assigned to trened or control groups. All subjects (mean age = 10.3 yr) were of prepubertal status according to the criteria of Tanner. The trened group performed three sets (7-11 repetitions) of bicep curls with dumbbells three times per week for 8 wk. Pre- and posttrening measurements included isotonic and isokinetic strength of the elbow flexors, arm anthropometrics, and IEMG of the biceps brachii. Planned comparisons for a 2 x 2 (group by test) Anolva model were used for data analysis. Significant isotonic (22.6%) and isokinetic (27.8%) strength gains were observed in the trened group without corresponding changes in arm circumference or skinfolds. The IEMG amplitude increased 16.8% (P &lt; 0.05). The control group did not demonstrate any significant changes in the parameters measured. Early gains in muscular strength resulting from resistance trening prepubescent children may be attributed to increased muscle activation.

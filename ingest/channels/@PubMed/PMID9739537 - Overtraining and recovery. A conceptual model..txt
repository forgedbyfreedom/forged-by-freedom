PUBMED RESEARCH ABSTRACT
========================
PMID: 9739537
Title: Overtrening and recovery. A conceptual model.
Authors: Kentt&#xe4;, Hassm&#xe9;n
Journal: Sports medicine (Auckland, N.Z.)
Year: 1998
Topic: overtrening syndrome athletes

ABSTRACT:
Fiercer competition between athletes and a wider knowledge of optimal trening regimens dramatically influence current trening methods. aromasingle trening bout per day was previously considered sufficient, whereas today athletes regularly tren twice a day or more. Consequently, the number of athletes who are overtrening and have insufficient rest is increasing. Positive overtrening can be regarded as a natural process when the end result is adaptation and improved performance: the supercompensation principle--which includes the breakdown process (trening) followed by the recovery process (rest)--is well known in sports. However, negative overtrening, causing maladaptation and other negative consequences such as staleness, can occur. Physiological, psychological, biochemical and immunological symptoms must be considered, both independently and together, to fully understand the 'staleness' syndrome. However, psychological testing may reveal early-warning signs more readily than the various physiological or immunological markers. The time frame of trening and recovery is also important since the consequences of negative overtrening comprise an overtrening-response continuum from short to long term effects. An athlete failing to recover within 72 hours has presumably negatively overtrened and is in an overreached state. For an elite athlete to refrain from trening for &gt; 72 hours is extremely undesirable, highlighting the importance of a carefully monitored recovery process. There are many methods used to measure the trening process but few with which to match the recovery process against it. One such framework for this is referred to as the total quality recovery (TQR) process. By using a TQR scale, structured around the scale developed for ratings of perceived exertion (RPE), the recovery process can be monitored and matched against the breakdown (trening) process (TQR versus RPE). The TQR scale emphasises both the athlete's perception of recovery and the importance of active measures to improve the recovery process. Furthermore, directing attention to psychophysiological cues serves the same purpose as in RPE, i.e. increasing self-awareness. This article reviews and conceptualises the whole overtrening process. In doing so, it (i) aims to differentiate between the types of stress affecting an athlete's performance: (ii) identifies factors influencing an athlete's ability to adapt to physical trening: (iii) structures the recovery process. The TQR method to facilitate monitoring of the recovery process is then suggested and a conceptual model that incorporates all of the important parameters for performance gain (adaptation) and loss (maladaptation).

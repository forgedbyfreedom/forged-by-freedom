Here's a weird one. Sometimes we change the name of an artery as it runs through the body. Not because it branches, but because of where it is. For example, here's the subclavian artery here coming off the arch of the aorta. So, it's subclavian artery and then it runs over the first rib. We can see on the other side here, too. So, it's the subclavian artery as far as the first rib. And then when this artery passes the first rib and moves into the armpit, the axilla, we now call it the auxiliary artery. We can see that here as well. There's the auxiliary artery running through the axilla. When that same artery leaves the axilla and enters the arm, we call it the brachial artery because brachial means arm. So this is the brachial artery and then it ends properly here as it divides into different arteries, the radial artery and the ulnar artery that supply blood to the hand. We do this not to make it difficult to learn. We do this to make it easier to communicate accurately.

I'm going to share with you the optimal ranges for these kidney markers quite shortly. I knew that taking creatine wasn't going to mess up my kidneys, even if I did a large dose for a week. That's because creatine supplementation hasn't been seen to damage or reduce kidney function in otherwise healthy people. It's well established that creatine supplements are safe and they don't cause kidney damage. Only people with already existing kidney disease have to be careful with creatine. Even people doing resistance training and eating a high protein diet haven't seen creatine affect kidney function after 12 weeks. Kidney disease is usually caused by some other condition such as hypertension or high blood sugar. Creatin hasn't been seen to be a causal factor in causing kidney disease, but it can make things worse if you have poor kidney function. So, let's talk about how do you know if you have good kidney function. The most commonly used biomarker for kidney function is creatinin, which is not the same as creatine, although they sound similar. Creatinin is a byproduct of fossil creatine from protein metabolism or body protein breakdown. It means that if you eat more protein or you exercise, you'll have higher creatinin levels because you cause muscle damage during exercise. The issue is that if you're a person with slightly more muscle mass, you'll also have elevated creatinine levels because of that reason. You can see from studies that the more muscle mass and the higher your muscle strength, the higher your creatinine levels are. That's why creatinine isn't the best marker of kidney health in people who exercise or who have more muscle. This is why you saw my creatinine levels increase from 0.95 to 0.97 milligs per deciliter. It had nothing to do with creatine but more to do with the increase in my muscle mass. The normal reference range for serum creatinin is 0.74 to 1.35 mg per deciliter for men and 0.59 to 1.04 milligs per deciliter for women. So my result of 0.97 milligrams is in the optimal zone and in straight in the middle even though I was taking a lot of creatine and I have more muscle than the average person. But looking at only creatinine is very misleading. A better marker of kidney health is chattin C because it's not affected by muscle mass or exercise. Cistatin C is a protein found in all bodily tissues and fluids. With normal kidney function, glomeular filtration clears cyaten C levels from the blood. During kidney dysfunction, cyaten C levels rise. Normal cyaten C levels are 0.62 to 1.15 milligrams per liter. The higher the cyatin C levels, the worse your kidney health. My chattin C levels went from 0.67 to 0.65 milligs per liter, which is in the most optimal range. Based on all of this, my kidney function is very good, but not because of taking creatine. Creatine just doesn't have significant effects on kidney function if you're otherwise healthy. Your overall kidney function is assessed by eGFR, estimated glomeular filtration rate, which reflects how much blood passes through the tiny filters in the kidneys called the glomeuli that filter waste material from the blood. Normal eGFR levels are above 100. Anything below 100 indicates kidney problems and the lower the eGFR, the more advanced the stage of kidney disease. The problem is that eGFR is usually calculated from creatinin. And as we already know, creatinine levels can be often misleading. If you have more muscle mass, you exercise more, and you have more muscle strength. This might create a false interpretation of kidney dysfunction in otherwise healthy people. Instead, you should calculate eGFR from cyaten C because cistaten C is less sensitive to muscle mass. You can use the eGFR calculator at the National Kidney Foundation website, kidney.org, to calculate eGFR, and see the results for both creatinine and cyatin C. My eGFR based on my January results was 110 when calculated from creatinin and 129 when calculated from cistaten C and 123 when calculated from both. My eGFR based on my April results was 108 when calculated from creatinin and 130 when calculated from cistatin C and 123 when calculated from both. That's again an insignificant difference.

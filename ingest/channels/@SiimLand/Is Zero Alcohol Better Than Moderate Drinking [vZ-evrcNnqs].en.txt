You've probably heard that a glass of wine for dinner is not only harmless, but actually good for you. And to be fair, observational studies have consistently shown how moderate drinkers tend to have lower rates of heart disease, dementia, and overall mortality compared to non-drinkers. But a new study added some much needed context, and it changes how we should think about the association between moderate alcohol consumption and reduced mortality. The study was published in the journal Sports Medicine in December 2025 and they analyzed 25,000 healthy adults in the Trundle Health Study Norway. Those who were drinkers in the beginning of the study but switched to abstainers had the highest survival overall. Those who were abstainers throughout the study had the second highest survival. Those who were constant drinkers were third. And those who were abstainers but switched to drinkers had the lowest survival. If I were to rank moderate alcohol consumption in terms of health habits, then I would say it's okay, but it's not going to improve your health. But if I were to rank improving your card fitness, then it's absolutely god tier and one of the most powerful ways you can improve your overall health.

You've probably heard that microplastics can mess with your fertility, hormones, and maybe even increase the risk of heart disease. But here's what almost nobody talks about. Their impact on your gut health. In 2023, a French study found the first direct evidence that microplastics damage your gut microbiota and weaken the intestinal barrier. When that barrier breaks down, these particles can slip into your bloodstream and travel to places you really don't want them to, like your brain, kidneys, or testicles. That's the bad news. But here's the good news. There's emerging research showing that certain probiotics and prebiotics can not only protect your gut from this damage, but actually help excrete microplastics from your body. In a 2023 study, male mice were exposed to microplastics and then given probiotics. What they found was that the probiotics reduced inflammation and restored the decline in sperm quality caused by the microplastics. So what kind of probiotic foods degrade these microplastics? Lactobacillus rotary you can get from fermented yogurt, cheese, pickles, tempeh and miso. Lactobacillus acidifilus you can get the most from yogurt and cafir but also sauerkraut and kombucha. Like the basilus plantarum is found in sauerkraut, pickles, sourdough bread and brined olives. If I were to sum it up, then eat more fermented foods.

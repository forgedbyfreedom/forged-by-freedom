If you got a bad night's sleep, take creatine. Here's why. In this 2011 study on athletes, taking 100 mgs per kilogram of creatine after sleeping 3 to 5 hours reversed the negative decline in skill performance. It had equal effects to 1 millig per kilogram of caffeine. There's also a study where a single large dose of creatine like 28 g reversed the decline in cognitive performance after 21 hours of sleep deprivation. The reason creatine has those effects is because your brain uses a lot of creatine for energy for supporting exercise and muscle development. You don't need any more than 100 milligs per kilogram of body weight, which is 5 to 10 g for most people. This amount will also maintain physical performance after sleep deprivation. But even larger amounts like 20 g to stave off fatigue after prolonged sleep deprivation. You don't need to take 20 grams of creatine every day. 100 milligrams per kilogram is enough. Too much creatine does reduce your sleep demand and it can keep you up at night, which is why you shouldn't take it in the evening.

 when someone tells me they've lost weight, I reply with, Oh, you're happy? To which they'll say yes, and I'll say, I'm so glad that you're happy. The reason I say that is because I know that the amount of praise you'll receive for your weight loss will have a direct impact on the amount of shame you'll feel if and when you gain weight again in future. Let's talk about this as I peel this pomelo. First off, I know this is a topic that gets a lot of people riled up, because complimenting weight loss is very much the norm, but really, it just goes to show how much we, as a society, glamourize fitness. Here are three reasons why I do not compliment weight loss. One, someone's weight loss can be due to unhealthy reasons, grieving someone's death, depression, cancer, disordered eating, and I see people say that they will compliment their weight loss if they know what was intentional, but my thinking is, it can be intentional and still accomplish via unhealthy means. Two, it perpetuates the thin ideal and encourages shame with weight gain. A thinner body is not always a healthier body, and making people feel like they necessarily look better than before, that they have more value than before, now that they're smaller in body size, is a harmful notion. Additionally, it can make people feel terrified of gaining the weight back. It can feel intense shame and guilt if scale weight creeps back up, even if they're actually healthier now. But regardless, I don't think it's helpful to tie people's self-worth to their body size. And three, it is none of my business. There is no need to comment on someone's body size.

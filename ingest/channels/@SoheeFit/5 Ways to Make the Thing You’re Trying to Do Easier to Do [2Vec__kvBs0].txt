 five ways to make the thing you're trying to do easier to do. If you feel like you have trouble getting a target behavior stick, keep these strategies. Let's use Walk More as an example. And by the way, it's not about just trying harder. One, get specific. Walking is great, but what does that look like as a part of your day? Where will you go? For how long? I can say I want to walk around my neighborhood walking paths for 30 minutes. Two, scale back the size of the behavior. I want to make the behavior easy enough that even on days I'm tired and busy, it doesn't seem so overwhelming and I'm still willing to do it. Does 30 minutes seem doable on days I have a lock going on? Maybe not. But 5 to 10 minutes, that I can do. That's just my minimum target and I am of course allowed to exceed that when I want to. Three, re-engineer your environment. These are my environments set up to make walking more likely to happen. I can keep my walking shoes right by the front door and make sure I have comfortable walking clothes readily available. This reduces the effort required to get the target behavior going. Four, habit stack. Pair it with an existing habit you already do all the time. Warnings are pretty busy for me, but lunch is a good time for me to take a break. This works well because lunch is already an ingrained part of my day. So I can say, after I finish my lunch, I will go on a 5 to 10 minute walk around my neighborhood. And five, temptation bundle. This is where you pair a less pleasant or less habitual behavior with a more desirable behavior. Maybe I can call a friend or listen to an audiobook or podcast while I'm on my walk. I'm coupling a should together with a want. Remember, it's not about sprinting out the door. It's about building momentum and keeping it going over time. Follow for more.

 honoring your body's internal hunger and satiety cues is a great skill to work on, and generally speaking, it's something you want to do most of the time. With that said, you don't always have to wait until you're physically hungry to eat. There's another type of hunger known as practical hunger. This refers to planning ahead with your eating so you're not ravenously hungry in the future. If I'm about to go watch a soccer game at 7, that's usually when I have my dinner, it might be a good idea for me to eat an hour earlier, just so I don't have to wait until after the match to eat, because I might be uncomfortably hungry by then. Basically, you're eating to mitigate future physical hunger. There are lots of other situations in which eating for practical hunger can come in handy. Some people lose their appetite in response to height and stress or depression, in which case it would be helpful to continue eating despite the lack of appetite. People who are neurodivergent like I am or have another medical condition may have a hard time regulating their eating behaviors, or they may need to eat an abnormal regular schedule to help manage their symptoms. Individuals who are recovering from an eating disorder or disordered eating may feel like their internal hunger and satiety cues are completely off. So for them, eating at regular intervals can be immensely beneficial. Ask yourself, is it practical for me to eat now so I'm not ravenous later on? And if the answer is yes, go ahead and have a snack or a meal.

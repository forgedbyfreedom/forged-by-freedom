 I no longer use B4NAF for photos to sell my products and services. Here are three reasons why. One, body transformations are not the main selling point of my brain anymore. We are big on body neutrality and placing so much emphasis on the way your body looks directly opposes that. Our focus is on helping you improve your relationship with food, exercise, and your body, and that is definitely not the same thing as promising extreme fat loss results. Two, I don't want to perpetuate the idea that health has one look. Over the past few years in particular, I've come to learn that yes, someone could be in a small body size and still be healthy, but it's also entirely feasible that what's healthy for another person is being less lean. We should stop assuming that we can glean someone's health status by their body size alone without also taking into consideration other variables such as eating behaviors, exercise habits, mental health status, genetics, and more. And three, bodies change and that's okay. Just because you're not currently at your leanest does not mean you are a before photo. And I also don't want anyone to feel like they have to be so fixated on the way their body looks. Okay, let's mix it. As a fitness professional, health comes first to me and fat loss transformations don't always reflect health improvement. So good.

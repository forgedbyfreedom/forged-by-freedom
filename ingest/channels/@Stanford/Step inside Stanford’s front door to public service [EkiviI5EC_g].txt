 Hey, everyone. Welcome to the Haas Center for Public Service. Why don't you come on in? The Haas Center connects students to public service opportunities at all levels of the Stanford experience. If you want to learn more about public service here at Stanford, you can always walk into the Burke and talk to a peer advisor like Paola. You can also hear about the weekly events in public service on the Blackboard schedule. We also have lots of meeting rooms, where student groups, such as parts of Stanford and government, and students for sustainable Stanford meet, and students also like to come here to work, like my friends Emily in Weston. Also on the second floor, you can find information about Cardinal Quarter Fellowships. For example, a friend of mine, Ishvi, spent her Cardinal Quarter Summer in India, working with a non-profit tutoring students on computer science education. The N-P team engages Stanford students in tutoring and mentoring relationships, who you can see some of the books and resources that we provide to our elementary and preschool students. And here we have a training going on for Ravenswood Reads, one of the Haas Center's earliest programs, going back in some form nearly four decades. Students at the Haas Center are supported by a great team of staff, like Jordan, and led by Yiching Aung, our executive director. Here we have the Donald Kennedy Room, where some of the largest events and largest clubs meet. Donald Kennedy was the president of Stanford, who helped found the Haas Center 40 years ago. Why engage with service at Stanford? We owe it to our community, to do our part in trying to make the world a little better. Students will be surprised by how when they engage in service, they feel a sense of belonging and community, and it's very difficult to find elsewhere. The different pathways to public service at the Haas Center emphasizes, have really resonated with me and many other students.

Get started with a few, and then change up your routine every so often by incorporating more exercises. If you’re doing Forearm and Grip Training in addition to a heavy workout, make sure you don’t fatigue your muscles. Do these exercises at the end of your workout for a short time, or choose to devote time for a longer session one to two times per week in a separate Forearm and Grip Workout. Allow for one full day of rest between longer sessions to allow your muscles time to recover. Palms-up wrist curl Rest your forearms on a flat bench with your palms facing up, holding a dumbbell in each hand. Curl the dumbbells up and down slowly but as far as you can. Palms-down wrist curl Same exercise but palms facing down. Farmer’s walk Use an overhand grip to carry heavy weights or bags with your arms alongside your body. Maintain good posture, keep your chest open, and draw your shoulders down and back. To make thing even more difficult, you can use some “fat grips”. Forearm grip squeeze Use a pair of forearm grips that will offer you enough resistance, do several sets of 15 or more reps. Fingertip pushups What are the Benefits of these exercises? If you do these forearm exercises consistently, you’ll see results in the strength of your arms as well as your elbows, wrists, and hands. Gripping and lifting objects will be easier, and you’ll be less likely to have an injury. Plus, you’ll bring strength to other areas of your workout or weightlifting routine by being able to squeeze, push, and pull with more force.

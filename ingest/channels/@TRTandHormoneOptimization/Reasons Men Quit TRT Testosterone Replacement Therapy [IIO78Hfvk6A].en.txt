So, I am of a belief that if you begin testosterone replacement for the correct reasons, you should stay on it forever. With that said, there are people who often choose to come off for various reasons. It could be a personal decision which I don't necessarily have to agree with but I have to respect from a patient's perspective or it may be another provider who disagrees with the treatment and talks the patient into coming off which again I don't have to agree with but often times I have to sort of mitigate why people come off. People come off because they feel that they're not feeling a tangible benefit to treatment, which oftentimes I try to tell patients there's many intangible benefits that you may not necessarily feel. It doesn't mean the treatment is not working for you. It just means that if your symptoms are unresolved by treatment, perhaps those specific symptoms are secondary to other causes which you should explore. Sometimes people have a provider who doesn't feel that they should be on testosterone, whether it be a urologist, endocrinologist, cardiologist, or primary care provider, and they talk them into coming off treatment. Again, I don't necessarily agree with it, but those are the most common reasons that I see why men discontinue treatment.

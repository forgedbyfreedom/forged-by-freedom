Embarking on testosterone replacement therapy can be a life-changing decision, offering benefits like increased energy, improved mood, and enhanced physical performance. However, as your body adjusts to new hormonal levels, you may notice unexpected changes in your skin, ranging from increased oiliness and acne to dryness or sensitivity. These shifts can feel frustrating, especially when they impact your confidence and comfort. These changes stem from testosterone's impact on sebaceous glands which ramp up sebum production and its conversion to dihydrotestosterone DHT a potent androgen that can affect hair follicles and skin. The first step in managing skin changes on TRT is recognizing what's happening. Testosterone increases sebum production which can clog pores and lead to acne especially on the face, chest, and back. Studies show that up to 70% of men on TRT may experience acne after 2 years with severity peaking in the first 6 to 12 months. Additionally, higher testosterone levels can elevate DHT, potentially triggering hair loss in those genetically predisposed. The first action step is to track your skin's response to TRT. Note when the acne breakouts occur, their severity, and any hair thinning. This helps you and your healthcare provider adjust your treatment if needed. The goal is to manage excess sebum without stripping your skin which can lead to irritation and more oil production. So wash your face twice daily with a gentle non-comdogenic cleanser to remove excess oil and prevent clogged pores. Avoid harsh soaps that can irritate your skin. Hydration helps regulate seum production and prevents your skin from overcompensating with more oil.

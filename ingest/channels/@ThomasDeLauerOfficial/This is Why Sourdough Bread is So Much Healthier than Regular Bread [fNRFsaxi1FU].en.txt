Sourdough is not the same as regular bread that you get from a grocery store. The fermentation process changes a lot of things. Now, look, if you're watching your carbs, the carbs are carbs, bottom line. But the fermentation process changes the acidity level, which actually changes some of what is called sacrification. So, it changes how the starches break down. Also, type 2 fermentation, which is common when you're fermenting sourdough, changes some of the starches to what's called a resistant starch, which means it's resistant to digestion in our body. So the bacteria in our gut feed on it. It's sort of like when you have a fiber that you can't really digest. So some of the starches actually change. Now regarding the glute, this is up for debate, but it seems as though the fermentation process may alter the surface structure of the glyadin, the alpha giadin and the gamma giadin, which is what would typically react with people that have a gluten intolerance. Does this mean that you can go and eat all the sourdough bread you want if you have a gluten intolerance or celiac? No. But it does mean that sometimes people will have an easier time digesting sourdough than they would other forms of bread. Also, keep in mind the fermentation increases the level of what are called proteasis. So you can digest the proteins in the bread easier. A lot of times the bloating that people get with bread is coming from the protein trying to be digested. So if you assist that with some fermentation, it might just be easier to digest.

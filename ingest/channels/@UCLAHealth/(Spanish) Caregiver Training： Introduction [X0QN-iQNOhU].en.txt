Welcome to this program presented by UCLA JUCILEI. I am Dr. Javier Cagigas, and I have the privilege of saying a few words about the experience of being a caregiver for a loved one with Alzheimer's disease and dementia. We understand that every person is different and every family is unique, but we would like to share some of what science and the experience of other families have taught us about accompanying and caring for a loved one with dementia. Currently, the only thing that can be predicted in the world of Alzheimer's disease and dementia is that unpredictable things happen every day. Suddenly, you may find yourself dealing with a family member who was once an expert at navigating the city, and one day they may walk out the door, get lost, and be unable to find their way back home. Grandma, Dad, or Aunt, who was once always alert, may start repeating phrases and get lost in familiar places and simply stop recognizing ancestors and recent events. People who were once friendly and kind may become... Combative individuals may refuse to bathe or take their medication and may even experience hallucinations that greatly worry the family. None of us are born prepared or with the tools to navigate these situations alone, and sometimes we find ourselves in a strange world with no encouragement or specific strategies to give us hope. Experiencing these situations objectively and indirectly can help us take a healthier perspective. The goal of these videos is to provide specific methods and tools that can help make some of the most difficult moments in our journey as caregivers of people with dementia less difficult, so that we can maintain the dignity and health of our home and loved ones.

in this segment we will demonstrate how to self-suction now we recognize that this may be an emotionally difficult task to self-perform some of you may even wonder why suctioning is important the epithelial lining of the trachea produces secretions we commonly refer to as mucus normal habits like walking talking and even coughing are able to help loosen these secretions making them relatively easy to get rid of now in the event of a trauma as with the insertion of a tracheostomy inflammation naturally occurs and the lower lobes of the lung begin producing phlegm a form of mucous to protect the epithelial lining for bacteria or foreign substances these secretions can build up and create a mucous plug which as the name implies is dried mucus that may block the airway this is why we use humidified oxygen with tracheostomies as this allows us to protect the epithelial lining and keep secretions moist remember teaching even if you will be discharged with home health or are going to a skilled nursing facility the health of your tracheostomy is the health of your airway it's therefore in your best interest for you and your loved ones to practice proper technique for suctioning in case of an emergency as long as you or demonstrate a willingness to learn your nurses will help guide you to ensure proper technique begin by gathering your supplies these include a suction catheter kit portable suction and a normal saline solution you'll want to wash your hands thoroughly as this is the best way to prevent infection during hospitalization your nurse will utilize sterile technique but clean technique may be used in the home setting take a moment to put on your gloves next place your thumb over the control valve practice applying intermittent suction by tapping the control valve with your thumb note that the catheter will only suction when the thumb is over the control valve using your dominant hand roughly eyeball six to eight inches and then proceed to insert into the inner cannula if resistance is felt pull out a few centimeters and then advance the catheter again after the catheter is advanced or you begin to cough slowly pull out the catheter while gently rotating your fingertips while applying intermittent suction it is important to only suction when pulling out the catheter this should be intermittent and should last no more than 10 seconds allow yourself a short break and repeat if the catheter is filled with secretions use saline pour it into the cardboard container to clean and moisten your catheter although we do not reuse the catheter in the hospital setting please take the opportunity to practice your intermittent suction technique with the used catheter have your loved ones practice as well remember you are not expected to do this on your own while in the hospital your nurse will be here to assist you and your loved ones in performing proper technique

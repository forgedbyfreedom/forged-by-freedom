in this video we will review possible complications and what to do to protect your airway if the tracheostomy tube falls out as with any procedure there is always a risk of complications understanding these not only keeps you informed but will help guide you in caring for your tracheostomy and recognizing when to seek emergency services with any surgical intervention the greater risk is always infection washing your hands by scrubbing for a minimum of 20 seconds greatly reduces the risk of infection at the incision site other infections like pneumonia may occur when stomach contents get into the lungs for this reason we will always keep the head of the bed elevated to a minimum of 30 degrees and make sure you pass a swallow evaluation prior to taking anything by mouth other complications such as bleeding around the incision site and increased swelling may occur and to some extent are expected when your tracheostomy is new swelling and bleeding will be closely monitored by your nurse and care team be sure to let your nurse know of any increased bleeding or if you have trouble breathing

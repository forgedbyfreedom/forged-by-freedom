hello I'm Dr Tiffany Lai a gynecologic oncologist at UCLA today I'd like to discuss with you and provide some information about cervical cancer cervical cancer affects approximately 300 000 women annually with about 14 000 new cases every year cervical cancer is a cancer that arises from the cells of the cervix which is the lower part of the uterus or bloom the majority of cervical cancers are due to an infection with the human papillomavirus or HPV which is a virus that can be transmitted through sexual intercourse women who are at higher risk of developing cervical cancer are those who have prolonged exposure to HPV younger age of sexual activity multiple sexual partners smoking and those who are immunocompromised due to medical conditions such as HIV or immunosuppressive medications following organ transplant many women with early cancers do not have any symptoms and are diagnosed following an abnormal pap smear and biopsy other symptoms associated with cervical cancer are abnormal vaginal bleeding or discharge bleeding after intercourse pelvic pain or changes in bowel movements or urination regular pap smears are important in screening for cervical cancer and cervical dysplasia which is a precursor of cervical cancer when a woman has an abnormal pap smear colposcopy is usually indicated to more closely examine the cervix and perform biopsies which can then diagnose cervical dysplasia or cancer if cervical dysplasia is identified treatment is recommended to remove the abnormal cells before they progress to when cervical cancer is diagnosed treatment is individualized and may include surgery or a combination of chemotherapy and radiation patients with early stage cervical cancer that is confined to the cervix are candidates for surgery which usually entails a hysterectomy or removal of the uterus and cervix as well as removal of the lymph nodes in the pelvis a combination of chemotherapy and radiation is generally recommended for women who may not be healthy enough for surgery or who have large tumors or evidence of spread outside of the cervix following treatment women with a history of cervical cancers are monitored closely due to the risk of recurrence cervical cancer is a preventable cancer in addition to getting routine pap smears HPV vaccination is an important method of cancer prevention and is most effective when given prior to HPV exposure at UCLA we strive for a comprehensive and multi-disciplinary approach to the prevention evaluation and treatment of cervical cancers in order to best individualize care for every

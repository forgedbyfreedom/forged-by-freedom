hi I'm Michael yay from UCLA health endocrine surgery here today to discuss common wound complaints after thyroid or parathyroid surgery by far the most common complaint is something called a healing Ridge this is a firm bump or swelling that forms under the surgical incision starting about two days after surgery that gets more prominent 10 to 14 days after surgery this is normal and everybody has it to some degree a healing Ridge is just your body's normal reaction to surgery as it lays down Scar Tissue initially but don't worry it always goes away gradually usually disappearing six to eight weeks after surgery the second most common wound complaint is itching or a rash forming around the wound and extending to the shoulders and chest you may find that the affected area looks like the shape of a diamond about one percent of people are allergic to the surgical disinfectant that we use to eliminate skin germs prior to surgery the best way to deal with this is to gently cleanse the area with a warm washcloth and a little soap the third most common complaint is an allergic reaction to the adhesive used to stick the bandage to the skin this shows up as redness and itching in a small circle right around the bandage it can be managed using hydrocortisone cream which is available over the counter at the pharmacy if you are having a more serious reaction such as severe swelling of the wound or blistering of the skin please call our office for advice once again this is Michael yay from UCLA health endocrine surgery thank you for watching

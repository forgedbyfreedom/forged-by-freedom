One of the most common questions I hear from patients is, "Why do I have symptoms of hypothyroidism, but my thyroid tests are normal?" It's a great question. It can feel confusing and frustrating when you're not well, but your labs say everything is fine. Let's talk about symptoms of hypothyroidism. These can include feeling tired or sluggish, gaining weight more easily, feeling cold when others are comfortable, constipation, dry skin, or even feeling down or depressed. These can be classic signs that the thyroid might not be working as well as it should. When someone has these symptoms, it makes sense for their doctor to check their thyroid levels. The usual tests are TSH and sometimes free T4. If these test results are not normal, especially if the TSH is high and the thyroid hormone is low, it usually means that the thyroid isn't making enough hormone, that's when we think about starting treatment with thyroid medication such as levothyroxine. Other times, labs are normal, but the symptoms are still there. That's when the conversation gets a little more tricky. When thyroid hormone levels are normal, but the symptoms look like hypothyroidism, we have to ask, is something else causing these symptoms. Because here's the thing, these symptoms aren't specific to thyroid disease. Fatigue, weight gain, constipation, and mood changes can happen for many different reasons. And even though we hope it's the thyroid because that's easy to treat, if your thyroid is doing its job, we need to look deeper. So, what else could be causing these symptoms? We start by considering other medical causes such as anemia or other blood problems, vitamin deficiencies, kidney or liver problems, hormonal changes such as pmenopause, depression or anxiety which can sometimes look like low thyroid symptoms. If all those tests look normal, we also think about lifestyle factors such as nutrition. Are you eating enough healthy food and calories? Physical activity. A lack of activity can cause tiredness and weight changes. Stress. Long-term stress can affect energy, mood, digestion, and sleep. And sleep habits. Poor sleep alone can cause fatigue, mood swings, and weight changes. While it's natural to want a clear answer, something like, "Your thyroid is low. Here's a pill to fix it." The truth is that the body is complex. Sometimes these symptoms come from a mix of smaller issues, not just one clear cause. If your thyroid labs are normal, that's good news. It means your thyroid is working well. But it also means we have to dig deeper. Finding the cause can take time, teamwork with your doctor, and sometimes a little trial and error. If you're having these symptoms and your thyroid levels are normal, don't give up. Keep working with your doctor to explore other possible causes. A full checkup and a big picture look at your health can often lead us in the right direction. Thanks for spending a few minutes with me today.

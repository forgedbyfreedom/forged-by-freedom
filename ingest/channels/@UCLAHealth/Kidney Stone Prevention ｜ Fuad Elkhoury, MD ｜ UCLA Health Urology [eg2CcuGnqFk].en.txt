I'm Dr. Fouad Al Corey, urologic surgeon and kidney stone specialist at UCLA Health. Kidney stones are very painful. They are also preventable. So, let's talk about how to prevent you from getting one. Prevention starts with your diet. Here are some tips. First and foremost, drink lots of fluids. Aim for two to three lers of water per day to maintain a high urine output, which dilutes stone forming minerals. You want your urine to be light yellow to clear. If it's dark, you're not hydrated enough. This is the most effective way to prevent stones. Next, watch your salt intake. High dietary sodium results in higher urinary calcium levels, increasing your stone risk. Aim for under two grams of salt per day. Reduce processed foods and restaurant meals which are often high in salt. Third, and this may sound counterintuitive, eat enough calcium. This helps prevent stones. Calcium binds with oxalate in your gut and keeps your body's calcium at a normal level, which lowers your kidney stone risk. Don't avoid calcium. Low calcium levels increase your kidney stone risk. Aim for around 1 to 1.2 g of calcium per day with foods such as milk, yogurt, and leafy greens like kale. Also, avoid calcium supplements. They provide too much calcium and can increase your stone risk. Animal protein such as beef, chicken, fish, and eggs increases your urine acidity and uric acid levels, which increases your stone risk. Keep animal protein portions small to around 6 ounces per day and enjoy in moderation. Similarly, oxalate rich foods such as spinach, almonds, cashews, beets, black tea, sweet potato, and dark chocolate increase your stone risk. Oxalate is a naturally occurring mineral found in many healthy foods. However, consuming too much, especially without enough calcium, can contribute to stones. Aim to pair foods with oxalate with calcium richch foods during meals to limit oxalate absorption. Finally, add more citrate to your diet. Citrate is a natural inhibitor of kidney stones. It binds with calcium in the urine and prevents crystals from forming. Add lemon or lime to your water. Eat oranges or other citrus fruits. Use vinegar based dressings. These will increase your citrate intake and lower your stone risk. Potassium citrate supplements can also help increase citrate if needed. So, in summary, to prevent kidney stones, you need to drink more fluid, eat less salt, animal protein, and oxalates, eat a normal amount of calcium through your food, and include citrate in your daily diet.

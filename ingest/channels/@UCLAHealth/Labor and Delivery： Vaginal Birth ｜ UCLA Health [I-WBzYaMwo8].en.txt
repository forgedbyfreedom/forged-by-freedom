vulva perineal care is important after a vaginal delivery especially if you have stitches to help prevent infection and increase comfort cleanse the perineal area from front to back each time you urinate or have a bowel movement you may continue to use the peri bottle you used in the hospital apply a clean perry pad each time you use the bathroom apply numbing spray or cream to your perineal area as recommended by your health care provider we don't encourage traditional baths while you're healing but you may enjoy a warm sitz bath several times a day for comfort and healing a sitz bath is a warm shallow bath that soaks your perineum if you have stitches they will dissolve on their own over a few weeks after giving birth your body will be going through some major adjustments here are some things to be mindful of when it comes to vaginal bleeding also called lokia the color will usually be dark red for the first 48 hours followed by light bleeding for up to several weeks after birth your flow will be heavy intermittently it is normal to see several small clots if you have been lying down for several hours you may experience a gush of blood when you first stand up this is to be expected and is no cause for alarm it is possible you will notice heavier bleeding two to three weeks after delivery when there is a shedding of the uterus lining where the placenta was attached it is normal to have bleeding or vaginal discharge for up to six to eight weeks after you may notice leaking of urine when coughing laughing or sneezing this is normal and usually temporary it is important to drink a lot of fluids drink eight glasses of fluid a day and 13 glasses a day if you are breastfeeding your urine should be pale yellow constipation after delivery is very common expect a normal bowel movement by your third or fourth day after delivery to help you return to a normal pattern of bowel movements we recommend you drink plenty of water and include high fiber foods in your diet such as fruits and vegetables prune juice bran and whole grain breads and cereals take stool softeners or laxatives as directed by your health care provider if you have no bowel movement by your fourth day after delivery or you begin having concerns contact your health care provider hemorrhoids are swollen blood vessels in the anal area they are not uncommon in pregnancy symptoms may include itching pain and sometimes bleeding constipation or diarrhea can aggravate hemorrhoids to decrease symptoms eat a diet high in fiber use over-the-counter hemorrhoid cream medication or witch hazel pads a sitz bath may be comforting clean the affected area after every bowel movement with medicated pads or baby wipes ask your provider about additional medications or suppositories if there is no relief of symptoms as you transition back home keep the following in mind you need frequent periods of rest and you should try to take naps whenever you can for the first 10 days we recommend that you concentrate on your own physical needs and the feeding of your infant avoid lifting anything that weighs more than your baby for six weeks the housework and visitors can wait if possible get help with daily chores resume exercise slowly your provider will know what is safest in your own personal case in most cases mild to moderate exercise is recommended no driving until you are pain free and not taking any narcotic pain medication we usually recommend pelvic rest for six weeks pelvic rest means no sexual intercourse tampons or douching it is not recommended to have sexual intercourse until you have physically recovered from your delivery if your vaginal bleeding has stopped and your perineum has healed you might be ready to resume sexual intercourse if you have the desire to do so

muscles are located at the base of the pelvis and serve three basic functions they contribute to tightening and releasing the urethra and anus to control the bladder and bowel they provide support and stability for our abdominal and pelvic organs and they have a role in sexual function functional pelvic floor muscles can contract relax and lengthen imagine the pelvic floor muscles as an elevator Contracting them is like lifting the elevator from the ground floor to the second floor relaxing them is like allowing the elevator to return to the ground floor lengthening them is like lowering the elevator to the basement these muscles should be able to perform these actions both reflexively as well as under our control our diaphragm plays a key role in coordinating with our pelvic floor muscles the diaphragm is our primary breathing muscle it sits above the abdomen at the base of the rib cage it drops when we breathe in allowing our lungs to fill with air and Rises when we breathe out releasing the air the diaphragm is also the main pressure regulator for the trunk giving us a dynamic base enabling our arms to carry objects and our legs to move us through the world let's begin with a simple exercise that will help you identify your pelvic floor muscles find a comfortable position on your back with your legs bent or supported by pillows then give yourself a few moments to relax your body first close your eyes and relax your face and neck muscles let your chest and abdomen relax now allow your back and buttock muscles to let go take a breath in through your nose and let that breath out as you continue breathing place your hands on your abdomen you should feel your abdomen rise as you breathe in and return as you breathe out again breathe in and feel your abdomen rise passively breathe out and feel it fall if you notice your upper chest rising and falling try to focus on allowing the air to fill your lower rib cage and abdomen pause or replay the video to practice this for a few moments before moving on now you are ready to find your pelvic floor muscles these are the muscles you would tighten to hold in gas or to stop the flow of urine take a breath in through your nose as you begin to Exhale tighten your pelvic floor muscles as though you are holding back gas or urine close the vagina or lift the testicles now now inhale and release those muscles again take a slow breath in through your nose and as you begin to Exhale tighten the pelvic floor muscles again slowly inhale and release those muscles repeat this for 10 repetitions remember to allow your abdominal wall to rise passively during inhalation and fall passively with the exhalation once you have mastered this exercise lying down you can try performing it while sitting and once you are confident doing the exercise while sitting you can stand up to practice here are some things to keep in mind while doing this exercise don't hold your breath don't push out your abdomen while tightening your pelvic floor muscles and don't tighten the muscles of the low back buttocks and thighs for pelvic floor strengthening scientific evidence tells us that 60 to 100 contractions per day

Today we will talk about testicular prostheses, an option for men who have lost a testicle due to injury or cancer. In this video I will explain what a testicular prosthesis is, why someone might choose to have one, what the procedure is like, and what to expect during recovery.  A testicular prosthesis is an artificial testicle that is placed in the scrotum.  It's made to look and feel like the real thing, so that it feels complete.  Most prostheses are made of silicone or other materials that are designed to be inside the body.  You may opt for a prosthesis if you have had a testicle removed due to cancer or trauma.  It will not replace hormones or fertility, but it can help the patient feel better about their body and improve self-confidence.  If you are facing testicular cancer, ask your surgeon about the possibility of placing a prosthesis during the operation to remove the testicle.  These are some of the reasons why men decide to have a testicular prosthesis. It helps your body look more natural. It can improve your self-esteem and confidence and can help reduce the feeling of emptiness in the scrotum.  It is a good option during or after testicular cancer treatment. Getting a prosthesis is a personal decision.  You can decide to do it at the time of testicular surgery or later if you were unsure or it wasn't offered to you.  The procedure is simple and usually goes like this.  He will be under anesthesia, so he won't feel anything.  I make a small cut in his scrotum.  The prosthesis is placed inside the scrotum and is carefully positioned ; a stitch ensures that it stays in place. The incision is closed with dissolvable stitches and the procedure lasts between 45 minutes and one hour.  He goes home the same day.  The prosthesis stays in his body forever.  Over time, it should feel like part of your body.  You will be able to resume most daily activities within a few weeks, but we recommend that you avoid lifting heavy objects or doing strenuous exercise during the first month. After surgery you may notice swelling or mild pain, and this will disappear in one or two weeks. Most men recover within two to four weeks.   It is best to avoid sexual relations and intense physical activity during this time to allow for a full recovery.  Most people do very well with a testicular prosthesis.  Complications are rare, but may include infection, movement or damage to the implant, or impacts on healing.

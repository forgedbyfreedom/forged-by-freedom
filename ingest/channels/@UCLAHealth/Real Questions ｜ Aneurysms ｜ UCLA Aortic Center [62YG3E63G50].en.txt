what are the options for treatment of an aortic aneurysm management of patients with aortic aneurysm in all instances involve control of risk factors cigarette smoking is not only associated with the formation of the aneurysm but also increase the risk of rupture cessation of cigarette smoking is essential in patients with conservative management of aortic aneurysms control of blood pressure is also important as uncontrolled hypertension is associated with an increased risk of rupture cholesterol medication or statins have been associated with improved outcomes in patients with aortic aneurysms the use of beta blockers which slows down the heart rate have been suggested to decrease the rate of growth of aortic aneurysms the decision between conservative management or repair of patients with aortic aneurysms takes into account the risk of rupture the patient's risk factors the risk of repair and patients preference in modern practice there are essentially two alternatives for repair of aortic aneurysms surgical repair involves an incision exposure of the affected segment of the aorta and replacement with a prosthetic or artificial graft in the abdomen the incision may be in the middle or in the left flank which avoids exposure of the intestines at ucla we prefer the left flank incision or retroperitoneal approach as we have seen faster recovery and decreased complications an alternative to surgical repair is endovascular repair this involves the insertion of stent grafts to exclude the aneurysm these stent grafts are inserted through usually the groin arteries and in modern practice we can do this percutaneously with minimal incisions endovascular repair has been associated with a decreased mortality and decreased complications it leads to faster recovery when aneurysms involve branches of the aorta stent graft technology can now be applied using fenestrated or branched grafts ucla is participating in clinical trials which gives us access to the latest endovascular technology for the treatment of complex aortic aneurysms a third alternative is to combine endovascular technology with standard open surgical techniques this hybrid approach was pioneered at ucla in 1999 and has allowed us to treat patients that are considered too high risk for surgical repair and are not anatomically suitable for complete endovascular repair

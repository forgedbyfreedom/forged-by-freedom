what is barrett's esophagus barrett's esophagus is a condition associated with chronic backwash of the contents of the stomach into the food pipe that connects the mouth to the stomach called the esophagus this esophagus is usually lined with a lining similar to what we see with our skin a squamous lining however over time with chronic reflux these patients have a change in the lining to better protect against acid from the stomach to an intestinal type mucosa this leads to visible changes on endoscopy as well as under the microscope which were first described by dr norman barrett and thus the condition is called barrett's esophagus barrett's esophagus is associated with an increased risk of developing esophageal adenocarcinoma and the risk increase risk is between 11 and 40 fold compared to those without barrett's esophageal adenocarcinoma is the fastest rising cancer in the united states patients at risk for barrett's esophagus typically have long-standing reflux particularly if it's poorly controlled are male are of caucasian ethnicity and often have a large hiatal hernia

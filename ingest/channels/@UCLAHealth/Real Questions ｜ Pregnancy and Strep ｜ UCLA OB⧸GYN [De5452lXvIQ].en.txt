What is group B strep and why do we screen for it in pregnancy? Group B strep is a common bacteria that colonizes 15 to 40% of women. This bacteria is found in the vagina and it's normal and it is usually asymptomatic for women. However, as the baby is born through the birth canal, you can actually infect the baby with the group Eye strep bacteria. This infection can cause menitis, pneumonia, and some babies can even die from it. Because of this, we check women for group B strep during their um 35th to 37th week of pregnancy. This is done with a vaginal and rectal swab. If you have the bacteria, then you will be treated with antibiotics during labor or if you break your bag of water. This is done in order to prevent transmission of the bacteria to the baby.

can gastroenterologists evaluate the small intestine the small intestine is the longest segment of the gi tract measuring more than 20 feet in most patients it's comprised of three segments the duodenum the jejunum and the ilium this area because of its length has been difficult to access with standard endoscopes in the past but we now have a variety of new techniques that allow us to visualize it fully the first capsule endoscopy involves placing a camera inside of a pill which is swallowed and takes two pictures a second as it travels through the small bowel it has a battery life of eight hours and the pictures are relayed to a box which is then compressed into made into a movie that allows us to visualize the entire small bowel in addition we now have direct scope access to much if not all of the small bowel through a couple of techniques one called balloon and not balloon enteroscopy allows us to access the small bowel using a bootstrapping technique of endoscopy another spiral anaeroscopy allows us to spiral our way deep into the small bowel these techniques allow us to biopsy lesions dilate strictures and treat bleeding vessels that are within the small bowel avoiding the need for surgery in many cases

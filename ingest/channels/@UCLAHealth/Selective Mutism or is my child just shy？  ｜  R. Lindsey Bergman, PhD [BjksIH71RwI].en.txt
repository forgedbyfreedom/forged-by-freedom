People often ask what's the difference between just regular shyness and a child with selective mutism, and it is true that a shy child sometimes won't answer a question when they meet a new person and might just kind of look down, and there is a similarity between the way that child will appear and the way a child with selective mutism will appear, and children with selective mutism are usually shy children, but shy children don't usually have selective mutism. One of the differences is that shyness is a temperamental trait, and it's not a disorder, it doesn't interfere in life, and a shy child will warm up over time, and speak to people that he or she meets. A child with selective mutism, after, you know, 6 months of knowing somebody and getting to know them really well, still does not talk to them. And, you know, it's a pretty drastic difference. If somebody were to suggest that a shy child had selective mutism after meeting the child and knowing them for, you know, a couple of days, and then saying that child has selective mutism, that wouldn't really make any sense. You'd need to know a child very well and still have them not speak to begin to think they had selective mutism because it's just a very different thing, in the sense of how much it causes interference in the child's life. It's a prolonged period of just not warming up and not speaking to somebody despite feeling comfortable with them in other ways.

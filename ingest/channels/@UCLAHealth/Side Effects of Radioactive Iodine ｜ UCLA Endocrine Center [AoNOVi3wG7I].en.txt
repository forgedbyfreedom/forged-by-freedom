hello I'm Dr Masha livitz an endocrine surgeon at UCLA health the main treatment for thyroid cancer is surgery thankfully most patients with thyroid cancer have low risk disease and an excellent prognosis usually surgery is the only treatment needed followed by watching to make sure the tumor doesn't come back however some patients have an increased risk to have the tumor come back for example if your cancer has spread to the lymph nodes in your neck or has grown into the tissues close to the thyroid and those cases we use radioactive iodine to help destroy any remaining cancer cells the most common side effects of radioactive iodine are a dry mouth swelling and tenderness of the salivary glands and changes in taste which are usually temporary chewing gum or sucking on sour candies can help with salivary gland problems radioactive iodine can also reduce tear formation which can cause dry eyes if you wear contact lenses you should remove them during the treatment radioactive iodine can temporarily affect fertility women should avoid getting pregnant for 6 to 12 months after getting radioactive iodine but there are no long-term fertility effects or problems in children born to parents who receive reductive iodine in the past one of the main concerns my patients ask about is the risk of getting another cancer from the radiation treatment there is a very small risk of getting a cancer such as leukemia but this risk is reported in most studies to be less than one percent a person receiving radioactive iodine ablation needs to stay away from others especially young children to avoid exposing others to radiation the decision of whether to use radioactive iodine will be different for each patient looking at the potential side effects versus the risks of thyroid cancer coming back or spreading for more information or to schedule an appointment please give us a call or visit our website thank you for watching

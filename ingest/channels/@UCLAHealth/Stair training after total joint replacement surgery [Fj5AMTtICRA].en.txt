when moving up and down curves steps or stairs you have a few options your physical therapist will guide you about the best choice for you one rule that will apply to all of your options is the saying up with the good down with the bad this simply means that after your surgery it is best to go up the step leading with your good or non-operated leg and when going down the step you will lead with your bed or operated leg first this allows your stronger leg to lift you up the step as well as lower you down the step when you first return home it's also a good idea to receive help when dealing with steps and stairs let's look at the options in more detail when there is only a single step such as a curb or porch threshold entrance you can simply use the walker to assist you bring your walker and your feet as close to the step as possible then carefully put all four legs of the walker up onto the step leading with the good non-operated leg step onto the curb push on the walker and lift up your operated leg will follow to go down the step get close to the edge of the step then carefully lower the entire walker down to the ground push down on the walker with your hands and step down with the bad leg first while your good leg lowers you then bring your good foot down when multiple stairs are involved it is unsafe to use a walker you will be better off doing the steps in the following manner while your caregiver folds and carries the walker for you the best option is to use a railing on one or both sides when available and either a cane or handheld assist from the person helping you when two rails are available you won't need a cane but many people don't have this option when going upstairs you can place one hand on the rail if there is one and your other hand on the cane if needed if you don't have a cane you may hold someone's hand it is best for their hand to be palm up and your hand to be palmed down when they are helping you the person assisting you will go up the stairs in tandem with you from behind for safety be careful to only do one step at a time keep hold of the rail and have your caregiver unfold and place the walker back in front of you at the top of the stairs when going down the stairs you will continue to use a cane or handheld assistance place your hand on the railing with the cane in the other hand if needed while your caregiver manages the walker lower the cane and step down to the step below with the operated leg first using your good leg to lower you repeat the sequence being careful to only do one step at a time keep a hold of the rail until your walker is replaced in front of you you

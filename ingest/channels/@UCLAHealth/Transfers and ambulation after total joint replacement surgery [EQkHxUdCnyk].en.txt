in this section we will review the best way to stand up and walk with the walker immediately after surgery and how to safely progress towards walking normally you will be using a front wheeled walker right after surgery when walking once you are seated at the edge of the bed have the front wheeled walker close by and move it directly in front of you both of your feet should be touching the floor with the foot of the non-operated leg right underneath the knee keep the foot of your operated leg slightly forward from your body as it will make it a little easier to stand up next place one or both hands on a stable surface like the bed or chair so you can push off to help yourself up it is okay for one hand to be on the front wheeled walker as long as you don't try to pull yourself up on it doing so may cause the walker to tip over lean forward slightly and push up with your arms letting your non-operated leg do more of the work stand up straight place both hands on the walker and slide your operated leg back as you stand so your feet are firmly underneath your body make sure you are not experiencing any dizziness or leg wobbling before trying to walk if feeling well start by pushing the walker forward followed by taking a step forward with your operated leg then step your non operated leg forward to meet it continue to take steps in this sequence after several steps if your pain is controlled and your operated leg feels strong you can start walking more normally these are the five criteria that determine if it's safe to walk without the help of your walker you are walking with a steady gait pattern you are not limping on the operated leg you have good balance your pain is under control on the operated leg you are just pushing the walker as you walk meeting all five of these criteria may be a sign that you're ready to get off the walker and transition to a cane or nothing at all but you can wait until your surgeon clears you from discontinuing your use of the front wheeled walker if you experience any of these symptoms again you may not be ready to transition from using the walker always play it safe and use the walker until you're ready to walk without it to prevent any injury harm or falls you

my name is Sarah Larsen I'm hematologist oncologist at UCLA one of the questions I'm frequently asked is what is immunotherapy immunotherapy is when we use the patient's own immune system in order to fight cancer there are multiple different approaches that we can use in immunotherapy what could be considered the ultimate immune therapy is an allogeneic stem cell transplant that's when we take stem cells from another person and give them to a patient this provides a whole new immune system another approach is engineered cell therapy this is when we take a patient cells we modify them and then we give them back to the patient once they've been modified with that anti-cancer therapy there's also more off of the shelf therapies such as monoclonal antibodies which you may recognize because they're used across a variety of cancers just like any therapy immunotherapy has potential benefits as well as side effects and the majority of immunotherapy the benefit is that the patient will experience long-term disease control and in some instances even cure which is the intent of an allogeneic stem cell transplant the side effects are specific to each of the immunotherapies I recommend that patients ask physicians where immune therapy fits into their treatment plan thank you you

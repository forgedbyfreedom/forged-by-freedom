Michael Yay here from the Endocrine Center here to talk about what to expect on the day of parathyroid surgery. First, we'll have you arrive about 1 and 1/2 hours ahead of your scheduled time after having fasted for approximately 10 hours. You'll be given a private place to change and a nurse will place an IV into your arm. Thereafter, you'll meet the various members of the team. the nurses, anesthesiologists, and of course, the surgical team will stop by to say hello. After the anesthesiologist finishes asking you a series of questions, you'll be given a medication to help you relax and feel a little bit drowsy. Then, you'll be rolled back to the operating room. The next thing you'll remember is waking up. You'll feel a little bandage on your neck and perhaps a little numbness around your ears and your jawline because of some medication we give here in the side of the neck to prevent pain. Over the next 4 hours, you'll be under observation. During that time, we're careful to take care of any pain or nausea you may have. You'll be given something to drink initially and then something more substantial to eat. At the 4-hour mark, you'll be ready for discharge. You'll be given your discharge medications as well as your instruction packet. Your follow-up appointment will be already scheduled for you. This is about 2 weeks uh after the surgery date and is typically done over video. If the time is not convenient for any reason, feel free to change it. A small number of patients, those who live far away or those who live alone, are asked to stay overnight in the surgery center. If that happens, most of those patients go home around 6:00 or 7 in the morning.

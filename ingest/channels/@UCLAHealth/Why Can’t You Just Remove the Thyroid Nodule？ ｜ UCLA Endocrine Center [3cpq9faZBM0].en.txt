Michael Yay here from the Endocrine Center addressing the question, why can't you just remove the thyroid nodule? This is a question we get all the time. There are three reasons. First of all, thyroid nodules are actually embedded within the thyroid. It's not like a like a fruit hanging off of a tree. Most of them are fairly buried within the thyroid and hard to separate. One, the second is remember there's a nonzero chance that that nodule could be cancerous. Right? So how do we tell how does the pathologist tell whether or not a given nodule is cancerous? One thing they inspect is the boundary, the interface between the nodule itself and the neighboring thyroid tissue. So we have to give them that margin for them to assess. The third and most important reason why we take out usually half of the thyroid instead of just the nodule is what if the nodule turns out to be cancerous? What if it turns out to have spread a little further than we initially imagined? Well, if we take out just the nodule, that would mean that we have to then reoperate on the patient on the same side of the neck that we did before. The danger of that is now there's scar tissue there and scar tissue makes critical structures like nerves and parathyroids harder to see, right? So, we'd rather completely clear out a side. That way, we know that we're never going to have to return to a scarred field in that area. That protects the patient against downstream risks that they may not be thinking of today. But, as responsible surgeons, we're thinking of all the possible things that could happen to you down the line. And we want to keep you safe even from those consequences.

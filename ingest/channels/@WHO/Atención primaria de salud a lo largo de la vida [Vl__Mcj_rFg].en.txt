Primary health care addresses the health needs of individuals throughout their lives, from welcoming a child into the world to providing services such as vaccines that protect us and our communities from infectious diseases. It supports us in childhood, adolescence, and adulthood with services that help us stay healthy, such as guidance on good nutrition and regular physical activity. It allows for close monitoring of risk factors, diagnostic testing, and can confirm some diagnoses without leaving the local community—at a rural midwife's post, a family doctor's office, an urban clinic run by a group of doctors, a social worker in a community center, or a pharmacy around the corner. Primary health care treats and manages various conditions through regular procedures, medication prescriptions, and prevention, especially for chronic conditions such as hypertension, diabetes, and asthma, as well as mental health and urgent care needs. provides services to everyone, caring for their health and managing their needs confidentially, in collaboration with regular healthcare providers and family members.

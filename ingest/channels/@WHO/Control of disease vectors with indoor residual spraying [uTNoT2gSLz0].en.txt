indoor residual spraying or IRS is one of two interventions recommended by who for large scale used to prevent malaria along with insecticide treated nets for IRS insecticides are sprayed inside houses and other buildings where disease transmitting insects are likely to rest these insecticides remain active for months and when certain insects like mosquitoes land on the sprayed surface the insecticide kills them IRS is used in a number of different situations which include large-scale repeated campaigns in endemic areas targeted spraying in forai in low transmission settings in response to imported cases in non-endemic areas or to protect populations affected by humanitarian emergencies IRS has been widely used to kill aniles mosquitoes that spread malaria but it can also be used to kill other insects that that transmit diseases to humans these insects known as vectors include IIs mosquitoes that transmit viruses that cause Deni Chikungunya yellow fever and zika virus disease

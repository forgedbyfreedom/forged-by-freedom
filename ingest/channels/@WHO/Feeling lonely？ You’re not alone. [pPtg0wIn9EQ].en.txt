What if a major health threat we've so far overlooked isn't a virus or a disease, but social disconnection? Human beings are wired to connect. From the moment we're born, our health, physical, mental, and social, depends on the relationships around us. Health isn't just the absence of disease. It's physical, mental, and social well-being. But across the globe, social disconnection is widespread. One in six people worldwide experience loneliness. And though it affects everyone, it's most common among young people and in lowincome countries. Science tells us that loneliness and isolation are linked to heart disease, depression, and even early death, killing 100 people every hour. This isn't just a personal issue. It's a public health challenge. And we can do something about it. The WH Commission on Social Connection has released a groundbreaking report. It shows what works from national policies and community programs to digital tools when used wisely and everyday human kindness. It offers a path forward for governments, schools, workplaces, and each of us. Because social connection isn't a luxury, it's a lifeline. And by making it a global priority, we can build healthier, more resilient societies together.

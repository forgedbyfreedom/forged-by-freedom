here are five things you should know about MERS MERS or the Middle East respiratory syndrome is a viral respiratory illness that is caused by a corona virus and was first identified in humans in Saudi Arabia in 2012 MERS is a zoonotic disease which means that it is transmitted between animals and people dromedary camels or one humped camels are the reservoir hosts for the MERS virus and they are the main source of infection in humans infection can occur through either direct or indirect contact with infected dromedary camels human-to-human transmission is possible there has been limited human to human transmission among family members in households however human to human transmission has repeatedly shown to be amplified in healthcare settings especially when infection prevention and control measures are inadequate signs and symptoms of MERS are nonspecific with a clinical spectrum ranging from no symptoms or mild respiratory symptoms to severe acute respiratory disease and death this makes it difficult to detect cases early and may lead to delays in isolation testing and care and the spread of the disease between people in healthcare settings currently there is no specific treatment for MERS it is supportive based on clinical presentation infection prevention and control measures are critical to prevent the spread of human-to-human transmission in healthcare settings they include standard precautions on triage procedures for all patients droplets and airborne precautions are required when performing aerosol generating procedures for more information about MERS please visit the MERS page on wh OHS website or consult the infectious disease handbook

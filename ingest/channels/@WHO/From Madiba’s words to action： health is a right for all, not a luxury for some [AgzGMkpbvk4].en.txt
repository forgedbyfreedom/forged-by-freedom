Miba said that health cannot be a question of income. It's a fundamental human right. And yet more than half the world's people in countries both rich and poor cannot access the health services they need. And two billion people face financial hardship from out of pocket health expenditures. These inequities apply to many diseases and conditions including cancer. Every year 10 million people die from cancer. Many of these deaths are preventable. The burden is rising fastest in low-income countries and communities where health systems are weakest and life-saving innovations are often out of reach. WHO and our partners are working to narrow these gaps by supporting more than 100 countries to expand access to treatment for children with cancer in low-income communities. Together with St. Jud Children's Research Hospital, we're working to deliver free medicines to at least 120,000 children by 2028. We're also working toward the elimination of cervical cancer in Africa. First dose HPV vaccine coverage has increased from 8% in 2017 to 47% in 2024. Thank you all for your ongoing commitment to taking forward MBA legacy and his vision.

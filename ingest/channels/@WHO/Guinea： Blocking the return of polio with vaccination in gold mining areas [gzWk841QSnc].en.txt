Guinea was a country that had been polio-free since 2015. The entire African region has been certified polio-free on August 25, 2020. But, we see in several countries a new importation and recirculation of different variants of poliomyelitis virus. There's a special feature for Guinea which is that it is the first country in Africa to have co-circulation of type 2 and type 3 polioviruses. And all this takes place in a very difficult, overcrowded area, a mining area with a mix of populations coming from neighbouring countries for gold mining. The living and working conditions on these gold mining sites lead to numerous health and environmental risks. These sites are considered to be home to many diseases that affect the whole country. But most of these diseases can be prevented by vaccination. My husband doesn't want to hear about vaccinating our children. So I get them vaccinated without telling him. Involvement of the women who come to these sites has facilitated the uptake and vaccination of children at these gold panning sites. The innovation that has been introduced is the use of GTS. A software in these phones helps to track the vaccination coverage in real-time. We see where vaccinators have gone. In this way, we can identify areas where vaccinators have not been present. And we communicate to health center managers so that they can direct the vaccinators in order to reach all children in those areas not yet covered. We believe that with this quality vaccination and the other vaccination missions to come, we will be able to interrupt the circulation of the polio virus very quickly in this area, which borders on neighbouring countries like Mali and Côte d'Ivoire, with all those risks, with all these movements of populations of importing polio viruses into other countries.

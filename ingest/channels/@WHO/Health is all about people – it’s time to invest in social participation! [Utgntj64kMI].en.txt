the kovite 19 pandemic has increased how societies value health we have never been so aware of government actions to protect our health and well-being yet too often people's insights and lived experiences remained unheard in government decision making governments must make a targeted effort to change this amplifying people's voices in health policy making by using participatory spaces as part of decision-making processes is the way forward if these spaces are well designed and used regularly they have a transformative potential to become a potent tool for more responsive and implementable policies there are many ways governments can engage with the population to capture their needs mechanisms such as open forums citizen juries consultative meetings and district health committees with fixed seats for civic and community groups are all methods of engagement but government resources and capacities are too often lacking to manage these spaces effectively the whole handbook on social participation for uhc provides practical guidance for policy makers to engage with the population by creating strengthening and sustaining participatory spaces policy makers must learn how to design spaces to empower people to express themselves frankly several examples drawn from countries experiences are laid out in the handbook on how to do this for example define clear criteria to select participants be explicit about roles and the added value each participant brings to the policy question communicate rules and mandates transparently to all participants transparent communication lends legitimacy to each participant and values inputs equally this is especially crucial for community and civil society groups more influential stakeholders such as academia medical doctors and government actors derive legitimacy from their professional positions or standing in society therefore a targeted effort is needed to lend legitimacy to those whose voices are generally less heard it's time to invest in social participation it's time to move towards regular and systematic public and civil society engagement for health decision making it's time to build a health system that belongs to the people and responds to people's needs where no one is left behind you

The 8th edition of the International Lead Poisoning Prevention Week aims to raise awareness of the impact of lead exposure on human health, particularly in children, and to promote measures to address it. This global event takes place annually, and in 2020 it ran from October 25th to 31st. The entire week was dedicated to the urgent need to eliminate lead from paint to prevent lead poisoning in children and workers. Despite the current challenges in combating lead poisoning, every effort must be made to address the devastating effects of lead exposure on human health, especially in children. Lead has detrimental effects on a child's brain development, causing a decrease in IQ, behavioral changes, reduced attention span and orientation, antisocial behavior, and declining educational levels. Its effects persist into adulthood, with long-term detrimental repercussions.  The economic and social situation of individuals, or lead paint, constitutes an avoidable source of lead exposure. It is possible to manufacture paints that do not contain lead; safe paints exist. It is time to ban lead paint. Currently, only 75 countries have implemented legally binding controls on lead paints, and we commend them for this. However, we want all countries in the world to do the same. That is why the WHO urges all other countries, manufacturers, companies in the sector, and civil society to do everything possible to eliminate lead from paints. We must end the use of lead paint worldwide, and this is an achievable goal. Leaded gasoline has been eliminated; it is now time to ban lead paint.

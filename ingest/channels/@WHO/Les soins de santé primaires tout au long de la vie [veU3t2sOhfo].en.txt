Primary healthcare addresses the majority of people's health needs throughout their lives. It intervenes from the moment a child is born with services like vaccination, which protects each of us and our communities from infectious diseases. Primary healthcare supports us through childhood, adolescence, and adulthood with services that keep us healthy, such as advice on good nutrition and physical activity. It closely monitors risk factors, offers diagnostic tests, and can confirm these diagnoses locally in your community, whether at a rural midwifery post, a family doctor's office, a city clinic with a group of doctors and a social worker, a community center, or the pharmacy on the corner. Primary healthcare provides treatment for certain conditions and prescribes medications. It also helps prevent chronic diseases such as hypertension, diabetes, and asthma, in addition to addressing mental health needs and providing emergency care.

All wars and outbreaks end eventually wounds heal, homes are rebuilt. But the mental health impact of emergencies often goes unnoticed and untreated. That's why this World Mental Health Day, WHO is drawing attention to mental health in humanitarian emergencies. One in five people in such emergencies suffer from a mental health condition beyond food, water and medicine. They also need mental health and psychosocial support to cope, recover and rebuild. That means integrating mental health care into general health services, training community workers to deliver it, and breaking down barriers including stigma, cost, and language. Around the world, who is working with governments, partners, and communities to make that happen because there is no health without mental health.

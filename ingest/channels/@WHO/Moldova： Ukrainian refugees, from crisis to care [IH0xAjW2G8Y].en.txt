When war broke out in Ukraine, Muldova quickly became a safe heaven for hundreds of thousand people fleeing violence. Muldova is a small country that shares border with Ukraine and communities stepped up immediately with compassion to help. Among the many stories of resilience is that of a family from Nikolive. Three generations of women who arrived in Muldova with little more than fear. They were welcomed not only with shelter but also bitter health care and psychological support they desperately needed. I can't forget little Roberto who bravely continued his fight against cancer at the oncology institute in Kishino. communities across Muldova worked tirelessly to support refugees, providing essential services from emergency medical care to safe transport and trauma support. Hospitals were quickly prepared to handle influx of patients thanks to national efforts to improve emergency care capacity. Over 1600 healthcare workers were trained by World Health Organization on basic and advanced emergency care, trauma management and disaster response. These trainings help Muldovan doctors and nurses become more confident and capable in handling both day-to-day emergencies and larger crisis. In one hospital in the city of Kosher, health staff use the knowledge gained to manage a major traffic accident. The investment in local expertise is not limited to Muldova as Muldovan medical professionals are now sharing their knowledge with colleagues in Ukraine and Central Asia helping to build stronger, more connected health care systems across the region. Moldova showed how even a small country with limited resources can make a big difference when people come together. The lessons learned and capacities built are here to stay. They have created more resilient health system. One that not only protects lives during crisis but also improves everyday care for all.

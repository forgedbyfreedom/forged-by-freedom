Honorable Chairman Seong-gu Seong, distinguished members of the delegation, I am honored to join the World Health Assembly to overcome COVID-19 and create new hope. First, I would like to convey my sincere gratitude to the many people around the world who are watching COVID-19 closely. I also extend my deep respect and gratitude to the epidemic prevention and control teams in all countries who are dedicating themselves to the health and safety of humanity. Today, I would like to introduce the path of freedom for all chosen by the people of the Republic of Korea to defeat COVID-19. Korea was not one of the first countries to face COVID-19, and we had to quickly find solutions to respond to this aggressive virus. Furthermore, in this moment of crisis, the Korean people made a bold choice. They expanded the freedom held by individuals into freedom for all. Instead of viewing this virus as a dangerous object for spreading it and blockading and eliminating it, they protected the safety of their neighbors first for their own safety. To move freely and continue economic activities, they voluntarily wore masks and participated wholeheartedly in social distancing. Even amidst social distancing, our hearts grew closer. Women devoted their utmost efforts through volunteering, and citizens gave birth through sharing. In the nationwide general election, strict epidemic prevention measures  Despite the difficulties, over one million voters participated in the election in 2009. Recording a voter turnout even higher than usual, we created a festival of democracy without a single infected person in the capital. The scope of our neighborly relations extended beyond national borders. While continuing exchanges without disrupting state affairs, we shared protective equipment, including diagnostic kits and masks, as circumstances permitted. Thanks to the citizens who became the main agents of disease control by practicing the spirit of freedom for all through high deliberation, the three major principles of openness, transparency, and democracy were able to demonstrate their strength. The government also supported the citizens' efforts through rapid and widespread diagnostic testing and creative methods. However, Korea has not yet achieved a complete victory. Although we are striving to ensure that daily life and vitality coexist by transitioning to a "Social Grid" and "Life Grid 2B" model, sporadic collective consciousness persists. Furthermore, the global pandemic continuing abroad poses a threat. If treatments and vaccines are not prepared, another pandemic may occur. However, we clearly know that by sharing information and cooperating together, we can overcome what the virus possesses.  It is the power unique to humanity. Although COVID-19 threatens even the confined spirit of freedom shared by humanity, solidarity and cooperation based on that spirit of freedom are the most powerful weapons that will win the war against it. Chairman, Representatives of Each Country 1, distinguished guests, I would like to make three proposals based on the spirit of freedom for all, so that the world can overcome the current crisis and prepare for the future. First, we must expand humanitarian assistance to regional countries and share our experiences. We must cooperate together to ensure that not a single person is left behind until everyone is free from COVID-19. The World is planning a total of 200 million humanitarian aid this year. We will also continue to share the experience and data we have accumulated with the international community, ranging from crisis response to football and books. We will actively cooperate with the WHO to protect the health of humanity together. Second, we must cooperate across borders for the development of vaccines and treatments. Developed vaccines and treatments should be distributed equitably across the world as public goods for global health. Korea fully supports the efforts of the WHO to develop vaccines and treatments. Korea [is] one of the world's 110 [areas]  We are participating as a sharing nation in the Global Fund, the international purchasing organization for medicines, and the International Vaccine Institute, and starting this year, we plan to contribute to the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI). Third, we must rapidly prepare and ensure the sustainability of related measures, including the WHO International Evangelical Conjecture. We must implement measures to respond more swiftly and effectively to emerging infectious disease crises that could arise at any time. Efforts are needed to disclose relevant information, such as disease outbreaks, more transparently among nations and to jointly establish low-alert systems and cooperation frameworks. I hope that the cooperation measures discussed at the G20 Summit and the Voice of Asian Plus Summit can be further concretized. Honorable Secretary-General Uisang and distinguished delegates, in the face of crisis, humanity must choose solidarity and cooperation rather than individual survival. The longer the crisis lasts, the more the world must respond with mutual trust and tolerance. If the international community more firmly shares the values ​​of freedom for all, I believe we can accelerate the overcoming of this current crisis and hold even greater hope for the post-COVID era. Thank you.

And diabetes is on the rise worldwide. One in eleven adults is diabetic, a proportion that continues to grow. The risk of developing diabetes is higher if you don't follow a healthy diet and exercise regularly. But what is diabetes? The body produces insulin, which transforms the carbohydrates we consume into energy. Without insulin, sugar from food and drinks accumulates in the blood. Most people affected have type 2 diabetes. This means that the body produces insulin, but it is not effective. A smaller number of people have type 1 diabetes because they cannot produce insulin on their own; they need insulin injections to survive. More than 400 million people are diabetic and face increased risks due to their high blood sugar levels. Diabetes can lead to heart attacks, strokes, kidney failure, and amputations. Now more than ever, children are at risk of developing diabetes. We must encourage everyone to choose appropriate foods and drinks, avoid overeating, stay active, and engage in sustained physical exercise for half an hour daily. Any activity is beneficial. Those who think they may have diabetes should consult a doctor, follow their instructions, and take any prescribed medications.  By prescribing these measures, we can slow down diabetes and overcome this disease.

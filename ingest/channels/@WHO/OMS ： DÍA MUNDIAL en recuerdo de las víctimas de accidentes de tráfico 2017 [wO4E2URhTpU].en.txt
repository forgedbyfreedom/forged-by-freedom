On this World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, we once again turn our thoughts to the millions who have lost loved ones on the world's roads this year. Every day, the media brings us news of tragic events, behind which are grieving families and friends whose lives have been forever changed. This is undoubtedly a time to remember and acknowledge that much more must be done now to prevent this tragic loss of life. Two years ago, governments around the world set the ambitious goal of reducing road traffic deaths and injuries by 50% by 2020. The Lifesaver Road Safety Technical Package, published in May of this year by the WHO in partnership with members of the UN Collaborative Group, provides guidance to governments and their partners on how to move towards this goal with its six strategies and 22 interventions. The package prioritizes what works and saves lives, and includes measures for the design of safe roads and vehicles, speed management, enforcement of traffic laws, and emergency care for victims after an accident. The package also underscores the importance of leadership.  Key to success is the establishment of targets and performance indicators in the areas of road safety. This ongoing process will facilitate rapid progress towards safer roads. Once completed, governments will know how far they have come and how far they need to go to save millions of lives. The best way to honor the memory of those who have suffered on the world's roads is for governments to fulfill the commitments they made at the outset of the Road Safety Action Plan 2011-2020 and for the Sustainable Development Goals before it is too late. My colleagues and I wish you a successful World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims.

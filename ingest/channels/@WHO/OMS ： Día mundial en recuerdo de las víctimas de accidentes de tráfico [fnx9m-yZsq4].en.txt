Today marks World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims. As every year, we commemorate the more than 1.2 million people who die each year on our roads, as well as the millions more who are injured. Many solutions exist to this calamity caused by human beings. Countries must accelerate the pace at which they take action to improve laws and their enforcement to address risk factors such as speed, to redesign roads with infrastructure that protects vulnerable users, such as sidewalks with curbs, and to ensure that vehicles worldwide are equipped with life-saving technologies. This year, World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims highlights the fact that after a road traffic accident, there is a huge opportunity to save lives and reduce the risk of disability. Countries can achieve this by ensuring timely emergency medical assistance, medical treatment, psychological support, and rehabilitation for injured patients. They must also investigate accidents and ensure justice for injured patients and the families of those killed in road traffic accidents. Too many countries fail to provide an adequate response after such accidents.  Road traffic accidents are a serious problem, and this is regrettable, as it is estimated that adequate emergency care could save half a million lives each year. To achieve Sustainable Development Goal 3.6 by 2020 and A-50% reduction in the number of deaths and injuries on our roads, we must work to ensure these measures are adopted. On this day of remembrance, we commemorate, in the best way possible, those who have lost their lives, urgently taking the necessary steps to make all our roads safe. Together with my colleagues, I wish you a successful Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims. Thank you very much.

On this World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, our thoughts once again go out to the millions of people who have lost loved ones on the world's roads this year. Every day, the media brings us news of tragic events affecting families and friends whose lives are changed forever. It is indeed important to remember and to recognize that much more must be done to prevent such loss of life. Two years ago, governments around the world set the ambitious goal of reducing road traffic deaths and injuries by 50% by 2020. Published in May of this year by the WHO and its partners, the Technical Module for Road Safety: Saving Lives guides governments and their partners on how to progress towards this goal. With its six strategies and 22 interventions, the module has done the difficult work of prioritizing what works. This includes road and vehicle design, speed management, traffic law enforcement, and the provision of emergency care to victims following an accident. This is a process underway this year; once finalized, it will mean that governments will see the progress already made and the work that remains to be done to save millions of lives. The best way to honor the memory of those who have suffered on the world's roads would be for governments to fulfill the commitments they made at the beginning of the Decade of Action for Road Safety 2011-2020 and the Sustainable Development Goals before it is too late. My colleagues and I wish you a happy International Day of Remembrance.

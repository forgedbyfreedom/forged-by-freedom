Today is World Road Traffic Victims Remembrance Day. As every year, on this occasion, our thoughts go out to the 1.2 million people killed and the millions of others seriously injured on the world's roads. Solutions exist to eradicate this human scourge. Countries must accelerate their efforts to improve laws and their enforcement regarding risks such as speeding, redesign roads with protective infrastructure like sidewalks, and ensure that all vehicles are equipped with life-saving technologies. This year's World Road Traffic Victims Remembrance Day highlights the fact that even when a traffic accident occurs, there is enormous potential to save lives and reduce disability. To achieve this, countries must implement timely emergency care, medical support, psychological assistance, and rehabilitation for the injured. Too many countries still have n't implemented this much-needed post-accident care, which is deeply regrettable, as it is estimated that appropriate emergency care in all countries could save half a million lives each year to help achieve the goal of Sustainable development aims to reduce the number of people killed and injured on our roads by 50% by 2020. We must ensure that these measures are taken on this World Day of Remembrance, paying tribute in the best possible way to those who have lost their lives and taking the necessary urgent steps to make our roads safer for everyone. My colleagues and I wish you a successful World Day of Remembrance.

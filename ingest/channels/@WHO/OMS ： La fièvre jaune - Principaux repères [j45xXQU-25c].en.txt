So yellow fever is a viral disease caused by a virus transmitted by a mosquito of the Aedes genus, Aedes aegypti. It's a disease characterized in the first few days by something quite common: a fever with aches and pains all over, a headache, fatigue, and general malaise. But after a few days, either people recover completely, or they enter the phase where jaundice develops—hence the name yellow fever—and in some cases, it progresses to a phase where there are hemorrhages and people die. Not everyone dies, of course, but it's still a very serious disease for which there is no specific treatment. That's why it's very important to be vaccinated. The yellow fever vaccine is very effective; firstly, it's administered in a single dose.  The injection provides very long-lasting protection, at least 10 years, but can potentially last a lifetime. It's one of the best vaccines currently available because the protection is so long-lasting and there are virtually no side effects. Therefore, it's very important to get vaccinated, especially when traveling to countries where yellow fever is endemic—that is, countries where the virus is known to circulate in mosquitoes or where monkeys carry the virus. So, it's crucial to get vaccinated before traveling to these countries.

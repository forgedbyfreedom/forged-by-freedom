Over the past 15 years, we have seen a dramatic decline in the global burden of malaria. Among the progress achieved are a 37% decrease in new malaria cases, a 60% decrease in malaria deaths, and a 65 % decrease in deaths of children under 5. Ten countries where malaria was prevalent in 2000 are now free of it, and nearly 60 countries have reduced the number of cases by at least 6%. The Millennium Development Goal target for malaria has been met. This progress has been achieved thanks to a massive expansion of effective malaria control tools. Mosquitoes have protected millions of people from infection, disease, and death. Rapid diagnostic tests have made it possible to provide the right treatment in a timely manner, and every day Artemisia inine-based combination therapies save lives. However, there is still a long way to go. In 2015 alone, more than 400,000 people died from malaria, and there were 214 million new cases in Africa, one in 100 children is still not protected by mosquito nets or indoor residual sprays, and about half of women at risk of malaria do not receive a single dose of preventive treatment. Malaria can be prevented and it can be treated. To accelerate action towards elimination, the World Health Assembly adopted the Global Technical Strategy for Malaria. This strategy follows the same timeline as the Sustainable Development Goals. It sets ambitious but achievable targets for 2030: reduce the number of malaria cases by at least 90 %.  reduce mortality due to malaria by at least 90%.  To eliminate malaria in at least 35 countries and prevent its re-emergence in all countries, global funding for malaria control will need to be tripled compared to current levels, reaching US$8.7 billion per year by 2030. United with all our partners, we can defeat malaria and transform the health and lives of millions of people worldwide.

Influenza is a potentially fatal respiratory illness. When a new influenza virus emerges, it can lead to a pandemic. The Ministry of Health coordinates a network of public health laboratories. The RICE guide detects new influenza viruses and monitors the evolution of existing viruses. The RICE guide provides information, analyses, and materials to manufacturers of vaccines, antivirals, and diagnostics. In 2011, all WHO member states adopted the Pandemic Influenza Preparedness Framework (PIPE). Its objective is to accelerate exchanges with the Swiss Guizhou Health Network (GUI-Swiss) and to provide more equitable access to vaccines and other benefits. The Partnership Contribution is an important element of the PIPE framework. It is an annual financial contribution to the WHO from manufacturers using the Guizhou Hills. The WHO uses its funds to finance projects in countries where pandemic influenza preparedness is insufficient. The projects aim to strengthen laboratories and surveillance. Manufacturers who pay the contributions ensure continued access to high-quality influenza materials and information.  In its analyses and reports, the WHO is silent on the matter. A questionnaire is sent to manufacturers to determine if they are contributing. Manufacturers identified as contributors receive a form allowing the WHO to calculate individual contributions using a mathematical formula. This formula was developed in collaboration with the pharmaceutical industry. Partnership contributions allow the world to be better prepared against the next flu pandemic. More information on the partnership contribution is available on the Pipeline website.

This year marks the first World Hepatitis Day, which aims to highlight the tragic consequences of this global public health threat. These are the facts: hepatitis affects everyone, everywhere. It has multiple forms and is capable of devastating lives worldwide. One in three people has been infected with a hepatitis virus, and one in twelve will become chronically infected, often suffering long-term health problems, particularly cirrhosis or liver cancer. Other consequences include job loss, discrimination, and stigmatization. Viral hepatitis is one of the most prevalent serious infectious diseases, yet many ignore the enormous number of human lives it claims. It is a problem that affects every country and every age group. Every year, more than one million people die from this disease. Because it often presents no symptoms, many people are unaware they have it and unknowingly transmit the virus to others. The virus can be passed from person to person in several ways and is also transmitted through contaminated food and water. We can protect ourselves in many ways.  The most important thing to prevent and control this life-threatening disease is to be well-informed. Talk to your doctor to get tested. Don't forget that hepatitis is treatable and sometimes curable. It's easy to prevent: never share needles or syringes, and always use protection when having sex. Remember, there's no reason to be ashamed of having hepatitis. Infected people have the same fundamental human rights as anyone else. This is hepatitis: know it, face it. Don't let hepatitis ruin your life.

This year marks the first World Hepatitis Day to raise awareness of the scale of this public health threat. Learn more about this disease. Hepatitis affects everyone on Earth. It comes in many forms and can destroy a life. Worldwide, one in three people is infected with hepatitis, and one in twelve has it chronically. This often results in long-term health problems such as cirrhosis and liver cancer, job loss, discrimination, and stigmatization. Although viral hepatitis is one of the most common and serious infectious diseases in the world, many people are unaware of the heavy toll it takes on human health. It is a problem found in every country and in every age group. Every year, more than a million people die from the disease. Since there are often no symptoms, most people are unaware they have the disease and unknowingly spread it. Hepatitis can be transmitted from person to person. In other ways, as well as through contaminated food and water, there are many ways to protect yourself. The best protection against this potentially fatal disease is to know more about it. Talk to your doctor about it. After all, there is treatment for hepatitis, and sometimes it can be cured. Prevention is easy: never share needles or syringes, and always protect yourself during sexual intercourse. Remember, there is no shame in having hepatitis. People infected with the disease have the same basic human rights as anyone else. Learn about hepatitis, face it head-on, and don't let it destroy your life.

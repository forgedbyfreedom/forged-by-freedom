[Music] All around us, in our homes and near the food we eat, live tiny, mysterious, and very energetic creatures. They are much smaller. These are microorganisms, very, very small living beings. There are three different types of microorganisms. The good ones help produce foods and drinks like cheese and yogurt, or fermented meats and vegetables. There are other microorganisms that are annoying; these are the bad ones. They make food spoil, smell bad, taste unpleasant, and turn brown, but they don't usually cause illness. The third type of microorganism is the one that should scare us. They do n't make food smell bad or turn brown, but they are very dangerous, and we need to be careful with them. They are called pathogenic microorganisms, and they can cause serious illnesses such as stomach aches, vomiting, diarrhea, fever, and even death. They are cunning, invisible, and dangerous. These tiny beings are very, very prolific. To reproduce, they need food, water, time, and warmth. They multiply very quickly. Luckily, although they seem strong and frightening, these microorganisms can be avoided. And they are vulnerable. You just need to follow five simple rules, five key behaviors to get rid of them.  Protect yourselves and your family from illnesses you can contract at home. It's easy to apply the rules. These are the five keys to food safety: maintain cleanliness to prevent contamination; separate raw and cooked foods so they don't contaminate each other; cook food thoroughly to kill microorganisms; keep food at safe temperatures (hot or cold) so bacteria don't multiply; use safe water and raw materials to avoid contamination. Food producers are primarily responsible for ensuring the food we buy is safe, but it's possible to protect ourselves and our families from illnesses contracted at home by following these food safety rules. Apply them; there's a lot you can do.

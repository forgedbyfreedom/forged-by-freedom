Influenza, also known as the flu, is a potentially fatal respiratory illness. When a new influenza virus emerges, it can lead to a pandemic. The WHO coordinates a network of public health laboratories known as KISS Risk, which identifies new influenza viruses and monitors the evolution of existing ones. KISS Risk provides detailed information, analyses, and materials on a regular basis to manufacturers of antiviral vaccines and diagnostics. In 2011, all WHO member countries adopted the Pandemic Influenza Preparedness Framework. This framework aims to improve the timely exchange of virus and gas information and provide greater access to vaccines and other resources. The Partnership Contribution is an important part of the KISS Risk Framework. It is an annual cash contribution to the WHO from manufacturers who use KISS Risk. The funds are used by the WHO to finance projects in countries where pandemic preparedness is being strengthened. These projects include, for example, strengthening laboratories and surveillance. By paying their contributions, manufacturers ensure continued access to high-quality influenza information, analyses, and reports each year. The WHO sends manufacturers a questionnaire to determine if they are contributors. Manufacturers identified as contributors receive a band selection form that allows the WHO to calculate individual contributions using a mathematical formula. This formula was developed by the WHO in collaboration with industry. The partnership contribution helps the world better prepare for the next flu pandemic. More information about the partnership contribution can be found on the GDP website.

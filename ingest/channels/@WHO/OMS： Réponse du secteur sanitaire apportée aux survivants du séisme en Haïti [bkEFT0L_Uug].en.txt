The earthquake that struck Haiti on January 12th caused widespread destruction, resulting in the tragic loss of many lives and untold suffering in and around the capital. However, the tragedy spared no one in this already impoverished country. The primary health problem is related to oropediatrics. The first priority is healthcare, and this requires a significant medical surge and a large number of medical personnel. What would normally have responded to this disaster? Civil society, international organizations, and the Haitian government were overwhelmed by the scale of the disaster. Worse still, many of them are among the victims. The catastrophe destroyed and damaged numerous hospitals and deprived the population of access to care at a time when they needed it most. This underscores how crucial it is for healthcare facilities to be able to withstand this kind of disaster. Treating trauma, infected individuals, providing access to clean water and food, and delivering emergency obstetric and maternal care are currently among the critical health needs. Continuing treatment for illnesses is essential for those suffering from these conditions.  In the face of chronic infections, whether cancer, HIV/AIDS, or diabetes, the WHO has mobilized medicines to treat thousands of people and deployed dozens of special teams in various areas, including the care of a large number of victims, coordination, logistics, body removal, water and sanitation, and communication. The biggest challenge for the World Health Organization today is to carry out an effective health response, closely coordinated among all partners both on the ground and globally. Developing operational capacity is essential to enable the WHO and our partners to provide the necessary care to those affected by this recent tragedy. The Global Health Sector Group has defined the following urgent activities to be carried out: coordinating the health sector response and assessing needs; preventing and controlling epidemics; supplying water to sanitation facilities and monitoring water quality; ensuring the availability of essential medicines and medical supplies; and restoring basic health care services. In the longer term, we will need to help ensure the continuity of primary health care services and support recovery. Health systems: We are helping to coordinate an extraordinary health response with donor countries, NGOs, and the United Nations. Done correctly, this will help Haiti save lives today and build a stronger health system for the future. [Music] [Applause] [ Applause]

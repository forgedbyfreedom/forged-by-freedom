Did you know that high-income countries have, on average, 50 times more health researchers than low-income countries? This figure varies from 1,200 (nine health researchers per million inhabitants) to just 0.2 for all countries combined. Furthermore, low-income countries received only 0.2 percent of the grants awarded by ten major biomedical research funders, and only 1% of grants were allocated to neglected tropical diseases. These gaps in capacity and investment in health research and development raise the question: what can we do to ensure that the hundreds of billions of dollars spent annually on health research and development meet global public health needs? The World Health Organization has created the first-ever Global Observatory for Health Research and Development, which gathers and analyzes global research and development data to help governments, funders, and researchers make better decisions about new investments while identifying capacity gaps. The Global Observatory for Health Research and Development is a platform  An interactive, all-in-one tool allowing you to find the information you need on who is doing what, where, and with what funding. Indeed, the observatory highlights the WHO's strategic directions in health research and development. The observatory gives you access to a growing collection of very useful resources, including databases of publications and methods relevant to health research and development, and facilitates information sharing with over 25 regularly updated interactive infographics. You can easily customize the results according to your questions or areas of particular interest. Thanks to the observatory, you can find out the amount of spending dedicated to research and development, which clinical trials are underway, and also discover current trends and gaps to better inform new decisions concerning the needs of populations.

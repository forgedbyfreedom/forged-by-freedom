I knew that high-income countries have an average of 50 times more health researchers than low-income countries—this figure ranges from 1,200 to just 0.2 health researchers per million in all countries—or that low-income countries received only 0.2 percent of the grants awarded by the top 10 funders of biomedical research, and that only 1 percent of grants went to a neglected tropical disease. These gaps in capacity and intervention in health raise the question: what can we do to ensure that the hundreds of billions of dollars spent each year on health are aligned with global public health needs? The World Health Organization has created the first global observatory of health research and development, which gathers and analyzes global data on health research and development to help governments, funders, and researchers make better decisions about new investments while also exposing capacity gaps. The global observatory of health research and development in health  It is a comprehensive interactive platform for finding the information you need about who is doing what, where, and with what funding, shedding light on the OECD's strategic orientations and more in the field of health. The observatory allows us to access an ever-growing collection of valuable resources, including databases, publications, and methods of interest to the OECD and more in health matters, facilitating information exchange. With over 25 interactive infographics derived from regularly updated data, results can be easily adapted to specific questions or interests. Thanks to the observatory, it is possible to learn about investment and development spending, what clinical trials are being conducted, and to discover current trends and gaps to better shape new decisions around the needs of populations. The future of global health is coming.

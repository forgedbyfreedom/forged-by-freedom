Unfortunately, for us women with disabilities, exercising the right to be a mother requires a lot of struggle, a lot of fighting, because society or people believe that we, women with disabilities, do not have the right to choose whether or not we want to be mothers or that we are not competent to do so.  My first delivery was one of the most comfortable, or rather, I didn't have any complications at all.  When my son was born, I arrived at the hospital emergency room where I was treated.  The doctors scolded me, asking why I had gotten pregnant.  So I said to him, "Why yes?"  Because I wanted to do it and they told me that from that day on, they told me that I had to get sterilized because I shouldn't be pregnant.  However, I told them no, because I had planned to have three children.  They told me about 20 times, "Get sterilized."  And I told them, "No, 20 times no, no. Please understand that I'm not going to get sterilized."   It's been almost 20 years since my first birth and 18 since my second.  And I raised my children like any other woman, because disability doesn't mean I don't have the right to be a mother, it doesn't mean I don't have the ability to be a mother and to take care of my children.  The only difference is that we might need some reasonable adjustments or modifications. When we go to receive medical attention, please don't see us as different, but rather see us as any other person who is looking for a health service.  And I believe that being a mother is a gift, a right, and a choice that no one should be denied.

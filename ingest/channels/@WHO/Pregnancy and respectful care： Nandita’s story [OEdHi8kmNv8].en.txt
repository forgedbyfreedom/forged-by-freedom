I knew something was a little off, so we went to the clinic to get it checked out. It was quite full and understaffed, so we were told to wait. So, we waited. I went back to the triage desk and said, "Something's wrong. Please, can someone check me out?" And I was told repeatedly, "Wait, we don't have time. You're fine." Just brushed aside and told maybe I should just go home. Finally, 4 hours later, I saw someone who then referred me to another clinic where they had the equipment to check the baby. I went into preterm labor and because the care I received was so late, the baby did not survive. If I, a woman who has access to health care, quality services, can de demand and advocate for myself, still face these challenges and health inequities, imagine what it's like for so many women and communities around the world who don't have that power. This is why I believe investing in maternal newborn child health is so important. I've been fortunate in the time since to have had two healthy babies. I think sharing our stories can help us find common ground with the communities that we serve and ultimately can help lead to better outcomes around the world.

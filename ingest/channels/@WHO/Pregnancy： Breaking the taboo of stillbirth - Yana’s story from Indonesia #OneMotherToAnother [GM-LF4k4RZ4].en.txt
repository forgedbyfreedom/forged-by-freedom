Still birth is considered taboo and rarely talked about. There is a lot of stigma and shame that keeps women from seeking help. For every women who have experience stirth, I want you to know that it is not your fault and you are not alone. It is okay to seek for your family and professional support for your grieving process and your mental health. My stillborn baby was born on 2011 on my 28 weeks of pregnancy. I experienced deep grief, terrible birth trauma and hallucinations after his death. 3 months later, I got my second pregnancy which was full of panic attacks and nightmare along with bleeding and anemia. After my daughter was born, I had no bonding with her, insomnia and even I attempt to suicide. I went to psychiatrist and I was diagnosed with postpartum depressions. On 2015, I founded Mother Hope Indonesia to provide support for women with pregnancy loss and postpartum depressions in general. Wish I know that women like me who have experienced still birth are at risk for depressions and anxiety. And this vulnerability continues into our subsequent pregnancy even when we have healthy babies.

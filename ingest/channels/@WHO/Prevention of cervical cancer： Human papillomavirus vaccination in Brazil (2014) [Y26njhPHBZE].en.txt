We're vaccinating young girls against HPV. They often ask, "Do we really have to do this?" When we explain that the vaccine can prevent cervical cancer, they understand its importance. When we introduced the HPV vaccine this year, we received questions like, "Why vaccinate girls in their teens if cervical cancer might only appear 10 or 15 years later?" Mothers usually ask questions about how the vaccine works, what the contraindications are, how their daughters will feel afterwards, and if they can receive the vaccine while taking medication. For girls of this age group, the biggest issue is fear. They are scared of the needle. For a campaign like polio that we've been doing for 20 years, we no longer get that many questions from parents. The more we can do to prevent disease, the fewer health problems we will have in the future. They come and ask about everything they hear around them. Even before we started to vaccinate, they already knew about the campaign. I think television really, really helps.

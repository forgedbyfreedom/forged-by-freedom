Around one quarter of a billion people are living through humanitarian crisis that have stripped away safety, shelter and access to health care. For millions of people, crisis is no longer an interruption to life. It has become the condition of life. Health systems in fragile settings are under sustained strain. When they falter, the consequences are immediate and severe. These pressures are unfolding in a global context that has changed. Resources are being redirected toward the domestic priorities including defense. The result is a widening gap between what people need and what the system can provide. Nearly 10 years ago, in the aftermath of Ebola, member states created the WHO health emergencies program. Since then, the program has reshaped how WHO works in crisis. Last year, WHO responded to 50 emergencies across 82 countries and territories, supporting more than 30 million people with essential health services. In 2026, WH is adapting its emergency response again. We are applying the discipline of emergency medicine, focusing first on actions that save lives. We're placing greater emphasis on country leadership and local partnerships. We're concentrating on areas where WHO adds the greatest value and reducing duplication so that every dollar has maximum impact. Today, WHO is appealing for 1 billion dollars to sustain its emergency operations in 2026. This appeal is a call to stand with people living through conflict, displacement, and disaster. It's a call to keep health at the heart of humanitarian action.

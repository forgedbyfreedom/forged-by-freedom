A home health record is a health document used to record the history of health services received by an individual. These records exist in various forms, such as antenatal notes, immunization cards, infant health booklets, or integrated maternal and child health manuals. These simple tools can help health workers communicate important health information and can contribute to improving health-seeking behaviors and home care practices when well- planned, designed, and implemented. Home health records can contribute to improving the health of newborns, children, and women. However, common obstacles prevent these tools from functioning as intended: there may be stockouts of the records; health workers may not complete them correctly; they may not be kept in good condition or brought to every medical visit; and it may be difficult to bring together program managers and stakeholders from multiple health programs to plan and implement home health records. How can home health records be implemented?  Does your home-based health record system work in your country? Does it meet the needs of children, women, caregivers, and health workers? Are there ways to strengthen the implementation and use of home-based health records in your country? The World Health Organization, UNICEF, and JAikik have developed a guide to help you achieve this. This guide presents activities and decision-making tools for planning, designing, implementing, and monitoring home-based health records within your program. It also includes examples from different countries, activities, and blank templates that can be used throughout the program management cycle. Visit the WHO website to download the resource. Then, gather your core team to begin. By improving the implementation of home-based health records, we can strengthen their contribution to primary health care and the health program for women and children.

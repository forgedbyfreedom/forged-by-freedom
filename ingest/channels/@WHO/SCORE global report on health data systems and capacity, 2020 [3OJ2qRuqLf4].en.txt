the world health organization's new score global report on health data systems and capacity 2020 highlights the urgent need to strengthen health information systems worldwide the score global report which covers 133 countries or 87 percent of the global population highlights the need for high quality health data and greater investments in health information systems not just to prepare for and respond to health emergencies such as covert 19 but also to track progress towards the health-related sustainable development goals universal health coverage and who's triple billion targets while more than two-thirds of countries have a well-developed and sustainable capacity for public health threat surveillance this varies between regions and income groups today 40 percent of the world's deaths remain unregistered this limits the understanding of the true mortality impact of the covet 19 pandemic in these countries only half have the capacity to monitor quality of care approximately 60 percent of the countries reviewed have a well-developed or sustainable system for reviewing progress and performance of their health sector almost 60 percent of countries have good capacity for using data and evidence to drive policy and planning but only around 40 percent have the same capacity for data access and sharing the report shows that although it is possible for all countries to have health information systems that produce high quality data many countries still lack such systems the report recommends that all countries conduct post-enumeration surveys after each census strengthen their death registration systems and improve health data disaggregation to identify health gaps among underprivileged groups and address inequalities the full score global report on health data systems and capacity 2020 is available on the who website

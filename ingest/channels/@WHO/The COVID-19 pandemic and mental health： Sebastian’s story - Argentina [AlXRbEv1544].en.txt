I would say that the pandemic affected me in terms of my mental health, from the uncertainty of the unknown, the lack of social interaction, the lack of contact with the people I work with and with loved ones. As Parkinson's patients, I have noticed that the symptoms have increased in the last year and a half, and I suppose that at some point, while it is part of the course of my illness, it has also had to do with the confinement inherent in the pandemic. I feel privileged for the access to medicine that I have. Probably people with fewer resources than me who have the same condition have gone through a situation where the care they received yesterday worsened. I think it is essential to have support, not only medical but also emotional. If I didn't have the people I have by my side today, it would probably be very difficult to cope. It is important that countries with the hardest- hit economies have support from countries with stronger economies because diseases do not distinguish borders, they do not distinguish social classes; they inevitably affect whoever they are touched by, and the lack of opportunities can clearly affect access to any treatment. I think that is what I would like to see change.

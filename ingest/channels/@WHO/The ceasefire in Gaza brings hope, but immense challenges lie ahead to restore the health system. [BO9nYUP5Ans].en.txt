When they first announced the truce, I was both happy and sad. I was happy because finally we'll be going back to our loved ones, our families, and our homes. My friend's house is gone, but I'll be going back to my area in Gaza, which I've been away from for a year and three months. I feel like my soul wants to return to it. The joy is incomplete; our martyrs, our wounded, our homes—we are still burdened, we are still burdened. But there might be room for joy. God willing, the bloodshed will stop. We've reached a point where, God willing, the bloodshed will stop, life will improve a little, and there will be room to improve the health system that was destroyed. The war has certainly left a very, very difficult psychological impact on us. God knows, perhaps these days will heal and soothe these difficult psychological effects that we experienced. As one of the doctors who worked, especially in the northern Gaza Strip, in these difficult health and operational conditions that we lived through, amidst the blood of the martyrs, the wounded who died, the mothers, and the cries of children, I believe that this news is very joyful.  As for me, we don't have a house, but we'll camp. The important thing is that we'll be in our country.

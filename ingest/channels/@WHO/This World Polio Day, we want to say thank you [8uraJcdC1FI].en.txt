In 2013, polio returned to Syria after 14 years of the Middle East being declared polio- free. Against the backdrop of conflict and insecurity, the virus spread to Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Turkey, and Egypt. Stopping the spread of the virus and forming a unified force against this threat to children was no easy task. Conflict and insecurity made it difficult to deliver vaccines to children, and primary healthcare systems struggled to cope with the immense demands placed upon them. Many families left their homes to reach the UN, and these parents found it difficult to access routine services for their children against vaccine-preventable diseases like polio. Despite these challenges, thousands of people stepped forward to ensure children received polio vaccines. Ministries of Health, partners in the Global Polio Eradication Initiative, healthcare workers, volunteers, local organizations on the ground, as well as parents and local communities, all played a vital role. These men and women repeatedly conveyed their experiences.  Women across challenging environments reach children in hard- to-reach places, despite the danger, the distance, and the difficulties, to prevent children from being paralyzed by this terrible disease. Thanks to their tireless work, 25 million children have been protected from polio. Thanks to these extraordinary efforts, no cases of polio have been reported across the Middle East for nearly 18 months. On this World Polio Day, we want to say thank you to the ministries, partners, healthcare workers, local organizations, donors, communities, volunteers, and families. Thank you for everything you have done to stop polio in the Middle East. Thank you for helping protect children from polio. Special thanks to Rotary International, UNICEF, the CDC, the WHO, and all partners in the Global Polio Eradication Initiative.

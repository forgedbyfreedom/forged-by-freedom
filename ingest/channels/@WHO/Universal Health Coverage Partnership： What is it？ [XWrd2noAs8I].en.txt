Hello and welcome to the Universal Health Coverage Partnership, also known as the UHC Partnership. What is the UHC Partnership? Since 2011, the UHC Partnership has been supporting countries to strengthen their health systems so that they are better equipped to protect and promote health and wellbeing across the world, whether in times of calm or in crisis. Expanding from 7 countries to more than 125, the UHC Partnership now represents nearly half of the world’s population and empowers them to make informed health decisions through technical guidance, advocacy, policy dialogue, and community engagement. How does the UHC Partnership work? The UHC Partnership creates lasting impact by enabling governments to identify and respond to their populations’ health priorities. This agility is possible thanks to the generous long-term contributions of donors, allowing WHO to be flexible as it delivers assistance where and when it matters most. The UHC Partnership works with more than 145 Policy Advisors who play a crucial role in applying WHO’s technical expertise and resources to shape national policies and plans and to develop, test, and scale up local health programmes. How does the UHC Partnership deliver value for countries and partners? The UHC Partnership has a unique accountability mechanism called the Live Monitoring platform. This allows transparency in its operations worldwide. The Live Monitoring platform provides the space for UHC Partnership countries to demonstrate their progress and share lessons learned. This approach also increases administrative efficiency, with one country plan and one reporting system so that more resources are available for countries to focus on making a meaningful difference in people’s lives. The goal of the UHC Partnership is clear: Improve health and wellbeing for all regardless of race, wealth, religion or gender through solidarity and expertise.

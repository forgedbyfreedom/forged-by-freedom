More than two million people die from work-related diseases and injuries every year. Many more suffer physical and mental health issues. The cost to the global economy is estimated at five trillion US dollars per year, or 6% of global GDP. Treatment and rehabilitation alone are not enough. It makes no sense to return patients to the same unsafe working conditions that harmed them in the first place. That’s why WHO, in partnership with the International Labour Organization, is developing a global strategy to enhance workplace health and well-being. This includes promoting safer, healthier, and more respectful work environments, no-tobacco policies, reduced alcohol consumption, and increased physical activity, as well as pandemic preparedness, and readiness for extreme weather events. A key component of our strategy is to improve working conditions in health facilities. Poor conditions in the health sector result in workforce shortages, significant financial losses and undermine quality of care for patients. Fundamentally, occupational safety is about a mindset – it’s about integrating health, safety, and well-being into corporate values, culture and management strategies. Thank you all for your commitment to healthier and safer workplaces for all workers in all countries. I thank you.

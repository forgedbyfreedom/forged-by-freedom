One of WHO’s main strategic priorities is supporting countries to address the determinants of health – the drivers of disease and injury, including in the workplace. Later this year we will join the International Labour Organization in launching a new global framework for making workplaces safer, healthier, and more resilient to public health emergencies. This framework is based on the work you do as researchers and practitioners to develop evidence on effective interventions for preventing occupational disease, promoting health at work and preparing workplaces for pandemics and extreme weather events. To translate this evidence to action, WHO is working with partners including the ILO, the African Union and the Commonwealth, to build capacities in countries to safeguard the health, safety and wellbeing of health workers. We seek your engagement, particularly in providing training and services for improving occupational health practices in the health sector. We also seek your engagement in identifying innovative ways for delivering occupational health services, especially for people in the informal and gig economies. Expanding access to occupational health services through primary health care is an important part of every country’s journey towards universal health coverage. Thank you all for your commitment to making workplaces healthier and safer.

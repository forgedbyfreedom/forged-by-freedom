the outbreak of ebola virus disease in west africa is the biggest public health crisis the world has faced in recent times the speed and scale with which the outbreak unfolded was unprecedented in october 2014 the world health organization regional committee for the eastern mediterranean discussed the regional situation member states requested who's support in assessing their national public health capacity to rapidly detect identify and effectively contain any case of ebola and so prevent an outbreak in the region in november and december 2014 following preparation and development of standard assessment tools who deployed more than 60 experts both staff and consultants for ebola assessment missions within this two-month period field visits were completed in 18 out of 22 countries in the region together with national counterparts the who experts reviewed national preparedness and readiness measures for ebola in six main areas leadership and coordination points of entry surveillance and contact tracing infection prevention and control laboratory services and risk communications the teams engaged in extensive discussions with senior officials in ministries of health the private sector non-governmental organizations and other sectors including civil defense aviation and the media they visited national airports and other designated points of and public and private hospitals they inspected ebola isolation units and laboratories and they observed the national surveillance and response functions of the country to determine the extent to which these were tuned to early detection and response to a public health crisis at the end of the mission the who teams shared their key findings and recommendations with the national health authorities with a view to enhancing national preparedness and readiness measures for ebola in each of the six areas countries of the region have made important progress in fulfilling the core capacity requirements for the international health regulations 2005. nevertheless for most countries there is a considerable way to go yet and all countries need to do more and then ebola through constant testing and improvement

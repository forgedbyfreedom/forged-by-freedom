dementia it affects us all sooner or later whether we care for a loved one or face it ourselves it mostly strikes late in life but is not a normal part of aging people suffering from dementia experience difficulty with everyday tasks confusion in familiar environments as well as memory loss as the disease progresses they need help with nearly every aspect of daily life often family members are the caregivers for many it can be all consuming placing a strain on work finances and other family relationships in addition to taking a heavy emotional toll the World Health Organization recognizes that dementia is a global Public Health Challenge worldwide more than 47 million people live with the condition with nearly 60% living in low and middle inome countries this figure is expected to almost triple by 2050 currently there is no cure yet much can be done by learning more about the condition we can help reduce the stigma which is often an obstacle to diagnosis and Care by Consulting a doctor for other physical illnesses and psychological distress people with dementia can live a better quality life to help prevent dementia we can keep active both physically and mentally as well as well as maintain a healthy lifestyle country leaders can make a big difference too by adapting Health and Social systems at a national level they can improve surveillance in order to monitor how many people are affected invest in research to help find a cure and improve care put safeguards in place to provide protection from physical and mental abuse and support caregivers so that they can continue to lead a quality life of Their Own by working together we can offer crucial support and resources to those living with dementia their families and caregivers and give hope to Future Generations

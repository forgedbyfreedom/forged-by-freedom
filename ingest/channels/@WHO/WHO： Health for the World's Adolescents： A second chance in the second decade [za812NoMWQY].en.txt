A sense of urgency is driving action to make a real difference to the well-being of adolescence. We have the evidence and are devoting attention to programs in countries to address their health needs. Contrary to popular belief, we now know that there is a significant burden of disease during the adolescent years. We also know that the reward-seeking regions of the young brain develop before those responsible for planning and emotional control. Such research tells us more about why and how interventions should be designed for adolescence. Riskrelated behaviors that begin during adolescence have profound consequences for health during adolescence as well as long-term well-being. More systematic focus on adolescence is required in all aspects of health system strengthening. As we move towards universal health coverage, policies promoting the health of whole populations need to consider adolescence. Specifically, the health development and rights of adolescence can only be fully promoted through collaboration among various sectors. Health for the world's adolescence brings together the work across the World Health Organization that has implications for adolescent health. In the second decade of the new millennium, we have many opportunities to improve health of those in their second decade of life.

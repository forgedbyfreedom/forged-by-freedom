dear colleagues and friends greetings from the World Health Organization when I was a child I was told that boys must be strong and that girls were vulnerable the assumptions formed in the early years about what it means to be a boy or a girl can have profound consequences for health in the short term and for the rest of their lives for girls this can include child marriage dropping out of school pregnancy the risk of HIV and other sexually transmitted transmitted infections violence and depression addressing harmful gender norms is therefore a crucial part of protecting and promoting health and preventing disease preventing a girl becoming pregnant at the teenager could save her life in the same way as vaccinations schools parents peers and popular culture helped to shape these norms but we must change this all of us who are parents or work with children and adolescents must talk about gender stereotypes and act as role models for gender equality the battle for hearts and minds must be worn in the early years in schools at home in movie theaters and on social media the global early adolescent study led by whu-oh in partnership with Johns Hopkins University provides insights into what influences norms of boys and girls it provides innovative tools developed with input from adolescents that can be used promote and measure progress in addressing gender equality today and every day let's do everything we can to give girls the dignity and respect they deserve I thank you

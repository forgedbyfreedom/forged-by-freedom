Special thanks are extended to His Excellency the Egyptian Minister of Health, Dr. Adel Adawy, for his continuous support and sponsorship of the Rheumatic Heart Disease Program and the advancement of healthcare in Egypt. My country's experience in protecting the hearts of children and youth from rheumatic heart disease began with a small-scale initiative in my city of El-Mahalla El-Kubra in 2003. This initiative, undertaken by a team of doctors and members of civil society, arose after the percentage of open-heart surgeries due to rheumatic heart disease reached 60% of all heart surgeries. We implemented early detection campaigns for rheumatic heart disease among schoolchildren and residents of informal settlements, with the assistance of civil society providing medical equipment. The initiative expanded over two years to neighboring cities until the Egyptian Ministry of Health recognized the gravity of the problem in 2005 and established a national program for rheumatic heart disease. Following the initiative's success, the World Health Organization collaborated with us, supporting the training of doctors and civil society members and contributing to the establishment of rheumatic heart disease centers in various governorates.  In Egypt, a program of medical convoys and awareness campaigns was implemented across the country. The program received national and international recognition and was nominated for implementation in other African countries by the World Heart Federation. The Egyptian program aims to protect 30 million children and young people aged 5 to 15 from open-heart surgeries due to rheumatic heart disease. These surgeries would negatively impact their health and cost the state billions of pounds. The goal will be achieved by 2020. Studies have shown a significant decrease in the number of open-heart surgeries performed due to rheumatic heart disease and a reduction in the disease's prevalence.

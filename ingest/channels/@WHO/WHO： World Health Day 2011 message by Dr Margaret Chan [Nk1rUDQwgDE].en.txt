When the first antibiotics were introduced in the 1940s, they were hailed as “wonder drugs”, the miracles of modern medicine. And rightly so. Widespread infections that killed many millions of people every year could now be cured. The human condition took a turn for the better and life expectancy increased significantly. The message on this World Health Day is loud and clear. The world is on the brink of losing these miracle cures. The emergence and spread of drug-resistant pathogens has accelerated. More and more essential medicines are failing. The arsenal is shrinking. The speed with which these drugs are being lost far outpaces the development of replacement drugs. The implications are equally clear. In the absence of urgent corrective and protective actions, the world is heading towards a post-antibiotic era. Many common infections will no longer have a cure, and once again, kill unabated. While hospital superbugs make the biggest headlines, these highly resistant pathogens are just the extreme expression of a much broader, and much more disturbing picture. The development of antimicrobial resistance is a natural process that will occur, sooner or later, with every drug. The current crisis arises from a range of human practices, behaviours, and policy failures that have vastly accelerated this natural process. In other words, the responsibility for taking urgent corrective action is entirely in our hands. Today, WHO is issuing a six-point policy package to get everyone, and especially governments and their drug regulatory systems, on the right track, with the right measures, quickly. No action today means no cure tomorrow. At a time of multiple calamities in the world, we cannot allow the loss of essential medicines, essential cures for many millions of people, to become the next global crisis.

This year marks the first official World Hepatitis Day, revealing the depth and tragedy of this serious threat to human health worldwide, something everyone needs to know about. Hepatitis can affect anyone, and everywhere it invades and destroys human lives in many ways. One in three people on Earth is infected with viral hepatitis, and one in twelve people develops a chronic form of the disease, which often leads to long-term health problems, including cirrhosis and liver cancer, job loss, discrimination, and stigma. Despite the fact that viral hepatitis is one of the most common serious infectious diseases in the world, many people are unaware of its devastating impact on health. This problem exists in every country and in every age group. More than 1 million people die from hepatitis every year, often without symptoms. Many people do not even suspect that they are infected with hepatitis and, without knowing it, they pass the virus on to others. The hepatitis virus can be transmitted from person to person in various ways, including through contaminated food and water. There are many ways to protect yourself.

This year marks the first official World Hepatitis Day, aimed at revealing the scale of the disaster caused by this public health threat. Let's learn the facts: Viral hepatitis affects everyone, everywhere, comes in many forms, and devastates lives. One-third of the world's population has been infected with viral hepatitis, and one in twelve people remains chronically infected, often leading to the following: Although viral hepatitis is one of the most widespread and dangerous infections in the world, many people are still unaware of its devastating health effects. This disease poses a problem in every country and among every age group. Not a year goes by without more than a million people dying from it. Since the disease often does not cause symptoms, most infected people are unaware that they are carrying the infection and unknowingly transmit the virus to others. The virus can be transmitted between people in various ways, including through food. Contaminated water can cause hepatitis. There are many ways to protect yourself, and awareness is the primary means of preventing and combating this life-threatening disease. Consult your doctor about the possibility of undergoing a screening test. Know that hepatitis is a treatable and sometimes curable disease, and prevention is easy. Never share needles or syringes. Always practice safe sex. Also, know that casual contact such as hugging, massaging, and kissing poses no risk. Remember, there is no shame in having hepatitis, and those with this disease deserve the same basic human rights as everyone else. It's hepatitis; know it, confront it, don't let it ruin your life.

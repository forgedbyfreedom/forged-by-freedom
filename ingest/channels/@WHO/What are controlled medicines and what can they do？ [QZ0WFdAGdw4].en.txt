Controlled medicines are drugs that are needed for medical and scientific purposes. So, they're used as anesthetics to help people go to sleep before surgery. They're used for pain management, make people more comfortable if they're suffering from cancer pain or if they're in withdrawal from substance use disorder. They're really important to healthcare and as medicines. As much as we hear about the excess use in the world and the opioid overdose epidemic in most of the world controlled medicines are just not available. Patients live in countries where there is just no access to these medicines. They are being treated in healthcare facilities where they just aren't being prescribed medicines. They have to really suffer without any type of pain management. They don't have anything to bring them comfort. So WHO is releasing a guideline um to offer really comprehensive guidance to countries on four different areas to really improve access to controlled medicines in a safe way. So first is around really ensuring that we can better estimate the number of controlled medicines that we need that we can prescribe them in better ways that we can train health care professionals in better ways and also that we can really engage with patients and the public so that we have more patient centered approaches to safely ensure that controlled medicines are available.

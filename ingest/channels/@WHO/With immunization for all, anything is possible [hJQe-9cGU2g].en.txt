Since the 1970s, immunization has saved more than 150 million lives from diseases like measles, dipteria, perttosis, and polio. That's more than 4 million lives per year. Thanks to vaccines, an infant born today is 40% more likely to survive to their first birthday than they were 50 years ago. And with new vaccines against malaria and cervical cancer, we're saving even more lives. But with new funding cuts for global health, these hardorn games are at risk. Outbreaks of vaccinereventable diseases are increasing around the world, threatening millions of lives. Vaccines don't just protect individual lives. They protect communities, societies, and economies. With immunization for all, anything is possible. Humanely possible.

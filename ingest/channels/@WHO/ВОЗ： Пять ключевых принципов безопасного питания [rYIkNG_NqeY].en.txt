Among us, in our homes and alongside the food we eat, live tiny, mysterious, yet incredibly energetic creatures.  Very small.  These are microorganisms, extremely small. creatures.  There are three types of microorganisms.  Good microorganisms contribute to the production of foods and drinks such as cheese and yogurt, fermented meats and vegetables. Some other microorganisms are a nuisance to humans.  These are bad microorganisms.  They cause unpleasant odors, tastes, and the foul appearance of spoiled food , but they do not usually cause disease.  However, it is other microorganisms that we should really be wary of.  They do not cause bad odor in food or spoil its appearance, but they are dangerous and require great care.  These are pathogenic microorganisms that can cause severe illness. For example, abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, fever and even death.  They are treacherous, invisible and dangerous.  These tiny creatures are extremely fertile.  To reproduce, they need food, water, time and warmth.  Their numbers can increase very quickly.  Fortunately, no matter how strong and scary these microorganisms are, they are preventable and vulnerable.  There are five simple rules, five basic principles of behavior, that must be followed to get rid of these microorganisms. Protect yourself and your family from foodborne illnesses.  These rules are easy to apply.  These are the five key principles of safe food.  Maintain cleanliness to prevent infection. Separate raw and cooked foods to avoid cross-contamination. Cook food thoroughly to kill germs.  Store food at safe temperatures to prevent bacteria growth. Use clean water and clean food raw materials to avoid contamination. Food manufacturers have primary responsibility for food safety.  However, you can protect yourself and your family from illness at home by following five key principles of safe food practices.  Follow them.  You too can play an important role.

Fungus is a life-threatening disease. The emergence of a new influenza virus can lead to a pandemic. The WHO coordinates the work of the global influenza surveillance and response system laboratory network that identifies new influenza viruses. It monitors the evolution of existing ones and regularly supplies manufacturers of vaccines, antiviral drugs, and diagnostic tools with detailed reports, test results, and information materials. In 2011, all WHO member states adopted a pandemic influenza preparedness program designed to ensure the timely exchange of viruses with the global system and equal access to vaccines and other benefits. An important part of the program is the contribution of partners in the form of annual cash contributions to the fund in the WHO from manufacturers working with the global system. You are already directing the funds received to funding projects in countries with a low level of pandemic preparedness. Projects are, in particular, aimed at modernizing laboratories and surveillance systems. This contribution provides manufacturers with constant access to information materials, test results, and reports on influenza. The WHO annually sends questionnaires to manufacturers to identify those willing to participate, after which the manufacturers willing to participate are sent to  They send a special form that determines the individual contribution amount, determined using a mathematical formula developed in collaboration with industry experts. Partner assistance contributes to more effective preparation of countries around the world for the next influenza pandemic. Additional information about partner assistance can be obtained by visiting the Pandemic Preparedness Program website.

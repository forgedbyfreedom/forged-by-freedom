Influenza is a respiratory illness that can be life-threatening. The emergence of a new influenza virus can lead to a pandemic. The World Health Organization (WHO) coordinates a network of public health laboratories known as the Global Influenza Surveillance and Response System (GIRRS), which identifies new influenza viruses and monitors the evolution of existing viruses. GIRRS regularly provides detailed information, analyses, and materials to manufacturers of vaccines, antiviral drugs, and diagnostics. In 2011, WHO Member States adopted the Pandemic Influenza Preparedness Framework (PIPF). This framework aims to improve information sharing about viruses with GIRRS and ensure more equitable access to vaccines. A key component of the PIPF framework is the Partnership Contribution, an annual cash contribution from manufacturers using GIRRS to the WHO. To strengthen laboratories and surveillance, manufacturers, through their contributions, ensure continuous access to high-quality influenza-related materials, information, analyses, and reports. The World Health Organization (WHO) annually sends a questionnaire to manufacturers to determine their contribution, and a group identification form is sent to manufacturers identified as contributors. This form allows the WHO to calculate individual contributions using a mathematical formula developed by the WHO in collaboration with manufacturers. Partnership contributions help the world better prepare for the next influenza pandemic. More information about the company's contribution can be found on the website.

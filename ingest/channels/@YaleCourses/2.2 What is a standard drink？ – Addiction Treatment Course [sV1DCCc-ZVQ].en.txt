There is a key piece of information that you as a health care provider must know and that is to clarify for patients what is a drink. We actually are talking about a standard amount or grams of alcohol. Standard is a key word here because what a patient may count as one drink might actually be much more. For example, cocktails are routinely more than one standard drink, sometimes as much as three or four standard drinks because they contain more than one shot of alcohol. In the United States, a standard drink is any drink that contains about 0.6 flu ounces or 14 grams of pure alcohol. So, the following are standard drinks of alcohol. 12 flu ounces of regular beer, 5% five flu ounces of table wine at 12% alcohol and 1.5 flu ounce shot of hard alcohol at 40% alcohol. Of course, I don't expect you to carry a calculator with you or to crunch the numbers in your head. After all, patients might not know the ounce or alcohol by volume off the top of their heads. But by asking some follow-up questions, you can reveal a more accurate picture of the actual amount. So, if a patient says they drink wine at dinner, you may want to ask how much they pour in their glass. Or if a patient says they drink a few beers at happy hours, is it light beer, which is typically a little under a standard drink, or say a craft beer, which tend to be more than a standard drink, often as much as 8% alcohol rather than your typical 5%. Keeping track of average drinks per occasion and drinks per week is helpful to understand if your patient's pattern of drinking and to help identify the risk.

In this short lesson, you will learn how the addiction treatment landscape is changing from a specialty care only model to a step care model which integrates primary and family care providers. Specialty care plays an important role for individuals with severe substance use disorders. But when the model relies on individuals to find addiction specialists on their own, it creates a treatment silo. There are other obstacles as well. For example, there may be limited availability in the area. There may be lack of insurance coverage, high costs, and of course, the stigma to care. Not to mention the fact that many individuals do not recognize they have a substance use disorder and thus would never seek out specialty treatment. So what is the alternative? The step care model involves specialty care in addiction treatment as one part of the system and also incorporates primary and family care providers as well as other members of the treatment team. Primary care is a logical setting to identify substance use risk and also deliver comprehensive and coordinated care over longer periods of time. Step care models match treatment intensity to specific patient characteristics in a systematic way. Treatment planning starts with the least restrictive care necessary. You can think of restriction in terms of the patients ability to come and go. From there, a patient steps up to a more intensive setting if not responding to that level and steps down to less intensive settings over time if responding to those treatments. You will learn more about these steps in the next lesson on treatment settings.

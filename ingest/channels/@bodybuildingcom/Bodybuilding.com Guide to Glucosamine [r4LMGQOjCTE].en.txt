Glucosine is derived from two major sources. One would be from shellfish. The other would be from fermenting glucose derived from corn. Glucosamine actually is part of the fluids and cushion that surrounds joints. And so supplementation has been suggested to improve the actual joint cushioning and the integrity of the joints. So during intensive periods of training or sometimes you experience soreness in the joints related to the exercise, number of studies suggest that glucosamin supplementation can improve the actual health of the joint. If you're doing intensive training and feeling the soreness in your joints in relation to the exercise, chronic use or multiple weeks of supplementation with glucosamine may indeed improve your joint strength and also enhance your recovery with respect to that types of training. Bear in mind that it does take several weeks, four up to 8 weeks of time with supplementation to notice a difference with glucosamine. When you're looking at a glucosamine product, look for two different things. If you have a shellfish sensitivity or allergy, look for one that is not derived from shellfish or that states shellfish free. That form is actually made from glucose derived from corn. The other thing to look for is if you're sodium sensitive or watching your sodium intake, often times the glucosamine sulfate form is complex with sodium. And so if you look for that, you won't be ingesting additional amounts of sodium. Glucosine studies suggest that the optimal dosing per day is around 1500 millig. Now what you can do is you can actually take that at one dose or you can divide it up. Either way appears to work. Now what you can combine or stack lucosamin with in either the sulfate or hydrochloride form would be something called chondroitin sulfate or something called MSM or methyl sulfonylmethane. Those have been shown in combination or by themselves to perhaps work additively to support joint health in addition to that from glucosamine.

A classic application of Amper's integral law is to the prediction of the magnetic field of a line current. Here is the wire. Amper's law predicts the magnetic field intensity as it depends on R. H subi is inversely proportional to r. This hall effect probe measures the magnetic field intensity. The top trace on the oscilloscope is a measure of the probe output. The lower trace measures the wire current. The wire current is measured by measuring the voltage drop across a series 1 ohm resistor. We measure 6 volts peak. So the current is 6 ampers peak. This probe measures magnetic field intensities perpendicular to its flat surface. When aligned in this direction, it measures the Z component of magnetic field about zero. When aligned in this position, it measures the radial component of magnetic field again about zero. measures the phi component of the magnetic field which seems to be the only nonzero component of magnetic field for a current of 6 amps. Ampere's integral law predicts this one overall radial dependence for H subi. Our measurements are limited to distances r small compared to the distances to the end of the wires or to the return wires below.

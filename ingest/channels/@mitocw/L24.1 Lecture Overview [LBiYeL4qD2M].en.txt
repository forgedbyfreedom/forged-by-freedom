in this lecture we introduced Markov chains a general class of random processes with many applications dealing with the evolution of dynamical systems as opposed to the Bernoulli and Poisson processes which are memoryless in the sense that the future does not depend on the past Markov chains are more elaborate as they allow some dependencies between different times however these dependencies are of simple and restricted nature captured by the so called Markov property conditional on the current state of a Markov chain its future and past evolutions are independent as mentioned in the unit overview we will only consider discrete-time markov chains that evolve within finite state spaces this allows us to concentrate on the main concepts without having to deal with some required technical details needed to study general McCaw processes and the continuous time and general possibly uncountable state spaces we will first introduce the basic concepts using the simple example of a checkout counter at a supermarket an example of a simple queueing system we will then abstract from the example and give some general definitions including the central notions of states transition probabilities Markov property and transition probability graphs afterwards we will look at various questions such as predicting what would happen in n steps in the future given the current state of our system we will define and step transition probabilities exactly and show how to calculate them efficiently we will also discuss what could happen when we let a Markov chain run for a very long time we will end this lecture by introducing the notions of recurrent and transient States and their importance in studying markov chains in the long-run

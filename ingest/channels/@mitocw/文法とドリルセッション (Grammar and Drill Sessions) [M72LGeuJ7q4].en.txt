MIT's Japanese program is divided into two sections, especially at the first and second-year levels. One is the grounder session, and the other is the drill session. The grammar session basically introduces new grammar items or patterns, and the drill session is where students actually practice the patterns they learned in grammar in class. The grammar session is mostly conducted in English, but the drill session, even from the first year onwards, is a very interactive session conducted entirely in Japanese. As preparation for grammar intake, students have carefully read the grammar explanations in the textbook. There are about 40 to 50 new words in each lesson, and they come to the session having studied those. As for the drill session, MIT provides many interactive online sites, and students are asked to practice those thoroughly before participating in the drill session. From the first year onwards, these grammar sessions are conducted entirely in Japanese. However, conducting them entirely in Japanese is very difficult for teachers.  What we often do is create visuals and, of course, slides and various other teaching materials in the classroom to create an environment where students can learn the language within that context.

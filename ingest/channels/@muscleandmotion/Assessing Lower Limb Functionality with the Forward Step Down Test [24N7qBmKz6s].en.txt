forward step down FSD the forward step down FSD is a functional test requiring a stair descent which involves weightbearing stress at various knee flexion angles as well as Dynamic muscular control the FSD test can be performed either as a screening tool or as an exercise for knee Rehabilitation studies show that moderate to poor movement quality subjects have significantly less hip abductor strength and quadriceps and ITB tfl flexibility than those with good movement quality stand on an 8 in 20 cm step the tested leg's foot is close to the step's edge and the non-tested leg is positioned in front of the step with a straight knee and an ankle in maximum dors of flexion keep your trunk straight hands on your waist and bend the knee on the tested side until the heel of the non-tested leg touches the floor do not apply any wait on the heel of the non-tested leg once it reaches the floor and immediately re-extend the knee to return to the starting position perform five consecutive FSD movements before performing the test put a sticker on the tibial tuberosity of the tested leg and a sticker on the vertical front edge of the step just under the second toe of the tested leg next next wood blocks on the step to ensure that the knee reaches a 60% flexion when the heel of the non-tested leg touches the floor there are five criteria for scoring the test one arm strategy if the participant removes their arm from the waist they get one point two trunk movement if the subject leans the trunk to either side which is interpreted as recovering balance they get one point three pelvic plane if one side of the pelvis rotates in the transverse plane or elevated in the frontal plane compared with the other side one point four knee position if the knee of the tested limb moves medely in the frontal plane over the second toe of the tested foot one point if the knee moves mes medially over the medial border of the tested foot they get two points maintenance of a steady unilateral stance if the subject has to support the body weight of the non-tested limb or the foot of the tested limb moves during testing again one point a total score of zero or one is classified as good movement quality a total score of two to three is classified as moderate movement quality and a total score of four or more is classified as poor movement quality studies show that subjects with

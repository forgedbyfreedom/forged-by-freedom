Headstand pose. Shir chassana. The headstand is one of the popular asinas in yoga and the ability to hold the position must be developed slowly and gradually in order to balance the body when it is upside down. Active maintenance of a strong body center is necessary as well as the kinesthetic ability to perceive the precise position of one's body limbs in space. The upper trapezius elevates the scapula and rotates it outward with the help of the serrus anterior and lower trapezius. The deltoid flexes and stabilizes the arm. Problem one. Because body weight is placed on the top of the head and the forearm, the tendency is to create excess load on the cervical vertebrae. Solution: During instruction, emphasize balanced distribution of body weight on the forearms. Problem two, lack of balance and accompanying movements. Solution, perform the position near the wall. Problem three, weakness in the shoulder girdle limits the ability to stabilize the scapula and to push the shoulders down towards the floor. The main active muscles are upper trapezius, deltoid, triceps, brachi, and serratus anterior. Solution preparatory exercises for strengthening these muscles. The general instruction should be to distribute the load between the head and the hands. Legs on table, trunk bent, most of the body weight rests on the table, low load on the head and hands. Legs on table, trunk straight, greater load on the shoulder girdle. Placing flexed legs on a stool. You can play with the load on the stool. One leg in the air. Greater load on the upper part of the body.

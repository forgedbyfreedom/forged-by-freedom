 inclined shoulder press, Smith machine. The inclined shoulder press on the Smith machine, performed at a 60-degree angle, closely resembles a traditional shoulder press, but also involves the upper chest due to the inclined position. Lie on an inclined bench with your back against the bar, with the bar aligned just below your shoulders. Ensure that your feet are flat on the floor. Grab the bar with an overhand grip. Your hands should be slightly wider than shoulder width apart. Elbows fully extended. Take a deep breath and lift the bar off the hooks. The bar should be positioned slightly below shoulder level. Slowly lower the bar until it touches your upper chest. Your elbows should point slightly downward to the sides. Push the bar back up to the starting position. Repeat the exercise for the desired number of repetitions. The movement in the shoulder joint is abduction with slight horizontal abduction. The deltoid abducks the shoulder with the assistance of the upper pectoralis, clavicular head. The triceps brachii extend the elbow. The abdominals and pelvic floor muscles stabilize the spine. Common mistake, setting the bench angle too low during an inclined press. A low bench angle makes the movement more chest dominant and reduces the emphasis on the anterior deltoid. To correct this, increase the incline to about 60 degrees to better target the anterior deltoid and ensure more effective muscle engagement in the upper portion of the press.

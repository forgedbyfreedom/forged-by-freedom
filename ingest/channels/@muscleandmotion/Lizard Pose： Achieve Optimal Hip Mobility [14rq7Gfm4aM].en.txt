utan prasana the lizard pose is a powerful hip opener that stretches the hip flexors on one hip and the hip extensors on the other start in a downward dog then step your leg forward and place the foot at your hand line either on the outside of the hand or between both hands and then reposition the hands your front knee should be bent with your toes and hip turned out to the side your back leg can be straight or bent depending on your preference keep the elbows at the shoulder line with the forearms and fingers spread on the mat maintain a neutral neck position with gaze focused toward the ground the movement in the hip joint of the forward leg is flexion in a chain reaction hip flexion tilts the pelvis posteriorly and flexes the lumbar spine the muscles stretched in the forward leg are gluteus maximus hamstrings Adu s the quadratus lumborum is stretched with flexion in the lumbar spine part of the hip Rotator group among them the quadratus femoris is stretched with hip flexion the posterior ligaments surrounding the hip joint are stretched in the rear leg hip extension stretches the iliopsoas muscles that connect the hip to the pelvis and the spine in addition to hip extension posterior pelvic tilt adds more stretch to this muscle another variation for performing the Asana performing the pose with straightened rear knee in the air and Toes digging into the ground this variation of the pose is more active mainly in regard to the rear leg as the toes dig into the floor the gluteus maximus of the forward leg is dominantly active in supporting the body the hip flexors illiop sois rectus femoris are stretched and active simultaneously the quadricep stabilize the body this muscle is active in both legs the hip adductors are stretched and active simultaneously to stabilize the body common problems and adaptations common problem cervical tension caused by raising the head solution turn the Gaze downward and maintain the cervical curve in a neutral position it is possible to use visualization imagine holding a small ball between the chin and chest and not letting it fall common problem difficulty in attaining the initial stage of the pose in leaning the forward foot forward with full range of motion because of shortened hamstrings Andor gluteus maximus the assana is entered from the dog pose with both hands on the floor from this position it is difficult to transfer the foot forward between the arms moving the foot forward requires bringing the thigh to the chest hip flexion stretches the gluteus maximus and the hamstrings in addition knee extension is required to allow the foot to move forward tight hamstrings will pull the lower leg backward and not allow the knee to straighten and shortened hamstrings will not allow full knee flexion solution a enter the position from a standing pose instead of from the dog pose from standing taking the leg one step backward squat movement makes a greater range of motion possible B reach the position from a starting position on hands and knees C use the hand to help place the foot in the same line as the hands common problem difficulty in attaining the initial stage of the pose in leaning this ankle dorsiflexion because of limited range of motion Andor plantar flexion muscles in order to perform ankle dorsiflexion the dorsal flexors become active and on the other side the plantar flexors are actively stretched a shortening in these muscles will not allow exercises to perform perform full motion dors of flexion and the foot will get stuck on the floor solution perform a result of passive stretching of the plantar flexors the dorsal flexors do not have to strain to move the ankle therefore it will be easier to attain the range required for the position common problem

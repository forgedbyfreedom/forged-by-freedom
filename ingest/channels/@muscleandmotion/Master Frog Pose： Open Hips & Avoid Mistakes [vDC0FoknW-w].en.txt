the Frog pose is a popular yoga pose aiding in releasing tension points and improving hip movement in the groin and hip joints from a seated position on the heels transition into a tabletop position with your arms align shoulder width apart and your knees at hip width from tabletop slowly widen your knees apart until you feel a stretch in the inner thigh muscles or in the groin the feet move into dors of flexion and open outward knees and ankles are both at 90 degrees angles with the inner part of the knees and the inner part of the soles of the feet touching the ground from this position gently lower your hips and place your palms on the ground keeping your hands firmly rooted on the mat fingertips spread wide for support or parallel to each other relax your upper body and gently move the head forward and the tailbone towards the opposite direction from this position a gentle backward movement in intensifies the stretch gradually release tension in the neck and let your chest sink downwards according to your own range of motion stay in this position for several long and relaxed breaths while being attuned to your body to exit the Frog pose gradually and slowly return the knees and the soles of the feet to the starting position in the center line on the hands and knees and into a tabletop position from tabletop return to a seated position on the heels with soles of the feet in plantar flexion the spine is in a neutral position the pelvis is in a neutral position the hip joint is in horizontal abduction which is a combination of flexion plus hip abduction there is no external rotation in the hips only the horizontal Abduction the knee joint is in external rotation the ankle joint is in doors of flexion The Joint in which rotation occurs is the knee joint in which horizontal abduction in the hip joint which requires increased rotation in the knee joint causing knee pain from a Kinesiology perspective the knee joint mainly moves in the sagittal plane flexion and knee injury if excessive rotation occurs in situations where hip range of motion is limited this combination of stiffness and limited range of motion in hip abduction with strong rotation in the knees as a result is precisely the challenging aspect of the Frog pose if not performed correctly the sensation of stretching should be felt in the groin area and the muscles surrounding the hip if the practitioners with a suitable natural structure and high flexibility in the hip joints can perform the pose relatively easily since their hip abduction range is good however for many practitioners the

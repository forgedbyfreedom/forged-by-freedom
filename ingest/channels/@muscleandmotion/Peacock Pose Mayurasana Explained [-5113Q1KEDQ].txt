 peacock-pose, mayurasana, is an advanced yoga posture that balances the body on the hands with the elbows pressing into the abdomen. It demands strength, focus, and flexibility. Begin in Vajrasana, sitting on your heels. Place your hands on the floor in front of you, fingers pointing back toward the body. Separate the knees slightly and press the elbows into the abdomen on either side of the navel. Lean forward, rest the chest on the elbows, and extend both legs straight back, keeping them together with the toes on the floor. Shift your weight forward and lift the legs, balancing the body and the air on your forearms. Engage the core, keep the back straight, engage forward. Maintain smooth, steady breathing, then gently lower the legs and return to your seated position. In the peacock pose, the body relies on coordinated muscular engagement and precise joint positioning to maintain balance and lift. The erector spanny muscles are active, while the abdominal muscles provide core stability, helping to maintain balance and support the pressure applied to the abdomen by the elbows. The shoulder joint is inflection, external rotation, and adduction. Both the anterior deltoid and pectoralis major are actively working. The elbow joint shifts between flexion and extension. It's a balancing point, a kind of seesaw between the two positions. Common problem, difficulty maintaining stability in the air, often caused by weakness in the core, especially the abdominal muscles and lower back extensors. To improve stability, try the following modifications. Place two yoga blocks under the feet and shin for added support, or lift only one leg off the ground to reduce the challenge. You can also support the abdomen with a block or small bench, keeping the elbows beside the body rather than directly beneath it. Using a bolster between the arms and abdomen can also help. With this support, explore variations such as lifting one leg at a time, or resting the forehead on the mat while raising both legs. Common problem, limited wrist extension causes pain and overload on the wrist bones. About 90 degrees of wrist extension is needed for safe weight bearing. Without it, forcing the position can strain or injure the base of the palms. Before practicing, bring the forearms and hands together, open the elbows and lower the hands. If the palms separate before 90 degrees, mobility is limited. If they stay together, the range is sufficient. Modification option, adjust hand placement by turning the fingers outward instead of inward to reduce pressure on the wrists. To build safe foundations, use preparatory exercises such as dorsiflexion stretches, wrist flexor stretches, finger palm flexion extension, and wrist flexion on blocks.

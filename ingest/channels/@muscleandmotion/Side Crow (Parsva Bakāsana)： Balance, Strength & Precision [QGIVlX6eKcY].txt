 side-chrome, parsva-bhakasana. An advanced variation of crow pose, side-chrome combines arm balance with a spinal twist. It builds core and shoulder strength, focus, and stability. Precision is key as small shifts in wrists, spine, or shoulder alignment affect balance. Common challenges include lifting into and holding the pose. In the common problems, we address each with solutions to make the asana more accessible. To practice, start in vajrasana, lift the hips, raise the knees, and place the toes on the mat. Twist right, bringing the left elbow across the outside of the right knee, and plant the palms on the floor. Shift weight forward, rest the knees on the arms, and lift both feet. Keep the gaze slightly forward and down, breathe steadily, and engage the core. The lumbar and thoracic spinal vertebrae are in rotation. The cervical vertebrae are in extension. On the right side, the external oblique and the quadratus lumborum are stretched and assist in rotation of the body. The multifidus is also stretched. The right shoulder joint is inflection. The anterior deltoid contracts and stabilizes the shoulder, and the pectoralis major also assists in stabilization. The right elbow joint is inflection. The triceps brachii contract to stabilize the arms. Both wrist joints are in palmar flexion, while the wrist flexor muscles contract. The scapula is in the lower back. The scapula is in protraction. The serratus anterior helps to stabilize the body. Common problem, thoracic stiffness and limited spinal rotation can prevent safe practice of this pose. Side crow requires a solid foundation of spinal mobility and range of motion, and attempting it without this may cause strain or injury. An initial check is recommended. Sit on a block, bring the opposite hand across the thigh, and join the palms together. If holding this position is difficult, spinal rotation should be improved with preparatory exercises. Common problem, limited wrist extension causes pain and overload on the wrist bones. About 90 degrees of wrist extension is needed for safe weight bearing. Without it, forcing the position can strain or injure the base of the palms. Before practicing, bring the forearms and hands together, open the elbows and lower the hands. If the palms separate before 90 degree, mobility is limited. If they stay together, the range is sufficient. To build safe foundations, use preparatory exercises such as dorsiflexion stretches, wrist flexor stretches, finger palm flexion extension, and wrist flexion on blocks. Always work with control and proper technique. Common problem, a weak core, shoulder girdle, or arms can make side crow hard to lift into and sustain. Solution one, elevate the pelvis on a stool or blocks to make shifting into the pose easier. Place hands on the mat and transfer weight forward while supported. Solution two, from the setup, lift one leg while keeping the other foot grounded. Solution three, start with both feet on a block to raise the pelvis, easing the twist and weight transfer. Begin by lifting just one leg. Common problem, stiffness in the lower back can prevent reaching the full pose. Solution, elevate the pelvis, sit on a block or stool to raise the hips. The higher the pelvis, the easier it becomes to enter the pose. From here, place the hands on the mat and gradually shift weight toward the arms while the pelvis remains supported. This elevation reduces strain and makes the assina more accessible. Common problem, lack of confidence and fear of falling forward. Solution, place a cushion or bolster in front of the body. This creates a safe landing surface in case of a fall and reduces the fear of injury. With this support, practitioners can attempt the pose more confidently, gradually building trust in their balance and strength.

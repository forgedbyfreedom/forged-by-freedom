Sidelineing lateral raise incline bench dumbbell. The side lying lateral raise on an incline bench places the deltoid in an elongated position at the start of the movement. Lie on your side on an incline bench, ensuring your body is fully supported. Hold a dumbbell in your top hand with a neutral grip, positioning it in front of your body at the midline to fully stretch the deltoid. Raise your arm laterally in a controlled motion until it reaches just above shoulder height. Slowly lower the dumbbell back to the starting position, maintaining constant tension in the shoulder. Repeat for the desired number of repetitions. Then switch sides. The middle deltoid abducts the shoulder with the assistance of the anterior and posterior deltoid. The supraspanatus plays a crucial role in initiating shoulder abduction. The upper and lower trapezius and the seratus anterior rotate the scapula upward. Lateral raise versus sidelineing. Lateral raise. In a standard lateral raise, the deltoid experiences peak resistance when the arm is parallel to the ground at 90° of shoulder abduction. In a sidelineing lateral raise, maximal resistance occurs much earlier at approximately 45 den of shoulder abduction. This places greater tension on the deltoid in a more stretched position than the standard lateral raise, making it particularly effective for strengthening the muscle in a more lengthened position.

 spinning bike. The spinning bike is a low-impact, high-intensity cardio exercise that improves endurance and allows precise control over workout intensity through resistance adjustments. The pedal stroke has four phases, each with a distinct role in specific muscle actions. In the power phase, the glutus maximus extends the hip, and the quadriceps extend the knee, driving the pedal downward. A slight heel drop engages the hamstrings early to assist with hip extension. In the pullback phase, the hamstrings flex the knee while the calves assist with ankle planter flexion, keeping the movement smooth and continuous. During the upstroke, the hip flexors lift the thigh, and the hamstrings continue to assist with knee flexion. This prevents the leg from becoming passive and reduces drag on the opposite side. The setup phase prepares the leg for the next push. The knee begins to move forward, and the ankle shifts toward dorsiflexion, positioning the body to reengage the glutes and quads as the cycle begins again.

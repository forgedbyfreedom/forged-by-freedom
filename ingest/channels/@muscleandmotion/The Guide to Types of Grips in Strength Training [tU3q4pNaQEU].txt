The closed grip is commonly utilized in both pulling and pushing exercises. The open grip variation is commonly used in pulling exercises. In pushing exercises such as bench press, the nickname suicide grip was earned due to safety concerns. Next is the underhand grip or supinated grip. This grip involves greater biceps engagement but requires a full range of wrist supination which may not be suitable for every exercise. Typical exercises for this grip include barbell curls and chin-ups. The mixed grip, also known as the alternate grip, combines one hand with an overhand grip, causing shoulder internal rotation, and the other with an underhand grip, causing shoulder external rotation. This grip is ideal for lifting heavier weights as it prevents the bar from rolling. It's particularly beneficial for heavy deadlifts and powerlifting because it reduces the strain on the brachioradialis and flexor carpial narus muscles, making it easier to maintain during intense lifts. The hook grip involves wrapping your fingers around the bar and hooking your thumb under your fingers. While it provides a secure grip for heavy lifts, it can be uncomfortable or painful for the thumb. The hook grip is particularly common in Olympic lifts, such as snatches clean and jerks. Compared to the mixed grip, the hook grip provides stability during explosive movements without limiting the range of motion. Additionally, it promotes a more balanced shoulder position as both hands are internally rotated. The false grip, also known as the thumbless grip, is a technique where the wrist, rather than just the fingers, engages with the bar or gymnastic rings. Here, the bar is positioned close to the wrist crease, which differs from a standard grip that aligns with the palm. This strategic grip is crucial for facilitating smoother transitions between complex movements. It is particularly valuable in exercises like the muscle-up, which integrates a pull-up with a dip. By employing the false grip, athletes can fluidly progress from the pulling phase directly into the dip phase without the need to readjust their grip. In summary, there are several distinct grips, each designed for specific purposes. These include the overhand grip, underhand grip, mixed grip, hook grip, and false grip. Each has different benefits and should be used according to your training goals.

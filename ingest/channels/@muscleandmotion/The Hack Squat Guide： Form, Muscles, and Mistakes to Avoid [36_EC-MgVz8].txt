 hack squat machine. The hack squat is a compound lower-body exercise that allows for heavy quadriceps loading in a stable, controlled environment. It minimizes lower back stress and offers a more beginner-friendly alternative to free weight squats. Stand on the platform with your back against the pad and shoulders under the support pads, and place your feet shoulder-width apart. Unlock the safety handles and slowly lower the sled by bending at your hips and knees, keeping your back flat against the pad. Descend until your thighs are parallel to the platform or slightly below, then push through your heels to return to the starting position. Repeat for the desired number of repetitions. The glutus maximus extends the hip with the assistance of the hamstrings, increasing their contribution as hip flexion becomes deeper. Due to the machine's back support, activation of the core and spinal stabilizers is minimal. The calves contribute to ankle stability throughout the entire movement. Common mistake, lifting the heels off the platform at the bottom of the squat, shifting the pressure toward the toes. To avoid this mistake, keep your entire foot flat on the platform, especially the heels. Adjust your foot position if needed and descend only as far as your ankle mobility allows while maintaining full foot contact. Common mistake, placing the feet too high on the platform. This often leads to compensating with lumbar spine flexion, causing the pelvis to lift off the machine toward the end of the range of motion instead of achieving proper hip flexion. As a result, glute engagement is reduced and unnecessary strain is placed on the lower back. To correct this, position your feet lower on the platform, allowing your knees to move forward as you descend. Common mistake, dropping too quickly. Dropping too quickly during the eccentric phase reduces the load and relies on the stretch reflex during the concentric phase. This is less effective for exercises focused on hypertrophy as it shifts the focus away from controlled muscle engagement and strength development in favor of explosive power.

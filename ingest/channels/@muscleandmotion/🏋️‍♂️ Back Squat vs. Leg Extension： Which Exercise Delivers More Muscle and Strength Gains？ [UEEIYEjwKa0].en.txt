Back squat versus leg extension. Which builds more muscle and strength? A recent study found that after eight weeks of training, the leg extension led to greater growth in the rectus feorous while the back squat increased the vastis lateralis especially in its lower region. Why does this happen? The rectus femmeris crosses both the hip and the knee. During a squat, the rectus feorous lengthens at one joint while shortening at the other, which limits its ability to generate tension. In the leg extension, the hip remains stable. So, the muscle focuses entirely on extending the knee, leading to more growth. When it comes to strength, back squat training significantly improves squat performance. But both groups showed similar increases in leg extension strength.

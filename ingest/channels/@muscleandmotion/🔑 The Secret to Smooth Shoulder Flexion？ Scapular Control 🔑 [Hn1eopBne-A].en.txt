Think shoulder flexion is all about the front deltoid? Not quite. To lift your arm properly, the scapula must remain stable and move in sync. Lack of stability causes the scapula to shift forward and tilt. Two key muscles help prevent that. The serratus anterior anchors the scapula to the rib cage and the lower trapezius pulls it downward. These muscles not only move the scapula but also stabilize it dynamically during arm movements. Without proper scapular control, shoulder flexion becomes less efficient. So next time you raise your arm, stabilize the scapula first.
